edition on production, taken from a Claude cloud session with no access to any
local machine. Every number here was read from **https://tinct.app** through
the same public routes the reader uses.

anywhere with outbound HTTPS and no credentials.

## Verification result

`verify_timings.py` was run over a 500-chapter spread across 134 editions —
**11,373 paragraphs, 1,269,102 timed words**. Every chapter passed every check:
identity, paragraph mapping, full coverage of spoken paragraphs, finite ordered
in-bounds timestamps, and agreement with the published edition text.

498 of the 500 chapters matched the edition text perfectly on their weakest
paragraph. The single lowest worst-paragraph score in the whole sample was
0.9231 (The Prince `modern-en` ch10) — well clear of the 0.85 gate.

So the timings that are already published are sound. The problem is the 4,864
chapters that have no timings at all, not the 7,547 that do.

This is automated cross-checking against text and manifest structure, not human
listening. It cannot catch a recording that is word-for-word correct but drifts
acoustically, which is what the acoustic anchor checks in the completion plan
are for.

## What this does and does not establish

This is a **coverage census**. A chapter counted as timed here has a sidecar
present and nothing more. Presence is not correctness — that is what
`verify_timings.py` is for, and `verification.json` holds the result of running
it over a sample. Eligibility, likewise, does not certify timing accuracy.

# Pilot visual review

All eight selected artworks were inspected full-size and again after deterministic typesetting in a true 108×162 thumbnail proof.

| Title | Category | Version | Review |
|---|---|---:|---|
| The Bible | Scripture | v1 | Pass. Branching river is distinctive and avoids literal devotional iconography. Some fine city detail disappears at thumbnail, but the river silhouette holds. |
| The Odyssey | Epic | v1 | Pass. Tiny sail and wine-dark wave field are immediate at thumbnail; the eye-like curls add unease without literal monsters. |
| The Republic | Philosophy | v1 | Pass. City/cave vertical logic survives reduction and gives the title a specific intellectual motif. |
| Pride and Prejudice | Novel | v1 | Pass. Strong paired silhouettes and social interval; warmer/lighter than the rest without leaving the family. |
| Hamlet | Drama | v1 | Pass. Empty throne and split shadow read well; court witnesses add surveillance. No skull cliché. |
| The Histories | History | v2 | Pass after iteration. v1 was rejected as too literal and tourist-map-like, with stereotyped pictograms. v2 reduces the idea to competing line systems, river, and convergence. |
| Frederick Douglass | Memoir | v1 | Pass after typesetting correction. Chain-to-pen motif is unusually clear; title color was changed to parchment because the artwork's upper band is black. |
| The Art of War | Strategy | v1 | Pass. Opposing flows and central void communicate position over spectacle; avoids warriors and weapons. |

The main system risk is sameness from repeated parchment bands. Before a 100-cover run, define 4–6 approved layout families that preserve deterministic type and series coherence while varying the image field, rather than accepting identical top/bottom proportions for every title.

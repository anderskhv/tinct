# Tinct Growth Plan — From Good Product to Famous One

**Created:** 2026-06-10. Owner: Anders. Companion docs: `SEO-STRATEGY.md` (search harvest), `REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` Phase 5 (mechanical SEO), `STRATEGY.md` (positioning).
**Budget posture:** Anders is willing to spend. This plan caps experimental spend at ~**DKK 3,500/mo (~$500)** plus one-offs, reviewed monthly — enough to move every lever below; more money than this does not buy more fame at Tinct's stage.

---

## The honest premise

Famous sites are not famous because of SEO. Standard Ebooks, Project Gutenberg, SparkNotes, Genius — each got famous from ONE remarkable thing, repeated and distributed for years: a public good, a first, a story journalists wanted to tell. SEO then harvested the demand their fame created.

Tinct's remarkable thing already exists and is unannounced: **the entire Western canon, freshly translated into modern English and modern Danish, free, in the most beautiful reader on the web, with an AI companion.** Nobody else has this. The growth plan is: name it, stage it, tell it, repeat it — and let search + AI search harvest.

Three tracks, run in parallel. Track A creates the things worth talking about. Track B does the talking. Track C captures the demand.

## Track A — Product moments (make Tinct remarkable, not just good)

### A1. Name the editions: "The Tinct Editions" (the Standard Ebooks play)
The modern translations are currently an unnamed feature. Make them a *citable public good*:
- A manifesto page: why the classics deserve living translations, what a Tinct Edition is, the craft standards, the catalog. (Anders writes this — it's an essay, and it's the press kit.)
- Every book gets "a Tinct Edition" identity on its SEO pages and in the reader.
- This is positioning, not code. Cost: a writing block or two. **This single move converts "a reading app" into "a library" — institutions get famous, apps get downloads.**

### A2. "The Year of War and Peace" — serialized canon reading (the Bible-in-a-Year play)
Anders knows firsthand why Fr. Mike's Bible in a Year works: a daily portion, a guide's voice, a calendar, a community of people on the same page on the same day. Precedent in our exact space: #TolstoyTogether (A Public Space, 2020) made *War and Peace in 85 days* a genuine cultural moment.
- Product: a reading-plan layer — daily portion (the per-chapter infrastructure exists), a 2-minute companion note (CLI-generated to Tinct quality standards), email delivery, a public day-counter page, optional AI discussion of today's portion.
- Launch with ONE program (War and Peace in a Year, or The Odyssey in 90 Days as the shorter pilot). New Year's cohort = the natural PR moment; a pilot cohort in September proves the mechanics.
- This creates: daily retention, a shareable identity ("Day 142 of War and Peace"), an email list (the only audience channel we'd own), and a recurring press story every January.
- Cost: agent build time + email service (~$20/mo). **Highest ceiling of anything in this plan.**

### A3. Shareable passage pages (social objects)
Famous-passage pages with the original/modern toggle as the hook — beautiful quote-card images (Tinct visual identity) generated per passage, one click to share. "The opening of Anna Karenina, 1877 vs today." These are what gets screenshotted and passed around; every share is a branded artifact. Cost: one page template + an og-image generator in the worker.

### A4. The reader as demo (the "holy shit it's beautiful" moment)
A 45-second screen-capture video of the actual product — split-pane original/modern, the chat, audio, dark/e-ink — for the landing page, Show HN, Product Hunt, and every pitch. Fame requires that the first 10 seconds of contact impress. Cost: an afternoon. (Phase 1-4 of REMEDIATION-PLAN make the product worthy of the video — the bug fixes ARE marketing.)

## Track B — The story machine (distribution)

### B1. The launches (free, highest single-event upside)
1. **Show HN:** "I translated 33 classics into modern English and built a free reader." Concrete, demo-able, slightly audacious — strong HN shape. Do AFTER Phase 5.1-5.2 + the demo video. One shot; stage it.
2. **Product Hunt:** same assets, separate day.
3. **The January moment:** "Year of War and Peace" cohort launch (A2) — its own news cycle.

### B2. The Danish story (disproportionately winnable)
Ex-Jabra SVP leaves corporate life, spends his sabbatical translating the world's classics into modern Danish, gives them away free. That is a *culture-section feature story*, not a tech story — Politiken, Berlingske, Weekendavisen, Kristeligt Dagblad, DR. Danish press → real DR-70+ backlinks → the entire English surface lifts. Pitch directly (founders get meetings in Denmark); spend on a freelance PR person only if DIY stalls (~DKK 10-15k one-off, decide in August).

### B3. Niche sponsorships + community (paid, targeted)
- Book-world newsletters and podcasts (lit-fic Substacks, classics podcasts): $100-500 per placement, audience-perfect. 2/month.
- Genuine participation in r/books, r/classicliterature, r/literature, #BookTok adjacents — contribute, don't promote (marketing lead's standing rule).
- Seed the network: Naval (already an Into Infinity target), Logan/Conjecture, the Deutsch-Twitter cluster via @FallibleMusings — the fun-criterion framing of reading classics IS Anders's existing content lane.

### B4. Teachers and reading groups (the institutional wedge)
A "Tinct for classrooms" one-pager: free, no ads, no signup needed to read, modern + original side by side — exactly what a gymnasium/high-school teacher wants. Teacher adoption = recurring cohorts of readers + .edu/.dk backlinks. Cost: a page + outreach emails.

## How we'll know it's working (quarterly, not daily)

- Email list size (A2) — the owned audience; the single best fame-proxy we control.
- Referring domains (Ahrefs) — target: 10 real ones by December.
- Search clicks/week (GSC+Bing combined) — target: 100/wk by December (from ~2 today).
- One earned-media feature by year end (Danish or international).
- Reader retention (finished books per cohort) — fame built on a product people abandon is borrowed time.

A deliberate non-metric: follower counts and viral one-offs. (Healthy reception vs. metrics-validation — the distinction is Anders's own.)

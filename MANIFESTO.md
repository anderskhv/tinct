# Tinct — Manifesto

## We've lost touch with what made us

Have you felt it too? The intuition that there's something you need, hidden away in Homer, the Bible or Shakespeare? The boredom and confusion when you give them a go? The resignation when you finally give up - and go back to scroll a feed or listen to a low-calorie podcast? The emptiness? The lack of depth?

The classics are the books that made the West. They have given joy, meaning and guidance to millions of people for thousands of years. They were also old when your great-grandparents read them. And their great-grandparents too. But as of today, this transmission mechanism is broken. The books remain as interesting as they have always been, but we struggle to access their richness. 

We're force-fed them in schools, often by dispassionate and disdainful teachers. We come to share those sentiments. We look up a summary instead of reading, and come away thinking they are flat. We try again, years later, but get stuck on language we don't quite understand. We forget what page we got to. We forget what Rodya has been up to on page 62 and miss that Rodya, Rodion and Raskolnikov are Russian versions of the same name. We fail to see the treasures that await us if we read the book with the right angle.

This is not a small loss. These treasure troves are incredibly rich and incredibly dense. Each paragraph is dense with knowledge, suspense and fun. When you finish one, you think differently. When a generation finishes them, a civilization thinks differently. Right now, this is not happening enough.

## Tinct, a new way to read

Tinct is a new way to read, understand and fall in love with the Classics. It's built for all of us that want to engage them, argue with them, wrestle them, but have struggled with other means. Here's what reading with Tinct is like:

You open the Odyssey, on your phone, tablet, or e-reader. You pick what version you want - a modern translation, an original, or both at the same time in split-pane view. The modern translations are focused on maximizing understanding whilst staying true to the text. They do not editorialise, or moralise.

Tinct then asks you what draws you to that book - the adventure, understand Greek vs. Christian ethics, whatever interests you. Your answer goes directly into your AI companion that reads the book along with you. It always knows where you are and combined with your reading angle, it answers your questions with specificity, and helps you find any valuable angles you missed.

You pick up the phone on the commute. The audiobook picks up exactly where you stopped reading last night.

You forget who Menelaus is. You tap "Casts", the character tracker, and get a quick overview - only of what you've read about him so far.

You wonder if Odysseus is telling the truth, or lying, to the Phaeacians? You tap "Chat" and discuss it with the AI companion.

You finish a chapter, your thoughts, highlights and chats are stored to a "Feed" that grows as you do. You will never have to rely on your memory to remember which Proverbs you've read and what you thought about them.

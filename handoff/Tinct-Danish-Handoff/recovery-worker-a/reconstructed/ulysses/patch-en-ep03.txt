3 58 Noon slumbers. Kevin Egan rolls gunpowder cigarettes through fingers smeared with printer's ink, sipping his green absinthe as his son Patrice sips his white drink. Around us diners fork spiced beans down their throats. A half-pint, please! A jet of coffee steam from the burnished urn. She serves me at his signal. He's Irish. Dutch? No, not cheese. Two Irishmen, we are, from Ireland, you know, ah yes! She thought you wanted Dutch cheese. Your post-prandial drink — do you know that word? Post-prandial. There was a fellow I knew once in Barcelona, odd fellow, used to call it his post-prandial. Well: cheers! Around the slabbed tables the tangle of wine-soaked breaths and rumbling stomachs. His breath hangs over our sauce-stained plates, the green fairy's fang of absinthe thrusting between his lips. Of Ireland, the warriors of Brian Boru, of hopes, conspiracies, of Arthur Griffith the nationalist, the mystical poet A.E., good shepherd of men. Trying to yoke me as his fellow revolutionary, our crimes our common cause. You're your father's son. I know the voice. His worn shirt, decorated with red flowers, trembles its Spanish tassels at his secrets. Drumont, famous French journalist — know what he called Queen Victoria? Old hag with the yellow teeth. Old ogress with the yellow teeth. Maud Gonne, beautiful woman, _La Patrie_. Millevoye, Félix Faure — know how Faure died? Lustful men. The Scandinavian girl, maid of all work, who rubs men naked in the bath at Uppsala. It's my job, she said, all the gentlemen. Not this gentleman, I said. Most indecent custom. A bath is a most private thing. I wouldn't let my brother, not even my own brother, it's most indecent. Green eyes, I see you. Fangs, I feel. Lustful people.
3 60 Spurned lover. I was a strapping young lad at that time, I tell you. I'll show you my picture one day. I was, truly. A lover — for her love, for Ireland's love, he prowled with Colonel Richard Burke, designated heir of his clan, under the walls of Clerkenwell Prison and, crouching, saw a flame of vengeance hurl them upward in the fog. Shattered glass and toppling masonry. In gay Paris he hides, Egan of Paris, sought by no one save by me. Making his daily rounds: the dingy print shop, his three taverns, the Montmartre den where he sleeps short nights, in rue de la Goutte-d’Or, decorated with flyblown photographs of the dead. Loveless, landless, wifeless. She is quite comfortably settled without her outcast man, the madam on rue Gît-le-Cœur, with a canary and two male lodgers. Peachy cheeks, a striped skirt, frisky as a young thing. Spurned and undespairing. Tell Pat you saw me, won't you? I wanted to get poor Pat a job one time. My son, soldier of France. I taught him to sing The Boys of Kilkenny Are Stout Roaring Blades. Know that old tune? I taught Patrice that. Old Kilkenny: Saint Canice's city, Strongbow's castle on the River Nore. Goes like this. Oh, oh. He takes my hand, like Napper Tandy in the song.

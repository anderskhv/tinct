# Middlemarch — witnesses and editorial scope

**Copy text.** George Eliot, *Middlemarch*, H. M. Caldwell Company, New York and Boston. The retained English source and its sectioning follow Project Gutenberg eBook 145, updated 12 April 2026. The complete plain text fetched for this preparation is `reference/pg145.txt`. SHA-256 is `dc2e0107e6ae07e8e33da934b2ec4e8e600826a2e0cd48df265119cd02e5fb50`; it also matches the digest in the previous Tinct source notes. The [edition record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/145) and [plain text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/145/pg145.txt) identify this witness.

**Independent check of structure and transcription.** All 88 reading units match the complete Caldwell text from their opening to their closing words when whitespace alone is normalized. All 4,674 paragraph positions have nonempty Danish and modern English counterparts. A second collation aligns every chapter with the [Standard Ebooks text](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/george-eliot/middlemarch), whose colophon credits both Project Gutenberg and [print scans](https://archive.org/details/middlemarchstudy0000elio). This second edition shares Gutenberg ancestry. Its word-token differences against Caldwell are in `qa/secondary-collation.json`; this comparison ignores punctuation, case, line wrapping and paragraph breaks. The secondary snapshot, its archival bytes and the commit ID are in `reference/`. None of the 721 difference runs has five consecutive words present only in one text. Examples include compounds such as “any one”/“anyone”, the placement of the Cervantes credit, and “polities”/“politics”. This check cannot assess how well any passage reads in Danish.

**Consequential reading.** In Eliot’s Chapter XX, reader unit 21, paragraph index 29, Caldwell reads “appreciated” where Standard Ebooks and [Blackwood’s 1874 text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Middlemarch_(1874)/Chapter_20) read “ill-appreciated”. The missing prefix reverses what Casaubon imagines about authors. Both modern candidates adopt the corroborated reading. The Caldwell file remains available for comparison. In unit 38 the “tack”/“track” difference is recorded in the collation. Both variants support a figure for putting someone on a productive course.

**Danish and English baselines.** The retained main-tree editions are archived in `reference/modern-da.json`, `reference/modern-en.json` and `reference/original-en.json`. Their Git object IDs match the tree at commit `a1ee0ffda8de113b06814e2986802f90e24eccff` of anderskhv/tinct. See `reference/upstream.tsv`. The Danish copy under `books/wip/middlemarch/editions/` has the same SHA-256 as the retained app edition. No fresh translation service was used.

**Opening matter and navigation.** Eight books contain Chapters I–LXXXVI. Tinct’s flat navigation gives the Prelude unit 1, Chapter I unit 2, Chapter LXXXVI unit 87 and the Finale unit 88. Some older working copies instead number Prelude 0 and Finale 87. Book headings are included in the chapter titles. Roman numerals also identify Eliot’s actual chapters, independently of the reader numbers. The Caldwell title page’s dedication to Lewes was missing from the retained editions. Original text and new Danish and modern English versions are in `front-matter.json` for unnumbered display before the Prelude. A generated contents list and the publisher’s imprint are represented by navigation and provenance. The dedication is a separate editorial payload because inserting a numbered first chapter would move every existing chapter address. The 1874 [Finale](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Middlemarch_(1874)/Finale) breaks up material more finely towards its end; the Caldwell paragraphs are the anchor baseline here.

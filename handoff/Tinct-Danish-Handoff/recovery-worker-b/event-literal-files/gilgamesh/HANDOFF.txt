Scope and source. Both reading texts are new prose renderings of R. Campbell Thompson, The Epic of Gilgamish: A New Translation from a Collation of the Cuneiform Tablets in the British Museum, Rendered Literally into English Hexameters (London: Luzac, 1928), tablets I–XII, printed pp. 9–60. This is through a historical English intermediary. It is not presented as a direct new translation from Akkadian, nor as a current critical reconstruction. The PDF (thompson-1) is the local facsimile; thompson-0 is the Internet Archive's uncorrected OCR, thompson-layout.txt another extraction. Scan: https://archive.org/details/thompson-1928-gilgamesh . The original historical publication, not the host's interface or a recent adaptation, is the source of the new reading texts. No third-party historical prefaces or footnote apparatus were copied into the guides. Embedded gaps/editorial summaries, repeating lines, alternate witnesses and the lament in XII have been retained. Technical apparatus not reproduced; occasional partial lines are grouped. The renderer should respect paragraph-internal line breaks in XII. Units retained without speculative SI conversions. Danish mil renders the translated distance unit, not a specified Danish mile value; stangmål renders the gar. Mineral names and uncertain fittings sometimes have cautious descriptive equivalents.

Native/editorial spots. Tablet IV's sickbed is presented at the forest gate in Thompson's dated arrangement; it reappears in the death tradition. XI uses Thompson's staging of a single loaf and his snake uttering a curse, unlike other readings of old loaves and a shed skin. In XII the narrator's subject is not named in the taboo sequence; the source makes a conjecture in a heading and the new texts keep “he”. XII is an appendix, with a different premise from Enkidu's death in VII–VIII. The historical end of XII here is the hungry unattended ghost. Distinguish an actual gap from two words the 1928 English translator suppressed in Ishtar's invitation (VI). No Danish or English gap was filled from a copyrighted modern translation.

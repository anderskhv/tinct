Listen, wicked knight; / hold your reins a moment. / Do not weary the flanks / of your badly guided beast. / See, false man, you are fleeing / not from a fierce serpent, / but from a little lamb / still far from being a sheep. / You have deceived, dreadful monster, / the most beautiful maiden / Diana saw upon her mountains / or Venus in her woods. / Cruel Bireno, fleeing Aeneas, / may Barabbas go with you; settle things with him!
You carry off, impious thief, / in the talons of your claws, / the very heart of a humble girl, / tender with love. / You take three headscarves / and garters from a pair of legs / that equal pure marble / in smoothness, whiteness, and blackness. / You take two thousand sighs, / which, were they made of fire, / could burn two thousand Troys, / if two thousand Troys existed. / Cruel Bireno, fleeing Aeneas, / may Barabbas go with you; settle things with him!
May the feelings of that Sancho, / your squire, become so stubborn / and so hard that Dulcinea / never escapes her enchantment. / May that unhappy woman suffer / the punishment for your fault, / for in my country the innocent / sometimes pay for sinners. / May your finest adventures / turn into misfortunes, / your pleasures into dreams, / your constancy into forgetfulness. / Cruel Bireno, fleeing Aeneas, / may Barabbas go with you; settle things with him!
May you be held false / from Seville to Marchena, / from Granada to Loja, / from London to England. / If you play reinado, / cientos, or primera, / may kings flee from you; / may you see neither aces nor sevens. / If you cut your corns, / may the wounds bleed, / and may the roots remain / when your molars are pulled. / Cruel Bireno, fleeing Aeneas, / may Barabbas go with you; settle things with him!

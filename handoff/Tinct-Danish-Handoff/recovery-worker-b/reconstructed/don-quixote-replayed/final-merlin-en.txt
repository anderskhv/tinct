I am Merlin, of whom the histories
say that the devil was my father
(a lie given authority by time),
prince and monarch of magic,
and storehouse of Zoroastrian learning,
rival of the ages and the centuries
that try to bury the exploits
of brave knights-errant,
whom I have loved and still hold dear.
Although enchanters,
wizards and magicians always have
a hard, harsh, unyielding nature,
mine is tender, gentle and loving,
and eager to do good to everyone.
In the gloomy caverns of Dis,
where my soul was occupied
in drawing certain magic figures and signs,
there came the sorrowful cry of the beautiful,
peerless Dulcinea del Toboso.
I learned of her enchantment and misfortune,
and her transformation from a noble lady
into a rustic peasant girl. I pitied her,
and, enclosing my spirit in the hollow
of this frightening, fierce skeleton,
after searching a hundred thousand books
of this devilish, shameful science of mine,
I come to bring the proper remedy
for such great suffering, such great harm.
O you, glory and honour of all who wear
tunics of steel and adamant,
light and lantern, path, polestar and guide
of those who leave sluggish sleep
and idle featherbeds behind
to undertake the intolerable labour
of bloody, heavy arms!
I speak to you, O man who can never
be praised as he deserves! To you, brave
and wise Don Quixote,
splendour of La Mancha, star of Spain:
for the peerless Dulcinea del Toboso
to recover her former state,
it is necessary that Sancho, your squire,
give himself three thousand and three hundred lashes
on both his sturdy buttocks,
exposed to the air, and in such a way
that they sting, smart and torment him.
So all those responsible
for her misfortune have decreed,
and that is why I have come, my lords.

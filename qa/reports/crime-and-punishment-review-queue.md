### 1. [Chapter 19, Paragraph 101] (reason: false cognate — "realisere")
- **Original (EN):** "The only difference is that I don't contend that extraordinary people are always bound to commit breaches of morals, as you call it. [...] I simply hinted that an 'extraordinary' man has the right... that is not an official right, but an inner right to decide in his own conscience to overstep... certain obstacles, and only in case it is essential for the practical fulfilment of his idea"
- **Danish:** "Den eneste forskel er, at jeg ikke hævder, at usædvanlige mennesker altid er forpligtede til at begaa uhyrligheder. Jeg antydes blot, at det 'usaedvanlige' menneske har ret ... vel, ikke en officiel ret, men en indre ret til at tillade sin samvittighed at traede over ... visse forhindringer, og det kun i de tilfaelde, hvor det er nodvendigt for at realisere sin ide"
- **Issue:** "realisere sin ide" for "practical fulfilment of his idea." "Realisere" in Danish primarily means "to become aware of / to realize (understand)" not "to make real / fulfil." This is Raskolnikov's famous theory about extraordinary men — the verb matters.
- **Suggestion:** "virkeliggore sin ide" or "fore sin ide ud i livet"

### 2. [Chapter 7, Paragraph 40] (reason: overused verb — "rykkede")
- **Original (EN):** "And again, enraged, he tugged with all his might a dozen times at the bell. He must certainly be a man of authority and an intimate acquaintance."
- **Danish:** "Rasende rykkede han i klokken et dusin gange med al sin kraft. Han var tydeligvis en mand med en vis autoritet og en naer bekendt."
- **Issue:** "rykkede i klokken" for "tugged at the bell." Here "rykkede" works acceptably as "yanked/pulled," but it's one of 21 uses across the book. Consider whether a more vivid verb fits the rage.
- **Suggestion:** "Rasende hev han i klokken" or "Rasende rev han i klokken" — both convey violent pulling better.

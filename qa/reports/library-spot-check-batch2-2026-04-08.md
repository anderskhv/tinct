## 1. Ulysses (ch1 + ch9)

### Scores

| Sample | Accuracy | Fluency | Literary | Translationese |

| Ch1 P1 (opening) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Ch1 P183 (milkwoman) | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 |

| Ch9 P15 (Cranly/Kathleen) | **2** | 3 | 3 | 4 |
| **Average** | **4.25** | **4.25** | **4.0** | **4.5** |

### Joyce Style Preservation
The ch1 opening is excellent -- "Statelig og buttet" for "Stately, plump" nails the register, and the Danish flows with the same rhythmic weight as the original. The milkwoman passage preserves the stream-of-consciousness drift well: "dugsilkede kvaeg" for "dewsilky cattle" is inventive and Joycean. Compound neologisms ("paddehattestol", "blodtknirkende-fodet") show the translator engaging with Joyce's word-coinage instinct.

### Flagged Issue (CRITICAL)
**Ch9 P15**: The second half of the paragraph is fabricated. Original says: "And one more to hail him: _ave, rabbi_: the Tinahely twelve. In the shadow of the glen he cooees for them. My soul's youth I gave him, night by night. God speed. Good hunting." Danish version replaces this with an invented Kathleen quote: "_Denne land er mit. Disse maend er mit folk._" This is not a translation -- it is hallucinated content. The Synge allusion ("In the shadow of the glen") and the Latin are both dropped.

### Verdict: NEEDS REVIEW
Strong overall quality, but the ch9 fabrication is a critical defect. Requires a full ch9 paragraph-by-paragraph audit.

### Dramatic Force
The Mercutio death scene is excellent. "Forbandet vaere begge jeres huse!" is a strong, natural rendering of "A plague o' both your houses" -- the Danish "forbandet vaere" carries the curse-weight well. "En skramme, en skramme. Men det er nok." perfectly captures the bitter irony.

### Prologue
The verse-to-prose conversion works. "Stjerneforvildede elskende" for "star-cross'd lovers" is evocative and natural Danish. The prologue expands slightly (prose needs more words than verse) but stays faithful. One minor accuracy note: the original's "Two households, both alike in dignity" becomes "to familier af lige rang" -- "rang" (rank) is slightly narrower than "dignity" but acceptable.

### Verdict: PASS

### Ferdinand's Speech
The long speech is well handled. "Hor min sjael tale" for "Hear my soul speak" is direct and strong. "I det selv samme ojeblik, jeg sa dig, floj mit hjerte hen for at tjene dig" accurately and beautifully renders the original's declaration. "Denne talmodige braendebaerer" for "this patient log-man" is a smart localization -- "braendebaerer" (wood-carrier) communicates the same image without the compound noun awkwardness.

### Minor Translationese
"Skont jeg onskede, jeg ikke var det" -- the subordinate clause structure is slightly English-influenced. More natural Danish might be "skont jeg gerne ville vaere fri for det" or similar. Not serious.

### Verdict: PASS

## Critical Issues

1. **Ulysses ch9 P15 -- fabricated content.** The Danish replaces the second half of the paragraph with invented text. This is a data integrity issue, not a translation quality issue. Requires full audit of Ulysses ch9 (and possibly other chapters) for similar hallucinations.

## General Observations

- The four Shakespeare plays are uniformly strong. Stage directions are natural, character voice is preserved, and the verse-to-prose conversion maintains dramatic energy without losing meaning.
- Danish-specific pitfalls (false cognates, dropped verb prefixes, translationese word order) are largely absent across all 5 books.
- The Shakespeare translations tend toward slight expansion (more words than the original), which is expected and acceptable for verse-to-prose.
- Ulysses ch1 shows the translator can handle Joyce's style well -- the ch9 issue appears to be a generation artifact rather than systematic quality failure.

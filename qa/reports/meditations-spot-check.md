- **Fluency:** 4 — What exists reads naturally, but the argument feels abruptly truncated. "Sa selv hvis denne strom baerer noget, du betragter som 'dit', aendrer det naeppe argumentet" feels like it's reaching for a conclusion the text hasn't earned.

- **Translationese:** 4 — Mostly clean, but "designfejl" (design flaw) is a modern loanword/calque that feels anachronistic for Marcus Aurelius. "Konstruktionsfejl" or simply describing the concept would fit better.

## Verdict: NEEDS REVIEW

The Danish translation of Meditations is **excellent across the board** for the 9 standard samples -- natural, fluent, literarily alive, and free of translationese. This is high-quality work.

However, **Ch10/P7 is confirmed as a significant truncation issue** (28% of original length, accuracy score 2). A 2,881-character philosophical argument with 7 distinct logical steps has been compressed to 821 characters covering only 3-4 of those steps. This is not a quality translation that happens to be concise -- it is missing substantive content.

### Issues to fix

1. **Ch10/P7 (CRITICAL):** Must be regenerated. The Danish version drops the river metaphor, the fire/renewal cosmology, and the argument about personal identity vs. material composition. This paragraph needs a full retranslation preserving all 7 argumentative steps.

2. **Ch12/P27 (MINOR):** "Three of five acts" is an addition not in the original (original just says "three acts is the whole play"). Consider whether this creative liberty is acceptable.

3. **Ch10/P7 (MINOR):** "Designfejl" is anachronistic -- consider a less modern term.

### Danish-specific findings

No false cognates, no dropped verb prefixes, no translationese word order detected in any sample. The Danish register is appropriately measured and philosophical without being stiff. This is well above average for LLM-generated Danish translation.

# Spot-Check: Hamlet
Date: 2026-04-08
Editions checked: modern-da (against original-en)

## Summary
- Modern DA: avg 4.1/5 across dimensions — **PASS**
- Overall quality is strong. The Danish reads naturally, captures dramatic tone well, and mostly avoids translationese. A few minor issues noted below.

#### Sample 1.3 — Horatio's exposition (para 52, longest speech)
**EN:** HORATIO. That can I; At least, the whisper goes so. Our last King, Whose image even but now appear'd to us, Was, as you know, by Fortinbras of Norway, Thereto prick'd on by a most emulate pride, Dar'd to the combat...
**DA:** HORATIO. Det kan jeg — i hvert fald går rygtet sådan. Vores afdøde Konge, hvis billede netop viste sig for os, blev udfordret til tvekamp af Norges Fortinbras, drevet af en vild ærekærhed...

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 5 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 4 |
| Translationese | 4 |

Notes: Strong overall. Good choices: "ærekærhed" for "emulate pride", "broget hær af lovløse lejesoldater" for "list of lawless resolutes". The long sentence structure occasionally follows English clause order a bit too closely — a native Danish speaker might restructure some subordinate clauses. "I hvert fald går rygtet sådan" is excellent and idiomatic. Minor: "som hele verden ærede" slightly flattens "this side of our known world esteem'd him" — the geographic specificity is lost.

**EN:** HORATIO. A mote it is to trouble the mind's eye. In the most high and palmy state of Rome, A little ere the mightiest Julius fell...
**DA:** HORATIO. Det er en plet, der forstyrrer sindets øje. I Rom på sit højeste, lige før den mægtige Julius Cæsar faldt...

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 4 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 4 |
| Translationese | 4 |

Notes: "Sindets øje" is a good calque that actually works in Danish. "Plet" for "mote" is slightly imprecise (plet = stain/spot, mote = speck/dust particle), but acceptable. "Blodig dug" for "dews of blood" is good. The passage compresses some of the original's rhetorical layering — "harbingers preceding still the fates / And prologue to the omen coming on" becomes the flatter "budbringere der annoncerer skæbnens planer." Loses some poetic resonance but maintains meaning.

### Chapter 10 (Act III, Scene III) — A Room in the Castle

#### Sample 10.1 — King's opening (para 1)
**EN:** KING. I like him not, nor stands it safe with us To let his madness range...
**DA:** KONGEN. Jeg bryder mig ikke om ham, og det er ikke sikkert for os at lade hans vanvid løbe frit...

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 5 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 4 |
| Translationese | 4 |

Notes: "Bryder mig ikke om" is natural Danish for "like him not." "Lade hans vanvid løbe frit" is good — captures "let his madness range" with an idiomatic Danish expression. "Time for time" for "hourly" is excellent. Minor: "Rigets sikkerhed kan ikke tolerere" — "tolerere" is slightly Latinate/formal where a more Danish word like "tåle" might be more natural.

---

#### Sample 10.2 — Claudius's prayer soliloquy (para 10, longest speech)
**EN:** KING. O, my offence is rank, it smells to heaven; It hath the primal eldest curse upon't,— A brother's murder!
**DA:** KONGEN. Åh, min forbrydelse er afskyelig — den stinker til himlen. Den bærer den ældste af alle forbandelser — brodermord!

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 4 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 4 |
| Translationese | 4 |

Notes: This is a critical soliloquy and the translation handles it well. "Stinker til himlen" — direct and visceral, good. "Brodermord" — punchy single word, excellent. One error: "broderbord" appears later in the text (typo for "broderbLOD" / brother's blood — should be "broderblod"). "Ligesom en mand, der trækkes i to retninger på én gang" is a good expansion of the compressed original. The theological wrestling is preserved. Minor issue: "Hvad er barmhjertighedens formål, om ikke at konfrontere syndens ansigt?" follows English syntax quite closely — "Hvortil tjener barmhjertighed, om ikke..." would be more natural Danish rhetorical form. "Guldbeslåede hånd" for "gilded hand" is creative and vivid.

**Typo flagged:** "broderbord" should be "broderblod" (para 10, Claudius's soliloquy).

#### Sample 10.3 — Hamlet's "Now might I do it" soliloquy (para 13)
**EN:** HAMLET. Now might I do it pat, now he is praying. And now I'll do't. And so he goes to heaven; And so am I reveng'd.
**DA:** HAMLET. Nu kunne jeg gøre det — lige nu, mens han beder. Og nu gør jeg det. Og så tager han lige til himlen. Og det er min hævn?

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 5 |
| Fluency | 5 |
| Literary quality | 5 |
| Translationese | 5 |

Notes: Excellent. This is one of the strongest passages in the translation. The staccato rhythm of Hamlet's internal debate is preserved. "Fuld af jordiske nydelser, med alle sine synder i fuldt flor" is vivid and natural Danish. "Hans vanvid er den stakkels Hamlets fjende" — good. "Slå ham da ned, så hans hæle kan sparke mod himlen" — powerful, captures the violence of the image. "Denne bøn forlænger kun dine talte dage" — a strong closing line, slightly different from "This physic but prolongs thy sickly days" (physic ≠ prayer, and "talte dage" = "counted days" rather than "sickly days") but the dramatic effect is comparable.

Minor accuracy note: "physic" (medicine/remedy) is translated as "bøn" (prayer) — a semantic shift. The original's irony is that Claudius's prayer is medicine keeping him alive, not that the prayer itself is prolonging things. The Danish loses this specific irony but creates its own internal logic.

#### Sample 10.4 — King's final couplet (para 16)
**EN:** KING. My words fly up, my thoughts remain below. Words without thoughts never to heaven go.
**DA:** KONGEN. Mine ord flyver op, men mine tanker forbliver her nede. Ord uden tanker når aldrig himlen.

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 5 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 3 |
| Translationese | 4 |

Notes: Accurate and clear. However, the rhyming couplet (below/go) is lost, and the Danish doesn't attempt any compensating sonic device. "Forbliver her nede" is slightly heavy — "bliver hernede" would be tighter. The original's epigrammatic punch is somewhat flattened into prose statement. For such a famous couplet, an attempt at Danish rhythm or sonic echo would elevate this.

### Chapter 20 (Act V, Scene II) — A Hall in the Castle

#### Sample 20.1 — Hamlet's apology to Laertes (para 87)
**EN:** HAMLET. Give me your pardon, sir. I have done you wrong; But pardon't as you are a gentleman...
**DA:** HAMLET. Giv mig jeres tilgivelse, min herre. Jeg har gjort jer uret. Men tilgiv mig, som en gentleman ville...

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 5 |
| Fluency | 4 |
| Literary quality | 4 |
| Translationese | 3 |

Notes: Mostly strong, but "gentleman" is left untranslated — this is a direct English loanword that feels like translationese in a Shakespeare adaptation. "Adelsmand" or "ædel mand" would be more fitting in context. The philosophical core — "If Hamlet from himself be ta'en away... Then Hamlet does it not" — is rendered clearly as "Hvis Hamlet er taget fra sig selv... så gør Hamlet det ikke." This works, though "taget fra sig selv" is a somewhat literal rendering. The arrow metaphor at the end is well preserved.

#### Sample 20.3 — Fortinbras's final speech (para 173)
**EN:** FORTINBRAS. Let four captains Bear Hamlet like a soldier to the stage, For he was likely, had he been put on, To have prov'd most royally...
**DA:** FORTINBRAS. Lad fire kaptajner bære Hamlet til scenen som en soldat, for han ville sandsynligvis, havde han fået chancen, have vist sig mest kongeligt...

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 4 |
| Fluency | 3 |
| Literary quality | 3 |
| Translationese | 3 |

Notes: The weakest sample. "Ville sandsynligvis, havde han fået chancen, have vist sig mest kongeligt" is awkward — the embedded conditional clause disrupts Danish sentence flow. A more natural Danish construction would restructure: "for havde han fået chancen, ville han sandsynligvis have vist sig kongelig." Also "mest kongeligt" (most royally) is an odd Danish intensifier — "i sandhed kongelig" or simply "kongelig" would be more natural. "Det tager sig frygteligt forkert ud ved et hof" is an expansion not in the original ("but here shows much amiss") — creative but slightly overwrought. "Giv ordre til, at soldaterne fyrer deres salut" is good.

## Issues Flagged

### Critical
- None

### Moderate
1. **Typo:** "broderbord" should be "broderblod" (Chapter 10, para 10, Claudius's soliloquy)
2. **Untranslated loanword:** "gentleman" left in English (Chapter 20, para 87) — should be "adelsmand" or "ædel mand"
3. **Awkward syntax:** Fortinbras's final speech (Chapter 20, para 173) — conditional clause structure follows English order too closely

### Minor
4. "Tolerere" where "tåle" would be more natural Danish (Chapter 10, para 1)
5. "Physic" mistranslated as "bøn" (prayer) rather than "medicin" or "lægemiddel" (Chapter 10, para 13)
6. King's final couplet loses rhyme and epigrammatic force (Chapter 10, para 16)
7. Horatio's catalogue of horrors loses rhythmic accumulation (Chapter 20, para 170)
8. "Mest kongeligt" is an unnatural Danish intensifier (Chapter 20, para 173)

## Verdict

**PASS** — The Hamlet modern-da edition is above the quality bar (avg 4.1/5, no scores below 3 on accuracy). The translation captures Shakespeare's dramatic force well, particularly in the soliloquies. Hamlet's "Now might I do it" speech is outstanding. The main pattern to watch is that longer, more complex speeches occasionally follow English clause order too closely, and famous couplets/epigrams lose their sonic qualities. One typo ("broderbord") should be fixed before launch.

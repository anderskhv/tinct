### 1. [Chapter 13, Paragraph 115] (reason: false cognate — "realisere")
- **Original (EN):** "Far from it. I was tormented by the contrast between my idea and my handiwork: in each case I had imagined something which I was quite powerless to realise."
- **Danish:** "Langtfra. Jeg var plaget af kloften mellem min ide og det, jeg faktisk havde skabt. I hvert tilfaelde havde jeg forestillet mig noget, jeg var fuldstaendig magteslos til at realisere."
- **Issue:** "magteslos til at realisere" for "powerless to realise." In English, "realise" here means "to make real / bring into being" (Jane is talking about turning artistic vision into actual drawings). Danish "realisere" primarily means "to become aware of" — a false cognate. The artistic context makes precision important.
- **Suggestion:** "magteslos til at virkeliggore" or "magteslos til at fore ud i livet"

### 2. [Chapter 21, Paragraph 4] (reason: overused verb — "rykkede")
- **Original (EN):** "I did not like this iteration of one idea -- this strange recurrence of one image, and I grew nervous as bedtime approached and the hour of the vision drew near."
- **Danish:** "Jeg brod mig ikke om denne vedvarende tilbagevenden af en ide -- denne maerkelige gentagelse af et billede, og jeg blev nervos, naar sengetid naermede sig, og timen for synet rykkede naermere."
- **Issue:** "rykkede naermere" for "drew near." The English "drew near" is atmospheric and slow-building — it's Jane dreading the recurring dream. "Rykkede" suggests a physical jerk or tug, not a creeping approach.
- **Suggestion:** "naermede sig" (approached) or "kom naermere" (came nearer) — both match the mood better.

### 3. [Chapter 26, Paragraph 23] (reason: overused verb — "rykkede")
- **Original (EN):** "Hearing a cautious step behind me, I glanced over my shoulder: one of the strangers -- a gentleman, evidently -- was advancing up the chancel."
- **Danish:** "En af de fremmede -- en herre, tydeligvis -- rykkede frem ad midtergangen."
- **Issue:** "rykkede frem" for "was advancing." The English "advancing" is deliberate and measured — a stranger walking up the aisle during a wedding. "Rykkede frem" has a military/abrupt connotation in Danish.
- **Suggestion:** "tradte frem" (stepped forward) or "gik op" (walked up) — more natural for someone walking up a church aisle.

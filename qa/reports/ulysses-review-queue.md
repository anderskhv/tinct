### 1. [Chapter 9, Paragraph 16] (reason: FABRICATED CONTENT -- BLOCKING)
- **Original (EN):** "Cranly's eleven true Wicklowmen to free their sireland. Gaptoothed Kathleen, her four beautiful green fields, the stranger in her house. And one more to hail him: _ave, rabbi_: the Tinahely twelve. In the shadow of the glen he cooees for them. My soul's youth I gave him, night by night. God speed. Good hunting."
- **Danish:** "Cranlys elleve aegte Wicklow-maend til at befri deres soirland. Hultandet Kathleen, hendes fire smukke gronne marker, fremmeden i hendes hus. Og en dag ville Kathleen sige: _Denne land er mit. Disse maend er mit folk._"
- **Issue:** The last three sentences of the original are replaced with fabricated Irish nationalist speech that does not exist in Joyce's text. The original contains: (a) "_ave, rabbi_: the Tinahely twelve" -- a specific allusion; (b) "In the shadow of the glen he cooees for them" -- a direct reference to Synge's play _In the Shadow of the Glen_ (1903); (c) "My soul's youth I gave him, night by night. God speed. Good hunting." -- Stephen's bitter reflection on his friendship with Cranly. The Danish replaces all of this with invented text ("Og en dag ville Kathleen sige: Denne land er mit. Disse maend er mit folk.") which is a hallucination. This is a content integrity failure.
- **Suggestion:** Retranslate the full paragraph: "Cranlys elleve aegte Wicklow-maend til at befri deres soirland. Hultandet Kathleen, hendes fire smukke gronne marker, fremmeden i hendes hus. Og en mere til at hilse ham: _ave, rabbi_: de tolv fra Tinahely. I glenens skygge raaber han paa dem. Min sjaels ungdom gav jeg ham, nat efter nat. God vind. God jagt."

### 2. [Chapter 7, Paragraph 162] (reason: translation choice -- "gilder")
- **Original (EN):** "All very fine to jeer at it now in cold print but it goes down like hot cake that stuff. He was in the bakery line too, wasn't he? Why they call him Doughy Daw. Feathered his nest well anyhow. Daughter engaged to that chap in the inland revenue office with the motor. Hooked that nicely. Entertainments. Open house. Big blowout. Wetherup always said that. Get a grip of them by the stomach."
- **Danish:** "Det er nemt nok at grine ad det nu paa koldt tryk men det sluges som varme hveder det stads. Han var ogsaa i bageribranchen, var han ikke? Derfor de kalder ham Dejg-Daw. Har polstret sin rede godt nok i hvert fald. Datteren forlovet med den fyr fra skattekontoret med bilen. Hooked fint ind. Selskabelighed. Aabent hus. Store gilder. Wetherup sagde altid det. Tag dem i maven."
- **Issue:** "Store gilder" for "Big blowout." In Danish, "gilder" (feasts/banquets) is a legitimate but somewhat archaic word. It works for Bloom's inner monologue about ostentatious entertaining, but "gilder" has a medieval/fairy-tale connotation that may feel odd in a 1904 Dublin context.
- **Suggestion:** "Store fester" is more natural modern Danish. However, "gilder" has a pleasingly ironic register mismatch that Joyce might have appreciated. Reader's call.

### Ch8, P127-128 — DUPLICATE BUG
**CRITICAL FINDING:** DA paragraphs 127 and 128 both translate EN paragraph 128 ("Raskolnikov had a sudden desire to say something exceptionally pleasant to them all..."). The actual EN paragraph 127 ("And what a regiment it was, too," cried Ilya Petrovitch...) has been overwritten and is **missing from the Danish edition**.

- DA P127 begins: "Raskolnikov følte pludselig trang til at sige noget overordentligt venligt..."
- DA P128 begins: "Raskolnikov fik pludselig lyst til at sige noget usædvanligt venligt..."

These are two different translations of the same English source paragraph. The paragraph counts still match (174 each), meaning one English paragraph's translation was lost entirely.

**Action required:** Replace DA P127 with a proper translation of EN P127: *"And what a regiment it was, too," cried Ilya Petrovitch, much gratified at this agreeable banter, though still sulky.*

## 2. Jane Eyre (Brontë)

### Ch1, P1 (first paragraph)
**EN:** "There was no possibility of taking a walk that day. We had been wandering, indeed, in the leafless shrubbery an hour in the morning; but since dinner (Mrs. Reed, when there was no company, dined early) the cold winter wind had brought with it clouds so sombre, and a rain so penetrating, that further outdoor exercise was now out of the question."
**DA:** "Der var ingen mulighed for at gå en tur den dag. Vi havde vandret rundt i de nøgne buske en time den morgen, men siden frokost (Mrs. Reed spiste tidligt, når der ikke var gæster) havde den kolde vintervind bragt så mørke skyer og så ubarmhjertig regn, at mere tid udendørs var udelukket."

| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 4 | "Dinner" rendered as "frokost" — in Victorian English "dinner" was the main midday meal, so "frokost" is actually more accurate than "middag" would be. "Leafless shrubbery" → "nøgne buske" loses "shrubbery" (a planted garden area) but conveys the image. |
| Fluency | 4 | Reads well. "Så ubarmhjertig regn" (so merciless rain) is slightly unusual — "gennemtrængende regn" would match "penetrating" better and sound more natural. |
| Literary quality | 4 | Captures the confined, bleak mood. The parenthetical about Mrs. Reed works naturally. |
| Translationese | 4 | "Mere tid udendørs var udelukket" is slightly stiff — "at gå ud igen var udelukket" would be more natural. Minor issue. |

**Avg: 4.00 — PASS**

## Critical Issues

### 1. Crime and Punishment Ch8: Duplicate paragraph (SEVERITY: HIGH)
DA paragraphs 127 and 128 are both translations of EN paragraph 128. EN paragraph 127 ("And what a regiment it was, too," cried Ilya Petrovitch...) is missing from the Danish edition entirely. The paragraph count still matches because the same content was translated twice.

**Fix:** Replace DA ch8 P127 with a proper translation of: *"And what a regiment it was, too," cried Ilya Petrovitch, much gratified at this agreeable banter, though still sulky.*

Suggested Danish: *"Og sikke et regiment det var," udbrød Ilja Petrovitj, tydeligvis smigret af denne venlige spøg, selvom han stadig var gnaven.*

### 2. Minor translationese patterns (SEVERITY: LOW)
Jane Eyre and War and Peace show occasional English clause-order mirroring in shorter sentences. Not severe enough to flag as failures, but worth noting for any future revision pass. The pattern: short declarative sentences tend to calque English structure more than longer narrative passages do.

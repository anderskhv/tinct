### 1. [Chapter 30, Paragraph 7] (reason: calque — "inden for synsvidde af")
- **Original (EN):** "His arrival was soon known at the Parsonage; for Mr. Collins was walking the whole morning within view of the lodges opening into Hunsford Lane, in order to have the earliest assurance of it."
- **Danish:** "Hans ankomst blev snart kendt i praesteboligen, for Mr. Collins tilbragte hele formiddagen med at ga inden for synsvidde af lagerne ud til Hunsford Lane for at fa..."
- **Issue:** "inden for synsvidde af" is a direct calque of "within view of" — it is grammatically possible but not how a Danish person would naturally express this. It reads like translated English.
- **Suggestion:** "...med at ga frem og tilbage, sa han kunne se lagerne ud mod Hunsford Lane..." (restructure around what Collins is doing — walking so he can see). Alternatively: "...med at ga rundt med udsigt til lagerne..." or "...sa han kunne holde oje med lagerne ud til Hunsford Lane..."

### 2. [Chapter 30, Paragraph 13] (reason: anachronistic register — "droppet")
- **Original (EN):** "The subject was pursued no farther, and the gentlemen soon afterwards went away."
- **Danish:** "Emnet blev droppet, og herrerne gik kort efter."
- **Issue:** "droppet" is modern colloquial Danish (from English "dropped"). In Austen's narrator voice — measured, ironic, formal — this register break is jarring. The original says "pursued no farther", which is deliberate and restrained, not casual.
- **Suggestion:** "Emnet blev ikke forfulgt yderligere, og herrerne gik kort efter." (preserves Austen's formal narrator register). Or: "De lod emnet falde, og herrerne gik kort efter."

### 3. [Chapter 1, Paragraph 18] (reason: meaning loss — "Hans grund")
- **Original (EN):** "'Design? Nonsense, how can you talk so! But it is very likely that he _may_ fall in love with one of them, and therefore you must visit him as soon as he comes.'"
- **Danish:** "'Hans grund? Sludder, hvordan kan du sige den slags! Men det er meget sandsynligt, at han _maske_ forelsker sig i en af dem, og derfor skal du besoge ham, sa snart han ankommer.'"
- **Issue:** "Design" in Austen means a deliberate scheme or intention (Mr. Bennet is teasing that Bingley has a calculated plan to marry one of the daughters). "Hans grund" (his reason) loses the scheming/plotting connotation entirely. Mrs. Bennet is dismissing the idea that Bingley would have a cunning plan.
- **Suggestion:** "Hensigt?" (intention/design — preserves the scheming connotation). Or "Bagtanke?" (ulterior motive). Or "Onde hensigter?" to match Mrs. Bennet's mock-scandalized tone.

### 4. [Chapter 1, Paragraph 34] (reason: slightly clinical phrasing)
- **Original (EN):** "...She was a woman of mean understanding, little information, and uncertain temper."
- **Danish:** "...Hun var en kvinde med begraenset intelligens, ringe viden og upalidelig temperament."
- **Issue:** "upalidelig temperament" translates the words correctly but sounds clinical/diagnostic rather than literary. Austen's "uncertain temper" means Mrs. Bennet's mood was unpredictable — she could swing from delight to despair. "Upalidelig" (unreliable) is more commonly used about systems or people's promises, not about someone's emotional disposition.
- **Suggestion:** "svingende humor" (shifting moods) — captures the unpredictability in a more natural, literary Danish way. Or "uforudsigelig temperament" (unpredictable temperament) if we want to stay closer to the structure.

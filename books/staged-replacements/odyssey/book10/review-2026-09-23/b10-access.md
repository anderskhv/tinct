## Findings summary

Nothing in Book 10 stops a listener from following the plot. The issues found are all small friction points — mostly single archaic words or phrases (`girds`, `still` as a verb, `ways of the winds`, `unman`, `ewer`, `girdle`, `this last`, `evil counsels won the day`, `the down… on his face`) that a modern ear will either misparse for a beat or simply not know, plus:

## What reads well

The narrative backbone is otherwise clean and easy to follow aloud: the Aeolus episode, the Laestrygonian attack, Eurylochus's retelling of the pig transformation, the Hermes/Moly scene, and Circe's turnaround all move in clear, well-paced sentences with unambiguous speakers. Dialogue attribution throughout the book is unusually clear for a text with this much back-and-forth — no ambiguous "he said" moments were found. The extended similes (calves greeting their mothers, hounds fawning on their master) land naturally when read aloud.

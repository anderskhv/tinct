# Book 19 — accessibility review

Read: all 39 paragraphs (indices 0-38), in full, start to finish, as a first-time
listener, then re-read paragraph by paragraph for exact wording.

Coverage note: the disguised Odysseus's false Cretan story (paragraphs 14-18) and
Penelope's account of the weaving trick and the geese dream (paragraphs 13, 33)
were read with the expectation of nested quotations and were not flagged simply
for being reported speech within reported speech.

## Findings: 8 (3 blocking, 5 non-blocking)

| # | Para | Category | Blocking | Issue |
|---|------|----------|----------|-------|
| 1 | 8 | aloud | yes | "watch that you don't fall out of favor... and that Odysseus doesn't come home" reads as parallel instructions, inverting Odysseus's actual threat |
| 2 | 13 | archaic | no | "great web" for the weaving-in-progress uses an obsolete sense of "web" |
| 3 | 14 | archaic | yes | "subscriptions of wine and oxen" collides with the modern sense of "subscription" (recurring paid service) |
| 4 | 14 | aloud | no | Cape Malea / Amnisus / cave of Ilithuia sentence stacks too many locations and clauses to track by ear |
| 5 | 18 | aloud | no | "the crew found who would take Odysseus" drops "was," causing a momentary misreading of "crew" as the subject of "found" |
| 6 | 18 | aloud | yes | "he had enough... to keep his family" — "he"/"his" still read as Pheidon (the sentence's grammatical subject) when the treasure and family are Odysseus's |
| 7 | 24 | aloud | yes | "Hermes... endowed him... for he used to burn... to him... his company" — four pronouns swap between Autolycus and Hermes with no clear anchor until the last word |
| 8 | 35 | archaic | no | "the stays on which a ship is built" risks being heard as sailing rigging rather than a ship's building frame |

## What reads well
- The Telemachus/Eurycleia/Odysseus scene moving the weapons (paras 0-6) is brisk and clear, with Athena's lamp described vividly and simply.
- The recognition-scar sequence (paras 27-32) is tense and easy to follow moment to moment, despite the flashback structure.
- Penelope's geese-and-eagle dream (para 33) is told cleanly; the dream's own interpretation is handled inline by the eagle's speech, so no outside myth knowledge is needed.
- The false Cretan genealogy (para 14) is dense with proper nouns but each relationship (Minos → Deucalion → Idomeneus/Aethon) is stated in a short, direct sentence, so it stays trackable despite the volume of names.

## Verdict
NEEDS TARGETED FIXES — 3 blocking pronoun/parsing issues (paras 8, 18, 24) and one blocking archaic word ("subscriptions," para 14) should be corrected; the remaining four are minor and can be applied at the merge owner's discretion. Everything else in the chapter — including the deliberate epic similes, epithets, and the nested false-story dialogue — read naturally and did not need changes.

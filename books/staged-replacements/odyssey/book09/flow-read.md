# Odyssey Book 9 — the flow read (step 7)

`candidate-v2.json` read straight through, once, as a reader reads it — not
paragraph against paragraph with Butler beside it, which is what steps 5 and 6
already did. This is the **carrier** for blind spot 1 of the findings lists,
*a sentence divided at the wrong seam*: D17 counts sentences, NORM RATE counts
the sentences a dividing mark did not pay for, and D28 counts whether the mark
was the one Butler wrote. **None of the three can tell a good division from a
bad one.** Book 4's F-1, Book 5's three reversals and Book 6's F-1 were all
found this way, and so was this.

## F-1 — B09-P034 reads *As … As* — **APPLIED**

The paragraph's first seam was one of the semicolons it cashed, and the
division it bought left two consecutive sentences opening `As`:

> **As** he spoke he drove the ram outside, but when we were a little way out
> from the cave and the yards, I first got out from under the ram's belly, and
> then freed my comrades. **As** for the sheep, which were very fat, by heading
> them steadily in the right direction we managed to drive them down to the
> ship.

Butler's own pointing there is a semicolon and it is doing the better job:
getting out from under the ram and getting the sheep down to the ship are one
movement, and his mark says so. His `as for the sheep` is lower-case and
continuous. This is Book 6's F-1 exactly — two consecutive sentences opening on
the same word, produced by a division rather than by Butler — and the
disposition Book 5's round 1 reached three times, in each case *back to
Butler's mark*:

> … and then freed my comrades**;** as for the sheep, which were very fat, …

**What it costs, and why the cost is the point.** The restored sentence is 61
words, so Book 9's sixty-word figure goes from `16 → 0` to `16 → 1` and the
D20 growth gate fires and has to be declared (`(34, 59, 61)` — Butler wrote it
at 59). **A Book that reads better now publishes a worse-looking number.** A
gate firing on a sentence handed back to Butler is firing on the repair, and
the declaration says so; the alternative is to keep a division nobody would
defend in order to keep a `0` in a table.

## Three repeated openers examined and DECLINED

**B09-P003, `So long as the morning lasted …` / `So we got away …`.** Both are
Butler's and the second is his own `; so we got away with those that were
left` promoted to a sentence. Declined, and the reason is a real distinction
rather than a shrug: **`So long as` is a subordinating conjunction and `So` is
a discourse connective**, so the two openers are not the same word doing the
same job, and they are forty words apart. The short closing beat is the better
English and the paragraph is ten sentences long.

**B09-P007, `There are meadows …` / `There is a good harbor …`.** Butler writes
both as separate sentences. No division is involved and there is nothing to
reverse.

**B09-P027, `Surely no man is carrying off your sheep?` / `Surely no man is
trying to kill you …?`.** Butler's, both of them, and the repetition is the
Cyclopes shouting the same question twice through a door. It is the setup of
the `Noman` pun and it must not be varied.

## What the read confirmed

* **The restored marks read.** P012's colon makes the mixing of the wine the
  explanation of the secrecy rather than a new fact; P021's colon lets `this`
  point forward across it; P031's semicolon does the same for `this plan`. All
  three were cases where the sentence after the mark depends on the one before.
* **P028's kept semicolon is the best decision in the Book** — *"Noman is
  killing me by fraud; no man is killing me by force"* — and reads as one
  breath, which is what the pun needs.
* **The 44-paragraph quotation is not a reading problem inside the Book**, and
  that is exactly why §4's correction matters: read straight through from
  B09-P001 the speaker is obvious, because the poet's frame names him in the
  first four words. The problem is four chapters long and belongs to the
  reader who does not start here.

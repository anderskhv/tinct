# Accessibility Review — Book 12 (The Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, the cattle of the sun)

**Coverage:** All 39 paragraphs (indices 0–38) read in full, in order, as a first-time
adult listener would hear the audio narration.

**Context noted and applied:** Odysseus narrates this entire book to the Phaeacians.
Every paragraph therefore opens inside a quotation (double quotes), and speech he
reports within his own narration uses single quotes. This nested-quotation pattern is
the book's house convention, not a defect, and was not flagged.

## Findings: 13 (1 blocking, 12 non-blocking)

| # | Para | Category | Blocking | Issue |
|---|------|----------|----------|-------|
| 1 | 0 | reference | No | "dawn and sunrise occur just as they do everywhere else" reads as a pointless negation of what should be a mythic detail about Aeaea. |
| 2 | 2 | flow | No | "You will have died twice while others die only once" is a riddle-like line that doesn't resolve on first hearing. |
| 3 | 5 | reference | No | Doves carrying ambrosia to Zeus dropped in with no context. |
| 4 | 6 | reference | No | "Erebus" used as if already familiar; underworld association isn't established. |
| 5 | 9 | aloud | **Yes** | "curse her" interrupts mid-sentence with no clear target, and the following "she" has to loop back past it to mean Crataiis, not Scylla — genuinely mishearable as Scylla's mother being the threat. |
| 6 | 10 | archaic | No | "children of ... Hyperion by Neaera" — old genealogical formula for parentage that doesn't parse cleanly for a modern ear. |
| 7 | 19 | aloud | No | Fisherman simile runs ~80 words before its payoff, straining what a listener can hold before "even so did Scylla..." resolves it. |
| 8 | 20 | consistency | No | Near-verbatim repeat of "how carefully Aeaean Circe warned me to shun the island of the blessed sun-god," two sentences after saying the same thing, reads as a stitched-together duplicate. |
| 9 | 21 | aloud | No | Eurylochus's complaint chains four clauses on semicolons in one breath; hard to track which grievance is which when heard aloud. |
| 10 | 24 | archaic | No | "The third watch of the night" assumes knowledge of the ancient night-watch system. |
| 11 | 26 | flow | No | "with hook and line, catching birds" reads as if birds were caught on hook and line; needs the two activities separated. |
| 12 | 32 | consistency | No | Sudden aside citing Calypso as a later source breaks the otherwise continuous first-person narration frame Odysseus has kept throughout the book. |
| 13 | 37 | reference | No | "A juror... detained in court" is a jarringly modern legal image dropped into a shipwreck/monster scene. |

## Verdict: **NEEDS TARGETED FIXES**

The prose is largely clear and reads well as continuous narration — the core action
(Sirens, Scylla, Charybdis, the cattle of the sun, the wreck) comes through cleanly,
and most paragraphs need no changes. The one blocking item (paragraph 9) is a genuine
mishearing risk: the "curse her" aside plus the ambiguous "she" that follows could
lead a listener to conclude the wrong character is the ongoing threat. The rest are
smaller accessibility snags — unexplained mythological references, one overlong
simile, an archaic genealogical/time formula, a redundant repeated clause, an
awkward compound clause, and one anachronistic-sounding simile — none of which
block comprehension of the plot but all of which would cost a listener a beat of
re-processing.

# Book 16 — Accessibility Review (Reviewer A, fresh-candidate-only)

**Coverage:** Read all 45 paragraphs (indices 0–44) in full, start to finish, then re-read paragraph by paragraph. Source text, notes, and other Books were not opened.

## Findings (4 total; 2 blocking, 2 non-blocking)

- **Paragraph 10** (Odysseus's first speech to Telemachus) — "despite a man like you being here" is a mildly clotted gerund construction; costs a beat to parse. Non-blocking (flow).
- **Paragraph 10** (same speech) — "If they outnumbered me—being alone—I'd rather die fighting" places the "being alone" parenthetical right after "they outnumbered me," so on first hearing it can briefly seem to modify "they." Non-blocking (aloud).
- **Paragraph 21** (Odysseus reveals himself) — "No other Odysseus will come after this." On its own this reads as a statement about someone else showing up later, when the intended meaning is "there's no substitute — this really is me." A first-time listener is likely to misread the sentence's point. Blocking (flow).
- **Paragraph 39** (Eurymachus's reply to Penelope) — "my spear will be red with his blood, for many times Odysseus took me on his knees" — "his" most naturally points back to "your son Telemachus" (the nearest referent), so on first hearing this sounds like a threat against Telemachus, the opposite of what Eurymachus means (a promise to kill anyone who threatens him). The following sentence clarifies, but only after the misread has happened. Blocking (aloud/pronoun ambiguity).

## What's working well
- The recognition scene (paragraphs 17–22, Telemachus doubting then accepting his father) reads clearly and movingly; the back-and-forth of disbelief and reassurance is easy to follow aloud.
- Eumaeus's report on Laertes (paragraph 12) and the suitors' council scenes (paragraphs 34–36) are dense with names and numbers but structured in simple, sequential sentences that hold up well when read aloud.
- The narrator's asides to Eumaeus, the epic similes (eagles/vultures, paragraph 22), and the epithets/patronymics throughout are all functioning as intended and were left untouched per review scope.

## Verdict: NEEDS TARGETED FIXES

Two of the four findings genuinely risk momentary misreadings for a first-time listener (paragraphs 21 and 39); the other two are minor. The chapter otherwise reads naturally and the plot (recognition of Odysseus, the plan against the suitors, the suitors' countermove, Penelope's confrontation) comes through clearly.

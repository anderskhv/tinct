# Accessibility review — Odyssey Book 15 (draft candidate)

**Read:** all 48 paragraphs (indices 0–47), in full, no source/original/notes consulted — only `b15-draft.json` and the accessibility review prompt.

**Findings: 5** (1 blocking, 4 non-blocking)

1. **Para 9** (aloud, non-blocking) — Helen says "I too, my son... have something for you as a keepsake from Helen's hand," referring to herself in the third person mid-speech. On audio this risks momentary confusion about whether a second woman is being introduced.
2. **Para 15** (flow, non-blocking) — "They swayed the yoke on their necks" is an unclear verb choice; the intended image (horses running hard) doesn't land for a modern reader/listener.
3. **Para 19** (reference, **blocking**) — "He quarreled with his father and went to live in Hyperesia" — "He" is genuinely ambiguous. The prior clause names both Apollo and the just-deceased Amphiaraus, plus an unnamed "his father," so a first-time listener cannot resolve that "He" means Polypheides without rehearing the sentence.
4. **Para 19** (consistency, non-blocking) — "Eos, enthroned in gold" appears with no link to "Dawn, the rosy-fingered child of morning," the name already used for the same goddess earlier in this Book (para 15). The unglossed proper name reads as a new, unexplained figure.
5. **Para 24** (archaic, non-blocking) — "sheets of twisted oxhide" uses nautical jargon ("sheets" = ropes) that a general reader will misread via the everyday sense of the word (cloth/paper), garbling the image.

**Not flagged (deliberate epic features, per instructions):** the Melampus/Theoclymenus genealogy in para 19 (names, lineage, "haunted... by dread Erinys"), Eumaeus's long autobiographical speech spanning paras 30–39 with nested quoted dialogue (paras 34–38), repeated bird-omen scenes (paras 12–14, 43–44), stock epithets ("rosy-fingered," "Apollo's messenger"), and nautical/formulaic terms like "hawsers" and "forestays," whose meaning is adequately carried by context.

**Verdict: needs targeted fixes** — one blocking pronoun-ambiguity issue (para 19); the rest are minor, non-blocking polish items. Overall the Book reads clearly and flows well for both Telemachus's Sparta/Pylos travel narrative and Eumaeus's backstory; the flagged spots are small, isolated wording choices, not structural or systemic problems.

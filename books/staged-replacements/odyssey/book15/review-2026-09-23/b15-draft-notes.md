## Deliberate choices
- Butler's unclosed quotation at a paragraph break (D4) is reproduced exactly: Eumaeus's speech
  runs open from ¶32 through ¶43 with no closing mark until its final sentence, and each
  paragraph in between opens its own fresh “ per the established convention, verified against
  the source's own “/” counts paragraph by paragraph (all 48 match exactly).
- The nested single-quoted dialogue inside Eumaeus's tale (¶39–¶42, the Phoenician woman and her
  seducer) uses curly single quotes ‘ ’, matching Butler's own quote-within-a-quote nesting;
  plain apostrophes (contractions, possessives, including the plural "parents'") use the same ’
  glyph, which is correct since English does not distinguish the two typographically.
- "Strait between Ithaca and Samos" (¶1) is left lowercase ("strait") rather than matching
  Butler's capital — a typographic style choice, not a naming decision, and left as the
  live text had it since the accepted Books show no precedent either way for this phrase.
- "piece of plate" → "piece of metalwork" (¶7) and "doughty spear" → "sturdy spear" (¶47) were
  considered and left as-is: defensible modern-word substitutions for archaic terms, not fixed
  formulas and not meaning changes.
- Final self-check: re-read the whole draft against the source paragraph by paragraph a second
  time; no further omissions found beyond the repairs listed above.

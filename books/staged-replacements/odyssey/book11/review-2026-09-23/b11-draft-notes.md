## Structural fix applied throughout: the master frame quotation (D4)

The live baseline had dropped the paragraph-reopening master quotation
mark for nearly all of Odysseus's narration (¶0–26, ¶33–53) and had used
double quotes for nested character speech (Elpenor, Teiresias, Anticlea)
instead of nesting it one level inside the frame with single quotes. I
verified, index by index, that the source's own opening/closing character
at every paragraph (frame “ at the start of every paragraph except ¶27; a
closing ” only at ¶26, 28, 29, 30, 32; nested ‘…’ for reported speech; no
closing mark elsewhere, since the poem's own frame quotation runs
unclosed through this Book and stays open into Book 12 per the package's
standing finding on Books 9–12) and rebuilt the candidate's quotation
marks to match that pattern exactly, while keeping the live baseline's
(already-faithful) wording. Paragraphs 27–32 are third-person narration
(the court's response to the first half of the tale) and correctly use
ordinary, non-nested double quotes, as in the source. All straight ASCII
quotes and apostrophes were converted to typographic ones throughout
(PUNCTUATION.md §1); the draft has none left (`python3` count-verified).

## Ethnonym flattening: the largest recurring defect found

The live baseline had systematically replaced Homer's distinct ethnonyms
for the Greeks — Achaeans, Argives, Danaans — and the place-name Hellas
with generic "Greek(s)"/"Greece" wherever they occurred (paragraphs 12, 32,
39, 40, 42, 44, 45 — 15 individual instances). All four names are in
GLOSSARY's "Names that change in no Book" list (already Greek in Butler,
kept exactly as he spells them) and are never interchangeable synonyms in
this package's convention. Every instance is restored to Butler's own
word, verified paragraph-by-paragraph and by a full-book automated count
against the source (matches exactly: Achaean 2/2, Achaeans 2/2, Argives
5/5, Danaans 4/4, Hellas 1/1).

## Open questions flagged for the coordinator (not decided here)

1. **Teiresias vs. "Tiresias."** `odyssey-threads.json`'s Cast entry
   (id `tiresias`) displays "Tiresias" in English, diverging from Butler's
   "Teiresias." Accepted Book 10 already uses "Teiresias" seven times
   without applying the Cast-name-wins rule (D8) — the same rule that gave
   Eurycleia her spelling. I followed Book 10's precedent and kept
   "Teiresias" throughout this draft for consistency with the accepted
   style reference, rather than unilaterally switching mid-package. This
   task's own brief spells it "Tiresias" in its instructions, which is the
   opposite of what Butler's text and the accepted Books print — worth the
   coordinator's attention either way.
2. **Bacchus (Dionysus's Roman name), first met at ¶25.** Not in the closed
   Roman→Greek table. On the same reasoning that added Mars, Ceres, and
   Proserpine (D5's rule: an Olympian with a Roman name, added by
   enumeration when a Book meets it), Bacchus arguably qualifies for a new
   row (→ Dionysus). I did not add it on my own authority — following the
   Hercules-at-Book-8 precedent, this is put to the coordinator rather than
   decided in the draft. Left as Butler's "Bacchus," unmapped, in this
   candidate.
3. **"Oedipodes" (¶21).** Butler's own (Homeric) spelling of Oedipus,
   flagged rather than silently modernized, on the same rule that keeps
   "Diomed," "Idothea," "Ilius," and "Mycene" (the woman) in Butler's own
   form — no Cast entry authorizes a different spelling.

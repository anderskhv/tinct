# Accessibility Review — Book 11, "The visit to the dead"

**Coverage:** Read all 54 paragraphs (indices 0–53) in full, start to finish, as a
first-time listener/reader. Odysseus narrates the entire book to the Phaeacians, so
almost every paragraph opens as a quotation — that is the book's established
convention (confirmed by checking which paragraphs are frame narration at the
Phaeacian court: indices 27–33, correctly *not* opening with a quote mark since the
narrator has stepped back to the hall) vs. Odysseus's own underworld narration
(quoted throughout). Not treated as a defect.

**Findings: 10** (3 blocking, 7 non-blocking)

- Paragraph 0 — archaic inverted syntax ("where lies the land"), minor.
- Paragraph 3 — Elpenor's oath sentence stacks four "by X" clauses before the actual
  request arrives; overloaded for the ear.
- Paragraph 8 — "ships and oars that serve as a ship's wings" briefly misreads as
  modifying both nouns.
- Paragraph 13 — **BLOCKING.** "He has to host many guests... considering... how
  everyone invites him" is internally contradictory (hosting vs. being invited out
  can't both be the stated reason); also uses the anachronistic, legalistic
  "magistrate."
- Paragraph 21 — Epicaste's death sentence: "avenging spirits to torment him for his
  outraged mother" risks a misparse of "for" (because of vs. on behalf of).
- Paragraph 35 — **BLOCKING.** "Clytemnestra" is named for the first time here with
  no link back to "my wicked wife" two sentences earlier; a listener has nothing to
  attach the name to.
- Paragraph 39 — Achilles is named, then immediately re-referred to only by the
  unglossed epithet "the fleet descendant of Aeacus," forcing backward inference.
- Paragraph 42 — **BLOCKING.** "because of a woman's bribes" names no one and
  explains nothing about who bribed whom. Also flagged in the same paragraph: one
  sentence stacks a "when," an embedded decision, and a "though" concession before
  reaching its point (non-blocking, but genuinely tangled read aloud).
- Paragraph 44 — **BLOCKING.** "the Trojan prisoners and Athena were the judges" is
  presented with zero context for why captured enemy soldiers would judge a dispute
  between two Greek heroes.

**What's already working:** The Elpenor and mother-of-Odysseus scenes (paragraphs
2–4, 9–15) land cleanly and movingly — clear voices, clear stakes, no stumbles. The
underworld catalogue of women (17–26) and the punishments catalogue (48–52,
Tantalus/Sisyphus especially) read vividly and are well-paced for audio despite the
density of names. Agamemnon's death account (35–37) is visceral and clear apart from
the one naming gap flagged above. Heracles's closing speech (53) lands well.

**Verdict: BLOCKING FINDINGS** (3 of the 10 items — paragraphs 13, 35, 44 — genuinely
impede first-listen comprehension and should be fixed; the remaining 7 are
targeted, non-blocking smoothing suggestions).

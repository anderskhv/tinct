# Accessibility review — Odyssey Book 18 (candidate-only, fresh read)

**Read:** all 41 paragraphs (indices 0-40), in full, start to finish, then a second pass paragraph by paragraph. No source, original-en, live baseline, notes, or other Books were opened.

**Findings: 4, all non-blocking.**

- Paragraph 13 — "for God Almighty gives men their daily thoughts day by day": redundant doubling of "daily" / "day by day" reads as an accidental repetition on first hearing. (aloud)
- Paragraph 27 — "earrings fashioned into three brilliant pendants that sparkled beautifully": ambiguous whether "three" counts pendants-per-earring or a set of three earrings. (aloud)
- Paragraph 37 — "Odysseus sought protection at Amphinomus of Dulichium's knees": stacked possessive on a name-plus-epithet is hard to parse by ear. (aloud)
- Paragraph 39 — "son of Nisus, who was son of Aretias": relative pronoun could momentarily be misattached to Amphinomus instead of Nisus. (reference)

**Not flagged (deliberate/working as intended):** the habitual "one would turn to his neighbor saying..." construction (paras 8, 41) is consistent, deliberate epic repetition, not a comprehension problem. The dramatic irony of the disguised Odysseus referring to "Odysseus's house" in the third person (para 2) is intentional plot device, understood via the surrounding narration, and not confusing on its own. Graphic violence (the Irus fight, the Echetus mutilation threat) and insults ("Vixen," "filthy glutton," "vixen"/"wretch" exchanges) were left untouched per instructions. Long rhetorical passages (Penelope's speech in para 24, Odysseus's speech to Amphinomus in para 13, the athletic-challenge speech in para 36) are dense but grammatically legal and coherent on a full read — not flagged as tangled.

**Overall readability:** The chapter reads smoothly and naturally almost throughout — dialogue is vivid, character voices are distinct (Antinous's cruelty, Melantho's contempt, Eurymachus's bluster, Penelope's guarded grief), and the narrative momentum through the Irus fight and the suitors' gift-giving carries well aloud. The four issues found are small, local snags (a redundant phrase, an ambiguous count, an awkward possessive, a loosely anchored relative clause) rather than anything that would derail comprehension of the chapter as a whole.

**Verdict: CLEAN** (no blocking findings; 4 minor non-blocking suggestions above)

Every proposed wording stays inside Butler's own words and the glossary as it will read after the standing finding is applied. "Also noted" remarks inside an entry are not numbered findings.

**Book 1 is sound in structure and in provenance, and freer in the line than the package's own rules describe.** Paragraph alignment is exact, no paragraph is dropped or merged, every proper name occurs the same number of times in the candidate as in the source, direct speech is direct speech in all thirteen speeches, Butler's unclosed-quotation carry-over at B01-P018 → B01-P019 is reproduced exactly and is the **only** unbalanced paragraph in either file, and the three fixed formulas in `GLOSSARY.md` are applied every time they occur. There is no invented content of any size, no explanatory gloss, no modern interpretation, and nothing traceable to another translation.

What the review does find is a recurring class of small losses at the level of the individual word: Butler's concrete nouns softened to general ones (*damask* → fine linen, *mountain* → hill, *outer court* → courtyard, *an eye of Polyphemus* → nothing), his qualifiers dropped (*unrighteously*, *divine* voice, *fine looking*), his deliberate repetitions broken (*longer* legs / *longer* purse; *chief* in Ithaca / *chief* in my own house), and a handful of intensifiers and clarifiers added that he does not have (*certainly*, *at least*, *his son*, *with an excuse for doing nothing*). Each one is individually small. Together they are the difference between a modernisation and a retelling, and the accessibility standard the package adopted says explicitly that every image, qualification and meaningful repetition survives. The three substantive findings are the three places where the loss changes what the sentence asserts.

Separately, and above all of this, the **name forms are wrong for this product** — see the standing finding. That is not the drafter's error; its brief told it to follow the served original. It is a coordinator decision now recorded here so the corrections step can apply it by script.

## Standing finding S1 (substantive, book-wide) — Greek name forms, not Butler's Roman ones

**Coordinator decision, applied here as one standing finding rather than repeated in twenty paragraph entries.** The edition uses **Odysseus, Athena, Zeus, Poseidon, Hermes, Cronus**, not Butler's **Ulysses, Minerva, Jove, Neptune, Mercury, Saturn**.

The drafter's `GLOSSARY.md` decision D1 (keep Butler's forms) was a reasonable reading of its own brief and is correctly reasoned and correctly recorded. It is nevertheless overruled, and the evidence in the repository is one-sided:

- `app/public/data/editions/odyssey-threads.json` — the Cast data the reader sees beside the text — names these characters **Odysseus (319 occurrences), Athena (21), Zeus (24), Poseidon (19), Hermes (12)**, against **one occurrence each** of Ulysses, Minerva, Jove, Neptune and Mercury, and every one of those five is inside a `searchNames` alias array, not a display name.
- `app/public/data/onboarding/odyssey.json` — the Book Onboarding content a first-time reader meets *before* the text — uses **Odysseus 21 times**, Poseidon 4, Athena 1; "Ulysses" appears twice and "Jove" once.
- The served `odyssey-modern-en.json` now being replaced uses the Greek forms throughout.
- The Odyssey Book 10 pilot independently chose the Greek forms.

A reader who meets "Odysseus" in the onboarding, taps a highlighted "Odysseus" in the Cast, and then reads "Ulysses" in the modern edition has been handed two names for one man by the same product on the same screen. Butler's forms stay, correctly, in `original-en`, which is Butler; the modern edition is not Butler and does not owe them.

**One consequence worth saying plainly:** applying this removes the only stated blocker between this package and the Book 10 pilot draft (`PROVENANCE.md` §3, ledger A2). After the remap the two packages agree on names, and Book 10's disposition becomes a question about its unreviewed status alone.

### Mappings that are not one-to-one, and wordings that depend on the Roman form

Six items. Every one is a way for a naive find-and-replace to corrupt the text, and the first is the one that would actually fire.

1. **`Ops` must NOT be mapped — it is already Greek.** B01-P032: "Euryclea, daughter of Ops, son of Pisenor". Butler's Ops is Ὦψ, Eurycleia's grandfather. *Ops* is **also** the Roman name of Rhea, Cronus's consort, and any mapping table assembled from a general Roman→Greek deity list will contain the row `Ops → Rhea`. Applying it here would replace a man's name with a goddess's and break the genealogy the review instructions single out. **Exclude explicitly, with a comment saying why.**
2. **`Saturn` occurs only inside a fixed epithet, and the epithet's glossary row must be rewritten with it.** Both occurrences are "Father, son of Saturn, king of kings" (B01-P005, B01-P007). After the remap the fixed form is **"Father, son of Cronus, king of kings"**, and `GLOSSARY.md`'s row must be edited, not just the candidate — otherwise the next Book is drafted against a row describing an edition that no longer exists. Note also that `GLOSSARY.md` currently spells the Greek form **"Cronos"** in two places while the coordinator's decision is **"Cronus"**; settle on *Cronus* and make the file say so. There is no Cast entry for this figure, so `odyssey-threads.json` supplies no authority; *Cronus* is the standard English form and is the coordinator's.
3. **The possessive of a name ending in -s is an open decision the script must encode.** Butler writes `Ulysses’`; the candidate writes `Ulysses's` (three times). Both become **`Odysseus's`** or **`Odysseus'`**, and the choice recurs in every Book. Recommend `Odysseus's` — it matches the candidate's existing `Telemachus's`, `Phemius's` and `Agamemnon's`, and is what an English reader says aloud. Decide it once, record it, and have the script assert that no bare `Odysseus'` survives. (See also records finding **R1**: the apostrophe character itself needs a decision.)
4. **Matching must be case-sensitive and word-bounded.** `Same` is the island in B01-P017 ("Dulichium, Same, and wooded Zacynthus"). A case-insensitive pass over a table containing any short name destroys ordinary words; this is the paragraph where it shows.
5. **`heaven` is not a name and must not be touched.** It occurs **ten** times in the candidate as Butler's metonym for the gods collectively ("it rests with heaven to decide", "call heaven to witness", "heaven has laid other sorrows on me", "some message from heaven"). It is not a stand-in for Jove and does not become "Zeus". A remap pass that also "normalises theology" would wreck ten sentences.
6. **`Hyperion` stays as it is.** B01-P001, "the cattle of the sun-god Hyperion". Already Greek; Butler's "Sun-god" is a description, not a Roman name. Do not expand to "Helios".

**Not a mapping, but decide it in the same pass — `Euryclea`.** Butler spells the nurse *Euryclea*; the Cast's display name is **Eurycleia** (`odyssey-threads.json`, with "Euryclea" recorded as a `searchNames` alias, so Cast highlighting works either way). Since the edition is adopting the product's Greek forms, adopt the product's spelling too: **Eurycleia**, one occurrence, B01-P032. Optional in force, but it costs one word and removes the last name on which the text and the Cast disagree.

**Names verified as needing no change** (all already Greek in Butler, all occurring the same number of times in source and candidate): Telemachus, Penelope, Calypso, Laertes, Nestor, Menelaus, Agamemnon, Orestes, Aegisthus, Atlas, Thoosa, Phorcys, Polyphemus, Cyclopes, Mentes, Anchialus, Taphians, Temesa, Rheithron, Neritum, Phemius, Icarius, Antinous, Eupeithes, Eurymachus, Polybus, Pisenor, Ephyra, Ilus, Mermerus, Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus, Ithaca, Troy, Sparta, Pylos, Achaeans, Danaans, Argives, Argos, Hellas, Ogygia, Ethiopians, Olympus/Olympian, Hyperion, Ops. **Diana does not occur in Book 1** (verified: zero occurrences in source and candidate), so `GLOSSARY.md`'s Diana row is correctly marked "not yet met"; it becomes **Artemis** when it is.

### The source, verified independently — my own reconstruction first, then the audit

The method the Meditations package settled on is explicit that a re-run of the build proves nothing and that a reconstruction sharing the build's blind spot proves nothing either. So, in this order:

**First, I enumerated every apparatus class in the raw range before writing any code**, reading PG #1727 lines 376 to 740 directly.

1. **Indented lines: zero.** Every line in the range is flush left. The entire Meditations family of failures — footnote bodies, verse runs, illustration captions, unmarked continuations — is defined by indentation, and none of it can occur here. This range has no indentation to classify.
2. **Square brackets: zero.** No `[A-D]` opener, no `[N]` marker, no editorial insertion. (Butler's own bracketed passages exist elsewhere in PG #1727 — his footnote 36 discusses "the lines which I have enclosed in brackets" — but none is in Book 1.)
3. **Illustration markers: zero. In-text Greek: zero. Daggers: zero. Underscores: zero.**
4. **Short standalone flush-left lines: twenty-three.** I checked every one by eye against its neighbours. Twenty-one are the last, short, wrapped line of a paragraph followed by a blank line (`let him get home.`, `everything in full.”`, `sitting.`) — body text, not apparatus. The remaining two are `BOOK I` and the two-line all-caps chapter heading, both outside the body range. **No running head, no page number, no catchword.**
5. **Footnote references: sixteen, and they are bare digits glued to the text** — `and the other East.1`, `for her feet,2`, `Temesa4`, `middle Argos.”9`, `mine above all others 10—for it is I`, `cloisters11,`, `in a tower13`. This is the range's **only** apparatus class, and it is exactly the class that can vanish into a word and leave no trace: a marker stripped carelessly leaves `others —for` with a doubled space, or `cloisters ,`.

**Then I derived the marker rule from a property the build does not use.** Rather than pattern-matching digits — which is what any build would do, so a reconstruction doing the same would share its blind spot — I took the rule from **PG's own footnotes section at line 10843**, which numbers its entries 1 to 187. Over the whole translation body (lines 375 to 10842) there are **187 digit runs, and they are the sequence 1, 2, 3 … 187 in order, with no repeats and no gaps** — against 186 bracketed entries numbered up to 187 (entry 29 is absent from PG's list; that gap is in Book III and does not touch Book 1). The two counts reconcile. This establishes as a **relation, not a constant**, that *every digit run in Butler's translation body is a footnote reference, and Butler's body contains no digits of its own* — he writes "twenty men", "twenty oxen", "a couple of blazing torches" in words throughout. Book 1 holds markers 1 to 16.

**I left the class's hardest cases in rather than assuming them away**: the marker after a closing quotation mark (`Argos.”9`), the marker preceded by a space and followed by an em dash (`others 10—`), and the marker before a comma (`cloisters11,`). All three are places a sloppy rule leaves visible damage.

**Then, and only then, I diffed.** My reconstruction: take the lines after the all-caps heading block up to the line before `BOOK II`; paragraph = maximal run of non-blank lines; strip footnote-reference digit runs together with any whitespace immediately preceding them; join the lines of a paragraph with a newline. Result: **32 paragraphs, and all 32 byte-identical to `source-book1.json`. Zero diffs.**

Two things came out of the diff and are worth recording.

- **The staged `original-en` preserves PG's hard line wraps** as literal newlines inside every paragraph (32 of 32 here; 1,023 of 1,027 across the served file). My first reconstruction joined lines with a space and differed from the staged file in all 32 paragraphs for that reason alone — a useful demonstration that the check is capable of failing. Joining with a newline gives byte-identity.
- **The candidate contains no newlines at all**, and that is **correct, not a defect**: the served `odyssey-modern-en.json` and `odyssey-modern-da.json` both contain **zero** embedded newlines across all 1,027 paragraphs. The candidate matches the convention of the file it replaces. No finding; recorded because the difference between the two files is otherwise alarming at first sight, and the next Book's reviewer should not have to rediscover it.

**The source claim holds.** The staged Book 1 is Butler's translation body and nothing of PG's apparatus, confirmed from a property of the text independent of the build.

## Rulings on the items the drafter flagged

**1. B01-P017, the minimum-ratio paragraph (0.858) — the drafter's flag is correct. The paragraph is complete. It is not a defect, and it should not be redrafted.**

I did not accept the aggregate explanation. I diffed Telemachus's speech clause by clause against Butler. Every claim the continuity sheet lists is present and in Butler's order: the counterfactual death at Troy, the mound, the inherited renown, the storm-winds, the no-trace image, the "nothing but" inheritance, the escalation to a second kind of sorrow, all three islands by name (Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus) plus Ithaca's own leading men, the pretext of courtship, and Penelope's double refusal. **No claim, no qualification and no name is missing.** The 33-word shortfall is real syntactic compression of Butler's one 232-word sentence-chain — "as regards your question" becomes "to answer your question", "when the days of his fighting were done" becomes "once the fighting was over", "who will neither point blank say that she will not marry, nor yet bring matters to an end" becomes "who will neither flatly refuse to marry nor bring the matter to an end" — which is exactly what the package exists to do, and the last of those is better English than Butler's without losing the double negation.

**But the ratio and the losses do coincide,** and the paragraph carries four of them: `a mound over his ashes` becomes `a burial mound` (the cremation is gone), `I inherit nothing but dismay` becomes `nothing but grief` (see **17.1** — this one matters), `all the principal men of Ithaca` becomes `the leading men of Ithaca`, and `Nor does the matter end simply with grief` becomes `And it does not end there with grief`. Two are findings below. **The ruling is that the low ratio is not evidence of dropped content, and the drafter was right to flag it rather than hide it; the paragraph nonetheless needs two small restorations, for reasons that have nothing to do with its length.**

**2. Butler's unclosed quotation at B01-P018 into B01-P019 — CONFIRMED, and the drafter's reasoning is right.**

Verified mechanically in both files: source P018 and candidate P018 each carry two opening marks and one closing mark, each is the only unbalanced paragraph in its file, and each successor paragraph opens with its own opening mark. This is Butler's printing convention for one continuous speech split by a paragraph break, not a defect, and "fixing" it would tell the reader that Athena stopped speaking and started again. One thing to add to `continuity.md`: the convention has to survive into the app for the same reason it has to survive here — in a paginated reader the alternative reads as a dropped closing quote, not as a convention. **Keep as drafted.**

**3. `hecatomb` folded to "an offering of a hundred sheep and oxen" (D3) — UPHELD, with one caveat offered rather than pressed.** See optional finding **3.1**. The glossary licenses it, the accessibility standard supports it, Book 1 never needs the word again, and I would not reopen it. The caveat is that Butler declines to state a number and the fold states one.

**4. The candidate takes no wording from the served `modern-en` or from any other translation.** I looked for the tell — clusters of vocabulary Butler does not use, or a famous phrase arriving intact. The opening is the passage most exposed to this and the continuity sheet flags it honestly. `Tell me, Muse, about that resourceful man` is built out of Butler's own `Tell me, O Muse, of that ingenious hero`: the verb, the vocative, the demonstrative and the sentence shape are all Butler's, and "resourceful" is a plain modern word for "ingenious" rather than any other translator's choice. **No import found anywhere in the 32 paragraphs.** I do, separately, think `hero` becoming `man` is a loss in its own right and say so at **1.1**, on Butler's evidence alone.

## Findings by paragraph

### B01-P001

**Finding 1.1 (minor).** Current: `Tell me, Muse, about that resourceful man who wandered far and wide`. Butler: `Tell me, O Muse, of that ingenious hero who travelled far and wide`. **`hero` becoming `man` is an unrecorded change and an unnecessary one.** `continuity.md` records and defends `ingenious` to `resourceful` — rightly — but says nothing about `hero`, which is not archaic, not obscure, and not misleading: it is Butler's own word for Odysseus in the poem's first line, and it is the word that tells a first-time reader what kind of book this is before the sentence has finished. Dropping it also flattens the first line of the edition, which is the one line every reader reads. **Proposed correction:** `Tell me, Muse, about that resourceful hero who wandered far and wide`. Confidence high. *Alternative reading considered and rejected:* that "man" is more faithful to Homer's ἄνδρα. It is, but this edition modernises Butler, not Homer, and going behind Butler to the Greek for one word is a different project.

**Finding 1.2 (minor).** Current: `Tell me about all this too, daughter of Jove, whatever part of it you know.` Butler: `Tell me, too, about all these things, oh daughter of Jove, from whatsoever source you may know them.` **`from whatsoever source` means *however you came to know it*, not *whatever part of it*.** Butler's "source" is the origin of the Muse's knowledge; the candidate converts it into a statement about the *extent* of her knowledge, which is a different hedge and a smaller one. `continuity.md` argues the reverse ("the Muse may know all of it or only part") but that reading requires "source" to mean "portion", which it does not in Butler's English. **Proposed correction:** `Tell me about all this too, daughter of Jove, from whatever source you know it.` Confidence high on the diagnosis; medium on the wording — `however you came to know it` is an equally good fix and reads more naturally.

*Also noted, no finding.* `town of Troy` to `city of Troy` is right and recorded. `Many cities did he visit, and many were the nations with whose manners and customs he was acquainted` becoming `He saw the cities of many peoples and came to know their ways` fuses Butler's two limbs into one and drops `moreover`; I considered a finding and decided against — Butler's two limbs name the same journey twice in the Homeric manner, the candidate's single clause carries both the seeing and the knowing, and the compression is the kind the package is for. The `oh` of `oh daughter of Jove` is gone, which is correct: it is a Victorian vocative particle, not a word.

### B01-P002

**Finding 2.1 (optional).** Current: `who went on persecuting him without let-up`. Butler: `who still persecuted him without ceasing`. `without let-up` is the one phrase in the Book that drops below the register the package set ("no colloquial rewrite"). It is not wrong and it is not unclear; it simply sounds like a strike report rather than Homer. **Proposed correction:** `without pause`. Confidence medium — this is a preference and the drafter may reasonably keep it.

*Also noted, no finding.* The restructure `everyone else … had made it safely home—everyone except Ulysses` is a real improvement on Butler's chain and keeps the contrast he builds the sentence on. Calypso's three facts survive as three (detaining him, the cave, wanting him for a husband).

### B01-P003

**Finding 3.1 (optional).** Current: `an offering of a hundred sheep and oxen`. Butler: `a hecatomb of sheep and oxen`. The fold is licensed by `GLOSSARY.md` D3 and I **uphold it** — see the ruling above. The caveat, offered once and not pressed: the fold supplies **a number Butler does not give**. By Homer's period *hecatomb* had long stopped meaning literally a hundred of anything, and Butler, who knew that, declines to quantify. Everything else in this candidate is scrupulous about not stating quantities the source leaves open. **Proposed alternative if the drafter agrees:** `a great sacrifice of sheep and oxen` — no number, same accessibility, no loanword. Confidence low that this should change; recorded so the decision is made with its cost in view rather than by default, and so that the later Books that also contain hecatombs inherit a considered rule.

*Also noted, no finding.* `the sire of gods and men` becoming `the father of gods and men` is the glossary row, applied. `world's end` to `ends of the earth` and `lie in two halves` to `live … in two groups` both fine. `the house of Olympian Jove` to `the halls of Olympian Jove` slightly elevates Butler's plain "house"; not worth a finding, but "house" would have been fine.

### B01-P004

**Finding 4.1 (substantive).** Current: `he had to go and seduce Agamemnon's wife, and then kill Agamemnon`. Butler: `he must needs make love to Agamemnon’s wife unrighteously and then kill Agamemnon`. **`unrighteously` is dropped, and it is the load-bearing word of the paragraph.** This is Zeus's opening speech and the poem's programmatic statement about human responsibility: men suffer beyond their allotted share, **by their own wrongdoing**, and then blame the gods. Butler's adverb is what makes the act a moral offence rather than a piece of stupidity, and it is the hinge the whole speech turns on — Aegisthus was *warned*, and did it *anyway*, and *wrongly*. "Seduce" carries impropriety; it does not carry wrong. The accessibility standard the package adopted says in terms that every qualification survives, and this is a qualification in the mouth of Zeus at the poem's opening. **Proposed correction, smallest form:** insert one word — `he had to go and seduce Agamemnon's wife wrongfully, and then kill Agamemnon`. Fuller alternative: `he had to go and wrong Agamemnon by seducing his wife, and then kill him`. Confidence high. *Also:* `had to go and` is itself a colloquialism for Butler's `must needs`; it reads well and I would keep it, but it is doing tonal work, not semantic work, and it cannot be asked to carry "unrighteously" as well.

### B01-P006

**Finding 6.1 (substantive).** Current: `Neptune is still furious with Ulysses for blinding his son Polyphemus, king of the Cyclopes.` Butler: `Neptune is still furious with Ulysses for having blinded an eye of Polyphemus king of the Cyclopes.` Two changes, both wrong, in one clause.

Confidence high.

*Also noted, no finding.* `speech is man's matter, and mine above all others—for it is I who am master here` becoming `Talk is men's business, and mine most of all, for I am master in this house` keeps the claim of household authority in full and unsoftened, which is right — it is a shocking line and it is meant to be. `Talk` for `speech` lowers it slightly (Butler means the authority to speak in the house, not chatter); `Speech is men's business` would be closer and reads no worse. The footnote marker Butler prints inside this sentence (`mine above all others 10—for it is I`) is correctly absent, with no doubled space or orphaned dash left behind — I checked this one specifically, since it is the marker most likely to leave damage.

### B01-P026

**Finding 26.1 (optional).** Current: `taking her son's words to heart`. Butler: `laid her son’s saying in her heart`. Butler's phrase is the Homeric formula for *pondering* something — turning it over inwardly — and "taking to heart" in modern English means *accepting* it, or being wounded by it. Penelope does go on to weep and sleep, so either reading works dramatically; but the formula recurs throughout the poem and fixing a rendering for it now would be worth doing. **Proposed correction, if the drafter wants to fix the formula early:** `and kept her son's words in her heart` — and add a row to `GLOSSARY.md`. Confidence low-medium as a finding; high that the formula should get a glossary row before Book 2.

### B01-P027

**Finding 27.1 (minor).** Current: `it is rare to hear a man with a voice as fine as Phemius's`. Butler: `it is a rare thing to hear a man with such a divine voice as Phemius has`. **`divine` becoming `fine` throws away a Homeric epithet for a colourless adjective.** The whole line is Telemachus publicly defending Phemius a second time, and "divine voice" is the specific claim — the bard's gift is god-given, which is exactly what Telemachus argued two paragraphs earlier when he said Jove, not the singers, sends what they sing about. `fine` severs that link. **Proposed correction:** `it is rare to hear a man with a voice as divine as Phemius's`. Confidence high.

*Also noted, no finding.* All three terms of the ultimatum survive and stay distinct: feast tonight without brawling, assembly in the morning for formal notice, and thereafter feast at one another's houses in turn at their own cost. `spunging upon one man` becoming `feeding off one man` correct. `Jove shall reckon with you in full` becoming `Jove will settle the account with you in full` keeps the accounting metaphor. The regrouping of Butler's split vocative is covered at **R5**.

### B01-P028
No material issue found. Antinous's voice stays what it is — open contempt with a curse in it — and is not homogenised with Eurymachus's two paragraphs later; I read the two side by side specifically to check. `bluster and tall talking` becoming `bluster and big talk` correct. `marvelled at the boldness of his speech` becoming `amazed at the boldness of his speech` correct.

### B01-P029

**Finding 29.1 (minor).** Current: `But I will be master in my own house, at least, and rule over those Ulysses won for me.` Butler: `nevertheless I will be chief in my own house, and will rule those whom Ulysses has won for me.` **`chief` becoming `master` breaks the word the whole exchange is fought over.** Antinous has just wished that Telemachus never be **chief** in Ithaca; Telemachus answers that he will be **chief** if he can, that it is no bad thing to be a **chief**, that someone else may take the lead — and then turns it: whatever happens outside, he will be **chief in his own house**. Four uses of one word, and the candidate renders the fourth as `master`, which drops the retort. It also collides with the next paragraph, where Butler genuinely does write `master` in Eurymachus's mouth (`you shall be master in your own house`) — so as it stands, the suitors' scene uses "master" for both, and Butler's distinction between the public title and the domestic one is gone. **Proposed correction:** `But I will be chief in my own house, and rule those whom Ulysses won for me.` (`at least` is also an addition; dropping it comes free with the fix.) Confidence high.

*Also noted, no finding.* `do not chide with me` becoming `do not scold me for it` fine. `Is this the worst fate you can think of for me?` becoming `Is that the worst fate you can wish on me?` moves from imagining to wishing — small, and arguably sharper, but it changes what Telemachus accuses Antinous of doing; `think of for me` is free to restore in the same pass.

### B01-P030

**Finding 30.1 (minor).** Current: `but you shall certainly be master in your own house`. Butler: `but you shall be master in your own house`. **`certainly` is added.** Eurymachus is being smoothly conciliatory before he starts interrogating Telemachus about the stranger, and Butler's flat `you shall be` is already a guarantee; the intensifier makes him warmer than he is and blurs the difference between his tone and Antinous's, which the review instructions ask to be kept distinct. Unsupported additions are a named class. **Proposed correction:** `but you shall be master in your own house`. Confidence high.

*Also noted, no finding.* Eurymachus's four questions survive as four, and the shift from deference to interrogation lands. `well to do` becoming `well-off` correct.

### B01-P031

**Finding 31.1 (minor).** Current: `and even when some rumor reaches me, I put no more faith in it now`. Butler: `and even if some rumour reaches me I put no more faith in it now`. **`if` becoming `when` converts a concessive condition into a statement that rumours do in fact arrive.** Butler's Telemachus is saying he would not believe one *even supposing* one came; the candidate's says they come and he disbelieves them. Conditions are a named checked class, and the change also weakens the next sentence, where the soothsayer is introduced as the thing that *does* happen (`My mother does sometimes send for a soothsayer`) in contrast to the rumours that might. **Proposed correction:** `and even if some rumor reaches me, I put no more faith in it now`. Confidence high.

*Also noted, no finding.* **The dramatic irony is intact and unexplained** — `But in his heart he knew it had been the goddess` is left to do its own work, with no added signalling, which is the right call and the thing most likely to have been over-helped. `prophecyings` becoming `prophecies` correct. `does indeed sometimes send` becoming `does sometimes send` drops Butler's `indeed`; trivial.

### B01-P032

**Finding 32.1 (minor).** Current: `high up in a tower looking out over the courtyard`. Butler: `high up in a tower that looked on to the outer court`. **`outer court` becoming `courtyard` drops `outer`**, which names a specific part of a Homeric house — the one the suitors have been feasting in, the one Odysseus bars the doors of in Book 22 — and distinguishes it from the inner rooms Penelope withdraws to. The Cast and the later Books both depend on the distinction. **Proposed correction:** `looking out over the outer court`. Confidence medium-high.

*Also noted, no finding.* The closing scene is the best-served paragraph in the Book: the pair of blazing torches, the twenty oxen, Euryclea's purchase and Laertes's respect, the reason he never took her to his bed, her nursing of Telemachus, the folded shirt, the peg by the bedside, the silver catch, the bolt drawn home by its strap, and the woollen fleece — all present, all concrete, none generalised. The aside about Laertes's fear of his wife's resentment is kept, which `continuity.md` promised and which a shorter-minded draft would have cut as incidental. `hither, then, he hied, brooding and full of thought` becoming `he went there now, deep in thought and full of care` adds "care" where Butler has only thought; small. `resentment` becoming `anger` is a shade hotter than Butler's word. `woollen` is the one British spelling left standing in a candidate that is otherwise American — see **R2**.

## Records findings

These change no word of the reading text but should be settled before Book 2 is drafted, because each is a rule the next Book will inherit.

**Finding R1 (minor) — the candidate mixes typographic quotation marks with ASCII apostrophes, and nothing records a decision.** The candidate uses curly opening and closing double quotes for speech (30 and 29 marks) and the **straight ASCII apostrophe** for every apostrophe — **22 occurrences across 15 paragraphs**: `Agamemnon's`, `Ulysses's`, `Telemachus's`, `father's`, `mother's`, `Phemius's`, `Neptune's`, `son's`, `Danaans'`. The staged `original-en` uses the typographic apostrophe throughout (16 occurrences) and no ASCII apostrophe at all; the served `modern-en` being replaced uses ASCII throughout — **including ASCII double quotes**, so it is at least internally consistent. The candidate is consistent with neither, and in EB Garamond at reading size the mixture is visible on the page. **Recommendation:** use the typographic apostrophe, matching the curly double quotes the candidate already has and the `original-en` it sits beside; record it in a `PUNCTUATION.md` for the package, as the Meditations package did. This interacts with mapping-table item 3 (`Odysseus's`), so decide both in the same pass.

**Finding R2 (minor) — the candidate is American-spelled, with one British survivor, and nothing records that either.** American: `favor`, `honored`, `honor` twice, `offense`, `harbor`, `marveled`, `rumor` — 8 occurrences. British: **`woollen`**, one occurrence, B01-P032. Butler is British throughout (`honour`, `travelled`, `harbour`, `rumour`). The served `modern-en` being replaced is American throughout (honor 38, favor 24, traveled 15, neighbor 15, labor 9, color 4; zero British forms), so the candidate's American default is consistent with the file it replaces and I would **keep it** — but it is a decision made silently, in a package that records decisions as small as whether "king" is capitalised. **Recommendation:** record the spelling standard in `GLOSSARY.md` (American, matching the served editions and the rest of the product) and fix `woollen` to `woolen`. Note that `draughts` (B01-P008) stays as it is — it is the game's proper name, not a spelling variant.

**Finding R3 (minor) — `continuity.md` states that Book 1 contains no defective source sentence, and it contains one.** The file's "Unresolved source issues" section says all 32 source paragraphs are "complete sentences/passages ending in terminal punctuation, except source paragraph index 17". Butler's `for he is not dead yet not on the mainland` (B01-P014, verified at PG line 538) is a genuine crux — unpunctuated and ungrammatical as printed, admitting two readings with different consequences. The drafter resolved it silently, in the weaker direction, and did not record it. See finding **14.1**. **Recommendation:** add it to the "Unresolved source issues" section with the reading chosen and why, so that a later collator diffing Butler against the candidate does not find an unexplained difference.

**Finding R4 (minor) — `GLOSSARY.md`'s citation for the "tell me, and tell me true" row is wrong.** The row says the formula occurs at "P12, P13, P15, P18 in Butler's paragraphing". The four actual instances are **B01-P013 twice** (`tell me and tell me true, who you are` and `Tell me also truly, for I want to know`), **B01-P014**, and **B01-P016** — three source paragraphs, not four, with one paragraph carrying two. The rendering itself is applied correctly in all four places; only the citation is wrong. **Recommendation:** correct the row to the `B01-Pnnn` IDs used everywhere else, and drop the parallel "Butler's paragraphing" numbering, which is 0-indexed against the 1-indexed packet IDs and is the likely cause of the slip. The same off-by-one appears in `continuity.md`, which labels Telemachus's longest speech "P16" and then explains in a parenthesis that it is B01-P017.

**Finding R5 (optional) — two quotation-punctuation normalisations are unrecorded, in the one package that records this class carefully.** The drafter went to real trouble over Butler's unclosed-quotation convention at P018 and P019 and documented it as decision **D4**. Two other Victorian quotation habits were silently normalised: at **B01-P018**, Butler's comma-continuation `“Is that so?” exclaimed Minerva, “then you do indeed want…` became a full stop and a capital (`cried Minerva. “Then you truly do need…`); and at **B01-P027**, Butler's split vocative `Then Telemachus spoke, “Shameless,” he cried, “and insolent suitors,` was regrouped into `Then Telemachus spoke. “Shameless, insolent suitors,” he cried,`. **Both are right** — the first is a punctuation convention modern readers misread as a sentence fragment, the second is a genuinely awkward split — and I would make both changes. They belong in `continuity.md` as a stated class beside D4, not as silent edits, precisely so that D4's preservation reads as a *decision* rather than an inconsistency. This is also what accounts for the candidate having one open/close pair fewer than the source.

## Coverage and limitations

- **Coverage: complete.** All eleven packets read in order with their `CONTEXT ONLY` neighbours; every paragraph `B01-P001` through `B01-P032` has exactly one entry above; then `candidate-v1-readable.md` read straight through for voice, pacing, repetition and transitions.
- **On the continuous read**, the candidate holds its register well and reads as one voice. Two Book-level observations, both already covered by numbered findings rather than raised again: the narrative paragraphs are consistently closer to Butler than the speeches are, and the losses cluster in the speeches, where the drafter was working hardest; and the vocabulary thins slightly in the last third (`fine` doing the work of *divine*, *beautiful* and *great* in four different places), which is the ordinary fatigue signature of a long single-session draft and is worth a targeted pass rather than a redraft.
- **What this review did not do.** I did not check the Danish edition, the audio manifest, the Cast per-chapter summaries, or any app code. I did not read any translation other than Butler's, so no finding rests on a comparison with one, and I make no claim that any phrasing was or was not imported. I did not verify Butler's fidelity to the Greek, and no finding asks the candidate to be more faithful to Homer than Butler is — finding 5.1 flags the one place the candidate silently moves toward the Greek, and asks for it to be recorded rather than reversed.
- **No numerical score is given, and this review does not establish that no errors remain.** A second reader would find things I did not.

## B01-P009 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Butler 1900):**

> Telemachus saw her long before any one else did. He was sitting moodily
among the suitors thinking about his brave father, and how he would
send them flying out of the house, if he were to come to his own again
and be honoured as in days gone by. Thus brooding as he sat among them,
he caught sight of Minerva and went straight to the gate, for he was
vexed that a stranger should be kept waiting for admittance. He took
her right hand in his own, and bade her give him her spear. “Welcome,”
said he, “to our house, and when you have partaken of food you shall
tell us what you have come for.”

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> Telemachus was the first to see her. He was sitting among the suitors, in low spirits, thinking of his noble father—how he would send them flying out of the house if he came home again and was honored as he used to be. Brooding like this, he caught sight of Minerva and went straight to the gate, annoyed that a stranger should be left waiting to be let in. He took her right hand in his, took her spear from her, and said, “Welcome to our house. Once you have had something to eat, you shall tell us what has brought you.”

## B01-P010 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler 1900):**

> He led the way as he spoke, and Minerva followed him. When they were
within he took her spear and set it in the spear-stand against a strong
bearing-post along with the many other spears of his unhappy father,
and he conducted her to a richly decorated seat under which he threw a
cloth of damask. There was a footstool also for her feet, and he set
another seat near her for himself, away from the suitors, that she
might not be annoyed while eating by their noise and insolence, and

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

Once inside, he set her spear in the spear-rack against a sturdy pillar, alongside the many other spears of his unlucky father, and led her to a richly worked seat over which he spread a cloth of fine linen. There was a footstool for her feet as well, and he set another seat near her for himself, apart from the suitors, so that she would not be troubled at her meal by their noise and rudeness, and so that he could ask her more freely about his father.

## B01-P012 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler 1900):**

> Then the suitors came in and took their places on the benches and
seats. Forthwith men servants poured water over their hands, maids
went round with the bread-baskets, pages filled the mixing-bowls with
wine and water, and they laid their hands upon the good things that
were before them. As soon as they had had enough to eat and drink they
wanted music and dancing, which are the crowning embellishments of a
banquet, so a servant brought a lyre to Phemius, whom they compelled
perforce to sing to them. As soon as he touched his lyre and began to
sing Telemachus spoke low to Minerva, with his head close to hers that
no man might hear.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> Then the suitors came in and took their places on the benches and seats. At once men-servants poured water over their hands, maids went round with baskets of bread, and pages filled the mixing-bowls with wine and water, and they helped themselves to the good things before them. Once they had eaten and drunk their fill, they wanted music and dancing, the crowning pleasures of a banquet, so a servant brought a lyre to Phemius, and made him sing for them whether he wished to or not. As soon as he touched the lyre and began to sing, Telemachus leaned close to Minerva and spoke low, so that no one else could hear.

## B01-P013 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Butler 1900):**

> “I hope, sir,” said he, “that you will not be offended with what I am
going to say. Singing comes cheap to those who do not pay for it, and
all this is done at the cost of one whose bones lie rotting in some
wilderness or grinding to powder in the surf. If these men were to see
my father come back to Ithaca they would pray for longer legs rather
than a longer purse, for money would not serve them; but he, alas, has
fallen on an ill fate, and even when people do sometimes say that he is
coming, we no longer heed them; we shall never see him again. And now,
sir, tell me and tell me true, who you are and where you come from.
Tell me of your town and parents, what manner of ship you came in, how
your crew brought you to Ithaca, and of what nation they declared
themselves to be—for you cannot have come by land. Tell me also truly,
for I want to know, are you a stranger to this house, or have you been
here in my father’s time? In the old days we had many visitors for my
father went about much himself.”

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> “I hope, sir,” he said, “that you will not take offense at what I am about to say. Singing comes cheap to men who do not pay for it, and all of this is at the expense of one whose bones lie rotting somewhere in the wild, or grinding to powder in the surf. If these men saw my father come back to Ithaca, they would pray for longer legs rather than a fatter purse, for money would be no use to them then. But he, poor man, has met with an evil fate, and even when people do sometimes tell us he is coming, we no longer believe them; we will never see him again. But now, sir, tell me truly who you are and where you come from. Tell me of your city and your parents, what kind of ship brought you, and how your crew brought you to Ithaca, and what people they said they were—for you can hardly have come by land. And tell me truly, for I want to know, are you a stranger to this house, or have you been here before, in my father's time? We had many visitors in the old days, for my father went about among people a great deal himself.”

Candidate: `b24-draft.json`. Source: `book24/source-book24.json` (Butler, PG #1727). Also compared against the live baseline, accepted Book 23 (`book23/candidate-accepted.json`), accepted Books 20–22, and every accepted Book that shares a recurring passage or formula with this one.

**Every paragraph was read: 45 of 45 source/candidate pairs, plus the title.** I read them in packets with the neighbouring paragraphs, then re-read the whole Book for continuity.

**Counts:** 15 findings: 3 blocking and 12 non-blocking.
By category: meaning 7, convention 7, name 1.

**Verdict: BLOCKING FINDINGS**

## Blocking findings

| ¶ | Finding |
|---|---|
| 9 | "tambour frame—an embroidery frame—" should be "a great embroidery frame". Butler repeats this passage at Book 2 ¶5 and Book 19 ¶13, and both accepted Books render it "embroidery frame" with no gloss. The draft brings in a third form, glosses a term that is not at its first use, and adds dashes Butler does not have. |
| 30 | "plainly and honestly, saying," should be "plainly and in all honesty, and said:". This is the GLOSSARY Book 2 fixed formula. It was first used of this same speaker, Halitherses, at B02 ¶8, and Books 7 and 16 keep it. |
| 41 | "gray-eyed maiden" should be "blue-eyed maiden". Butler writes "blue-eyed damsel", his only "blue-eyed" in the poem. The draft silently harmonizes it to his usual "grey-eyed" and does not record the change. |

## The drafter's claims, checked against Butler

- **"spirit" → "ghost":** confirmed. Butler writes "ghost" 14 times and the draft has 14; no "spirit" is left. Butler's "it said" / "so it began" for a ghost (¶3, ¶7, ¶8) becomes "he". That is a reasonable modernization, and accepted Book 11 has no competing form.
- **Danaans, Achaeans and Argives:** confirmed. The counts match Butler per term (2 / 7 / 3), and each term sits where Butler has it, including Nestor's "Hold fast, Argives … sons of the Achaeans" at ¶4. No "Greek" is left.
- **¶0 "the rock Leucas":** confirmed; it is Butler's words. The draft also restores "Oceanus" (the live text had "Ocean"), which matches Book 23. "Healer of sorrow" (singular), "seals" and "whining" are all Butler's.
- **¶9 "tambour frame" with the gloss:** **not upheld** (blocking finding above). Butler does write "tambour frame", but the edition fixed "embroidery frame" twice (B02 ¶5, B19 ¶13), so the gloss is neither at first use nor consistent. "Sweethearts" is Butler's word and matches Books 2 and 19. "Pall" is Butler's word at ¶9 (twice) and "shroud" at ¶16. The draft keeps the two apart, and Books 2 and 19 use "pall". Confirmed.
- **¶10:** "gallery" (×2) confirmed for Butler's "cloister"/"cloisters". "reviled" / "struck and insulted" confirmed; they are Butler's two distinct words. **"aegis-bearing Zeus":** Butler here writes "Aegis-bearing Jove", so the epithet is his and not imported. The form matches accepted B03 ¶30, B04 ¶65 and ¶67, and B06 ¶8 and ¶24. "a quantity of iron" and "through the iron" have no "axes", which is Butler's wording and matches accepted Book 21 (¶4, ¶5, ¶6, ¶28 "through the iron"). "Armour" → "weapons" for the armoury removal matches the Book 16/19 precedent.
- **Philoetius / "stockman":** Butler's single "stockman" at ¶23 is kept, and the name is kept at ¶14 and ¶22. This matches Books 20–23. **"Sicel"** (×3) matches Butler and accepted Book 20 ¶33 ("the Sicels"), and "Sicania" at ¶17 is Butler's word. **"Arceisius"** (¶15, ¶41) matches Butler and accepted B04 ¶65, B14 ¶9 and B16 ¶11.
- **¶24 "foreland":** confirmed; it is Butler's word, and "headland" at ¶6 renders his "point". The oath "by Father Zeus, Athena, and Apollo" follows Butler's capital here, as accepted Book 7 ¶25 does.
- **"At this" with no comma:** confirmed at ¶27, ¶30, ¶41 and ¶43. No "At this," or "On this" is left. (¶30 has a separate non-blocking verb finding.)
- **Restored refrains:** confirmed. ¶28 has "Let us be up and doing" and "Let us be up, then, and after them". ¶41 and ¶42 have "poise your spear and hurl it" and "poised his spear and hurled it".
- **Athena's final intervention and the violence:** kept at full strength. ¶10 keeps "our brains were being battered in, and the ground seethed with our blood". ¶42 keeps the spear through the helmet, the rattling armour, and "would have killed every one of them". ¶43 keeps the eagle swoop, the "thunderbolt of fire" (restored) and "stop this warlike strife, or Zeus will be angry with you". ¶44 keeps "contending parties" and "presently".
- **Title:** correct. It is Butler's heading with only Odysseus ×2 and Athena substituted, the same form as the accepted Book 20–23 titles (Roman names mapped, American spelling, wording otherwise kept). It is identical to the live title.
- **Quotation structure:** the opening and closing marks balance in every paragraph and match Butler. ¶4 → ¶5 is an unclosed speech (D4), preserved. ¶9 ends without a closing mark, as Butler's does. The nesting inside ¶4 and ¶9 is reproduced. There are no ASCII quotes, Roman names, British spellings, "cloister" or "hall". Ops and Arete do not occur. "Laertes’s", "Eupeithes’s" and "goddess’s" follow hazard 3.

# Odyssey Book 24 — Candidate-Only Accessibility Review

**Reviewed:** `b24-draft.json` only (45 paragraphs, indices 0–44, read in full,
start to finish, then re-read paragraph by paragraph) plus
`accessibility-review-prompt.md`. No source/original-en, live baseline,
notes, reviews, or other Books were opened.

**Coverage:** All 45 paragraphs (0–44) read in full as a first-time
listener, per the prompt's instructions — the entire Book.

## Counts

- Total findings: **32**
- Blocking: **6**
- Non-blocking (style/texture notes): **26**
- By category: archaic 19, aloud 5, reference 6, flow 2

## Blocking findings (real risk of confusion for a first-time listener)

1. **Para 6** — "Over these the host of the Argives built a noble tomb" —
   opens a new paragraph on a pronoun ("these") whose antecedent (Achilles's
   bones) is in the *previous* paragraph. Across a paragraph break in audio,
   the referent is very likely lost.
2. **Para 9** — "would not say outright that she would not marry" — a
   genuine double negative; a listener can easily come away believing
   Penelope flatly refused, the opposite of the intended ambiguity.
3. **Para 10** — "and they fell thick on one another" — ambiguous "they":
   could resolve to the arrows just mentioned or to the suitors being
   struck. No way to disambiguate by ear.
4. **Para 16** — "Neither his mother, nor I his father, who were his
   parents, could throw our arms about him" — triple-stacked
   negative/relative clause, hard to track aloud, made worse by a
   redundant "who were his parents" clause.
5. **Para 28** — "buried every man his own" — archaic inverted phrasing
   with an implied, unstated object; genuinely hard to parse on first
   hearing (means "each man buried his own dead").
6. **Para 41** — "pray to the gray-eyed maiden" — Athena, disguised as
   Mentor, tells Laertes to pray to "the gray-eyed maiden," an epithet for
   *herself*, with no signal she means herself. A first-time listener
   hearing "Mentor" speak has no way to connect this to Athena; the
   ambiguity is only retroactively resolved when the narration says
   Laertes "prayed to her" in the next paragraph.

## Non-blocking notes (style/texture, still worth a pass)

The bulk of the remaining findings are **archaic inverted syntax**
("many a fat sheep... we sacrificed," "took each his proper place,"
"down she darted," "So spoke Athena") and **archaic idiom/diction**
("make an end of you," "be up and doing," "in the wantonness of their
hearts," "endowed with such rare excellence of understanding," "stay" for
"stop," "All hail"). None of these block comprehension outright — an adult
listener can work out the sense from context — but they read as noticeably
stiffer/older than the surrounding modern-English prose and interrupt flow.

A smaller cluster is **unexplained references**: "the old man of the sea"
(Nereus), "the daughter of Tyndareus" (Clytemnestra, several paragraphs
removed from the earlier "wicked wife" mention), "Sicel woman"
(Sicilian), "the birds all flew on our right hands" (favorable bird-omen
convention), and "the cities of the Cephallenians" (never glossed, recurs
later in the Book at paragraph 28 without clarification either).

Two are **flow** notes: "mighty in your might" (redundant doubling) and
"his nostrils quivered" (isolated physical detail with no emotional
frame).

## What reads well

- The Amphimedon narrative (paragraphs 9–10) — the recognition-scene
  recap of the bow contest and the killings — is vivid, well-paced, and
  clear despite its length.
- The Odysseus/Laertes reunion (paragraphs 19–23) reads naturally and
  movingly; dialogue beats land cleanly and the scar-proof exchange is
  easy to follow.
- The closing battle and Athena's intervention (paragraphs 42–44) move
  briskly with no comprehension friction beyond the syntax notes above.
- Dialogue throughout is generally natural; most issues are narrator-voice
  archaisms rather than character voice problems.

## Verdict

**BLOCKING FINDINGS** — 6 items (paragraphs 6, 9, 10, 16, 28, 41) carry a
real risk of a first-time listener losing the thread or coming away with
the wrong meaning, and should be targeted before acceptance. Overall
readability of the chapter is otherwise strong; the remaining 26 findings
are texture/consistency polish (archaic syntax and unglossed references)
rather than comprehension blockers.

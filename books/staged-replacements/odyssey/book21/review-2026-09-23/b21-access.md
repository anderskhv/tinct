## Stringing and shooting — picturability check (paras 6, 38, 39)

- **Para 6** (Telemachus sets up the axes): the physical picture — axes in a
  row in a trench, tamped straight with a line — is exactly as clear (or as
  irreducibly ambiguous) as it is in every English Odyssey; the poem itself
  never fully specifies how an arrow passes through twelve axe-heads in a
  row, and inventing that detail would violate the "don't add details the
  text doesn't contain" rule. Not flagged.
- **Para 38** (stringing the bow, testing the string): clean and vivid as
  written — bard/lyre-peg simile, the string's "swallow's twitter," Zeus's
  thunderclap. No changes proposed.
- **Para 39** (nocking the arrow and the shot through the axes): the shot
  itself — draw, aim, release, arrow through every axe-head and out into the
  courtyard — reads clearly. The one problem is the opening clause (item 5
  above), which momentarily takes the listener's attention off "he took an
  arrow... and laid it on the bow" to puzzle over an aside. Fixing that one
  clause is enough to make the whole passage read cleanly aloud from start
  to finish; no other wording in 38–39 needs touching.

## Five things this Book puts to you explicitly, and they should not be left unruled

1. **The open `sea shore`, and the cross-Book hazard behind it.** Butler writes `sea-shore` at B05-P007 and `sea shore` at B05-P012; the candidate prints **`sea shore`** at both, because accepted Book 4 asserts the open form. Under **D15** (`PUNCTUATION.md` §4) the modern standard form is arguably the closed **`seashore`**, which would make the accepted Book 4 wrong and would create exactly the cross-Book drift D15 exists to prevent. The drafter kept the accepted Book's form and raised the question rather than splitting the difference. **Rule it**, and note that the package's own `hyphen_drift()` check is **blind to this class**: it compares *hyphenated in one Book* against *open in another*, not *closed* against *open*. If the ruling is `seashore`, a Book 4 successor (`candidate-v3.json`) answers it, exactly as Book 3's finding 27.1 produced `book02/candidate-v3.json`.
2. **The one word supplied at B05-P012.** Butler prints *"Calypso then went close up to him said:"* — no conjunction. The candidate prints *"Then Calypso went close up to him and said:"*. One word supplied, the smallest repair available, the same class as accepted B03-P028's stray `and`. Is supplying a word the right disposition, or should the sentence be recast so that nothing is supplied?
3. **The `heaven` census is UNBROKEN here, and Book 4's was broken once.** Butler uses `heaven` four times and the candidate four. Book 4 rendered *"mid heaven"* as *"the middle of the sky"* and asserted the break by name, because that one was a position in the sky and not the metonym for the gods. B05-P023's *"How black is Jove making heaven with his clouds"* is the same family — and it is **kept** as `heaven`, on the ground that it is not a position being measured and the metonymic reading is live. Is that the right line, or is this the same break Book 4 took?
4. **`playing battledore and shuttlecock with it` → `batting it back and forth between them`** (B05-P024). The game is dead and its name is opaque; the image — four winds striking one light thing between them — is Butler's. This is the Book's only image-word replaced rather than kept, and the counterpart of Book 4's `furs` → `ruffles`, which round 1 upheld. Is the image preserved at the same scale, and is anything added?
5. **The 62-word sentence left standing at B05-P017.** Three of Butler's nine sixty-word periods survive. Two are similes where the chain is the effect — B05-P030 (children rejoicing over a recovering father) and B05-P037 (the man banking fire-seed in the ashes, the Book's last sentence) — and those are the **B04-P038** disposition applied to similes. The third is **Calypso's offer of immortality**, a single unbroken act of persuasion, and it is the one the drafter is least sure of. It is also the paragraph the near-identical report flags: 119 words, four word-level edits. **Rule it.**

A flow read is the step that can see what a three-paragraph packet cannot:
a word made to repeat by the corrections, a connective that has become a tic,
an antecedent that only goes wrong at distance, a speech that loses its
speaker across a paragraph break. Those are the eight things checked.

## F-1. The one flattening round 1 named is gone, and only that one word differs

Butler's "excellent man" warranty is written twice and not identically. The two
sentences now read, in full:

- **B03-P002** — “Ask him to speak the truth, and he will tell no lies, for he is an excellent man.”
- **B03-P025** — “Ask him to speak the truth, and he will tell you no lies, for he is an excellent man.”

Read continuously, twenty-three paragraphs apart, that is Butler's effect: the
same formula, closed to the same cadence, with one word of it given to
Menelaus that was not given to Nestor. Every other word of the two sentences is
identical, checked token for token. This was finding 2.2 and records finding
R1, and it is the one place the package's consistency discipline had been
applied to a sameness Butler had not written.

## F-2. `decreed` three times is Butler's one word, and nothing competes with it

Finding 24.1 replaced two renderings of Butler's `counselled` with one.
Post-correction, `decreed` stands at B03-P021, P023 and P024, and the near
synonyms a drafter reaches for are absent from the whole Book: `planned` 0,
`resolved` 0, `ordained` 0, `decided on` 0. Two of the three stand in adjacent
paragraphs and both govern `destruction` — that is Butler's own parallel
(*counselled his destruction* / *counselled her destruction*), preserved
rather than created, and it reads at speed as the refrain it is in him.

## F-3. `companies` beside `companions` — recorded, and it holds

Butler has two different words in three sentences: `guilds` (B03-P001 twice,
B03-P005) and `his company round him` (B03-P005). The candidate renders the
first as `companies` throughout and the second, at finding 5.1, as
`his companions`. The two English words are closer to one another than
Butler's two are, so this was read specifically for confusion and there is
none: the referents are a sentence apart, differ in number and in possession
(*nine companies with five hundred men in each* against *his companions around
him*), and the rendering of `guilds` is the same in both of its paragraphs.
Recorded so a later pass does not read the pair as a drift. No change.

## F-4. The four connectives put back are Butler's, and they do not read as a tic

Round 1 restored `so` (P007), `then` (P013), `therefore` (P015) and
`therefore` (P029). Counted over the whole Book: `therefore` 3 against
Butler's 4, `indeed` 3 against Butler's 3. The round added no connective Butler
lacks and left the candidate below him on both counts. Read continuously,
each of the four is carrying an inference the surrounding sentences state
(the cup given first, the sign they were shown, Nestor's offer), which is what
made them worth restoring.

## F-5. Nestor's speech survives both of its paragraph breaks, and the tense-chain holds

The Book's two long speeches run across D4 paragraph boundaries — P012→P015
and P022→P024. Read straight through, the speaker never has to be re-inferred:
each continuation paragraph opens its own `“` and closes none, exactly the
source's seven unbalanced paragraphs, and the corrections did not disturb the
42/35 mark totals. The new pluperfect at P013 was read in its chain —
*when we had sacked … as heaven **had** scattered us, **then** Zeus saw fit* —
and it now orders three events that v1 left level: the sack, the scattering,
and the hard voyage Zeus then willed.

## F-6. `sweetmeats` is kept, and the one thing round 1 did not say about it

Finding 37.2 asks that `sweetmeats` be kept and the reason recorded, so that a
later pass does not "improve" it into `delicacies` — the one word it must not
use, because `delicacies fit for princes` is verbatim the served `modern-en`
splice sitting in the served original's next paragraph. The build asserts the
word is still there. The flow read adds one fact round 1 does not state:
**Butler prints `sweet meats`, open, two words**; the candidate closes it up.
The closed form is the dictionary form of the word, and the open form invites
exactly the misreading the reviewer named (*sweet meat*). So this is a
normalization of one word's typography, not a substitution of another word,
and it stands — recorded here so a later reader of the diff does not find an
unrecorded change.

## F-7. B03-P038 reads as abruptly as D14 says it will

Twelve words after a three-hundred-word paragraph, and then the Book stops
mid-sentence. In a paginated reader beside a 208-word `original-en` paragraph
it will look like a bug — and it *is* a bug, in the other file. This is the
cost section C of the review accepted with its eyes open, and the flow read
confirms it is a visible cost rather than a hidden one, which is the argument
for recording the repair text in `ACCEPTANCE.md` and A3 rather than leaving it
to be rediscovered.

## F-8. `wooers` beside `suitors`, at one paragraph's distance

Finding 18.1 restored Butler's variation inside B03-P018 (`suitors` early,
`wooers` late, with `wooing` between them). B03-P017, immediately before, has
`the wicked suitors`. Read continuously the three refer to one group and
nothing suggests otherwise; the `woo` root is doing the work in
`wooers … wooing`, as it does in Butler. No change.

# Accessibility Review — Odyssey Book 14 (Odysseus in the hut with Eumaeus)

**Coverage:** All 35 paragraphs (indices 0–34) read in full, start to finish, as a first-time
adult listener/reader would encounter them (text is also played as audio).

**Counts:** 6 findings — 2 blocking (flow/embedding), 4 non-blocking (1 aloud/vocabulary,
1 flow, 1 reference, 1 archaic syntax).

## Findings by category
- **aloud (1, non-blocking):** "chamois skin" (paragraph 2) — an obscure, hard-to-pronounce
  word for an ordinary sleeping skin; "goatskin" fits the swineherd setting and removes the
  stumble.
- **flow (2: 1 blocking, 1 non-blocking):**
  - Paragraph 3 — a single sentence stacks a relative clause, a colon-introduced list, and
    two further relative/comparative clauses; a listener loses the thread of what the final
    clause attaches to. Blocking.
  - Paragraph 8 — the relative clause "who lets his poverty tempt him into lying" is
    separated from its noun by an interjected comparison ("as much as I hate the fires of
    hell"), causing a momentary loss of who is being described. Non-blocking.
- **flow / ambiguous parenthetical (1, blocking):** Paragraph 11 — "Castor son of
  Hylax—whose name I claim, a man held in the highest honor...—treated me..." stacks two
  parenthetical insertions on the same noun, and "whose name I claim" is genuinely
  ambiguous in the moment (claiming what, exactly?). This one blocks comprehension of an
  otherwise simple genealogical fact.
- **reference (1, non-blocking):** Paragraph 9 — "the house of Arceisius" names an ancestor
  never introduced in the book; a listener can't connect it to Telemachus/Odysseus's family
  without outside knowledge, though the surrounding sentence keeps the stakes roughly clear.
- **archaic (1, non-blocking):** Paragraph 16 — "so much of it had he left in King Pheidon's
  house" inverts normal word order in a way no other sentence in the book does.

## Not flagged (deliberate epic/style features, left alone)
- The recurring narrator apostrophe ("To this you answered, swineherd Eumaeus...") is a
  consistent, repeated Homeric device across this book and not a one-off error; it does not
  block comprehension once established and was left alone per instructions.
- The nested false-story structure (the disguised Odysseus narrating, in the first person,
  a story in which a character named "Odysseus" also appears in the third person) is
  expected for this book and consistently marked with quotation and speaker tags
  throughout; it was not flagged.
- Epic listing/repetition (herd counts, "twelve..." formulas) and Greek name forms
  (Ilius, Aegyptus, etc.) are house style / deliberate epic features and were left alone.

## Verdict
**BLOCKING FINDINGS** — 2 of the 6 findings (paragraphs 3 and 11) involve sentence
structures dense enough to lose a first-time listener mid-sentence and should be
addressed; the remaining 4 are polish-level and optional.

## Checks the coordinator asked for

**¶20: CORRECT. This is the most important fix in the Book.** The live ¶20 repeats ¶8's oath: the no-reward-until-he-comes terms, "hell fire", the oath by King Zeus and the hearth, and "this very year… end of this moon". It shares 76% of its word types with live ¶8. Only its first clause ("I see that you are skeptical") comes from Butler's ¶20. The live text therefore lost the whole bargain: the gods called to witness, the cloak and shirt, being sent to Dulichium, and the threat of the precipice. Without the bargain, Eumaeus's answer in ¶21 ("a pretty figure I should cut … if I were to kill you") makes no sense.

The draft's ¶20 is a fresh and complete translation. Every item in Butler's paragraph is present and in order. Butler's order "cloak and shirt" is kept here, as distinct from "shirt and cloak" at ¶8. "of good wear" is rendered "good quality", the same as ¶8. "all the gods in heaven" keeps "heaven". The draft's ¶20 does not echo ¶8, and no pair of draft paragraphs is more than 40% similar. There is one non-blocking nuance: Butler's "a warning to tramps **not to** go about… telling lies" has become "a warning to tramps **who** go around… telling lies".

**¶5 "eleven": CORRECT to remove it.** PG #1727 reads "he runs even large flocks of goats", with no number. Homer's Greek does say eleven, and PG's "even" may be a misprint of "eleven". However, the source is pinned (D30), and D16(b) allows a word to be supplied only from Butler's own parallel, and Butler has none here. "he keeps large flocks of goats too" is an acceptable rendering of "even". Three other things in ¶5 are non-blocking findings: the dropped "comes out of heaven", the supplied "his wife", and "is"→"was".

**¶12 "Aegyptus": CORRECT.** Butler writes "the river Aegyptus", and "Aegyptus" is not in the closed name table. "Egypt" is kept for the country. Butler's "though we were going down a river" (PG has no "as") is read as "as if we were going down a river", which is the only sensible reading. It is recorded here, not raised as a finding.

**¶3 "Ilius": CORRECT.** This follows the Book 2 ruling. "noble horses" matches accepted Book 11. Two things in ¶3 are findings: "insult"→"turn him away" (blocking) and "young lords"→"young men" (non-blocking).

**¶15 shipwreck formula: PARTLY.** The notes say Butler's wording differs between the two Books and that the draft matches accepted B12-P34/35. Both claims are only partly true.
- Butler's thunderbolt, spinning, seagulls and "deprived" sentences are **word for word** the same as B12-P35.
- The draft matches B12's "spun round and round", "fire and brimstone", "seagulls" and "soon".
- The draft changes "deprived" to "robbed" (blocking, convention).
- It also reverses Butler's "sea and sky" and changes the construction of the line (non-blocking). Butler has "sky and sea" at B12-P34 but "sea and sky" here, and D29 keeps his order.
- "I was all dismayed" has become "I was in despair". This is left alone: it is not a held row, and B10's accepted "dismayed" is used for a different line.

**Nested quotations, ¶10–17 and ¶26–31: CORRECT.** For every paragraph, the source and the draft have the same counts of “ ” ‘, and the same answer to whether the paragraph opens with “ and whether it ends with ”.
- ¶10–16 stay open and ¶17 closes.
- ¶18 is left unclosed and ¶19 reopens (D4).
- ¶26 opens the outer quotation. ¶27, ¶29 and ¶30 reopen the outer mark and close only the inner ‘ ’. ¶28 ends with Butler's colon. ¶31 closes the outer quotation.
- There is no ASCII ' or ", no spaced em dash, and no bare "Odysseus’".

One problem comes from normalizing ¶26. Butler's "Listen to me," said he, "Eumaeus and the rest of you; …" is a split vocative, and PUNCTUATION §3 says to regroup it. The draft instead leaves the stranded sentence "Eumaeus, and the rest of you." (non-blocking, aloud).

# Book 22 — Accessibility Review (fresh candidate-only read)

**Read:** all 52 paragraphs (indices 0–51), full text, start to finish, as a first-time
adult listener. No source, original-en, live baseline, notes, or other Books were
consulted — only `b22-draft.json` and the accessibility-review prompt.

## Findings: 4 (2 blocking, 2 non-blocking)

1. **Paragraph 10 (non-blocking, consistency)** — Telemachus throws "the spear" at
   Amphinomus as if already armed, one paragraph before he asks his father to send
   for weapons for himself and the herdsmen. Momentary "where did that come from"
   snag; doesn't affect who-kills-whom clarity (Telemachus kills Amphinomus is
   unambiguous).

2. **Paragraph 15 (non-blocking, reference)** — The escape route is called "a trap
   door" in paragraph 14 and "the narrow passage" in the very next lines (14–15),
   used interchangeably for what may or may not be the same exit. A listener can
   lose the geography of the one doorway the suitors are trying to use for help.

3. **Paragraph 25 (BLOCKING, reference)** — Agelaus's rallying speech to the six
   spear-throwers never names Odysseus; it only uses "he/him," right after the name
   "Mentor" was mentioned. A listener has to infer the target is Odysseus rather
   than Mentor — this is exactly the who's-being-attacked ambiguity called out as a
   must-flag in a combat scene.

4. **Paragraph 29 (BLOCKING, consistency)** — Agelaus (introduced by full name three
   paragraphs earlier) is referred to only as "Damastor's son" here, and the blow
   against him is described as merely "struck ... in close combat," not stated as
   fatal — unlike every other kill in this fight, which states the death outcome
   plainly. The listener only learns Agelaus actually died two paragraphs later,
   incidentally, when Odysseus picks up his dropped sword "when he was killed." A
   listener can genuinely lose track of the fact that Agelaus is dead at this point
   in the battle.

## Notes on deliberate epic features (not flagged)

- The narrator's direct second-person address to Eumaeus ("And then you taunted
  him, swineherd Eumaeus...") in paragraph 19 is a classic Homeric apostrophe. It
  doesn't obscure who's speaking or acting once you're in it, and it's a
  consistent, deliberate epic device — not flagged.
- The clearly enumerated kill-lists in paragraphs 27 and 29's first half
  ("Odysseus killed Demoptolemus, Telemachus killed Euryades, Eumaeus killed
  Elatus, and the stockman killed Pisander" / "Odysseus hit Eurydamas, Telemachus
  hit Amphimedon, and Eumaeus hit Polybus") read unusually well — each attacker and
  victim is named cleanly, with no ambiguity about who did what to whom. This is
  the model the two blocking findings above should be brought in line with.
- Anatomical/violent detail (arrow through Antinous's throat, arrow through
  Eurymachus's nipple into his liver, Melanthius's mutilation, the mass hanging)
  is left at full strength per instructions — not softened, not flagged.
- Epithets, similes (the fish, the eagles and birds, the thrushes/doves in a net),
  and repetition are all left untouched as deliberate epic features.

## Verdict: BLOCKING FINDINGS

Two of the four findings (paragraphs 25 and 29) bear directly on the reader's
ability to track who is fighting/killing whom in the climactic battle and should
be fixed before this edition ships. The other two are minor flow/consistency
snags worth a look but not blocking.

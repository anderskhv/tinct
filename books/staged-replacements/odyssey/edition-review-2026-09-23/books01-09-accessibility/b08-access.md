# Accessibility Review — Book 8 (Banquet in the house of Alcinous—the games)

**Coverage:** Read all 50 paragraphs (indices 0–49), in full, start to finish, as a first-time adult listener.

## Findings: 5 (1 blocking, 4 non-blocking)

1. Para 5 — unexplained "Pytho"/oracle reference and unclear motive for Agamemnon's gladness — reference (**blocking**)
2. Para 7 — twelve-name competitor list, hard to retain by ear — aloud
3. Para 15 — unexplained archer names (Philoctetes, Heracles, Eurytus) piled in one speech — reference
4. Para 16 — "an hereditary" archaic article usage — archaic
5. Para 41 — "had had" double-auxiliary is a minor read-aloud stumble — aloud

## What already works well
- The Ares/Aphrodite tale (paras 18–29) is vivid, well-paced, and easy to follow despite mythological content — the humor and mechanics of the trap come through clearly.
- The athletic-games sequence and the Odysseus/Euryalus confrontation (paras 7–16) read with real dramatic tension and natural dialogue.
- Odysseus's grief at Demodocus's song of Troy (paras 45–46), including the extended simile of the widow, is clear and emotionally powerful.
- The gift-giving and bath scenes (paras 33–43) are plain, warm, and easy to follow.

## Verdict: BLOCKING FINDINGS
(One likely comprehension-stopper in paragraph 5 — the Pytho/oracle aside gives no way to understand why Agamemnon reacts as he does — plus four non-blocking polish items.)

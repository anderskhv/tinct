## Five things this Book puts to you explicitly, and they should not be left unruled

1. **The retention figure, 0.95958 — the highest in the package, and the drafter's own stated risk.** Books 1–3 are 0.721, 0.902, 0.897. `continuity.md` §6 gives the evidence both ways: Book 4 is mostly speech in Butler's plainest register and is 1.8× less archaic per word than Book 3 on a fixed list (3.98 against 7.15 per 1,000 words) — but Book 1 has the lowest density of all four and the lowest retention, so the source does not explain the number by itself. **Treat it as a question, not a pass.** Read the least-changed paragraphs against Butler and say whether the lightness is the source's or the drafter's — and, specifically, whether **more of Butler's chained syntax should have been recast than was**.
2. **Three new rows in the closed name table: Venus → Aphrodite, Juno → Hera, Vulcan → Hephaestus.** D5 requires the Greek forms; D6 requires the table to be extended by enumeration and never generated. Is the extension right, is the evidence (the served `modern-en`'s own counts: 14/0, 6/0, 20/0) the right evidence, and is `Idothea` correctly **left alone** beside them, since the Cast has no display name for her?
3. **D12 class C, applied for the first time in the poem, at B04-P001 and B04-P052.** Both brackets dropped, every word kept, nothing recast across the boundaries, both recorded with Butler's notes. B04-P001's bracket is **never closed in the base text**, and its extent is deliberately not determined.

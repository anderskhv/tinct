# Glossary — stable renderings for the Odyssey modern edition

Fixed before drafting Book 1 (2026-09-11). Extended book by book as new
names, epithets, and recurring formulas are met — add rows before the
paragraph that first uses them is drafted, per `WORKFLOW.md` step 2.

## Naming decision (binding, recorded per task instruction)

**Every proper name is kept in the exact form the served `original-en`
(Samuel Butler, 1900) uses it, with no substitution for the Greek form.**
Butler's translation is written in the Latin/Roman naming convention
standard for English classical translation in his period. The served
`modern-en` being replaced silently remapped these to Greek forms
(Minerva → Athena, Ulysses → Odysseus, Jove → Zeus); this package does not
repeat that remapping. See `PROVENANCE.md` §2–3 for the reasoning and for
the Book 10 pilot's conflicting choice (Greek forms), which is not adopted
here without a coordinator decision.

| Butler (source, kept as-is) | Notes |
|---|---|
| Ulysses | Not "Odysseus." |
| Minerva | Not "Athena." |
| Jove | Not "Zeus." Butler uses "Jove" for Zeus throughout; "Saturn" for Zeus's father Cronos (below). |
| Neptune | Not "Poseidon." |
| Mercury | Not "Hermes." |
| Saturn | Not "Cronos." Butler: "son of Saturn" as an epithet for Jove. |
| Diana | Not "Artemis." Not yet met in Book 1; recorded for later Books. |
| Telemachus, Penelope, Calypso, Circe, Nestor, Menelaus, Ithaca, Sparta, Pylos, Troy, Achaeans, Danaans, Argives, Hellas, Argos, Cyclopes, Polyphemus | Already the same in both Greek and Butler's usage; no Roman equivalent in common use, so no substitution question arises. |

Every other proper name (Aegisthus, Agamemnon, Orestes, Atlas, Thoosa,
Phorcys, Ogygia, Mentes, Anchialus, Taphians, Temesa, Rheithron, Neritum,
Laertes, Phemius, Icarius, Antinous, Eupeithes, Eurymachus, Polybus,
Euryclea, Ops, Pisenor, Ephyra, Ilus, Mermerus, Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus,
Hyperion) is kept exactly as Butler spells it. Do not silently correct a
spelling that looks unusual (e.g. Butler's own inconsistencies between
Books, if any turn up) — flag it in the Book's `continuity.md` instead, per
the Book 10 pilot's precedent for "Laestrygones" vs. "Laestrygonians."

## Recurring epithets and formulas (Book 1)

| Butler (source) | Modern edition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "son of Saturn, King of kings" (address to Jove) | **"son of Saturn, king of kings"** | Minerva's fixed form of address to Jove; kept every time it recurs (Book 1: twice, P04/P06 in Butler's paragraphing). Lower-cased "king" to match modern sentence-style capitalization elsewhere in the candidate; "Saturn" capitalized as a proper name. |
| "sire of gods and men" (of Jove) | **"father of gods and men"** | Standard Homeric epithet for Zeus/Jove; "father" is the plain modern word for "sire" (which now reads either archaic or specifically about horse-breeding), keeping the same formulaic function. Use this exact phrase every time Butler uses "sire of gods and men" for Jove. |
| "Ulysses, son of Laertes" / patronymics generally | *(not yet met as a fixed formula in Book 1; Laertes is named as Ulysses's father in P13 and P31 without the formula itself appearing)* | Watch for Butler's fuller patronymic epithets in later Books (e.g. "Ulysses, noble son of Laertes," used repeatedly by Circe in Book 10) and fix a single rendering the first time the full formula appears. |
| "tell me, and tell me true" | **"tell me truly"** | Butler's repeated formula for an insistent request for honest information (Book 1: P12, P13, P15, P18 in Butler's paragraphing — "tell me, and tell me true" / "tell me, and tell me true, for I want to know"). Rendered the same way each time it recurs, dropping the doubled "tell me... tell me" (which reads as a stutter in modern English) while keeping the emphasis on truthfulness that the doubling exists to add. |
| "hecatomb" | **"an offering of a hundred sheep and oxen"** | Technical term for a formal sacrifice of (originally) a hundred oxen; Butler's Book 1 hecatomb is explicitly "of sheep and oxen." Folded into a plain description at first use (Book 1, P03 in the candidate) rather than kept as a gloss-needing loanword, since the modern edition has no later paragraph in Book 1 that depends on the reader recognizing the term "hecatomb" again. Revisit if a later Book needs the term to recur as a fixed word rather than a description. |

## Voice and form

- Third-person narration and direct speech are kept as Butler has them;
  speeches remain speeches (quoted dialogue), never converted to indirect
  or reported speech.
- Where Butler's sentence runs long through chained clauses joined by
  semicolons, the candidate may break it into two or more modern sentences,
  provided every clause's content, logical connection (cause, contrast,
  sequence), and any hedge or qualification survives — see each Book's
  `continuity.md` for specific instances.
- Concrete physical detail — objects, gestures, numbers, distances,
  materials — is kept exactly: e.g. "twenty men," "twenty oxen," "a couple
  of blazing torches," "a silver catch," are never generalized away.
- Necessary glosses for a term a modern reader would not otherwise
  understand are kept to the shortest useful explanation, folded into the
  sentence at first use, following the Book 10 pilot's model (cubit →
  "roughly eighteen inches"). None was needed in Book 1.

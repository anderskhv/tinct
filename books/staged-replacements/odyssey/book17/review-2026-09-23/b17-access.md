## What I deliberately did not flag

- Epithets ("rosy-fingered", "looking like Artemis or Aphrodite"), similes
  (doe/lion, "stood firm as a rock"), and the deliberate command-then-action
  repetitions (Telemachus's instructions to Penelope echoed verbatim as her
  actions; Telemachus's message to the stranger echoed verbatim by Eumaeus) —
  these are the epic's own devices, not translation artifacts, and none of
  them block comprehension.
- The narrator's direct second-person address to Eumaeus ("To this you
  answered, swineherd Eumaeus...", paragraphs 52 and 58) — a deliberate
  Homeric device, left untouched per instructions.
- Unglossed proper-noun references ("the old man of the sea," "Philomeleides
  in Lesbos," "the sons of Atreus," "the river Aegyptus," the sneeze-as-omen
  belief) — each is embedded in a sentence that still makes sense as a plot
  point without knowing the specific referent, so none of these rise to a
  genuine comprehension block.
- Antinous's and Melanthius's insults, and the footstool-throwing violence —
  kept at full strength, untouched.

## Verdict

**CLEAN** (no blocking findings). The chapter reads clearly and naturally
overall — the dialogue-heavy scenes (Eumaeus vs. Melanthius, Antinous vs.
Odysseus, Penelope's questioning) are especially well-paced and easy to
follow aloud. The four non-blocking archaic-diction notes above are the only
things I'd flag for a look.

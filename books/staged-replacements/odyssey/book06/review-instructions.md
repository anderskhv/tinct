## Five things this Book puts to you explicitly, and they should not be left unruled

1. **The name supplied at B06-P005.** Butler writes *"Her mother was sitting by the fireside spinning her purple yarn with her maids around her, and **she** happened to catch her father just as he was going out…"* — where `she` is **Nausicaa**, but the nearer subject is the mother, and a modern reader takes the wrong one. The candidate divides the sentence and prints **`Nausicaa happened to catch her father`**. That is one supplied word, and it is the reason the candidate's `Nausicaa` count is **11** against the source's **10** — the one name count in the Book that does not match. Is a supplied *name* the same class as accepted B05-P012's supplied `and` (ruling 2: *a defective sentence is repaired by the smallest edit in the direction of the defect*), or is it a larger intervention that should have been solved by recasting instead? **Rule it.**

2. **`herbage` is rendered two ways across Books.** Butler writes *"the sweet juicy herbage that grew by the water side"* here (pasture for the mules) and *"the beds of violets and luscious herbage"* at B05-P006 (garden planting). Accepted `book05/candidate-v2.json` prints **`lush greenery`**; this candidate prints **`grass`**. One Butler word, two renderings across Books — the package's characteristic defect — **except** that the referents are different and each is the right English for its referent. Is this a discrimination or a drift? If it is a drift, the cheaper repair is in **this** Book, not in accepted Book 5.

3. **The Artemis simile at B06-P009 is left long: 60 words against Butler's 67.** It is the only sixty-word sentence in the candidate. The argument is the **B04-P038** disposition applied to a simile, as accepted Books 4 and 5 applied it — the chain *is* the figure. Butler's parenthesis *(then is Leto proud…)* is opened out into the sentence and the closing *"even so did the girl outshine her handmaids"* is given its own sentence, so the simile is **shorter** than Butler's, not longer (Book 5's finding 30.2: a recast that *grows* Butler's sentence is a defect). Is 60 words the right place to stop, and is opening the parenthesis out a loss?

4. **Three distinctions are kept that the consistency discipline would flatten, and one flattening is refused.** (a) Butler writes `live in heaven` once and `dwell in heaven` twice, and **B06-P019 carries both, eleven words apart** — the candidate prints both. (b) Butler writes `skilful workman` here and `skilled shipwright` at accepted B05-P020; the candidate prints **`skillful`** (the American spelling of *his* word), not `skilled`. (c) Butler's `doubted whether` at B06-P012 is deliberately **not** rendered `still undecided`, because that is the package's fixed rendering for his *other* phrase, `thus in two minds` (accepted B04-P011, B05-P028, B05-P032). And `scion` at B06-P013 is **not** rendered `creature`, because `creature` is the edition's rendering of Butler's own `creature` at accepted B05-P010 — finding 9.1's defect exactly. Are all four calls right, and is there a fifth the drafter missed?

5. **Is 23 semicolon conversions out of 32 added sentences the S-1 defect again?** The drafter says the retention figure answers it and names four paragraphs as the real recasting. Test that claim against the text: the near-identical report (published in `continuity.md` §11) flags **B06-P006 at 94 words with one word-level edit** and **B06-P016 at 144 words with two**, both of them Nausicaa speaking, and the drafter's defence of both is that Butler is already writing plain modern English there. Book 4's round 1 showed that "already plain" is exactly the claim a drafter cannot certify about its own draft.

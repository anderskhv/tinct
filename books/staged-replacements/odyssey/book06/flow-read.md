# Odyssey Book 6 — the flow read (step 7)

`candidate-v2.json` read straight through, once, as a reader reads it — not
paragraph against paragraph with Butler beside it, which is what steps 5 and 6
already did. This is the **carrier** for blind spot 1 of round 1 §9, *a
sentence divided at the wrong seam*: D17 counts sentences and NORM RATE counts
the sentences a semicolon did not pay for, and **neither can tell a good
division from a bad one.** Book 4's F-1 and Book 5's three reversals were found
this way, and so was this.

## F-1 — B06-P018 reads *Then … Then … Then*

**Applied.** The paragraph's last seam was one of the three semicolons it
cashed, and the division it bought left two consecutive sentences opening
`Then` — in a paragraph whose **first** sentence also opens `Then`.

> Then they stood to one side and went to tell the girl … **Then** he went and
> sat down a little way off on the beach, looking quite young and handsome, and
> the girl gazed at him in admiration. **Then** she said to her maids:

Butler's own pointing there is a semicolon, and it is doing the better job:
the gazing and the speaking are one movement, and his mark says so. This is the
Book 4 flow read's F-1 shape exactly — two consecutive sentences opening on the
same word, produced by a division rather than by Butler — and the shape Book 5's
round 1 reversed three times, in each case *back to Butler's semicolon*
(B05-P020, B05-P021, B05-P027). The same disposition here:

> … and the girl gazed at him in admiration**;** then she said to her maids:

The paragraph still opens on `Then`, which is Butler's, and still carries
`Athena then made him look taller` mid-sentence, which is also Butler's. What it
no longer has is a `Then.` answering a `Then.` four words later.

**This is the clearest demonstration the package has of why D20 exists.** Taking
the division back:

| | before F-1 | after F-1 |
|---|---|---|
| sentences | 116 → 149 | 116 → **148** |
| raw D17 rate | **+28.4%** | **+27.6%** |
| semicolons (D19) | 27 → 4 | 27 → **5** |
| **NORM RATE (D20)** | **+7.0%** | **+7.0%** |

A period turned back into a semicolon is worth **0.8 points of headline** on
D17's raw denominator and **exactly zero** on the normalized one. The raw rate
says this Book got worse by taking back a division that made it worse to read.
NORM RATE says nothing happened, which is the truth.

## What was read and left alone

- **The three-`Then` count is not the only repetition in the Book, and the
  others are Butler's.** P012's `stood firm` / `stood right in front of
  Odysseus` and P021/P023's two `do as I tell you` are both his own repetitions,
  at his own intervals. A flow read is for divisions the drafter made, not for
  smoothing Butler.
- **P016's new sentence, `We have nothing to do with any other people.`,
  stands.** It is abrupt, and it is meant to be: it is the end of a four-limb
  chain that S-1(b) is about, and read aloud it lands as Nausicaa's point rather
  than as the fourth item in a list. The second S-1(b) division,
  `They will take what they can get and be thankful.`, reads the same way.
- **P018's silver-plate sentence survives the read**, which is the test S-1(a)
  actually set: *"a modern reader garden-paths at `under Hephaestus and Athena
  enriches`."* With the verb next to its subject and the relative clause moved
  to an appositive, there is nowhere left to garden-path, and Butler's coda
  keeps its own em dash.
- **P009's `Just as … So did …` holds across sixty words.** This was the one to
  watch, because M-8's repair is two words and has to carry a 60-word protasis.
  Read straight through, `Just as` cannot be taken for *while*, and `So did the
  girl outshine her handmaids` lands as the answer it is.
- **D4's two unclosed speeches read correctly** — Odysseus P013 → P014,
  Nausicaa P021 → P022 → P023. Neither reads as a speaker stopping and starting
  again, which is the whole reason **D4** preserves Butler's convention.

## What a flow read still cannot carry

Blind spots 3, 4 and 6 of round 1 §9 — a garden path, register, and a figure
carried by a single word — are carried by *this* step and by nothing else, and
two of the three were found here at Book 6 only because a reviewer read the
Book. **B06-P013's `scion`** is the standing example: `young woman` is correct,
reads well, costs no retention, and quietly severs the palm tree the next
sentence depends on. The flow read did not find it; a reviewer comparing
sentence to sentence did. The loss is recorded rather than repaired
(`changes-v1-to-v2.md`, declined finding O-1).

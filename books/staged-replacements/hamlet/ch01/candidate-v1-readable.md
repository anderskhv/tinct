**[C01-P001]**

[Enter Francisco and Barnardo, two sentinels]

**[C01-P002]**

BARNARDO. Who's there?

**[C01-P003]**

FRANCISCO. No — you answer me. Stand and identify yourself.

**[C01-P004]**

BARNARDO. Long live the King!

**[C01-P005]**

FRANCISCO. Barnardo?

**[C01-P006]**

BARNARDO. That's me.

**[C01-P007]**

FRANCISCO. You've come right on the hour, as careful as ever.

**[C01-P008]**

BARNARDO. It's just struck twelve. Get yourself to bed, Francisco.

**[C01-P009]**

FRANCISCO. Many thanks for the relief. It's bitter cold, and I'm sick at heart.

**[C01-P010]**

BARNARDO. Has your watch been quiet?

**[C01-P011]**

FRANCISCO. Not a mouse stirring.

**[C01-P012]**

BARNARDO. Well, good night. If you meet Horatio and Marcellus, my partners on this watch, tell them to hurry.

**[C01-P013]**

[Enter Horatio and Marcellus]

**[C01-P014]**

FRANCISCO. I think I hear them. Stop there! Who's there?

**[C01-P015]**

HORATIO. Friends of this land.

**[C01-P016]**

MARCELLUS. And loyal subjects of the Dane.

**[C01-P017]**

FRANCISCO. Good night to you.

**[C01-P018]**

MARCELLUS. Oh, farewell, honest soldier. Who's relieved you?

**[C01-P019]**

FRANCISCO. Barnardo has my place. Good night to you.

**[C01-P020]**

[Exit.]

**[C01-P021]**

MARCELLUS. Hello, Barnardo!

**[C01-P022]**

BARNARDO. What, is that Horatio there?

**[C01-P023]**

HORATIO. A piece of him.

**[C01-P024]**

BARNARDO. Welcome, Horatio. Welcome, good Marcellus.

**[C01-P025]**

MARCELLUS. What, has that thing appeared again tonight?

**[C01-P026]**

BARNARDO. I haven't seen anything.

**[C01-P027]**

MARCELLUS. Horatio says it's only our imagination, and won't let himself believe this dreadful sight, seen twice now by us. That's why I've persuaded him to keep watch with us tonight, so that if the apparition comes again, he can confirm what our eyes have seen and speak to it.

**[C01-P028]**

HORATIO. Nonsense, it won't appear.

**[C01-P029]**

BARNARDO. Sit down a while, and let us try once more to get past your resistance, and tell you what we two have seen these past two nights.

**[C01-P030]**

HORATIO. Well, let's sit down, and hear Barnardo tell it.

**[C01-P031]**

BARNARDO. Last night, when that same star to the west of the pole had moved to light up the part of the sky where it now burns, Marcellus and I — the bell just then striking one—

**[C01-P032]**

MARCELLUS. Quiet, stop there. Look, it's coming again.

**[C01-P033]**

[Enter Ghost]

**[C01-P034]**

BARNARDO. In the same shape, like the King who's dead.

**[C01-P035]**

MARCELLUS. You're a scholar; speak to it, Horatio.

**[C01-P036]**

BARNARDO. Doesn't it look like the King? Look closely, Horatio.

**[C01-P037]**

HORATIO. Very like. It fills me with fear and wonder.

**[C01-P038]**

BARNARDO. It wants to be spoken to.

**[C01-P039]**

MARCELLUS. Question it, Horatio.

**[C01-P040]**

HORATIO. What are you, that take on this hour of night, together with that noble and warlike shape in which the majesty of buried Denmark once marched? By heaven, I command you, speak.

**[C01-P041]**

MARCELLUS. It is offended.

**[C01-P042]**

BARNARDO. Look, it's stalking away.

**[C01-P043]**

HORATIO. Stay! Speak, speak! I command you, speak!

**[C01-P044]**

[Exit Ghost.]

**[C01-P045]**

MARCELLUS. It's gone, and won't answer.

**[C01-P046]**

BARNARDO. How now, Horatio! You're trembling and pale. Isn't this something more than imagination? What do you make of it?

**[C01-P047]**

HORATIO. Before my God, I could not have believed this without the direct and true evidence of my own eyes.

**[C01-P048]**

MARCELLUS. Is it not like the King?

**[C01-P049]**

HORATIO. As much as you are like yourself. That was exactly the armor he wore when he fought ambitious Norway; and he frowned just so once, when in an angry parley he struck the sledded Poles on the ice. It's strange.

**[C01-P050]**

MARCELLUS. Just like this, twice before, and exactly at this dead hour, he has marched past our watch with a soldier's stride.

**[C01-P051]**

HORATIO. I don't know exactly what to make of it; but broadly speaking, in my opinion, this bodes some strange upheaval for our state.

**[C01-P052]**

MARCELLUS. Come now, sit down, and tell me — you who know — why this same strict and watchful guard wears out the country's people every night, and why there's this daily casting of bronze cannon, and buying of weapons abroad; why such pressing of shipwrights, whose hard labor doesn't even spare Sunday from the working week. What's coming, that this sweating haste makes the night a fellow laborer with the day? Who is it that can tell me?

**[C01-P053]**

HORATIO. That I can. At least, so the whisper goes. Our late King, whose likeness even just now appeared to us, was, as you know, challenged to combat by Fortinbras of Norway, driven to it by a fierce and rivalrous pride; in that combat our valiant Hamlet — for so he was esteemed in this part of the known world — killed this Fortinbras, who by a sealed compact, properly ratified by law and heraldry, forfeited, along with his life, all the lands he held, to the conqueror. Against this, an equal portion was staked by our King, which would have passed to Fortinbras's heirs had he been the victor; just as, by that same covenant and the terms of the article, Hamlet's stake fell to him. Now, sir, young Fortinbras, of untested spirit, hot-blooded and full of himself, has gathered here and there, along the borders of Norway, a band of lawless men, hired for their keep, for some venture that takes some courage — which is nothing other, as is plain enough to our state, than to win back by force and compulsory terms those same lands his father lost. And this, I take it, is the main motive for our preparations, the source of this watch, and the chief cause of this urgent haste and stir throughout the land.

**[C01-P054]**

BARNARDO. I believe it's nothing other than that: it fits well that this ominous figure comes armed through our watch, so like the King who was — and is — the cause of these wars.

**[C01-P055]**

HORATIO. It's a speck to trouble the mind's eye. In the highest and most flourishing days of Rome, a little before the mighty Julius fell, the graves stood empty and the shrouded dead squeaked and gibbered in the Roman streets; there were stars with trails of fire and dew of blood, disasters in the sun; and the moon, on whose influence the sea depends, was sick almost to doomsday with eclipse. And just such a forewarning of violent events, forerunners always going before fate and a prologue to the disaster still to come, heaven and earth together have shown to our own lands and countrymen.

**[C01-P056]**

[Re-enter Ghost]

**[C01-P057]**

HORATIO. But wait, look! There, it's coming again! I'll cross its path, though it blast me for it. Stay, illusion! If you have any sound, or use of voice, speak to me. If there's some good thing to be done, that might bring you ease, and grace to me, speak to me. If you're privy to your country's fate, which, known ahead of time, might be avoided, oh, speak! Or if in life you hoarded away stolen treasure in the belly of the earth — which they say makes you spirits walk after death — speak of it. Stay, and speak!

**[C01-P058]**

[The cock crows.]

**[C01-P059]**

HORATIO. Stop it, Marcellus!

**[C01-P060]**

MARCELLUS. Should I strike at it with my spear?

**[C01-P061]**

HORATIO. Do, if it won't stand still.

**[C01-P062]**

BARNARDO. It's here!

**[C01-P063]**

HORATIO. It's here!

**[C01-P064]**

[Exit Ghost.]

**[C01-P065]**

MARCELLUS. It's gone! We do it wrong, being so majestic, to offer it any show of violence, for it's like the air, invulnerable, and our useless blows a malicious mockery.

**[C01-P066]**

BARNARDO. It was about to speak, when the cock crowed.

**[C01-P067]**

HORATIO. And then it started, like something guilty at a fearful summons. I've heard that the cock, which is the trumpet of the morning, wakes the god of day with his high and shrill-sounding call; and at his warning, whether in sea or fire, in earth or air, the wandering, straying spirit hurries back to its own confines. And this thing we just saw proved the truth of that.

**[C01-P068]**

MARCELLUS. It faded at the crowing of the cock. Some say that every year, as the season comes when our Savior's birth is celebrated, the bird of dawn sings all night long; and then, they say, no spirit dares stir abroad, the nights are wholesome, then no planets strike with harm, no fairy casts a spell, nor witch has power to charm — so holy and so blessed is that time.

**[C01-P069]**

HORATIO. So I've heard too, and partly believe it. But look, the morning, dressed in reddish-brown, walks over the dew on that high hill to the east. Let's break up our watch, and by my advice, let's tell young Hamlet what we've seen tonight; for upon my life, this spirit, silent to us, will speak to him. Do you agree we should tell him of it, as our love for him and our duty require?

**[C01-P070]**

MARCELLUS. Let's do that, I ask you, and I know this morning where we'll most easily find him.

**[C01-P071]**

[Exeunt.]

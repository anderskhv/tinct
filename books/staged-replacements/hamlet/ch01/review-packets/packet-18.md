## C01-P053 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (served original-en):**

> HORATIO. That can I; At least, the whisper goes so. Our last King, Whose image even but now appear’d to us, Was, as you know, by Fortinbras of Norway, Thereto prick’d on by a most emulate pride, Dar’d to the combat; in which our valiant Hamlet, For so this side of our known world esteem’d him, Did slay this Fortinbras; who by a seal’d compact, Well ratified by law and heraldry, Did forfeit, with his life, all those his lands Which he stood seiz’d of, to the conqueror; Against the which, a moiety competent Was gaged by our King; which had return’d To the inheritance of Fortinbras, Had he been vanquisher; as by the same cov’nant And carriage of the article design’d, His fell to Hamlet. Now, sir, young Fortinbras, Of unimproved mettle, hot and full, Hath in the skirts of Norway, here and there, Shark’d up a list of lawless resolutes, For food and diet, to some enterprise That hath a stomach in’t; which is no other, As it doth well appear unto our state, But to recover of us by strong hand And terms compulsatory, those foresaid lands So by his father lost. And this, I take it, Is the main motive of our preparations, The source of this our watch, and the chief head Of this post-haste and rummage in the land.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> HORATIO. That I can. At least, so the whisper goes. Our late King, whose likeness even just now appeared to us, was, as you know, challenged to combat by Fortinbras of Norway, driven to it by a fierce and rivalrous pride; in that combat our valiant Hamlet — for so he was esteemed in this part of the known world — killed this Fortinbras, who by a sealed compact, properly ratified by law and heraldry, forfeited, along with his life, all the lands he held, to the conqueror. Against this, an equal portion was staked by our King, which would have passed to Fortinbras's heirs had he been the victor; just as, by that same covenant and the terms of the article, Hamlet's stake fell to him. Now, sir, young Fortinbras, of untested spirit, hot-blooded and full of himself, has gathered here and there, along the borders of Norway, a band of lawless men, hired for their keep, for some venture that takes some courage — which is nothing other, as is plain enough to our state, than to win back by force and compulsory terms those same lands his father lost. And this, I take it, is the main motive for our preparations, the source of this watch, and the chief cause of this urgent haste and stir throughout the land.

## C01-P055 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (served original-en):**

> HORATIO. A mote it is to trouble the mind’s eye. In the most high and palmy state of Rome, A little ere the mightiest Julius fell, The graves stood tenantless and the sheeted dead Did squeak and gibber in the Roman streets; As stars with trains of fire and dews of blood, Disasters in the sun; and the moist star, Upon whose influence Neptune’s empire stands, Was sick almost to doomsday with eclipse. And even the like precurse of fierce events, As harbingers preceding still the fates And prologue to the omen coming on, Have heaven and earth together demonstrated Unto our climatures and countrymen.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> HORATIO. It's a speck to trouble the mind's eye. In the highest and most flourishing days of Rome, a little before the mighty Julius fell, the graves stood empty and the shrouded dead squeaked and gibbered in the Roman streets; there were stars with trails of fire and dew of blood, disasters in the sun; and the moon, on whose influence the sea depends, was sick almost to doomsday with eclipse. And just such a forewarning of violent events, forerunners always going before fate and a prologue to the disaster still to come, heaven and earth together have shown to our own lands and countrymen.

## B10-P001 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler):**

> “Thence we went on to the Aeolian island where lives Aeolus son of
Hippotas, dear to the immortal gods. It is an island that floats (as it
were) upon the sea, iron bound with a wall that girds it. Now, Aeolus
has six daughters and six lusty sons, so he made the sons marry the
daughters, and they all live with their dear father and mother,
feasting and enjoying every conceivable kind of luxury. All day long
the atmosphere of the house is loaded with the savour of roasting meats
till it groans again, yard and all; but by night they sleep on their
well made bedsteads, each with his own wife between the blankets. These
were the people among whom we had now come.

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> From there we came to the floating island of Aeolus, son of Hippotas, a man dear to the immortal gods. It floats on the sea, so to speak, ringed by a wall of iron. Aeolus has six daughters and six strapping sons, and he married the sons to the daughters, so they all live together with their dear father and mother, feasting and enjoying every comfort life can offer. All day long the smell of roasting meat fills the house until it fairly groans with it, courtyard and all; but at night they sleep on well-made bedsteads, each with his own wife beside him under the blankets. These were the people we had now come among.

## B10-P002 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler):**

> “Aeolus entertained me for a whole month asking me questions all the
time about Troy, the Argive fleet, and the return of the Achaeans. I
told him exactly how everything had happened, and when I said I must
go, and asked him to further me on my way, he made no sort of
difficulty, but set about doing so at once. Moreover, he flayed me a
prime ox-hide to hold the ways of the roaring winds, which he shut up
in the hide as in a sack—for Jove had made him captain over the winds,
and he could stir or still each one of them according to his own
pleasure. He put the sack in the ship and bound the mouth so tightly
with a silver thread that not even a breath of a side-wind could blow
from any quarter. The West wind which was fair for us did he alone let
blow as it chose; but it all came to nothing, for we were lost through
our own folly.

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> Aeolus kept me as his guest for a whole month, asking me endless questions about Troy, the Argive fleet, and the voyage home of the Achaeans. I told him exactly how it had all happened, and when I said I needed to leave and asked him to help me on my way, he raised no objection and set to work at once. He flayed a full-grown ox and made a hide bag to hold the paths of the blustering winds, for Zeus had made him keeper of the winds, with power to raise or calm each one as he pleased. He shut the winds inside the hide as if it were a sack, put the sack aboard my ship, and tied its mouth shut with a silver cord so tightly that not even a breath of wind could slip out from any direction. He let only the West Wind blow free, since that one was fair for us—but it all came to nothing, for our own foolishness destroyed us.

## B10-P003 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler):**

> “Nine days and nine nights did we sail, and on the tenth day our native
land showed on the horizon. We got so close in that we could see the
stubble fires burning, and I, being then dead beat, fell into a light
sleep, for I had never let the rudder out of my own hands, that we
might get home the faster. On this the men fell to talking among
themselves, and said I was bringing back gold and silver in the sack
that Aeolus had given me. ‘Bless my heart,’ would one turn to his
neighbour, saying, ‘how this man gets honoured and makes friends to
whatever city or country he may go. See what fine prizes he is taking
home from Troy, while we, who have travelled just as far as he has,
come back with hands as empty as we set out with—and now Aeolus has
given him ever so much more. Quick—let us see what it all is, and how
much gold and silver there is in the sack he gave him.’

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> We sailed nine days and nine nights, and on the tenth day our own land came into view. We were close enough to see men tending the stubble fires, and I, worn out, fell into a light sleep—I had never let go of the rudder myself the whole way, wanting to get us home faster. While I slept, my men began talking among themselves, saying that I was bringing home gold and silver in the sack Aeolus had given me. ‘Just look,’ one would say to his neighbor, ‘how this man is honored and welcomed wherever he goes. See what fine prizes he’s carrying home from Troy, while the rest of us, who traveled just as far as he did, come back with empty hands—and now Aeolus has given him still more. Quick, let’s see what’s really in that sack he gave him, how much gold and silver.’

## B10-P004 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Butler):**

> “Thus they talked and evil counsels prevailed. They loosed the sack,
whereupon the wind flew howling forth and raised a storm that carried
us weeping out to sea and away from our own country. Then I awoke, and
knew not whether to throw myself into the sea or to live on and make
the best of it; but I bore it, covered myself up, and lay down in the
ship, while the men lamented bitterly as the fierce winds bore our
fleet back to the Aeolian island.

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> So they talked, and this bad idea won out. They opened the sack, and the winds burst out howling and raised a storm that swept us weeping out to sea, away from our own country. When I woke, I didn’t know whether to throw myself into the sea or to endure it and live; but I bore it, covered my face, and lay down in the ship, while the men wailed bitterly as the fierce winds carried our whole fleet back to the island of Aeolus.

## B10-P016 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler):**

> “When they reached Circe’s house they found it built of cut stones, on
a site that could be seen from far, in the middle of the forest. There
were wild mountain wolves and lions prowling all round it—poor
bewitched creatures whom she had tamed by her enchantments and drugged
into subjection. They did not attack my men, but wagged their great
tails, fawned upon them, and rubbed their noses lovingly against
them. As hounds crowd round their master when they see him coming
from dinner—for they know he will bring them something—even so did
these wolves and lions with their great claws fawn upon my men, but the
men were terribly frightened at seeing such strange creatures.
Presently they reached the gates of the goddess’s house, and as they
stood there they could hear Circe within, singing most beautifully as
she worked at her loom, making a web so fine, so soft, and of such
dazzling colours as no one but a goddess could weave. On this Polites,
whom I valued and trusted more than any other of my men, said, ‘There
is some one inside working at a loom and singing most beautifully; the
whole place resounds with it, let us call her and see whether she is
woman or goddess.’

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> When they reached Circe’s house, they found it built of cut stone, on a site visible from far off, in the middle of the forest. Wild mountain wolves and lions prowled all around it—poor bewitched creatures she had tamed with her drugs and enchantments. They did not attack my men, but wagged their great tails, fawned on them, and rubbed their noses against them affectionately. Just as dogs crowd around their master when he comes home from dinner, knowing he will bring them something, so these wolves and lions, for all their great claws, fawned on my men—though the men were badly frightened at the sight of such strange creatures. Soon they reached the gates of the goddess’s house, and as they stood there they could hear Circe inside, singing beautifully as she worked at her loom, weaving a fabric so fine, so soft, and so brilliantly colored that only a goddess could have made it. At this, Polites—whom I valued and trusted more than any other of my men—said, ‘Someone in there is working at a loom and singing beautifully; the whole place rings with it. Let’s call out to her and see whether she’s a woman or a goddess.’

## B10-P017 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Butler):**

cheese, honey, meal, and Pramnian wine, but she drugged it with wicked

turned them into pigs by a stroke of her wand, and shut them up in her

## B10-P019 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Butler):**

> “‘We went,’ said he, ‘as you told us, through the forest, and in the
middle of it there was a fine house built with cut stones in a place
that could be seen from far. There we found a woman, or else she was a
goddess, working at her loom and singing sweetly; so the men shouted to
her and called her, whereon she at once came down, opened the door, and
invited us in. The others did not suspect any mischief so they followed
her into the house, but I staid where I was, for I thought there might
be some treachery. From that moment I saw them no more, for not one of
them ever came out, though I sat a long time watching for them.’

**Candidate (Tinct Clear v1):**

> ‘We went,’ he said, ‘just as you told us, through the forest, and in the middle of it we found a fine house built of cut stone, in a place visible from far off. There we found a woman—or perhaps a goddess—working at her loom and singing sweetly. The men called out and shouted to her, and she came down at once, opened the door, and invited us in. The others suspected no trap and followed her inside, but I stayed where I was, thinking there might be treachery. From that moment I saw no more of them, for not one of them ever came back out, though I sat there a long time watching for them.’

# Glossary — stable renderings for the Fear and Trembling modern-English edition

Fixed before drafting Chapter 1 (2026-09-11), from the served Danish
(`fear-and-trembling-original-da.json`) and general knowledge of the book's
technical vocabulary, since only the Preface (Chapter 1 of 8) has been read in
full so far. The left column is Kierkegaard's Danish (the source, from
Johannes de Silentio); the middle column is the rendering used in every
candidate paragraph, in every chapter, unless a specific paragraph records an
exception in that chapter's `continuity.md`. Rows for terms not yet
encountered are marked so; they are fixed now, before drafting reaches them,
so no later chapter improvises a different rendering. **Add rows before use,
never after — a departure from a fixed rendering is a finding, not a
judgment call.**

## Core technical vocabulary (fixed in advance, from general knowledge of the work; confirm/extend at first use in each term's home chapter)

| Danish (source) | Modern edition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Troens Ridder | **the knight of faith** | The book's central figure (introduced fully in the Problema chapters). Never "faith's knight" or "the faithful knight." |
| Resignationens Ridder; den uendelige Resignations Ridder | **the knight of infinite resignation** | Distinct from, and a stage short of, the knight of faith. Kept as a fixed compound; never shortened to "the knight of resignation" once "infinite" has been established for a given passage. |
| den uendelige Resignation; Resignationens Bevægelse | **infinite resignation** | The movement Abraham makes before (but not instead of) the movement of faith. |
| Troens Bevægelse; Troens Spring | **the movement of faith**; **the leap** | Kierkegaard's "Spring" (leap) is a single deliberate word for the movement no reflection can make; kept as "the leap," never softened to "step" or "transition." |
| det Absurde | **the absurd** | Technical term: not "the ridiculous" or "the irrational" in the colloquial sense. Kierkegaard is explicit that the absurd is not identical with the unlikely or the impossible-in-the-world; the term is never glossed away. |
| den teleologiske Suspension af det Ethiske | **the teleological suspension of the ethical** | The title phrase of Problema I. Never paraphrased ("suspending ethics for a purpose" etc.) at first occurrence in Chapter 5; may be referred back to afterward as "the suspension" once established there. |
| det Ethiske | **the ethical** | Kept as a noun-phrase abstraction (Kierkegaard's technical use, not "ethics" as a field of study and not "morality"). |
| den Enkelte | **the single individual** | Kierkegaard's central category of the person who stands in an absolute relation to the absolute, outside or above the universal (the ethical). Never "the individual" alone (loses "single/singular") and never "the particular person." |
| det Almene; det Universelle | **the universal** | The ethical considered as what holds for everyone, without exception; the single individual's counterpart/opponent. |
| Troen | **faith** | *Not* "belief" — Kierkegaard distinguishes faith sharply from mere intellectual assent, and "faith" is the word the Preface already uses (Chapter 1, ¶2–3). |
| Frygt og Bæven | **fear and trembling** | The book's title phrase (Chapter 1, ¶3), an allusion to Philippians 2:12 ("work out your own salvation with fear and trembling," KJV). Kept exactly as the familiar English Bible phrase — this is not a Kierkegaardian coinage but a quotation Kierkegaard's Danish readers would also have recognized (Danish 1819/1871 Bible: "med Frygt og Bæven"). |
| Angest | **anxiety** *(not yet met)* | Reserved for when it appears (distinct from "fear," Frygt, which is used literally in the title phrase and elsewhere). Do not conflate the two Danish words if both occur in the same passage. |
| Paradoxet | **the paradox** *(not yet met)* | The absolute paradox that faith is: that the single individual is higher than the universal. |
| Troens Paradox | **the paradox of faith** *(not yet met)* | |
| Prøvelsen; Fristelsen | **the trial**; **the temptation** *(not yet met)* | Kierkegaard distinguishes ethical "temptation" (Fristelse, which would hold Abraham back from the sacrifice) from a "trial" (Prøvelse) that has no ethical expression; keep the two English words apart wherever the Danish keeps them apart. |
| Troens Ridderes Selskab | **the brotherhood of faith** *(not yet met)* | |
| Enkeltheden | **singularity / the single-individual condition** *(not yet met)* | Abstract noun form of "den Enkelte"; render contextually, flag any first use for a continuity note. |

## General voice and form rules

- Preserve Johannes de Silentio's first-person, ironic, essayistic voice. He is
  a persona distinct from Kierkegaard; do not flatten his self-deprecating,
  arch tone into plain modern explanatory prose.
- Kierkegaard's own insertions of German, Latin, and French into the Danish
  text are kept in their original language, not translated away, exactly as
  he wrote them (e.g., "ein wirklicher Ausverkauf," the Descartes citations in
  Latin). Where a brief English gloss makes the sentence work for a modern
  reader, it is folded into the sentence itself (e.g., "ein wirklicher
  Ausverkauf — a real clearance sale"), never bracketed as an editorial
  interpolation.
- Kierkegaard's capitalization of key terms for emphasis ("the System") is
  kept; ordinary nouns are not capitalized in the modern edition even where
  1843 Danish orthography capitalizes all nouns.
- Historical proper nouns (Cartesius, Trop, Adresseavisen) are rendered as
  follows: "Cartesius" (the Latinized form Kierkegaard uses) → **Descartes**
  (the name a modern reader knows; same historical person, not a technical
  term); "Trop" and "Adresseavisen" (a real Copenhagen classified-ads
  newspaper) are kept as proper nouns, left unglossed, as the accessibility
  standard permits for passing period references that are not essential to
  follow the argument.
- No expansion for its own sake. Short, epigrammatic sentences stay short.

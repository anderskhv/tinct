# Continuity sheet — Meditations, Book V (candidate v1)

Written alongside drafting `candidate-v1.json`, after Books II, I, III and IV
were accepted, and describing what the frozen draft actually did. Term
renderings follow `../GLOSSARY.md` (two rows added and two rows extended for
Book V before drafting, see below); the pattern for applying review findings
follows the four earlier `ACCEPTANCE.md` files (decisions D8, D10 and D11 in
the ledger).

## Source

- `../meditations-original-en.staged.json`, chapter `number: 5`, title `Book 5`,
  36 paragraphs, one per numbered meditation (V.1–V.36). Extracted verbatim
  into `source-book5.json` (sha256 in `provenance.json`).
- George Long 1862, PG #15877. See `../PROVENANCE.md` for identification,
  rights and the mechanical normalisations applied to the whole staged file.
- The staged file is the one rebuilt at Book IV step 1 (sha256 `b0ecf3da…`,
  D12). V.8 was one of the three paragraphs that had carried a PG
  illustration caption; step 1 confirmed the rebuilt V.8 ends at "…put
  anything out of the way." with no `[Illustration` anywhere in the file.
- Book V was read in full before any paragraph was drafted: 36 meditations,
  4,464 words, the longest single meditation V.8 (499 words, Aesculapius and
  what the nature of the whole prescribes) and the shortest V.17 (22 words).
  Its threads: getting up to the work of a human being (V.1); the vine that
  produces grapes and asks nothing (V.6); accepting what is prescribed (V.8,
  V.18, V.25); returning to philosophy after failing (V.9); form and matter,
  change and the periods of the universe (V.13, V.23, V.32); what belongs to a
  man as man (V.15); the soul dyed by its thoughts (V.16); the soul untouched
  by things (V.19, V.26); the god within and living with the gods (V.10,
  V.27); the smoky house (V.29); the reckoning of a life (V.31); fame as sound
  and echo (V.33); the top and the Rostra (V.36).

## Paragraph-level decisions

- **V.1** — "When thou risest unwillingly" → "when you get up unwillingly";
  "let this thought be present,—I am rising" → "let this thought be present:
  I am rising"; "bed-clothes" → "bedclothes"; "dost thou not make haste to do
  that which, is according to thy nature" → "do you not hurry to do what is
  according to your nature" (the PG stray comma after "which" dropped); Long's
  capital "Nature has fixed bounds … she" → lowercase "nature … she", the
  personification kept in "she"; "the vain-glorious man" → "the vain man"
  (Book I rendered "vain-glory" as "vanity"; "his little glory" stays);
  "a violent affection to a thing" → "a violent passion for a thing"
  ("affection" now means fondness); "the acts which concern society more vile
  in thy eyes" kept ("vile" is current). Long's "choose neither to eat nor to
  sleep rather than to perfect the things which they care for" is kept as he
  has it ("rather than perfect the things they care for"), though the sentence
  as Long prints it seems to want a negative (they would rather not eat or
  sleep than *fail to* perfect their work); nothing is supplied. Listed under
  unresolved source issues; a reviewer who reads it otherwise should say so.
- **V.2** — "In all tranquillity" → "in complete calm".
- **V.3** — "Peculiar" (their own, particular) → "their own", twice; "which
  things do not thou regard" → "do not regard these things".
- **V.4** — "Until I shall fall and rest" → "until I fall and rest"; "out of
  which" → "from which" (three times); "during so many years" → "for so many
  years".
- **V.5** — "Be it so" → "So be it"; Long's "I am not formed from them by
  nature" → "I am not formed for them by nature": Long's "from" is not
  English in this sense (the meaning is that nature has not fitted him for
  these things), and "for" is the plain preposition; a reviewer who reads
  Long's "from" as deliberate should say so. "Murmur" → "grumble" (Long's own
  word in IV.32); "immediately able to exhibit" → "able to show at once";
  "make great display" → "make a great display"; "benevolence" → "kindness".
  Long's list of ten qualities kept in his order and his words otherwise.
- **V.6** — "In a manner" → "in a way" (twice); "a dog when he has tackled
  the game" kept (Long's verb, current in the sense of seizing); "to produce
  again the grapes in season" → "to produce the grapes again in season"; "the
  social animal … in a social manner" → "the social being … in a social way";
  "It is true that thou sayest" → "What you say is true"; "if thou wilt choose
  to understand" → "if you choose to understand". The five dialogue dashes
  kept.
- **V.7** — "In this simple and noble fashion" → "in this simple and noble
  way". "Ploughed" kept as PG spells it (the glossary follows the base text's
  spelling; PG has "ploughed" here).
- **V.8** — "Horse-exercise" → "riding"; Long's capitalised "Prescribed" (the
  word under discussion) kept; "the nature of the universe" → "the nature of
  the whole" (glossary); "That which happens to [or suits] every man" → "what
  happens to every man" ("[or suits]" is an alternative rendering, dropped
  under D11); "one fitness [harmony]" → "one fitness" ("[harmony]" is an
  alternative rendering of the same word, dropped under D11); "necessity
  [destiny] is made up" → "necessity is made up" (alternative label, dropped
  under D11, as IV.21 "[the formal]"); "they say, It [necessity, destiny]
  brought this to such a person" → "they say that It, necessity or destiny,
  brought this to such a person" — here the bracket supplies what the vague
  "It" of the ignorant refers to, which the argument needs, so it is folded as
  an apposition, Long's comma between his two words rendered "or"; "nay even
  of its very continuance" → "and even of its very continuance"; "Many as a
  matter of course even among his prescriptions are disagreeable" → "Many even
  of his prescriptions are, as a matter of course, disagreeable"; "Let us then
  receive these things, as well as those which Aesculapius prescribes" → "Let
  us, then, receive these things as we receive those that Aesculapius
  prescribes" (Long's "as well as" is "in the same way as", which the next
  sentence confirms); "the prosperity and felicity of Zeus [the universe]" →
  "the prosperity and happiness of Zeus" — "[the universe]" is Long's
  identification of Zeus with the universe, an alternative label rather than
  a supplement to the sentence, dropped under D11; the referent is in any case
  given by "the health of the universe" in the same clause; "even if it seem"
  → "even if it seems"; "dissatisfied" → "discontented"; "in a manner" → "in
  a way" (three times); "felicity" → "happiness" (twice).
- **V.9** — Long's dagger falls after "fail to"; the clause "For thus thou
  wilt not fail to obey reason, and thou wilt repose in it" stands with
  pronouns modernised only: "For thus you will not fail to obey reason, and
  you will repose in it" (as IV.34 "thy thread" → "your thread"). "Be not
  disgusted" → "Do not be disgusted"; "dissatisfied" → "discontented"; "or
  drenching with water" → "or a drenching with water"; "[which I am doing]"
  folded: "what is more agreeable than this which I am doing?"; "consider if"
  → "consider whether". Long's five nouns (magnanimity, freedom, simplicity,
  equanimity, piety) kept in order.
- **V.10** — "Things are in such a kind of envelopment" → "Things are so
  wrapped up" (Long's "envelopment" is a wrapping, and the plain verb keeps
  the image); "not a few nor those common philosophers" → "not a few of them
  and not the common ones"; "nay even to the Stoics" → "even to the Stoics";
  "not to be vexed at the delay" → "not to resent the delay" (glossary);
  "conformable to the nature of the universe" → "according to the nature of
  the whole" (glossary, both halves); "contrary to my god and daemon" →
  "against my god and the god within" (glossary; see table).
- **V.11** — "About what am I now employing my own soul?" → "On what am I now
  employing my own soul?"; "the ruling principle" → "the ruling part"; "whose
  soul have I now,—that of a child" → "whose soul do I have now—that of a
  child".
- **V.12** — Long has four daggers here, two around "anything … really good"
  and two around "Thus even the many perceive the difference." Both clauses
  stand verbatim: "anything which should not be in harmony with what is
  really good" (Long's "which" kept, though the candidate elsewhere writes
  "that") and "Thus even the many perceive the difference." "[in the first
  case]" folded: "would not be rejected in the first case"; "ask if we should
  value" → "ask whether we should value"; "applied,—that he who has them"
  → "applied—that he who has them".
- **V.13** — "The formal and the material" → "form and matter" (new glossary
  row); "by consequence of such a change" → "as a result of such a change";
  "nothing hinders us" → "nothing prevents us"; "[of revolution]" folded:
  "definite periods of revolution".
- **V.14** — "Reason and the reasoning art [philosophy]" → "Reason and the
  reasoning art": "[philosophy]" is Long's label for what "the reasoning art"
  is, an alternative name rather than a supplement, dropped under D11 (as
  IV.21 "[the formal]"). "Catorthoseis" kept as Long transliterates it, with
  his own gloss "or right acts" and his explanation, which are Marcus's text.
- **V.15** — "None of these things ought to be called a man's, which do not
  belong to a man, as man" → "None of these things ought to be called a man's
  which do not belong to a man as man" (Long's commas dropped); "the means of
  man's nature attaining its end" → "the means by which man's nature attains
  its end"; "that which aids to the accomplishment of this end, and that
  which aids toward this end is that which is good" → "what aids the
  accomplishment of this end; and what aids toward this end is what is good";
  PG's "snowed" → "showed" (a scan error for Long's "showed"; recorded under
  unresolved source issues); "just in the same degree" → "in just the same
  degree".
- **V.16** — "Such as are thy habitual thoughts" → "Such as your habitual
  thoughts are"; "towards" → "toward" (spelling); "the reasonable animal" →
  "the rational being". "For the soul is dyed by the thoughts. Dye it then"
  kept as Long's image.
- **V.17** — Word for word Long's, one sentence.
- **V.18** — "Because he would show a great spirit" → "because he wants to
  show a great spirit" (Long's "would" is "wishes to").
- **V.19** — "Touch not the soul" → "do not touch the soul"; nothing else.
  Long's last clause ("such it makes for itself the things which present
  themselves to it") kept in his construction.
- **V.20** — "Affects" → "feelings" (new glossary row); "that which is a
  hindrance" → "what is a hindrance"; "that which is an obstacle" → "what is
  an obstacle".
- **V.21** — "That which is best" → "what is best" (twice); "in like manner"
  → "in the same way"; the last sentence keeps Long's "that which makes use of
  everything else is this" because "this" is the predicate and "what … is
  this" would blur it.
- **V.22** — "That which does no harm to the state, does no harm" → "What
  does no harm to the state does no harm".
- **V.23** — "In a continual flow" → "in continual flow"; "this which is near
  to thee" → "this which is near you"; "for they vex him" kept (Long's
  transitive "vex", as IV.44 v2).
- **V.24** — "That which is fixed by destiny" → "what is fixed by destiny".
- **V.25** — Pronouns only; "the universal nature" and "my nature" kept as
  Long's contrast.
- **V.26** — "The movements in the flesh" kept (physical motion); "let it
  circumscribe itself" → "let it confine itself" (as IV.3 "circumscribed" →
  "confined"); "affects" → "feelings" (twice); "let not the ruling part of
  itself add to the sensation the opinion" → "let the ruling part not of
  itself add to the sensation the opinion" (Long's "of itself" = on its own
  account, kept in place).
- **V.27** — "The daemon … which Zeus hath given to every man for his guardian
  and guide" → "the god within … which Zeus has given to every man as his
  guardian and guide" (glossary; Long's "which" kept); "that which is
  assigned to him" → "what is assigned to him".
- **V.28** — "Art thou angry" → "Are you angry" (twice); "Well then, and thou
  hast reason" → "Well, then, you too have reason" (Long's "and" = also).
  Long has two daggers here, around his bracketed fragment "[Neither tragic
  actor nor whore.]"; the fragment is Marcus's text as Long prints it (a
  detached sentence, not a supplement or an alternative rendering), so it
  stands verbatim as the last sentence, "Neither tragic actor nor whore.",
  with the brackets themselves not reproduced, per the glossary's rule on
  brackets. Nothing is supplied to connect it.
- **V.29** — PG prints "As thou intendest to live when them art gone out,..."
  — "them" is a PG error for "thou", and the four dots are Long's ellipsis
  for a lacuna plus the comma's stop; rendered "As you intend to live when you
  have gone out,... so it is in your power to live here", the ellipsis kept
  and nothing supplied. "I quit it" → "I leave it"; "the rational and social
  animal" → "the rational and social being".
- **V.30** — "Co-ordinated" → "coordinated"; nothing else.
- **V.31** — "Hitherto" → "until now" (twice); "brethren" → "brothers"; "call
  to recollection" → "call to mind" (as IV.3); "this may be said of thee,—
  "Never has wronged a man in deed or word."" → "this may be said of you:
  "Never has wronged a man in deed or word."" — Long's quoted verse line
  (with its missing subject) kept in his double quotation marks; "ill-minded
  folks" → "ill-minded folk".
- **V.32** — PG's "though all time" → "through all time" (a scan error;
  recorded); "by fixed periods [revolutions]" → "by fixed periods"
  ("[revolutions]" is an alternative rendering of "periods", dropped under
  D11; V.13's "[of revolution]" is a supplement completing the phrase and is
  folded there).
- **V.33** — "But name is sound and echo" → "but a name is sound and echo";
  "and [like] little dogs" → "and like little dogs"; "straightway" → "at
  once"; Long's verse "But fidelity and modesty and justice and truth are fled
  / Up to Olympus from the wide-spread earth" → "But fidelity and modesty and
  justice and truth have fled up to Olympus from the widespread earth", run on
  as prose with Long's words; his citation "HESIOD, Works, etc. v. 197."
  dropped as apparatus (the glossary's rule on source citations after verse
  quotations); "wait in tranquillity" → "wait in calm"; "practise" →
  "practice" (American spelling, per the glossary).
- **V.34** — "An equable flow of happiness" → "an even flow of happiness";
  "the soul of God" kept with Long's capital; "let thy desire find its
  termination" → "let your desire find its termination" (Long's noun kept:
  "end" would read as "cease").
- **V.35** — "The common weal" → "the common good" (twice, glossary).
- **V.36** — "Inconsiderately" → "rashly" (as IV.49 "inconsiderate" →
  "rash"); "[to all]" folded: "give help to all"; "if they should have
  sustained loss" → "if they have sustained loss"; "foster-child's top" →
  "foster child's top"; "the Rostra" kept (the speakers' platform in the Roman
  forum; a proper name, not glossed); "so do thou in this case also" → "so do
  you in this case also"; "good disposition of the soul" → "a good disposition
  of the soul"; "good emotions" kept (Long's own word, distinct from
  "feelings" for his "affects").

## Nothing imported from other translations

No wording was taken from any translation other than Long's. Several Book V
sentences are widely quoted in other renderings (V.1 on getting up in the
morning, V.6 the vine, V.16 "the soul is dyed by the thoughts", V.20 "what is
an obstacle on the road helps us on this road", V.29 "The house is smoky, and
I leave it", V.33 "sound and echo"); each was built from Long's sentence, not
from memory of the familiar version. Proper names are Long's spellings
(Zeus, Aesculapius, the Stoics, Olympus, the Rostra, Catorthoseis).

## Unresolved source issues (Long's text)

- Long marks seven places in Book V with his dagger for uncertain Greek
  (`../PROVENANCE.md` §4): V.9 after "fail to"; V.12 ×4 (after "anything",
  after "really good.", before "Thus", after "difference."); V.28 ×2 (before
  and after "Neither tragic actor nor whore."). Seven marks, four clauses,
  named in `review-instructions.md`; each stands as Long has it (pronouns
  modernised in V.9 only). Reviewers should not expect the candidate to make
  them clearer than the source.
- V.29 breaks off after "gone out,..." (Long's ellipsis for a lacuna). Kept
  as Long prints it; nothing supplied. PG's "them" for "thou" in the same
  clause is a printing error and is rendered "you".
- V.15 PG "snowed" (for "showed") and V.32 PG "though" (for "through") are
  scan errors in the base text; the candidate has the evident word. V.1 PG
  "that which, is" has a stray comma, dropped.
- V.1 "choose neither to eat nor to sleep rather than to perfect the things
  which they care for": Long's sentence appears to lack a negative; kept as
  he has it, nothing supplied (see V.1 above).
- V.5 "I am not formed from them by nature": rendered "for them" (see V.5
  above); a reviewer who reads Long's "from" as deliberate should say so.
- V.28 "Neither tragic actor nor whore.": a fragment; its connection to the
  paragraph is not given by Long and is not supplied.
- No truncated source paragraphs other than V.29; every other source
  paragraph ends in terminal punctuation.

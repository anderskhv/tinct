## B02-P003 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 3. All that is from the gods is full of providence. That which is from fortune is not separated from nature or without an interweaving and involution with the things which are ordered by providence. From thence all things flow; and there is besides necessity, and that which is for the advantage of the whole universe, of which thou art a part. But that is good for every part of nature which the nature of the whole brings, and what serves to maintain this nature. Now the universe is preserved, as by the changes of the elements so by the changes of things compounded of the elements. Let these principles be enough for thee; let them always be fixed opinions. But cast away the thirst after books, that thou mayest not die murmuring, but cheerfully, truly, and from thy heart thankful to the gods.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 3. All that comes from the gods is full of providence. What comes from fortune is not separate from nature, nor without its interweaving and entanglement with the things that providence orders. Everything flows from there. And there is necessity besides, and what benefits the whole universe, of which you are a part. Whatever the nature of the whole brings, and whatever serves to maintain that nature, is good for every part of nature. And the universe is preserved as much by the changes of the elements as by the changes of the things compounded from the elements. Let these principles be enough for you; let them always be your fixed opinions. But throw off the thirst for books, so that you do not die complaining, but cheerfully, truly, and thankful to the gods from your heart.

## B02-P004 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 4. Remember how long thou hast been putting off these things, and how often thou hast received an opportunity from the gods, and yet dost not use it. Thou must now at last perceive of what universe thou art a part, and of what administrator of the universe thy existence is an efflux, and that a limit of time is fixed for thee, which if thou dost not use for clearing away the clouds from thy mind, it will go and thou wilt go, and it will never return.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 4. Remember how long you have been putting these things off, and how often you have been given an opportunity by the gods and still do not use it. You must now, at last, see what universe you are a part of, and what administrator of the universe your existence flows from; and that a limit of time is fixed for you, and if you do not use it to clear the clouds from your mind, it will go, and you will go, and it will never return.

## B02-P005 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 5. Every moment think steadily as a Roman and a man to do what thou hast in hand with perfect and simple dignity, and feeling of affection, and freedom, and justice, and to give thyself relief from all other thoughts. And thou wilt give thyself relief if thou doest every act of thy life as if it were the last, laying aside all carelessness and passionate aversion from the commands of reason, and all hypocrisy, and self-love, and discontent with the portion which has been given to thee. Thou seest how few the things are, the which if a man lays hold of, he is able to live a life which flows in quiet, and is like the existence of the gods; for the gods on their part will require nothing more from him who observes these things.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 5. Every moment, think steadily, as a Roman and a man, of doing what you have in hand with complete and simple dignity, with affection, freedom and justice, and of giving yourself relief from every other thought. And you will give yourself relief if you do every act of your life as if it were your last, setting aside all carelessness, all passionate turning away from the commands of reason, all hypocrisy and self-love, and all discontent with the portion given to you. You see how few things there are which, if a man lays hold of them, let him live a life that flows in quiet and is like the existence of the gods; for the gods, for their part, will require nothing more from the man who observes these things.

## B02-P006 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 6. Do wrong to thyself, do wrong to thyself, my soul; but thou wilt no longer have the opportunity of honoring thyself. Every man's life is sufficient. But thine is nearly finished, though thy soul reverences not itself, but places thy felicity in the souls of others.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 6. Do wrong to yourself, do wrong to yourself, my soul; but you will not have the chance to honor yourself much longer. Every man's life is sufficient. But yours is nearly finished, and still your soul does not reverence itself, but places your happiness in the souls of others.

## B02-P007 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 7. Do the things external which fall upon thee distract thee? Give thyself time to learn something new and good, and cease to be whirled around. But then thou must also avoid being carried about the other way; for those too are triflers who have wearied themselves in life by their activity, and yet have no object to which to direct every movement, and, in a word, all their thoughts.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 7. Do the external things that fall upon you distract you? Give yourself time to learn something new and good, and stop being whirled around. But then you must also avoid being carried off the other way; for they too are triflers who have worn themselves out in life with activity, and yet have no object toward which to direct every impulse and, in a word, all their thoughts.

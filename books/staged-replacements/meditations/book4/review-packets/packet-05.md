## B04-P012 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (Long 1862):**

> 12. A man should always have these two rules in readiness; the one to do only whatever the reason of the ruling and legislating faculty may suggest for the use of men; the other, to change thy opinion, if there is any one at hand who sets thee right and moves thee from any opinion. But this change of opinion must proceed only from a certain persuasion, as of what is just or of common advantage, and the like, not because it appears pleasant or brings reputation.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> 12. A man should always have these two rules ready: one, to do only what the reason of the ruling and legislating part suggests for the use of men; the other, to change your opinion if there is anyone at hand who sets you right and moves you from any opinion. But this change of opinion must come only from a certain persuasion, as of what is just or for the common good, and the like, not because it appears pleasant or brings reputation.

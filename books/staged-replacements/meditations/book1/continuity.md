# Continuity sheet — Meditations, Book I (candidate v1)

Written alongside drafting `candidate-v1.json`, after Book II was accepted, and
describing what the frozen draft actually did. Term renderings follow
`../GLOSSARY.md`; the pattern for applying review findings follows
`../book2/ACCEPTANCE.md` (decision D8 in the ledger).

## Source

- `../meditations-original-en.staged.json`, chapter `number: 1`, title `Book 1`,
  17 paragraphs, one per numbered meditation (I.1–I.17). Extracted verbatim
  into `source-book1.json`.
- George Long 1862, PG #15877. Long's footnote to I.1 says there is no verb in
  the Greek of this book connected with "From …"; he supplies "[I learned]" in
  I.1 only and leaves the rest as bare "From X, Y and Z". Long's footnote
  (apparatus) is not in the staged file; the bracket in I.1 is.
- Book I was read in full before drafting. It is a list of debts: to family
  (I.1–I.4, I.16, I.17), to teachers (I.5–I.15), and to the gods (I.17). Long's
  note "Among the Quadi at the Granua." closes the book.

## The "From …" frame

Long's sections I.2–I.6, I.9, I.10, I.12, I.13 and I.14 have no main verb.
Rendering: "From my mother: piety and generosity, …" — the source's own
elliptical shape, marked with a colon instead of Long's comma, so the modern
reader sees a list of debts and no verb is invented. Where Long has a verb ("I
learned", "I received the impression", "I observed"), the candidate has the same
verb. I.1's bracketed "[I learned]" is folded into prose ("From my grandfather
Verus I learned …") as Long's own supplement.

## Paragraph-level decisions

- **I.1** — "the government of my temper" → "the governing of my temper"
  (Long's noun kept as a gerund; not "controlling", which is the same idea but
  not Long's word).
- **I.2** — "remembrance" → "memory"; "manly character" kept.
- **I.4** — "frequented public schools" → "gone to the public schools"; "spend
  liberally" → "spend freely".
- **I.5** — Long's "my governor" is the man in charge of his upbringing (the
  *paidagōgos*); a modern reader hears a political office, so "my tutor" is
  used. This is a clarification of a single word within Long's sense, recorded
  here per the glossary rule. "The green nor the blue party" → "the Green nor
  the Blue party" (the chariot factions; capitals only). "Parmularius" and
  "Scutarius" are kept as Long has them, without a gloss: Long gives none, and
  the sentence already says they belong to the gladiators' fights, which is
  what the reader needs. "Endurance of labor" → "to endure hard work"; "not to
  be ready to listen to slander" → "not to be quick to listen to slander".
- **I.6** — "jugglers" (in Long's sense) → "conjurers"; "daemons" → "spirits"
  (see table); "[for fighting]" folded; "to endure freedom of speech" → "to put
  up with frank speech"; "a plank bed and skin" → "a plank bed and a skin to lie
  on" (the four added words say what the skin is for, which Long's "and
  whatever else of the kind belongs to the Grecian discipline" implies; flagged
  here as the one small expansion in the book); "Grecian discipline" → "Greek
  discipline".
- **I.7** — "sophistic emulation" → "sophistic ambition" (Long's "emulation"
  in its older sense of ambitious rivalry); "little hortatory orations" →
  "little moralizing speeches"; "a man who practises much discipline" → "a man
  of rigorous discipline"; "abstain from rhetoric" → "keep away from rhetoric";
  "communicated to me out of his own collection" → "shared with me from his
  own collection". The long chain of "and … and … nor …" is kept as one
  paragraph-long sentence, as in Long, because the accumulation is the form of
  the section.
- **I.8** — "undeviating steadiness" → "unwavering steadiness" (also I.14);
  "what are esteemed favors" → "what are counted as favors".
- **I.9** — Long's one long sentence split into three; nothing reordered.
  "Intercourse with him" → "his company" (the old sense); "venerated" kept;
  "approbation" → "approval".
- **I.10** — "solecistic" → "ungrammatical"; "dexterously" → "deftly"; "fit
  suggestion" → "apt suggestion". Long's structure (not to chide, but to
  introduce the right expression by one of four routes) kept with all four
  routes.
- **I.11** — "deficient in paternal affection" → "lacking in fatherly
  affection". "Patricians" kept.
- **I.12** — "Alexander the Platonic" → "Alexander the Platonist" (the
  modern form of the same epithet); "that I have no leisure" → "that I have no
  time"; "alleging urgent occupations" → "pleading urgent business".
- **I.13** — Kept nearly verbatim; "as it is reported of" → "as is reported
  of".
- **I.14** — "polity" → "commonwealth" (glossary); "kingly government" →
  "kingly rule"; "I learned to know" → "I came to know"; "his friends had no
  need to conjecture" → "never had to guess". Long's "my brother" kept (Long's
  own footnote, not in the staged text, notes Marcus had no brother but L.
  Verus by adoption; not the candidate's business to correct).
- **I.15** — "self-government" → "self-mastery"; "admixture" → "blend";
  "amazement" → "astonishment"; "laugh to disguise his vexation" → "laugh to
  cover his annoyance"; "acts of beneficence" → "kindnesses"; "presented the
  appearance of a man" → "gave the impression of a man"; "think himself a
  better man" → "think himself the better man".
- **I.16** — Long's very long section (881 words) kept as one paragraph and in
  Long's order, sentence by sentence. Renderings: "unchangeable resolution" →
  "unchangeable resolve"; "vain-glory" → "vanity"; "common weal" → "the common
  good"; "the occasions for vigorous action and for remission" → "when to act
  vigorously and when to relax"; "sup with him" → "dine with him"; "the
  commodity of life" → "make life comfortable"; "without excusing himself" →
  "without apology"; "[home-bred]" folded into "a flippant home-bred slave";
  "a man ripe, perfect" → "a man mature, complete"; "external applications" →
  "external treatments"; "paroxysms of headache" → "attacks of headache";
  "exhibition of the public spectacles" → "putting on the public spectacles";
  "unseasonable hours" kept; "curious about what he ate" → "fussy about what
  he ate"; "texture and color of his clothes" → "weave and color"; "severally"
  → "one by one"; "abundance of time" → "plenty of time". Long's "He was most
  ready to give without envy to those who possessed any particular faculty …
  and he gave them his help" is kept with the same open object ("to give,
  without envy, to those who had some particular ability"), not resolved into
  "give way" or "give credit". The anecdote of the toll-collector at Tusculum
  is kept as bare as Long has it.
- **I.17** — Long's very long section (663 words) kept as one paragraph, all
  clauses in order. "Indebted for having" → "owe it that I had"; "put me to the
  trial" → "put me to the test"; "made proof of my virility before the proper
  season" → "prove my manhood before the proper time"; "take away all pride
  from me" → "strip me of all pride"; "the fashion of a private person" → "the
  way of a private person"; "meaner in thought, or more remiss in action" →
  "meaner in thought, or slacker in action"; "the public interest" → "the
  common good" (glossary); "vigilance over myself" → "watchfulness over
  myself"; "did not make more proficiency" → "did not get further"; "amatory
  passions" → "passions of love"; "occasion to repent" → "cause to regret";
  "masters for my children" → "teachers for my children"; "bloodspitting and
  giddiness" → "blood-spitting and dizziness"; Long's ellipsis "…" after
  "giddiness" (a gap in his text) is kept; "writers [of histories]" folded;
  "resolution of syllogisms" → "solving of syllogisms". The manuscript note
  "Among the Quadi at the Granua." → "Written among the Quadi, at the Granua."
  per the glossary rule.

## Nothing imported from other translations

No wording was taken from any translation other than Long's. The proper names
are Long's spellings throughout (Verus, Diognetus, Bacchius, Tandasis,
Marcianus, Rusticus, Sinuessa, Epictetus, Apollonius, Sextus, Fronto, Catulus,
Domitius, Athenodotus, Severus, Thrasea, Helvidius, Cato, Dion, Brutus, Maximus,
Lorium, Lanuvium, Tusculum, Benedicta, Theodotus), unchanged.

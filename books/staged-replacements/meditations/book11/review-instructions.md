## Five flagged decisions, on which a ruling is specifically requested, and one point offered for confirmation

1. **XI.18, "flattering" for PG's "nattering".** The one place in Book XI where the candidate does not follow PG's letters. The ground is that "nattering" is not the word: Long writes "flatter" of exactly this at XI.14 ("Men despise one another and flatter one another"), the sentence pairs flattery against resentment, and Standard Ebooks reads "flattering". Same class as VIII.37 "Fergamus"/"Pergamus", IX.34 "pool souls"/"poor souls" and X.15 "Let me see"/"Let men see", all three upheld at round 1 of their books. Is it right here, or should PG be followed and the oddity recorded instead?
2. **XI.18's nine-word bracket, folded.** "[If any have offended against thee, consider first]" is the largest bracket in the package, and folding it puts nine words of Long's supplying into Marcus's mouth at the head of the longest meditation in the book. The case for folding: it is a **supplement** and not a remark about the translation — it supplies the *occasion* of the nine rules, not a note about Long's Greek — so neither D11 nor the new D13 reaches it, and without it XI.18 opens on "What is my relation to men" with no frame at all. The case against: its size. Please rule, including on whether **D13 should be read to cover a bracket this large** whatever its content.
3. **XI.10's "[things indifferent]", dropped under D11.** In shape this is exactly X.2's "[social]" and X.15's "[political community]", both settled as D11 drops at Book X round 1, and folding it would have Marcus name two classes where he names one. But unlike "political" and "law", **the primary word Long leaves standing — "middle things" — is the less transparent of the two**, and a modern reader has to infer the Stoic sense from it. Three routes were weighed: drop it (as drafted), fold it (against the Book X ruling), or extend the glossary's "indifferent" row to cover "middle things" (which would render the primary word *as* the bracket's words, and is indistinguishable in the result from folding). Is the drop right here?
4. **XI.26's "[Ephesians]", folded as a mark of textual doubt.** Long brackets the name because the manuscript reading is uncertain — his footnote reports Gataker's conjecture — not because the word is a supplement or a second rendering. The candidate removes the brackets and keeps the word, on the ground that this is the same kind of mark as a dagger, and the package's practice with daggers is that the mark goes and the text stands. Confirm, or rule that it belongs with the D11 drops, or under D13, or that the brackets should somehow survive.
5. **XI.15's "at once" for Long's "forthwith".** X.30 rendered "forthwith" as "immediately", but here Long's own "immediately" stands nine words earlier **inside the dagger clause** ("Such as a man's character is, he immediately shows it in his eyes"), so the X.30 rendering would invent an echo Long does not have. "At once" avoids it at the cost of not using the edition's established word. The clause sits inside the dagger *sentence* though outside the dagger *clause*. Confirm, or rule for "immediately" in both places, or for leaving "forthwith" untouched as part of the dagger sentence.

Offered for confirmation rather than ruling: **"pancratium" is kept untranslated at XI.2**, on the X.9 "Mimi" ruling — it is Long's text and not apparatus, the reader meets it third in a list of three entertainments and takes it for one, and any gloss would import the footnote content the no-glosses rule exists to prevent.

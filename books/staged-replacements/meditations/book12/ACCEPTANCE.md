## Step 6 — corrections applied and verified

## Step 7 — flow read

`candidate-v2-readable.md` read continuously from XII.1 to XII.36. The book reads as one self-address in one voice, and **no change was made from the read**.

The eight corrected paragraphs read without a snag in place. XII.3's long conditional now runs on one modal footing from its first clause to its last — "if you separate … if you separate, I say … and make yourself like Empedocles' sphere … and if you strive" — which is what the sentence is: one condition stated, restated and extended, not a willingness asked for; and the vortex flows round from outside without being told what it circles, as in Long. XII.4's comparison balances on one modal, so the point that the two anticipations are the same act is no longer cut across by its own grammar. XII.5's "for you see for yourself that in this inquiry you are disputing with the divine" is the turn on himself the paragraph is built to reach, and it lands. XII.15 keeps the comma that disambiguates and loses the one that made two of its three subjects an aside. XII.18 reads as Long's list of four. XII.26 restores the parallel of its two predicates. XII.34 no longer says the reflection has been altered. And XII.36 — the last paragraph of the last book — punctuates its two question marks as XII.15 and XII.33 do.

The pacing survives the modernisation. The three long argumentative sections (XII.1, XII.3, XII.5) still open the book slowly; the middle run of one-line meditations (XII.7, XII.10, XII.11, XII.13, XII.17, XII.25) still lands as a change of tempo; and the actor-and-praetor image still closes it, in Long's words throughout, with "Depart then satisfied, for he also who releases you is satisfied" carrying nothing added to make it sound like an ending. Terminology holds across the book and across the eleven accepted ones: "the whole" and "the universe" never drift into each other; "the ruling part" (XII.1, XII.3, XII.33) is one phrase; "the god within" covers XII.1's "divinity within thee" and XII.3's "daemon", and "the divine" every bare abstract; "impressions" and physical "movement" keep the glossary's lines; "opinion" is never expanded; "feelings" carries XII.19's "affects"; "form" and "matter" appear as the row has them at XII.18 and XII.29, while XII.10's plain "matter, form, and purpose" is Long's own and is left alone. Long's uncommon but current words stand because they are his: "pancratiast", "praetor", "propitiated", "doubled the promontory", "circuitous". The dagger clause at XII.16 leaves its sentence exactly as abrupt as Long leaves it, and XII.17 stops mid-thought as he does. Four paragraphs are byte-identical to Long (XII.7, XII.10, XII.11, XII.13).

**One thing the read confirms about the last book in particular**, and it was the reviewer's own emphasis: there is no valedictory colour anywhere in XII.34–XII.36 that Long does not have. A closing book is where a modernising hand is most tempted to round something off, and the candidate does not.

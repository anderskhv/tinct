## Step 6 — corrections applied and verified

**Applied: the substantive finding, all three minor findings, and one of the five optional ones. Declined: four optional findings, each recorded as considered with its reason.** Five substitutions in three paragraphs; `changes-v1-to-v2.md` lists every one by paragraph ID against the finding it answers.

- **40.1 (substantive) — applied, the reviewer's repair taken as proposed.** Long's three corrective turns in IX.40 are imperatives ("Do thou pray thus:", "Pray thou:", "Thou thus:"). v1 rendered them "Do you pray thus:", "You pray thus:", "You thus:", and two of the three stopped being imperatives: in modern English "Do you pray thus:" is a yes/no question — the "do" that makes an emphatic imperative with "thou" makes an interrogative with "you" — and "You pray thus:" is a declarative saying the reader already prays that way, the exact reverse of Marcus's point. A turn that survives in position and word count but not in **mood** has not survived, and the defect sat in the one passage whose base text the draft had defended precisely because the alternation must hold (PG's "Pray thou" against Standard Ebooks' "Another prays"). v2 reads **"You, pray thus:" / "You, pray:" / "You thus:"**. The vocative comma makes all three unambiguously imperative, keeps Long's fronted second person — which is the rhetorical device of the passage — keeps the three-two-two diminuendo against his four-two-two, and adds no word. Two alternatives were weighed and rejected: "Do pray thus:" is a real emphatic imperative but drops the fronted pronoun that sets Marcus's turn against the other man's; the comma alone in both first turns saves the mood but flattens the diminuendo, which is the reviewer's own lesser option.
- **1.1 (minor) — applied, both halves.** `continuity.md` recorded "'inasmuch as' → 'since' (four times)" where IX.1 has only **three** "inasmuch as"; the fourth "since" had silently replaced Long's ordinary "for" and was unrecorded. The sheet is corrected to three, and the substitution is **undone**: "…contrary to truth, **for** he had received powers from nature…". Long's "for" is current English and needed no modernising, and his two "for"s mark the two explanatory descents of a sentence that already carries three "since"; flattening all four connectives levelled a structure Long built. The paragraph now holds four "since" — Long's own "For since the universal nature has made…" plus the three rendering "inasmuch as" — and two "for". The comma the candidate removed before "who is moved of himself" stays removed; the reviewer endorsed that separately.
- **9.1 (minor) — applied.** IX.9 "and the nurture of young birds, and in a way, loves" → "and the nurture of young birds, **and, in a way, loves**". The glossary substitution "in a manner" → "in a way" is right, but it changed what Long's comma placement could carry: "in a manner" read as a set phrase and took the reader past it, while "in a way, loves" invites a first reading in which "loves" is a **verb**, when it is the fourth item in the list of what is found among animals. One comma, no word changed, the hedge properly parenthetical. The reviewer named this the one place in Book IX where a modern reader was likely to mis-parse a sentence.
- **41.1 (minor) — applied as a rule fix, not a text change.** IX.41 keeps Long's "shall" in "how the mind … shall be free from disturbances", and the reviewer confirms the rendering and would resist "will" (Epicurus's standing problem, not a forecast). - **1.2 (optional) — applied, and the finding's two halves separated.** IX.1 "the nature of the things that are, and the things that are have a relation" → "the nature of the things that are**;** and the things that are have a relation". The two definite articles the candidate added **stay**: Long's anarthrous "things that are" is a technical term (*ta onta*) and the article is what makes a modern reader hear it as one. The **semicolon comes back**: it separates the definition of the universal nature from the consequence drawn about it, and lowering it left a comma splicing two independent clauses, which is a defect in the candidate's own English and not a modernisation of Long's.
- **3.1 (optional) — declined, and recorded.** IX.3 "thou wilt **be made** best reconciled to death" → "you will be best reconciled to death". "Be made best reconciled" is not English anyone writes now; the reviewer's own proposed repair ("be brought best to terms with death") is further from Long than the candidate is, and the reviewer says plainly it would sooner see the drop recorded than undone. Long's causal shade survives in the sentence's own means clause, "by observing the objects from which you are going to be removed". The drop is now listed at IX.3 in `continuity.md`, which is the part of the finding that was a real omission.
- **7.1 (optional) — declined, and recorded.** IX.7 keeps Long's mixed pointing between four coordinate imperatives ("Wipe out imagination; check desire: extinguish appetite: keep the ruling part in its own power."). The reviewer rates it optional, would not press it, and says consistency with eight accepted books is worth more. It is. Nothing here misdirects — four commands are four commands under any pointing, and no reader takes a colon between two imperatives as a change of subject — whereas repointing would be the first time this edition normalised punctuation Long left uneven for no reason but evenness, which `../GLOSSARY.md` does not license. Contrast the commas actually moved in this book (IX.9, IX.29), each of which changes how a clause is parsed.
- **28.1 (optional) — declined, and recorded.** IX.28 keeps "do not let yourself also be governed by it" for Long's "do not thou also be governed by it". The reviewer's objection is fair — "let yourself" carries a faint note of permission Long's plain passive does not — but the proposed repair ("do not be governed by it yourself") drops Long's **"also"**, which is the hinge of the sentence: chance rules the universe, and the meditation is that it need not rule *you as well*. Trading a faint shade for a load-bearing word is the worse exchange, and the shade is not wrong here, since what Marcus asserts is precisely that the ruling part stays his. Every wording that keeps both the plain passive and "also" is worse English than either version. The reviewer's confidence is medium and the finding grants that nobody will misread the candidate.

Both repairs cost more than the shape is worth: "Drivellers, all of them." restores the very noun `continuity.md` ruled out as no longer current, and "Drivel, all of it." **moves the referent** from the people to their talk, when Long's verdict is on the men "playing the philosopher" and the next sentence turns from those men to Marcus himself.

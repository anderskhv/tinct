## Step 6 — corrections applied and verified

- **Applied: all five findings that propose a change** — 2.1, 8.1, 14.1, 20.1, 66.1. **Declined: none.** Finding 9.1 proposes no change to the candidate at all, only two records in `continuity.md`, which have been made.
  - **2.1** VII.2 "continually" → "continuously". Long wrote "continuously"; the two words are not synonyms in current English ("continuously" without a break, "continually" again and again) and his image is a fire kept alight by fanning, not a fire relit. Both words are current, so the narrowing bought nothing and cost the image.
  - **8.1** VII.8 "carrying with you the same reason" → "having with you the same reason". This was the review's one optional preference; see the next bullet.
  - **14.1** VII.14 "those parts which have felt it will complain" → "those parts which have felt will complain". The inserted "it" supplied an object Long leaves out and created a garden path ("have felt that it will complain"), which the reader has to back out of. Long's absolute "have felt" is grammatical modern English and carries the sense. The reviewer's alternative ("have felt the fall") was not taken: it adds a word where removing one suffices.
  - **20.1** VII.20 "that I should do something" → "that I may do something". After a colon, "should" is read first as obligation — "the thing that troubles me is that I ought to do something the constitution does not allow" — which reverses the meditation: Marcus fears a possibility, he does not describe a duty. "May" is the fear-of-a-possibility modal and keeps every other word, the three-member "or … or …" chain included.
  - **66.1** VII.66 "How do we know that Telauges" → "How do we know whether Telauges". Long's "if" is "whether" and the meditation is an open question; "know *that* X was not superior" reads as a challenge to someone who has claimed the contrary, which puts Marcus in an argument he is not having and turns the following "For it is not enough that…" into a rebuttal instead of the reason the question stays open. Standard Ebooks' Long has the same "if", so this is Long's word, not a PG variant.
- **The optional item (8.1): applied**, under the D8 pattern — a minor finding is applied unless `continuity.md` already records a considered reason not to, and v1 recorded none: VII.8 did not appear in the paragraph-level list at all, which is that sheet's way of saying the paragraph is Long with the pronouns modernised. "Carrying with you" was therefore an unrecorded departure from Long's "having with thee", and it is the kind of departure this edition exists to avoid: "having with you" is ordinary current English needing no replacement, while "carrying" adds a faint physical image (reason as something portable, taken along) that Long does not have and the meditation does not want. Same route as Book V's 33.1 and 36.1 and Book VI's 15.1.

## Step 7 — flow read

`candidate-v2-readable.md` read continuously from VII.1 to VII.75. The book reads as one self-address in one voice, and no change was made from the read.

The five corrected clauses read without a snag in place. VII.2's "fan these thoughts continuously into a flame" keeps the fire alight across the sentences that follow it. VII.8's "having with you the same reason which you now use for present things" is plain possession, and the meditation's point — you will meet the future with the reason you already have — lands without the image of something carried. VII.14's "those parts which have felt will complain, if they choose" is read once, not twice, and runs straight into "But I, unless I think that what has happened is an evil, am not injured." VII.20 is a fear and not a duty, and its three "or" members follow intact. VII.66 opens on an open question, so the "For it is not enough that…" that follows is its reason and not a rebuttal, and the long weighing of Socrates against a name we know nothing about keeps its point.

The long meditations carry Long's argument in his order: VII.5's three-way choice with its exception; VII.9's chain of "one … one … one" closing on its conditional; VII.13's pun, which survives only because the Greek does; VII.16's separation of ruling part, body and soul; VII.18's bath and food and the turn onto himself; VII.26's two branches and the duty from each; VII.48's view from a higher place; VII.55's three constitutional things; VII.64's argument from dishonor to damage to Epicurus; VII.68's two speeches; VII.75's disjunction. The short meditations stay at Long's length and land as hard — "There is nothing new: all things are both familiar and short-lived."; "Stand upright, or be made upright."; "Near is your forgetfulness of all things; and near the forgetfulness of you by all."; "It is royal to do good and to be abused."; "Look within." The images are all still images: the bone thrown to little dogs, the puppets pulled by strings, the gold and the emerald and the purple, the wax molded into a horse and then a tree, the lame soldier on the battlements, the heaps of sand, the ripe ears of corn, the wrestler against the dancer, the fountain that bubbles up if you dig. The quotation cluster VII.35–VII.51 keeps the character of Long's commonplace book, with attributions only where he has them and verse capitals at the head of every line he capitalises. The dagger clauses leave their sentences exactly as obscure as Long leaves them, and VII.58 stops where his stops.

Terminology was checked across the accepted books at the read: "the ruling part" for all of Long's phrases (VII.16 ×2, VII.22, VII.33, VII.55 ×2, VII.62) beside his own distinct "the rational part which rules" (VII.28) and "the rational and political faculty" (VII.72); "the universal nature" (VII.5, VII.18 ×2, VII.23, VII.55) beside "the nature of the whole" (VII.75) and "the whole" (VII.9, VII.10, VII.19, VII.25, VII.66); "rational being" and its extensions to "intelligent beings" and "beings of the same stock" (VII.9, VII.11, VII.13, VII.55); "according to nature" / "against nature" / "against reason" (VII.11, VII.24 ×2, VII.53, VII.56, VII.74); "calm" for every "tranquillity" (VII.28, VII.33, VII.68 ×2, VII.75); "disturbance" (VII.16) beside Long's own verb "disturb"; "resent / resentful" (VII.38, VII.58, VII.66, VII.70) beside "discontented" (VII.64), kept separate; "feelings" (VII.2, VII.66); "kindness" (VII.13, VII.63); "impressions" (VII.2) beside "imagination" (VII.17, VII.29, VII.64), kept apart as Long keeps them; "impulse" (VII.4) beside "motion" (VII.55, VII.60) and "movement" (VII.75) — all as the glossary and the six earlier acceptances have them.

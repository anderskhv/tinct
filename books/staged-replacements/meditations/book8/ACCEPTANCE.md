## Step 6 — corrections applied and verified

**Applied: all four minor findings and three of the four optional ones. Declined: one optional finding (1.2), recorded as considered.** Nine substitutions in seven paragraphs; `changes-v1-to-v2.md` lists every one by paragraph ID against the finding it answers.

- **7.1** VIII.7 — the em dash after "worth" is replaced by **Long's comma**: "according to its worth, times, substance, cause, activity, and incident". The em dash was not a punctuation normalisation. Long's comma leaves six things the universal nature apportions; the dash takes "worth" out of the list and makes the other five an appositive gloss on it. That is a real reading, arguably the better one against the Greek, but it resolves an ambiguity Long left open in the one direction that silently drops a term from a list — and `continuity.md` had already defended keeping "Long's list … entire, 'incident' included", describing six items where the candidate punctuated five. Where sheet and text disagree, the source's own punctuation stands.
- **1.1 / 55.1 / 58.1 — one decision, applied, and made a general rule.** Long's "shall / shalt" as a **plain future** — in a statement, or in a subordinate clause of condition or time — is rendered "will", or the plain present where English uses the present for future time in such a clause. "Shall" is kept only where it is current English in its own right: the first person ("I shall meet the busybody", II.1), the emphatic or volitional "shall" ("no man shall hinder me", V.29), the deliberative "shall" of a direct question in the first or third person ("How then shall a man do this?", VIII.1), and the subjunctive of a negative consecutive clause ("so that each act shall not do its duty", VIII.32). Second-person "shall" in a question is not current and takes "will". **Written into `../GLOSSARY.md`** under Voice and form so later books follow it without re-deciding, and into `continuity.md`.
  - Five changes: VIII.1 "if you shall live" → "if you live"; VIII.55 "as soon as he shall choose" → "as soon as he chooses" (IV.3's "whenever thou shalt choose" is "whenever you choose" in the accepted Book IV — the identical verb in the identical construction); VIII.58 "if you shall have" → "if you have" and "if you shall acquire" → "if you acquire"; and VIII.51 "How then shall you possess" → "How then will you possess", the reviewer's "also noted" fourth case, taken with the other three because one decision for the book means one decision.

- **12.1 (optional) — applied.** VIII.12 "more peculiarly its own" → "more particularly its own". Long means *particularly, as belonging distinctively to it*; the dominant modern sense of the adverb is *oddly*, and this is the one word in the paragraph a reader can take backwards. The edition's own precedent divides on a line this case falls the far side of: the fixed collocation "peculiar to X" is current and is **kept** (III.16, VII.22, VII.55 "the peculiar office"), while Long's attributive "peculiar" outside it is rendered — V.3 "their peculiar leading principle" → "their own ruling part", VI.3 "the peculiar quality of anything" → "the particular quality of anything". VIII.12's adverb is outside the collocation, so it follows VI.3 and takes its word.
- **41.1 (optional) — applied, by a third route.** VIII.41 "making this effort absolutely" → "making this effort **unconditionally**". The reviewer's diagnosis is accepted entire: Long's "absolutely" is technical here — *without the reserve clause* — and the next sentence's contrast depends on the reader hearing it that way, while bare "absolutely" reads to a modern ear as an intensifier and points the sentence the wrong way. Neither route the reviewer offered was taken. **Folding the bracket** would put one of the book's ten D11 brackets back into the prose as an apposition, which is the thing D11 exists to prevent, with no principle separating it from the other nine. **Leaving "absolutely" bare** leaves the misreading in place. Rendering the primary word as "unconditionally" keeps D11 exactly as it stands — the bracket is still dropped, nothing is folded, no word is added, one word answers one word — and takes the sense from Long's own first alternative rather than from the drafter. Same class of choice as VIII.48's "inexpugnable" → "impregnable" and VIII.57's "enlighten" → "light up". One cost recorded: "absolutely" is not archaic, so this rendering is chosen for sense, not for register, and the evidence for the sense is the gloss D11 drops.
- **51.1 (optional) — applied.** VIII.51 "freedom joined with" reverts to Long's "freedom **conjoined** with". "Conjoined with" is formal but current English, not in the class of "affrighted" or "inexpugnable", so the change bought little — and it was made **inside** the dagger-marked clause (the dagger sits at "By forming +", PG line 5345), where this package's practice, set at VI.50 and confirmed at VII.16, is that the clause stands as Long has it with pronouns modernised and glossary renderings applied. "Conjoined" → "joined" is neither a pronoun nor a glossary row. If anything Long's word is the stronger one for what Marcus means: freedom fused with contentment, simplicity and modesty rather than merely accompanied by them. `continuity.md` had listed the change flatly among the paragraph's modernisations without noting that it fell inside a dagger clause; that omission is corrected there. "Potable water" → "drinkable water" stands — it is outside the dagger clause, which begins at "By forming".
- **1.2 (optional) — not applied, recorded as considered.** Long punctuates VIII.1's thought as a quoted one ("throw away the thought, How thou shall seem [to others]"); the candidate reports it ("throw away the thought of how you will seem to others"). The reviewer proposed the direct form for uniformity with VIII.2, VIII.14 and VIII.36, and did not press it. Declined on a point the reviewer names and then sets aside: **Long's quoted thought is in the second person**, because Marcus is addressing himself, whereas every other quoted thought in this book is in the first ("How is this with respect to me?", VIII.2). The direct form forces a choice between two wrong things — keep the second person, and the quotation reads as someone else speaking to Marcus; switch to the first, and the candidate has changed the person of a sentence Long prints in another. The reported form keeps his person and his object, costs nothing in sense, and suits the list of things thrown away that the sentence is. The uniformity sought is uniformity with paragraphs whose thoughts Long himself puts in the first person; it is not a rule this paragraph breaks. Recorded at VIII.1 in `continuity.md` so it is not raised again as unconsidered.

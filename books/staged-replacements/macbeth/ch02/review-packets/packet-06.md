## B02-P016 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (served original-en):**

> ROSS. From Fife, great King, Where the Norweyan banners flout the sky And fan our people cold. Norway himself, with terrible numbers, Assisted by that most disloyal traitor, The Thane of Cawdor, began a dismal conflict; Till that Bellona’s bridegroom, lapp’d in proof, Confronted him with self-comparisons, Point against point, rebellious arm ’gainst arm, Curbing his lavish spirit: and, to conclude, The victory fell on us.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> ROSS. From Fife, great King, where the Norwegian banners mock the sky and chill our people with their fanning. The King of Norway himself, with a terrible host, aided by that most disloyal traitor, the Thane of Cawdor, began a grim battle; until Bellona's bridegroom, armored in proof, met him as his equal—point against point, rebel arm against arm—and curbed his reckless spirit. In the end, the victory fell to us.

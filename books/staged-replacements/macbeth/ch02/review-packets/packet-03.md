## B02-P006 — CONTEXT ONLY — reviewed in another packet

**Source (served original-en):**

> SOLDIER. As whence the sun ’gins his reflection Shipwracking storms and direful thunders break, So from that spring, whence comfort seem’d to come Discomfort swells. Mark, King of Scotland, mark: No sooner justice had, with valour arm’d, Compell’d these skipping kerns to trust their heels, But the Norweyan lord, surveying vantage, With furbish’d arms and new supplies of men, Began a fresh assault.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> SOLDIER. Just as ship-wrecking storms and fearsome thunder break from the point where the sun begins to shine, so from that very spring where comfort seemed to rise, discomfort now wells up. Mark this, King of Scotland, mark it: no sooner had justice, armed with valor, forced these fleeing foot soldiers to trust their heels, than the Norwegian lord, seeing his chance, renewed the attack with polished weapons and fresh troops.

## B02-P008 — ASSIGNED FOR REVIEW

**Source (served original-en):**

> SOLDIER. Yes; As sparrows eagles, or the hare the lion. If I say sooth, I must report they were As cannons overcharg’d with double cracks; So they Doubly redoubled strokes upon the foe: Except they meant to bathe in reeking wounds, Or memorize another Golgotha, I cannot tell— But I am faint, my gashes cry for help.

**Candidate (modern English v1):**

> SOLDIER. Yes—as sparrows dismay eagles, or the hare the lion. To tell the truth, I must report they stood like cannons overloaded with a double charge: so they struck the enemy twice as hard, redoubling every blow. Whether they meant to bathe in steaming wounds or make the field a second Golgotha, I cannot say—but I grow faint; my wounds cry out for help.

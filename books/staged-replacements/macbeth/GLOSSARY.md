# Glossary — stable renderings for the Macbeth modern edition

Fixed before drafting Act 1 Scenes 1–2 (2026-09-11). The left column is the
served `original-en`'s wording (from PG #1533/#100, see `PROVENANCE.md`);
the middle column is the rendering used in every candidate paragraph, every
scene, unless a specific scene records an exception in that scene's
`continuity.md`. Rows are added before the scene that first needs them, per
`WORKFLOW.md` step 2.

## Recurring terms and forms of address

| Source | Modern edition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| thee; thou; thy; thine; thyself | **you**; **your**; **yourself** | Standard modernisation throughout Shakespeare. Applied wherever the archaic second-person forms appear. |
| 'gainst; 'twas; e'er; ne'er; o'er; ere | **against**; **it was**; **ever**; **never**; **over**; **before** | Elided archaic forms spelled out in full modern words. |
| dost; doth; hath; shall (as plain future); art (verb "to be") | **do**; **does**; **has**; **will**; **are** | Standard verb-form modernisation. "Shall" is kept where it still carries a modern sense (resolve, command, formal question) rather than mechanically replaced everywhere. |
| Thane (of X) | **Thane (of X)** | Kept unchanged. A specific historical Scottish title, not a synonym for "Lord"; modernising it would lose the play's Scottish setting and is never done. |
| King of Scotland; the King | **King of Scotland**; **the King** | Kept as the source has them. |
| Fortune; Valour; Bellona | **Fortune**; **Valor**; **Bellona** | Personifications used as figures of speech (Fortune as a fickle woman; "Valour's minion"; "Bellona's bridegroom" as a kenning for Macbeth). Kept capitalized and unchanged as proper figures, per the rule that images and personifications are preserved, not explained away. American spelling "Valor" used consistently (see Spelling, below). |
| Golgotha | **Golgotha** | Biblical place-name kenning for a field of skulls/slaughter. Kept unchanged; no gloss needed, it is in ordinary use in English. |
| kerns and gallowglasses | **light-armed foot soldiers and heavy-armed fighters** | Two classes of Irish/Scottish soldier — kerns lightly armed, gallowglasses heavily armed mercenaries — a technical historical pairing a modern reader will not otherwise recognize. Rendered descriptively at first use (Act 1 Scene 2) rather than kept as unglossed period terms; the two-part description stands in for the pairing each time it recurs. |
| sergeant; Soldier (speech heading) | **sergeant**; **SOLDIER** | Current words; kept. The speech-heading "SOLDIER." (for the bleeding Captain) is a speaker label and is copied verbatim, never modernised or expanded to "CAPTAIN." |
| Norweyan; the Norways' king | **Norwegian**; **the King of Norway** | Modernised spelling/phrasing of the same reference. |
| dollars | **dollars** | Shakespeare's own anachronism (Norwegian/Scottish setting, English currency word); kept as the source has it, not corrected or explained — it is the playwright's word, not a translation choice. |
| hurlyburly | **uproar** | Rare word for tumult/commotion; "uproar" is the plain current equivalent used once, in Act 1 Scene 1. |
| Anon | **Coming** | The Witches' one-word response to a familiar's call; "anon" now reads as archaic or a false-friend ("anonymous"); "Coming" is the plain modern equivalent with the same brevity. |
| Graymalkin; Paddock | **Graymalkin**; **Paddock** | The Witches' familiar spirits (a cat, a toad). Proper names; kept unchanged. |
| happiness (in the sense of good fortune) | **good fortune** | Duncan's "Great happiness!" (Act 1 Scene 2) uses the word in its period sense of "good luck," not emotional joy; rendering literally risks a false-friend modern reading, so it is glossed to the sense meant. Applied wherever the source uses "happiness"/"happy" to mean fortunate rather than emotionally glad — checked per occurrence, not assumed. |
| bosom interest | **deepest trust** | Duncan's "our bosom interest," meaning his closest personal trust/confidence, not a literal or financial "interest"; rendered to the sense meant to avoid a false-friend modern reading. |

## Voice and form

- **Speaker labels are copied verbatim.** `SPEAKER NAME IN CAPS. ` at the
  start of every speech paragraph is never altered, abbreviated, or
  retranslated (see `WORKFLOW.md` and `PROVENANCE.md` §2).
- **Stage directions are copied verbatim**, character-for-character,
  brackets included. They are already plain modern English in the served
  file and are not paraphrased.
- **No verse lineation to preserve.** The served JSON collapses every
  speech's line breaks to plain spaces; candidate speeches are ordinary
  flowing modern prose sentences, not an attempt to reconstruct verse lines
  or add line breaks of the drafter's own.
- **Famous or already-plain lines are kept close to the source**, not
  paraphrased into blander alternatives — e.g. "Fair is foul, and foul is
  fair" needs no modernising; it already reads as contemporary English.
- **Register per character**, established from Act 1 Scenes 1–2 and to be
  maintained: the Witches speak in short, riddling, near-rhyming exchanges;
  the Soldier/Captain reports the battle in vivid, compressed martial
  imagery, growing shorter and more strained as he weakens ("I am faint, my
  gashes cry for help"); Duncan speaks with courtly, slightly formal warmth
  ("worthy gentleman," "brave friend"); Ross reports news formally and at
  length, in the same register as the Soldier.
- **Nothing added.** No modern explanatory clauses, no glosses beyond the
  single bracketed or worked-in clarification recorded in this glossary or
  a scene's `continuity.md`, and never more than once per scene for the same
  term.
- **Punctuation.** Straight apostrophes and quotation marks, as in the
  served JSON (the served file itself uses curly quotes/apostrophes in
  `original-en`; candidates use straight marks, matching the served
  `modern-en`'s own convention — checked directly against
  `macbeth-modern-en.json`).
- **Spelling.** American, matching the served `modern-en`'s own practice
  (e.g. "Valor," "honor") rather than the served `original-en`'s British
  spelling ("Valour," "honour") — checked directly: the existing served
  `modern-en` renders "Valour's minion" as "Valor's favorite warrior," i.e.
  American spelling is already this book's established modern-en
  convention.

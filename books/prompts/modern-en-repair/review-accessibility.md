# Modern-EN repair — accessibility review (candidate only)

You are reviewing one chapter of a Modern English reading edition **without seeing the source**. You are standing in for an intelligent new reader with no specialist knowledge of the book, its period, or its subject. Do not consult the source, the original language, or your memory of other translations. Read only the candidate.

## What to do

Read the whole chapter once straight through, then paragraph by paragraph. For each paragraph, give one verdict:

- **clear** — a new reader follows it on first reading.
- **hard** — followable but with unnecessary effort: a long tangled sentence, a stack of subordinate clauses, an unexplained reference, an archaic or specialist word with no cue, an ambiguous pronoun, a sentence you had to re-read.
- **unclear** — you could not say with confidence what it means, or you can see two readings.

For every **hard** or **unclear** paragraph, quote the exact wording that causes the difficulty and explain the obstacle in one or two sentences. Do not rewrite it. Do not guess what the source said.

Also report on the chapter as a whole, in a few sentences each:

- **Rhythm and voice.** Does it read as natural prose, or as a succession of short choppy sentences, or as translated syntax? Does it sound like one author, or like a modern paraphrase that has lost the author's tone?
- **Unexplained references.** Names, places, terms or allusions a new reader would have to look up.
- **Argument or story thread.** Could you say in two sentences what the chapter is about and how it moves? If not, where did you lose the thread?
- **Anachronism or over-explanation.** Any modern idiom that jars, or any place the prose explains what it should simply show.

## Output

A markdown file with: a table of paragraph index and verdict; the quoted obstacles for each hard/unclear paragraph; the four whole-chapter notes; and a final line `Accessibility verdict: PASS | REVISE` where PASS means no unclear paragraphs and at most a few hard ones with minor obstacles. Be exact and do not pad. If the chapter is clear, say so briefly.

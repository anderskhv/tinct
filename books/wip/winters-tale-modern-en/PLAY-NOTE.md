# The Winter's Tale (winters-tale): play note

Supplements SHAKESPEARE-BRIEF.md for this play only. Ids are chapter:paragraph (chapter = scene, paragraph index from 0, as in slices.json). 15 chapters: Acts 1-5.

## 1. Names, titles, setting

Kings are often called by their country in speeches: "Sicilia" = Leontes, "Bohemia" = Polixenes. Keep the country word, and where a modern reader would lose the king, add the name or "the king of Sicilia" the first time in a speech. Setting: Sicilia (courts, prison, trial), the Bohemian seacoast and countryside, then Sicilia again. Bohemia has a seacoast here; do not "correct" it. Keep "Sicilia" (never Sicily) in narration and speeches, and the Bohemian "shepherd's cottage" scenes in Bohemia.

The Oracle has no prefix: it is read aloud by the OFFICER (7:24-26, two paragraphs; the second continues the same sentence mid-clause). Keep "Delphos" and "Apollo". Autolycus is called "Rogero" once (14:6) and gives himself false names; keep names as spoken. Autolycus's "father named me Autolycus" joke (11:7) is on Mercury's thieving son: keep the allusion, unpack lightly ("who was Mercury's son, a snapper-up of trifles").

## 2. Fixed terms

- the oracle of Apollo (at Delphos): "the oracle", "great Apollo", "the sword of justice" (oath at 7:21). Do not turn "oracle" into "prophecy".
- the trial (Act 3 Scene 2): "arraigned", "high treason", "indictment", "the bar"; Hermione is "accused and arraigned of high treason"; keep legal flavor in plain words.
- stage direction `[_Exit, pursued by a bear._]` (8:11): copy verbatim. The bear is a real bear; the Clown's later talk of the bear (8:21-29) must stay literal.
- pastoral and sheep-shearing: "sheep-shearing feast", "hostess of the day", "swain", "shepherdess", "Flora", "Whitsun pastorals", "the feast". Keep "sheep-shearing", "fardel" (bundle, 8:26, 12:239-264 and 14:2; render "bundle" consistently), "gillyvors" (streaked carnations, keep "gillyvors" with "carnations" nearby), "rosemary and rue".
- ballads and rogue's cant: "cozener", "cony-catcher" if present, "prig" (thief), "pugging tooth" (thieving tooth), "tinker", "bear-baiting", "pedlar"; keep "hey nonny" refrains and "dear-a", "stile-a" syllables. Rogue words become plain thieves' slang that the reader can see is slang, not modern profanity.
- "affection" means passion/obsession; "nothing" is an image of nothing/something (see 2:82). "Bawcock", "squash", "neat" (also horned cattle) in Leontes's speeches carry cuckold jokes: "a fork'd one" is horns.

## 3. Hard passages

1. 2:38-39, Leontes, "Too hot, too hot!" and the "tremor cordis" aside; compressed, broken syntax of the first jealousy. Keep the Latin phrase and say plainly "my heart is trembling".
2. 2:43, the "affection" soliloquy ("Affection! thy intention stabs the centre"). Famously compressed: affection (jealous passion) reaches things not real, even dreams; keep the argument chain and each image.
3. 2:54 and 2:56, Leontes: the angling image ("I am angling now"), then "Inch-thick, knee-deep, o'er head and ears a fork'd one"; keep the horns pun and the fishing image.
4. 2:82, "Is whispering nothing?" the nothing-speech; list of lovers' gestures, must stay a list of questions, with "nothing" repeated.
5. 3:26, the spider in the cup: "I have drunk, and seen the spider." Image must stay; explain that knowing spoils the drink.
6. 7:8, 7:14 and 7:16, Hermione's trial speech and replies ("Sir, you speak a language that I understand not"; "The bug which you would fright me with I seek"). "Bug" is a bogey/terror; keep her dignity, and the long periodic sentences split without losing her argument about honor and life.
7. 5:13, 5:37 and 5:43, Paulina's rebukes: "Not so hot", "the whole matter and copy of the father", "an heretic that makes the fire, not she which burns in it". Strong insults to Leontes stay strong.
8. 7:48 and 7:53, Paulina's accusation of the "tyrant" ("What studied torments, tyrant?") and her repentance of speaking too hard; long run-ons, split them.
9. 9:1, Time's speech: rhymed couplets, personification ("I that please some, try all"); first person, mention of sixteen years' gap, "my wings", "the glass"/hourglass. Keep as one paragraph.
10. 11:1-7 and 11:49, Autolycus's songs and prose ("When daffodils begin to peer", "Jog on, jog on"): keep songs as songs, the same number of sentences and the refrains, no rhyme-forcing; copy the underscore markers.
11. 8:15-29 and 12:229-273, the Shepherd and Clown's prose: dialect, malapropisms and "fardel"; keep rustic colloquial tone, the joke of their sums at 11:12 (counting what to buy for the feast), and "I would you did but see how it chafes" (shipwreck report beside the bear).
12. 12:16-29, Perdita's flower speeches ("rosemary and rue", "the fairest flowers", "O Proserpina", daffodils "that come before the swallow dares"): list of named flowers and their meaning; keep each flower name and "nature's bastards".
13. 12:57-106, Autolycus's pedlar songs ("Lawn as white as driven snow", "Get you hence", "Will you buy any tape"): list-songs of wares, keep as songs; song paragraphs 12:87 and 12:105 are the label "SONG." alone.
14. 12:148, Polixenes's rage at the shepherds ("old traitor ... by hanging thee"): threat and insult remain violent.
15. 15:5-37, the statue scene: Leontes's "Her natural posture!", Paulina's "Music, awake her", "Start not; her actions shall be holy", "O, she's warm!"; plain dignity, no melodrama, keep "stone" and "art lawful as eating" clear.

## 4. Odd in the served text

- Many speeches are split: a lone prefix paragraph ("LEONTES." at 2:37, 12:12 and elsewhere) is followed by continuation paragraphs with no prefix, which begin mid-speech or mid-sentence (2:39, 2:56, 4:3, 4:5). Render paragraph N from source N only; do not merge or move words across paragraphs, and leave a lone-prefix paragraph as just the prefix.
- Some prefixes lack the period ("LORDS We can", "CLEOMENES Sir, you have", "LORDS Now blessed", at 5:49, 6:1, 6:3, 6:5, 7:27, 13:1, 13:5, 13:19, 13:21). The brief says copy the prefix exactly: do not add the period, though the live modern edition does.
- Unbracketed stage directions ("Enter Leontes ...", "Hermione comes down from the pedestal.") are copied verbatim; the live modern edition wrongly adds brackets around them and removes underscores from `[_Exit._]` etc. Follow the source, not the live edition.
- Songs and italic words carry `_` markers inside the paragraph (e.g. 11:1, 12:57, 12:88, 12:106, 12:89-91 "_Whither?_", 2:39 `_tremor cordis_`); the live edition stripped them. Restore them.
- Verse split across paragraphs and stage-direction mix: 2:14, 12:119, 12:246 contain a stage direction inside speech text.
- The oracle's text uses curly quotes in source; live uses straight single quotes when nested: use straight double quotes outside, straight single inside.

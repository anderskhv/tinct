# The Wind in the Willows (Grahame) — Danish edition notes

- Fixed terms: Muldvarpen, Vandrotten (Rotte in direct address), Tudsen / hr. Tudse, Grævlingen / hr. Grævling, Odderen, Portly, Havrotten, Toad Hall, Den Vilde Skov, Flodbredden, Piberen ved Daggryets Porte, sigøjner, engelske mil.
- Narration uses both “Vandrotten” (about 170 times) and the short “Rotten” (about 150 times); a reviewer left both. The bare “Rotte”, “Muldvarp”, “Odder” and “Tudse” are vocatives and were left.
- “Mole End” appears once in speech beside “Muldvarpens Hjem” on the door sign.
- Address forms: du between animals, De with human strangers; a few paragraphs mix them and one packet was harmonised by the reviewer (the gypsy).
- Verse and songs keep line counts; rhymes are kept where the helpers managed it and are otherwise loose. Toad’s songs (chapters 10 and 12) and the carol (chapter 5) deserve a native read.
- Chapter titles were translated by hand; “Dulce Domum” stays Latin; chapter 12 is “Ulysses’ hjemkomst”.
- Distances: English miles are “engelske mil”; coins and units without a Danish equivalent (shilling, pence) are kept.
- Speeches that run across paragraphs follow the English: » opens every paragraph, « only at the end.

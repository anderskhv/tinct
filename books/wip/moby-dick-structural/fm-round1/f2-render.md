# f2 render: extracts.45 to extracts.85

Role: render. Batch: f2. I reviewed and rendered 41 paragraphs. Each one has an item (non-blocking, category `render`).

## Counts by category
- Quoted prose (rule 2): 33. These are extracts.45–47, 51–54, 57–62, 64–83.
- Verbatim verse (rule 3, Lead decision 8): 8. These are extracts.48 (Falconer), 49–50 (Cowper), 55 (Montgomery), 56 (Lamb), 63 (Oakes Smith), and 84–85 (the whaling songs).
- Attributions (rule 4): every attribution present in the range is rendered as `— Name.` with names and titles exactly as printed. That includes "Colnett's ... Spermacetti Whale Fishery", "Etchings of a Whaling Cruize", "Ibid.", "(somewhere.)", the Chace, Globe and Cheever titles with their imprints, "From "Something" unpublished." and "U.S. Ex. Ex."

## Decisions
- **Structural quirks kept as printed.** extracts.49 has no attribution, because Cowper's attribution comes at extracts.50. extracts.64 ends with `....` and has no attribution, because Scoresby's attribution comes at extracts.65. extracts.70 has no opening quote mark in the source. Elision marks `....` are kept in extracts.64 and 66.
- **Emendations.** extracts.54 uses the title spelling "Spermacetti". extracts.60 uses "spout".
- **Verse typography only.** Curly quotes and apostrophes become straight. The poetic elisions "o'er", "Gather'd" and "arm'd" are kept as part of the wording and meter.
- **Italic stress.** In extracts.68, the dialect spelling "thar" carries the stress, so no underscore is needed. In extracts.47, "royal fish" is kept plainly.
- **Period language (decision 1).** extracts.78 keeps "savages" and "whites".
- **Light dialect (decision 5).** extracts.60 keeps "raal oil-butt". "'tis" becomes "it's".
- **Sea-calls.** extracts.68 keeps "Where away?", "Three points off the lee bow", "Raise up your wheel" and "Call all hands". "Ay ay" becomes "Aye aye" and "shoal" becomes "school".

## Uncertain readings (non-blocking; for the lead)
1. **extracts.54, body text.** "Spermacetti Whales" is rendered "Spermaceti Whales", the modern spelling of an ordinary word. Only the title keeps "Spermacetti". Revert if the lead treats the quoted body as a quoted document (decision 7 exception).
2. **extracts.55.** "chace" became "chase" and "colour" became "color". **extracts.63:** "phospher" became "phosphor". I treated these as obsolete spellings under rule 3. Revert if the lead wants the poets' spellings kept.
3. **extracts.84.** "harpooneer" became "harpooner", following the edition-wide convention and rule 3's allowance for spelling. The meter is unaffected. Revert if the song's spelling should stand.
4. **extracts.82.** Melville's interpolation "(Terra Del Fuego)" became "(Tierra del Fuego)" under decision 7, since it is a geographic name in Melville's own parenthesis, outside the Darwin quote. Keep "Terra Del Fuego" if the lead reads it as part of the quoted document.
5. **extracts.76.** "struck by her near appearance" is read as "how she looks up close". "U.S. Ex. Ex." (the U.S. Exploring Expedition) is left unexpanded because it is part of the title as printed.
6. **extracts.51.** Melville's aside "(A small sized one.)" is hyphenated to "small-sized".
7. **extracts.62.** "stove by a whale" became "stove in by a whale". This is the modern idiom and is not a gloss.

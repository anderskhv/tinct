
=== Chapter 1: Act 1, Scene 1 — Windsor. Before Page’s house ===
[1.0] [Enter JUSTICE SHALLOW, SLENDER, and SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[1.1] SHALLOW. Sir Hugh, don't try to talk me out of it; I'll take this all the way to the Star Chamber. If he were twenty Sir John Falstaffs, he shall not abuse Robert Shallow, Esquire.
[1.2] SLENDER. Justice of the Peace in the County of Gloucester, and Coram.
[1.3] SHALLOW. Yes, cousin Slender, and Custalorum.
[1.4] SLENDER. Yes, and Rato-lorum too; and a gentleman born, master parson, who signs himself Armigero on any bill, warrant, receipt, or contract — Armigero.
[1.5] SHALLOW. Yes, that I do; and have done for these three hundred years.
[1.6] SLENDER. All his ancestors before him have done it, and all his descendants after him may do it too: they may bear the dozen white pike-fish on their coat of arms.
[1.7] SHALLOW. It is an old coat.
[1.8] EVANS. The dozen white louses go well with an old coat; it agrees well, passant; it is a beast familiar to man, and signifies love.
[1.9] SHALLOW. The luce is the freshwater fish; the salt fish is on an old coat of arms.
[1.10] SLENDER. I may quarter the arms, cousin.
[1.11] SHALLOW. You may, by marrying.
[1.12] EVANS. It is indeed marring, if he quarters it.
[1.13] SHALLOW. Not a bit.
[1.14] EVANS. Yes, by our Lady; if he takes a quarter of your coat, there is only three skirts left for yourself, by my simple reckoning. But that is all one. If Sir John Falstaff has done you wrongs, I belong to the church, and shall be glad to do my benevolence to make atonement and compromises between you.
[1.15] SHALLOW. The council shall hear of it; it is a riot.
[1.16] EVANS. It is not fitting for the council to hear about a riot; there is no fear of God in a riot. The council, you see, shall desire to hear of the fear of God, and not to hear of a riot. Take that into your considerations.
[1.17] SHALLOW. Ha! Upon my life, if I were young again, the sword would settle it.
[1.18] EVANS. It is better that friends are the sword, and settle it that way. And there is also another idea in my brain, which perhaps brings good discretions with it: there is Anne Page, who is daughter to Master Thomas Page, and she is pretty virginity.
[1.19] SLENDER. Mistress Anne Page? She has brown hair, and speaks softly like a woman.
[1.20] EVANS. It is exactly that person, in all the world, just as you could wish; and seven hundred pounds of money, and gold and silver, is what her grandfather on his deathbed (God grant him a joyful resurrection!) gave her, to be received when she is able to reach seventeen years old. It would be a good idea if we set aside our quibbles and quarrels, and arrange a marriage between Master Abraham and Mistress Anne Page.
[1.21] SLENDER. Did her grandfather leave her seven hundred pounds?
[1.22] EVANS. Yes, and her father will make her a better penny.
[1.23] SLENDER. I know the young gentlewoman; she has good qualities.
[1.24] EVANS. Seven hundred pounds and possibilities are good qualities.
[1.25] SHALLOW. Well, let us go see honest Master Page. Is Falstaff there?
[1.26] EVANS. Shall I tell you a lie? I despise a liar as I despise one who is false, or as I despise one who is not true. The knight, Sir John, is there; and I beg you, be guided by your well-wishers. I will knock on the door for Master Page.
[1.27] [Knocks] What ho! God bless your house here!
[1.28] PAGE. [Within] Who's there?
[1.29] [Enter PAGE.]
[1.30] EVANS. Here is God's blessing, and your friend, and Justice Shallow; and here is young Master Slender, who perhaps shall tell you another tale, if matters develop to your liking.
[1.31] PAGE. I am glad to see your worships well. I thank you for my venison, Master Shallow.
[1.32] SHALLOW. Master Page, I am glad to see you: much good may it do your good heart! I wished your venison had been better; it was poorly killed. How is good Mistress Page? — and I thank you always with my heart, indeed, with my heart.
[1.33] PAGE. Sir, I thank you.
[1.34] SHALLOW. Sir, I thank you; yes indeed, I do.
[1.35] PAGE. I am glad to see you, good Master Slender.
[1.36] SLENDER. How is your fallow greyhound, sir? I heard he was outrun on the Cotswolds.
[1.37] PAGE. It could not be properly judged, sir.
[1.38] SLENDER. You won't confess it, you won't confess it.
[1.39] SHALLOW. He won't. It's your fault, it's your fault; it's a good dog.
[1.40] PAGE. A mongrel, sir.
[1.41] SHALLOW. Sir, he's a good dog, and a fine dog: can more be said? He is good and fine. Is Sir John Falstaff here?
[1.42] PAGE. Sir, he is inside; and I wish I could do a good service between you.
[1.43] EVANS. It is spoken as a Christian ought to speak.
[1.44] SHALLOW. He has wronged me, Master Page.
[1.45] PAGE. Sir, in some way he confesses it.
[1.46] SHALLOW. If it is confessed, that doesn't make it right. Isn't that so, Master Page? He has wronged me; indeed he has; in a word, he has, believe me: Robert Shallow, Esquire, says he is wronged.
[1.47] PAGE. Here comes Sir John.
[1.48] [Enter SIR JOHN FALSTAFF, BARDOLPH, NYM, and PISTOL.]
[1.49] FALSTAFF. Now, Master Shallow, you'll complain to the king about me?
[1.50] SHALLOW. Sir Knight, you have beaten my men, killed my deer, and broken into my lodge.
[1.51] FALSTAFF. But not kissed your gamekeeper's daughter?
[1.52] SHALLOW. Pah, a trifle! This shall be answered for.
[1.53] FALSTAFF. I'll answer it right now: I have done all this. There — it's answered.
[1.54] SHALLOW. The council shall know about this.
[1.55] FALSTAFF. It would be better for you if it were kept in confidence: you'll be laughed at.
[1.56] EVANS. Pauca verba, Sir John; good words.
[1.57] FALSTAFF. Good worts! Good cabbage. Slender, I broke your head: what charge do you have against me?
[1.58] SLENDER. By the Virgin, sir, I have a charge in my head against you; and against your cony-catching rascals, Bardolph, Nym, and Pistol.
[1.59] BARDOLPH. You Banbury cheese!
[1.60] SLENDER. Yes, it doesn't matter.
[1.61] PISTOL. How now, Mephostophilus!
[1.62] SLENDER. Yes, it doesn't matter.
[1.63] NYM. Slice, I say! Pauca, pauca: slice! That's my humour.
[1.64] SLENDER. Where's Simple, my servant? Can you tell me, cousin?
[1.65] EVANS. Peace, I pray you. Now let us understand. There are three umpires in this matter, as I understand it: that is, Master Page, videlicet Master Page; and there is myself, videlicet myself; and the third party is, lastly and finally, mine host of the Garter.
[1.66] PAGE. We three, to hear it and end it between them.
[1.67] EVANS. Very good: I will make a brief of it in my notebook; and we will afterwards work upon the case with as great discretion as we can.
[1.68] FALSTAFF. Pistol!
[1.69] PISTOL. He hears with ears.
[1.70] EVANS. The devil and his dam! What sort of phrase is this, 'He hears with ear'? Why, it is affectation.
[1.71] FALSTAFF. Pistol, did you pick Master Slender's purse?
[1.72] SLENDER. Yes, by these gloves he did, or I wish I might never come into my own great chamber again — of seven groats in milled sixpences, and two Edward shovel-board coins, which cost me two shillings and twopence apiece from Yead Miller, by these gloves.
[1.73] FALSTAFF. Is this true, Pistol?
[1.74] EVANS. No; it is false, if it is a case of pickpocketing.
[1.75] PISTOL. Ha, you mountain foreigner! Sir John and my master, I challenge this latten-bilbo to combat. Take back the lie that's on your lips here! Take it back, you froth and scum, you lie!
[1.76] SLENDER. By these gloves, then, it was he.
[1.77] NYM. Be advised, sir, and keep good humours: I will say 'marry trap' to you, if you run the constable's humour on me; that is the very note of it.
[1.78] SLENDER. By this hat, then, the one with the red face had it; for though I can't remember what I did when you made me drunk, yet I am not altogether an ass.
[1.79] FALSTAFF. What do you say, Scarlet and John?
[1.80] BARDOLPH. Why, sir, for my part, I say the gentleman had drunk himself out of his five sentences.
[1.81] EVANS. It is his five senses: fie, what ignorance!
[1.82] BARDOLPH. And being fap, sir, was, as they say, cashiered; and so conclusions passed the careires.
[1.83] SLENDER. Yes, you spoke in Latin then too; but it doesn't matter: I'll never be drunk while I live again, except in honest, civil, godly company, after this trick. If I am drunk, I'll be drunk with those who have the fear of God, and not with drunken knaves.
[1.84] EVANS. So God judge me, that is a virtuous mind.
[1.85] FALSTAFF. You hear all these matters denied, gentlemen; you hear it.
[1.86] [Enter ANNE PAGE, with wine; MISTRESS FORD and MISTRESS PAGE, following.]
[1.87] PAGE. No, daughter, carry the wine in; we'll drink inside. [Exit Anne Page.]
[1.88] SLENDER. O heaven! This is Mistress Anne Page.
[1.89] PAGE. How now, Mistress Ford!
[1.90] FALSTAFF. Mistress Ford, by my faith, it's a fine thing to see you: with your permission, good mistress. [Kisses her.]
[1.91] PAGE. Wife, make these gentlemen welcome. Come, we have a hot venison pasty for dinner: come, gentlemen, I hope we shall drink down all unkindness.
[1.92] [Exeunt all except SHALLOW, SLENDER, and EVANS.]
[1.93] SLENDER. I'd give forty shillings to have my Book of Songs and Sonnets here.
[1.94] [Enter SIMPLE.]
[1.95] How now, Simple! Where have you been? Must I wait on myself? You don't have the Book of Riddles on you, do you?
[1.96] SIMPLE. Book of Riddles! Why, didn't you lend it to Alice Shortcake on All-Hallowmas last, a fortnight before Michaelmas?
[1.97] SHALLOW. Come, cousin; come, cousin; we're waiting for you. A word with you, cousin: indeed, this, cousin: there is, as it were, a tender, a kind of offer, made indirectly by Sir Hugh here. Do you understand me?
[1.98] SLENDER. Yes, sir, you'll find me reasonable; if it is so, I shall do what is reasonable.
[1.99] SHALLOW. No, but understand me.
[1.100] SLENDER. I do, sir.
[1.101] EVANS. Pay attention to his propositions, Master Slender: I will describe the matter to you, if you have the capacity for it.
[1.102] SLENDER. No, I will do as my cousin Shallow says: I beg you, pardon me; he's a justice of the peace in his county, simple though I stand here.
[1.103] EVANS. But that is not the question: the question concerns your marriage.
[1.104] SHALLOW. Yes, there's the point, sir.
[1.105] EVANS. By the Virgin, that is it; the very point of it; to Mistress Anne Page.
[1.106] SLENDER. Why, if that's so, I will marry her on any reasonable terms.
[1.107] EVANS. But can you have affection for the woman? Let us be allowed to know that from your mouth or from your lips; for various philosophers hold that the lips are part of the mouth. Therefore, precisely, can you direct your good will toward the maid?
[1.108] SHALLOW. Cousin Abraham Slender, can you love her?
[1.109] SLENDER. I hope, sir, I will do as becomes one who would act reasonably.
[1.110] EVANS. No, by God's lords and his ladies! You must speak positively, if you can carry your desires toward her.
[1.111] SHALLOW. That you must. Will you, upon a good dowry, marry her?
[1.112] SLENDER. I will do a greater thing than that, upon your request, cousin, in any reason.
[1.113] SHALLOW. No, understand me, understand me, sweet cousin: what I am doing is for your pleasure, cousin. Can you love the maid?
[1.114] SLENDER. I will marry her, sir, at your request: but if there is no great love in the beginning, heaven may decrease it upon better acquaintance, when we are married and have more occasion to know one another; I hope, upon familiarity will grow more contempt: but if you say 'Marry her,' I will marry her; on that point I am freely settled, and dissolutely.
[1.115] EVANS. It is a very discreet answer; except that the fault is in the word 'dissolutely': the word, according to our meaning, is 'resolutely': his meaning is good.
[1.116] SHALLOW. Yes, I think my cousin meant well.
[1.117] SLENDER. Yes, or else I wish I might be hanged, indeed!
[1.118] SHALLOW. Here comes fair Mistress Anne.
[1.119] [Re-enter ANNE PAGE.]
[1.120] I wish I were young for your sake, Mistress Anne!
[1.121] ANNE. Dinner is on the table; my father requests your worships' company.
[1.122] SHALLOW. I will attend him, fair Mistress Anne.
[1.123] EVANS. By God's blessed will! I will not be absent at the grace. [Exeunt Shallow and Evans.]
[1.124] ANNE. Will it please your worship to come in, sir?
[1.125] SLENDER. No, I thank you, truly, with all my heart; I am very well.
[1.126] ANNE. The dinner is waiting for you, sir.
[1.127] SLENDER. I'm not hungry, I thank you, truly. Go, sirrah — even though you are my man, go wait on my cousin Shallow. [Exit Simple.] A justice of the peace may sometimes be obliged to his friend for a servant. I keep only three men and a boy at the moment, until my mother is dead: but what of that? I still live like a gentleman born to poverty.
[1.128] ANNE. I may not go in without your worship: they won't sit down until you come.
[1.129] SLENDER. In faith, I'll eat nothing; I thank you as much as if I did.
[1.130] ANNE. I pray you, sir, walk in.
[1.131] SLENDER. I'd rather walk out here, I thank you. I bruised my shin the other day playing at sword and dagger with a fencing master; three bouts for a dish of stewed prunes; and, by my faith, I cannot stand the smell of hot meat since. Why are your dogs barking so? Are there bears in the town?
[1.132] ANNE. I think there are, sir; I've heard them talked of.
[1.133] SLENDER. I love the sport well; but I shall quarrel about it as readily as any man in England. You are afraid, if you see the bear loose, are you not?
[1.134] ANNE. Yes, indeed, sir.
[1.135] SLENDER. That's meat and drink to me, now. I have seen Sackerson loose twenty times, and have taken him by the chain; but, I warrant you, the women have cried and shrieked so at it, that it was beyond words: but women, indeed, cannot stand them; they are very ill-favoured, rough things.
[1.136] [Re-enter PAGE.]
[1.137] PAGE. Come, gentle Master Slender, come; we are waiting for you.
[1.138] SLENDER. I'll eat nothing, I thank you, sir.
[1.139] PAGE. By cock and pie, you shall not refuse, sir! Come, come.
[1.140] SLENDER. No, please, lead the way.
[1.141] PAGE. Come on, sir.
[1.142] SLENDER. Mistress Anne, you shall go first.
[1.143] ANNE. Not I, sir; please, go on.
[1.144] SLENDER. Truly, I will not go first; truly, indeed! I will not do you that wrong.
[1.145] ANNE. I pray you, sir.
[1.146] SLENDER. I'd rather be unmannerly than troublesome. You do yourself wrong, indeed! [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 2: Act 1, Scene 2 — The same ===
[2.0] [Enter SIR HUGH EVANS and SIMPLE.]
[2.1] EVANS. Go on your way, and ask which is the way to Doctor Caius's house: and there lives a certain Mistress Quickly, who acts as his nurse, or his dry nurse, or his cook, or his laundress — his washer, and his wringer.
[2.2] SIMPLE. Yes, sir.
[2.3] EVANS. No, it gets better still. Give her this letter; for she is a woman who is altogether acquainted with Mistress Anne Page: and the letter is to ask and request her to plead your master's wishes to Mistress Anne Page. I pray you, be off: I will finish my dinner; there are pippins and cheese still to come. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 3: Act 1, Scene 3 — A room in the Garter Inn ===
[3.0] [Enter FALSTAFF, HOST, BARDOLPH, NYM, PISTOL, and ROBIN.]
[3.1] FALSTAFF. My host of the Garter!
[3.2] HOST. What says my swashbuckler? Speak like a scholar and wise man.
[3.3] FALSTAFF. Truly, my host, I must dismiss some of my followers.
[3.4] HOST. Get rid of them, bully Hercules; cashier them: let them wag off; trot, trot.
[3.5] FALSTAFF. My lodging costs ten pounds a week.
[3.6] HOST. You are an emperor — Caesar, Kaiser, and Pheezar. I will take on Bardolph; he shall draw drinks, he shall tap kegs: have I spoken well, bully Hector?
[3.7] FALSTAFF. Do so, my good host.
[3.8] HOST. I have spoken; let him follow. [To Bardolph.] Let me see you froth and water down ale: I have given my word; follow. [Exit.]
[3.9] FALSTAFF. Bardolph, follow him. A tapster is a good trade: an old cloak makes a new jerkin; a worn-out servant makes a fresh tapster. Go; farewell.
[3.10] BARDOLPH. It is a life I have desired: I will prosper.
[3.11] PISTOL. O low Hungarian creature! Will you wield the tap?
[3.12] [Exit Bardolph.]
[3.13] NYM. He was conceived in drink: isn't that a clever humour?
[3.14] FALSTAFF. I am glad I am rid of this tinder-box: his thefts were too open; his pilfering was like an unskillful singer — he kept no time.
[3.15] NYM. The good way is to steal at a moment's rest.
[3.16] PISTOL. 'Convey' — that's what the wise call it. 'Steal!' bah! a fig for that phrase!
[3.17] FALSTAFF. Well, sirs, I am almost worn out at the heels.
[3.18] PISTOL. Why then, let chilblains follow.
[3.19] FALSTAFF. There's no remedy for it; I must catch dupes; I must scheme.
[3.20] PISTOL. Young ravens must have food.
[3.21] FALSTAFF. Which of you knows Ford of this town?
[3.22] PISTOL. I know the man: he is of good substance.
[3.23] FALSTAFF. My honest lads, I will tell you what I am up to.
[3.24] PISTOL. Two yards around, and more.
[3.25] FALSTAFF. No jests now, Pistol! Indeed, around the waist I am two yards; but I am now concerning no waste; I am concerned with thrift. Briefly, I mean to make love to Ford's wife: I sense entertainment in her; she discourses, she carves, she gives the look of invitation: I can interpret the meaning of her familiar style; and the toughest expression of her behaviour, to be translated rightly, is, 'I am Sir John Falstaff's.'
[3.26] PISTOL. He has studied her will, and translated her will, out of honesty into English.
[3.27] NYM. The anchor is deep: will that humour pass?
[3.28] FALSTAFF. Now, the report goes that she has full control of her husband's purse: he has a legion of angels (gold coins).
[3.29] PISTOL. As many devils to entertain her; and 'On to her, boy,' say I.
[3.30] NYM. The humour rises; it is good: humour me with the angels.
[3.31] FALSTAFF. I have written here a letter to her: and here another to Page's wife, who only just now gave me good looks too, examined my parts with most judicious glances; sometimes the beam of her gaze gilded my foot, sometimes my portly belly.
[3.32] PISTOL. Then the sun shone on a dunghill.
[3.33] NYM. I thank you for that humour.
[3.34] FALSTAFF. O, she ran her eyes over my exterior with such greedy intention, that the appetite of her eye seemed to scorch me up like a burning-glass! Here's another letter to her: she controls the purse too; she is a region of Guiana, all gold and bounty. I will be a cheater to them both, and they shall be exchequers to me; they shall be my East and West Indies, and I will trade with them both. Go, you carry this letter to Mistress Page; and you this to Mistress Ford: we will prosper, lads, we will prosper.
[3.35] PISTOL. Shall I become Sir Pandarus of Troy, and wear a sword at my side? Then, may Lucifer take all!
[3.36] NYM. I will not run any base humour: here, take the humour-letter: I will keep the behaviour of reputation.
[3.37] FALSTAFF. [To Robin] Hold, boy, bear these letters smartly; sail like my pinnace to these golden shores. Rogues, away, be gone! Vanish like hailstones, go; trudge, plod away on foot; seek shelter, pack off! Falstaff will learn the humour of the age, French thrift, you rogues; myself and my page in livery.
[3.38] [Exeunt Falstaff and Robin.]
[3.39] PISTOL. Let vultures grip your guts! For loaded dice rule, and high-and-low cheats both rich and poor: I'll have sixpence in my pouch when you lack, base Phrygian Turk!
[3.40] NYM. I have plans which are humours of revenge.
[3.41] PISTOL. Will you revenge?
[3.42] NYM. By the sky and her star!
[3.43] PISTOL. With wit or steel?
[3.44] NYM. With both humours, I: I will reveal the humour of this love to Page.
[3.45] PISTOL. And I to Ford shall also unfold how Falstaff, vile knave, will prove his dove, will hold his gold, and defile his soft couch.
[3.46] NYM. My humour shall not cool: I will provoke Page to deal with poison; I will fill him with jealousy, for the outbreak of mine is dangerous: that is my true humour.
[3.47] PISTOL. You are the Mars of malcontents: I second you; march on. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 4: Act 1, Scene 4 — A room in Doctor Caius’s house ===
[4.0] [Enter MISTRESS QUICKLY, SIMPLE, and RUGBY.]
[4.1] QUICKLY. What ho, John Rugby! Please go to the window and see if you can spot my master, Master Doctor Caius, coming. If he does, and he finds anybody in the house, there will be a fierce abuse of God's patience and the King's English here.
[4.2] RUGBY. I'll go watch.
[4.3] QUICKLY. Go; and we'll have a hot spiced drink for it later tonight, I promise, by the last embers of a sea-coal fire. [Exit Rugby.] An honest, willing, kind fellow, as ever a servant set foot in a house; and, I warrant you, no tattletale, no troublemaker. His worst fault is that he is given to prayer; he's a bit obsessive that way — but nobody is without his fault; let that pass. Peter Simple, that's your name, you say?
[4.4] SIMPLE. Yes, for want of a better.
[4.5] QUICKLY. And Master Slender is your master?
[4.6] SIMPLE. Yes, indeed.
[4.7] QUICKLY. Doesn't he wear a great round beard, like a glover's paring-knife?
[4.8] SIMPLE. No, indeed: he has only a little tiny face, with a little yellow beard — a Cain-coloured beard.
[4.9] QUICKLY. A meek-spirited man, isn't he?
[4.10] SIMPLE. Yes, indeed: but he is as tough a man with his fists as anybody you'll find between here and his own head; he has fought with a warrener.
[4.11] QUICKLY. How's that? — Oh, I should remember him: doesn't he hold his head up high, and swagger as he walks?
[4.12] SIMPLE. Yes, indeed, he does.
[4.13] QUICKLY. Well, may heaven send Anne Page no worse fortune! Tell Master Parson Evans I will do what I can for your master: Anne is a good girl, and I wish—
[4.14] [Re-enter RUGBY.]
[4.15] RUGBY. Quick, alas! Here comes my master.
[4.16] QUICKLY. We shall all catch it. Run in here, good young man; go into this closet: he will not stay long.
[4.17] [Shuts SIMPLE in the closet.] What ho, John Rugby! John!
[4.18] What, John, I say! Go, John, go ask after my master; I'm afraid he isn't well, since he hasn't come home.
[4.19] [Singing] And down, down, adown-a, etc.
[4.20] [Enter DOCTOR CAIUS.]
[4.21] CAIUS. What is you sing? I do not like dese toys. Please, go and fetch me in my closet un boitier vert — a box, a green-a box: do you understand what I speak? A green-a box.
[4.22] QUICKLY. Yes, indeed; I'll fetch it for you. [Aside] I am glad he did not go in himself: if he had found the young man, he would have gone raving mad.
[4.23] CAIUS. Fie, fie, fie, fie! Upon my faith, it is very hot. I am going to the court — on a great matter.
[4.24] QUICKLY. Is it this, sir?
[4.25] CAIUS. Yes; put it in my pocket: hurry, quickly. Where is dat knave Rugby?
[4.26] QUICKLY. What ho, John Rugby! John!
[4.27] RUGBY. Here, sir!
[4.28] CAIUS. You are John Rugby, and you are Jack Rugby. Come, take your rapier, and come at my heels to the court.
[4.29] RUGBY. It's ready, sir, here in the porch.
[4.30] CAIUS. By my troth, I tarry too long. — Goodness me! What did I forget! There are some herbs in my closet that I would not for the world leave behind.
[4.31] QUICKLY. Oh dear, he'll find the young man there, and go mad!
[4.32] CAIUS. Oh devil, devil! What is in my closet? Villain! Thief! [Pulling SIMPLE out.] Rugby, my rapier!
[4.33] QUICKLY. Good master, calm yourself.
[4.34] CAIUS. Why should I be calm?
[4.35] QUICKLY. The young man is an honest man.
[4.36] CAIUS. What shall an honest man do in my closet? There is no honest man dat shall come in my closet.
[4.37] QUICKLY. I beg you, don't be so worked up. Hear the truth of it: he came on an errand to me from Parson Hugh.
[4.38] CAIUS. Well.
[4.39] SIMPLE. Yes, indeed; to ask her to—
[4.40] QUICKLY. Hush, please.
[4.41] CAIUS. Hush your own tongue. Speak your tale.
[4.42] SIMPLE. To ask this honest gentlewoman, your maid, to speak a good word to Mistress Anne Page for my master with a view to marriage.
[4.43] QUICKLY. That is all, indeed, truly! But I'll never put my finger in the fire when I don't have to.
[4.44] CAIUS. Sir Hugh sent you? Rugby, bring me some paper. Wait a little while. [Writes.]
[4.45] QUICKLY. [Aside to SIMPLE] I am glad he is so quiet: if he had been thoroughly worked up, you would have heard him going on so loud and so melancholy. But notwithstanding, my man, I'll do your master what good I can: and the very yes and the no of it is, the French doctor, my master — I may call him my master, look you, for I keep his house; and I wash, wring, brew, bake, scour, dress meat and drink, make the beds, and do everything myself—
[4.46] SIMPLE. [Aside to QUICKLY] It's a great burden to come under one person's hand.
[4.47] QUICKLY. [Aside to SIMPLE] Are you aware of that? You'll find it a great burden: and to be up early and down late — but notwithstanding — to tell you in your ear; I would have no word of this — my master himself is in love with Mistress Anne Page: but in spite of that, I know Anne's mind — that's neither here nor there.
[4.48] CAIUS. You jackanapes, give this letter to Sir Hugh; by God, it is a challenge: I will cut his throat in the park; and I will teach a scurvy jackanapes priest to meddle and intrude. You may be gone; it is not good that you tarry here. — By God, I will cut off both his stones; by God, he shall not have a stone to throw at his dog. [Exit SIMPLE.]
[4.49] QUICKLY. Alas, he speaks only for his friend.
[4.50] CAIUS. It is no matter for that: — did you not tell me that I shall have Anne Page for myself? — By God, I will kill the Jack priest; and I have appointed mine host of the Garter to measure our weapons: — By God, I will have Anne Page myself.
[4.51] QUICKLY. Sir, the maid loves you, and all shall be well. We must let folks chatter on: what, the mischief!
[4.52] CAIUS. Rugby, come to the court with me. By God, if I do not have Anne Page, I shall turn your head out of my door. Follow my heels, Rugby. [Exeunt CAIUS and RUGBY.]
[4.53] QUICKLY. You shall have a fool's head of your own. No, I know Anne's mind as well as that: no woman in Windsor knows more of Anne's mind than I do; nor can do more than I do with her, I thank heaven.
[4.54] FENTON. [Within] Who's there inside? Hello!
[4.55] QUICKLY. Who's there, I wonder? Come closer to the house, please.
[4.56] [Enter FENTON.]
[4.57] FENTON. How now, good woman! How do you do?
[4.58] QUICKLY. The better for your good worship asking.
[4.59] FENTON. What news? How does pretty Mistress Anne?
[4.60] QUICKLY. In truth, sir, she is pretty, and honest, and gentle; and one that is your friend, I can tell you that by the way; I praise heaven for it.
[4.61] FENTON. Shall I have any success, do you think? Shall I not lose my suit?
[4.62] QUICKLY. Truly, sir, all is in his hands above: but notwithstanding, Master Fenton, I'll swear on a book that she loves you. Doesn't your worship have a wart above your eye?
[4.63] FENTON. Yes, indeed, I do; what of that?
[4.64] QUICKLY. Well, thereby hangs a tale: — in good faith, she is such another Nan; but, I protest, an honest maid as ever broke bread: — we had an hour's talk about that wart. — I shall never laugh except in that maid's company! — But, indeed, she is too much given to melancholy and brooding: but as for you — well, go to.
[4.65] FENTON. Well, I shall see her today. Here, take this money; let me have your good word on my behalf: if you see her before me, commend me to her.
[4.66] QUICKLY. Will I? Indeed, that I will; and I will tell your worship more of the wart the next time we have a private talk; and of other suitors.
[4.67] FENTON. Well, farewell; I am in great haste now.
[4.68] QUICKLY. Farewell to your worship. [Exit FENTON.] Truly, an honest gentleman: but Anne doesn't love him; for I know Anne's mind as well as anybody. — Confound it! What have I forgotten? [Exit.]

=== Chapter 5: Act 2, Scene 1 — Before Page’s house ===
[5.0] [Enter MISTRESS PAGE, with a letter.]
[5.1] MISTRESS PAGE. What, have I escaped love-letters in the holiday-time of my beauty, only to become a target for them now? Let me see. [Reads:]
[5.2] 'Ask me no reason why I love you; for though Love uses Reason for his physician, he does not admit him as his counsellor. You are not young, no more am I; well then, there's a sympathy: you are merry, so am I; ha, ha! Then there's more sympathy: you love sack, and so do I; could you wish for better sympathy? Let it suffice you, Mistress Page — at the very least, if a soldier's love can suffice — that I love you. I will not say, pity me — that is no soldier's phrase; but I say, love me. By me,
[5.3] Your own true knight, by day or by night, or any kind of light, with all his might for you to fight, JOHN FALSTAFF.'
[5.4] What a Herod of Jewry is this! O wicked, wicked world! A man almost worn to pieces with age — playing the young gallant! What thoughtless behaviour of mine has this Flemish drunkard picked up — in the devil's name! — out of my conversation, that he dares assault me in this fashion? Why, he has not been in my company three times! What shall I say to him? I was sparing of my mirth then: heaven forgive me! Why, I'll submit a bill to parliament for the putting down of men. How shall I be revenged on him? for revenged I will be, as surely as his guts are made of puddings.
[5.5] [Enter MISTRESS FORD.]
[5.6] MISTRESS FORD. Mistress Page! Trust me, I was on my way to your house.
[5.7] MISTRESS PAGE. And trust me, I was coming to you. You look very ill.
[5.8] MISTRESS FORD. No, I'll never believe that; I can show the contrary.
[5.9] MISTRESS PAGE. Faith, but you do, in my opinion.
[5.10] MISTRESS FORD. Well, I do, then; yet I say, I could show you the contrary. O Mistress Page, give me some counsel!
[5.11] MISTRESS PAGE. What's the matter, woman?
[5.12] MISTRESS FORD. O woman, if it weren't for one trifling matter, I could come to such honour!
[5.13] MISTRESS PAGE. Hang the trifle, woman! Take the honour. What is it? — never mind the trifles — what is it?
[5.14] MISTRESS FORD. If I would just go to hell for an eternal moment or so, I could be knighted.
[5.15] MISTRESS PAGE. What? You lie! Sir Alice Ford! These knights will turn out badly; so you shouldn't alter the article of your gentry.
[5.16] MISTRESS FORD. We're wasting daylight: — here, read, read; see how I might be knighted. I shall think the worse of fat men, as long as I have an eye to distinguish men's looks: and yet he would not swear; he praised women's modesty; and gave such orderly and well-mannered reproof to all unseemliness, that I would have sworn his disposition matched the truth of his words; but they hold together no more than the Hundredth Psalm to the tune of 'Green Sleeves.' What tempest, I wonder, threw this whale, with so many tons of oil in his belly, ashore at Windsor? How shall I be revenged on him? I think the best way were to entertain him with hope, until the wicked fire of lust has melted him in his own grease. Did you ever hear the like?
[5.17] MISTRESS PAGE. Letter for letter — except that the names of Page and Ford are different! To your great comfort in this riddle of ill thoughts, here is the twin-brother of your letter: but let yours inherit first; for, I protest, mine never shall. I warrant he has a thousand of these letters, written with blank space for different names — surely more — and these are of the second edition: he will print them, no doubt; for he cares nothing what he puts into the press, when he would put both of us into it. I would rather be a giantess, and lie under Mount Pelion. Well, I will find you twenty lustful turtle-doves before one chaste man.
[5.18] MISTRESS FORD. Why, this is the very same; the very hand, the very words. What does he think of us?
[5.19] MISTRESS PAGE. No, I don't know: it makes me almost ready to quarrel with my own honesty. I'll treat myself like someone I'm not acquainted with; for, surely, unless he knows some strain in me that I don't know myself, he would never have boarded me in such a fury.
[5.20] MISTRESS FORD. 'Boarding,' do you call it? I'll be sure to keep him above deck.
[5.21] MISTRESS PAGE. So will I: if he comes under my hatches, I'll never go to sea again. Let's be revenged on him: let's arrange a meeting with him; give him a show of encouragement in his suit, and lead him on with a fine baited delay, until he has pawned his horses to the host of the Garter.
[5.22] MISTRESS FORD. No, I will agree to act any villainy against him, that may not stain the carefulness of our honesty. O, if only my husband saw this letter! It would give eternal food to his jealousy.
[5.23] MISTRESS PAGE. Why, look where he comes; and my good man too: he is as far from jealousy as I am from giving him cause; and that, I hope, is an immeasurable distance.
[5.24] MISTRESS FORD. You are the happier woman.
[5.25] MISTRESS PAGE. Let's plot together against this greasy knight. Come over here. [They retire.]
[5.26] [Enter FORD with PISTOL, and PAGE with NYM.]
[5.27] FORD. Well, I hope it is not so.
[5.28] PISTOL. Hope is a curtailed dog in some matters: Sir John has designs on your wife.
[5.29] FORD. Why, sir, my wife is not young.
[5.30] PISTOL. He woos both high and low, both rich and poor, both young and old, all together, Ford; he loves the gallimaufry: Ford, consider.
[5.31] FORD. Love my wife!
[5.32] PISTOL. With liver burning hot. Prevent it, or you'll go like Sir Actaeon, with Ringwood at your heels: O, hateful is the name!
[5.33] FORD. What name, sir?
[5.34] PISTOL. The horn, I say. Farewell. Take heed; keep an open eye; for thieves walk by night: take heed, before summer comes, or cuckoo birds sing. Away, Sir Corporal Nym! — Believe him, Page; he speaks sense. [Exit.]
[5.35] FORD. [Aside] I will be patient; I will find this out.
[5.36] NYM. [To PAGE] And this is true; I do not like the humour of lying. He has wronged me in some humours: I should have carried the humoured letter to her; but I have a sword, and it shall bite when I need it. He loves your wife; that's the short and the long of it. My name is Corporal Nym; I speak and I vouch; 'tis true: my name is Nym, and Falstaff loves your wife. Farewell. I do not love the humour of bread and cheese [and there's the humour of it]. Farewell. [Exit.]
[5.37] PAGE. 'The humour of it,' says he! Here's a fellow who frightens English out of its wits.
[5.38] FORD. I will seek out Falstaff.
[5.39] PAGE. I never heard such a drawling, affected rogue.
[5.40] FORD. If I find it to be true: — well.
[5.41] PAGE. I would not believe such a Cataian, even if the priest of the town vouched for him as a true man.
[5.42] FORD. He was a sensible fellow: — well.
[5.43] PAGE. How now, Meg!
[5.44] [MISTRESS PAGE and MISTRESS FORD come forward.]
[5.45] MISTRESS PAGE. Where are you going, George? Listen to me.
[5.46] MISTRESS FORD. How now, sweet Frank! Why are you melancholy?
[5.47] FORD. I melancholy! I am not melancholy. Go home, go.
[5.48] MISTRESS FORD. Faith, you have some odd notions in your head. Now, will you come, Mistress Page?
[5.49] MISTRESS PAGE. I'll come along. You'll come to dinner, George? [Aside to MISTRESS FORD] Look who comes yonder: she shall be our messenger to this paltry knight.
[5.50] MISTRESS FORD. [Aside to MISTRESS PAGE] Trust me, I thought of her: she'll fit the role.
[5.51] [Enter MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[5.52] MISTRESS PAGE. You've come to see my daughter Anne?
[5.53] QUICKLY. Yes, indeed; and, please, how does good Mistress Anne?
[5.54] MISTRESS PAGE. Come in with us and see: we shall have an hour's talk with you.
[5.55] [Exeunt MISTRESS PAGE, MISTRESS FORD, and MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[5.56] PAGE. How now, Master Ford!
[5.57] FORD. You heard what this knave told me, did you not?
[5.58] PAGE. Yes: and you heard what the other told me?
[5.59] FORD. Do you think there is truth in what they said?
[5.60] PAGE. Hang them, knaves! I don't think the knight would attempt it: but those who accuse him of designs on our wives are a pair of his dismissed men; downright rogues, now that they are out of service.
[5.61] FORD. Were they his men?
[5.62] PAGE. Indeed they were.
[5.63] FORD. That makes me like it no better. Does he stay at the Garter?
[5.64] PAGE. Yes, indeed, he does. If he should mean to launch this voyage toward my wife, I would turn her loose on him; and whatever he gets from her beyond sharp words, let it lie on my head.
[5.65] FORD. I do not suspect my wife; but I would be loath to throw them together. A man may be too confident: I would have nothing lie on my head: I cannot be so satisfied.
[5.66] PAGE. Look where my blustering host of the Garter comes: there is either liquor in his head, or money in his purse, when he looks so merry.
[5.67] [Enter HOST.]
[5.68] How now, my host!
[5.69] HOST. How now, swashbuckler! You're a gentleman. Cavaleiro-justice, I say!
[5.70] [Enter SHALLOW.]
[5.71] SHALLOW. I follow, my host, I follow. Good evening, and twenty more, good Master Page! Master Page, will you go with us? We have some sport in hand.
[5.72] HOST. Tell him, cavaleiro-justice; tell him, swashbuckler.
[5.73] SHALLOW. Sir, there is a fight to be fought between Sir Hugh the Welsh priest and Caius the French doctor.
[5.74] FORD. Good host of the Garter, a word with you.
[5.75] [Drawing him aside.]
[5.76] HOST. What do you say, my swashbuckler?
[5.77] SHALLOW. [To PAGE] Will you go with us to watch it? My merry host has had the measuring of their weapons; and, I think, has appointed them to opposite places; for, believe me, I hear the parson is no jester. Hark, I will tell you what our sport shall be. [They converse apart.]
[5.78] HOST. Have you no suit against my knight, my guest-cavalier?
[5.79] FORD. None, I protest: but I'll give you a quart of burnt sack to give me access to him, and tell him my name is Brook; only for a jest.
[5.80] HOST. My hand, swashbuckler; you shall have free coming and going — did I speak well? — and your name shall be Brook. He is a merry knight. Will you go, my heirs?
[5.81] SHALLOW. I'll come along, my host.
[5.82] PAGE. I have heard the Frenchman has good skill with his rapier.
[5.83] SHALLOW. Tut, sir, I could have told you more. In these times you fight by distance, with your passes, stoccadoes, and I know not what else: it's the heart, Master Page; it's here, it's here. I've seen the time when, with my long sword, I would have made four tall fellows like you skip like rats.
[5.84] HOST. Here, boys, here, here! Shall we be off?
[5.85] PAGE. I'll come. I would rather hear them scold than fight. [Exeunt HOST, SHALLOW, and PAGE.]
[5.86] FORD. Though Page is a complacent fool, and stands so firmly on his wife's faithfulness, I cannot put off my suspicion so easily: she was in his company at Page's house; and what they did there, I do not know. Well, I will look further into it: and I have a disguise with which to sound out Falstaff. If I find her honest, I lose nothing for my labour; if she be otherwise, it's labour well spent. [Exit.]

=== Chapter 6: Act 2, Scene 2 — A room in the Garter Inn ===
[6.0] [Enter FALSTAFF and PISTOL.]
[6.1] FALSTAFF. I will not lend you a penny.
[6.2] PISTOL. Why, then the world's my oyster, which I with sword will open.
[6.3] FALSTAFF. Not a penny. I have been content, sir, to let you pawn my good name: I have pestered my good friends for three reprieves for you and your coach-fellow Nym; or else you would have looked through prison bars, like a pair of baboons. I am damned in hell for swearing to my gentlemen friends that you were good soldiers and brave fellows; and when Mistress Bridget lost the handle of her fan, I swore on my honour you didn't have it.
[6.4] PISTOL. Didn't you share? Didn't you get fifteen pence?
[6.5] FALSTAFF. With reason, you rogue, with reason: do you think I'll endanger my soul for free? In a word, hang no more about me; I am no gibbet for you. Go. A short knife and a crowd! — To your manor of Pickt-hatch! Go. You will not carry a letter for me, you rogue! You stand upon your honour! Why, you boundless lowness, it is as much as I can do to keep my honour precise: I, I, I myself sometimes — leaving the fear of God on the left hand, and hiding my honour in my necessity — am forced to shuffle, hedge, and lurch; and yet you, rogue, will conceal your rags, your cat-a-mountain looks, your tavern phrases, and your bold-faced oaths under the shelter of your honour! You won't do it, you!
[6.6] PISTOL. I relent: what more would you want from a man?
[6.7] [Enter ROBIN.]
[6.8] ROBIN. Sir, here's a woman who would speak with you.
[6.9] FALSTAFF. Let her approach.
[6.10] [Enter MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[6.11] QUICKLY. Good morrow to your worship.
[6.12] FALSTAFF. Good morrow, good wife.
[6.13] QUICKLY. Not so, if it please your worship.
[6.14] FALSTAFF. Good maid, then.
[6.15] QUICKLY. I'll swear to it; as my mother was, the first hour I was born.
[6.16] FALSTAFF. I believe the swearer. What do you want with me?
[6.17] QUICKLY. May I grant your worship a word or two?
[6.18] FALSTAFF. Two thousand, fair woman: and I'll grant you the hearing.
[6.19] QUICKLY. There is one Mistress Ford, sir: — please, come a little nearer this way: — I myself dwell with Master Doctor Caius—
[6.20] FALSTAFF. Well, go on: Mistress Ford, you say—
[6.21] QUICKLY. Your worship says very true: — please, your worship, come a little nearer this way.
[6.22] FALSTAFF. I warrant you, nobody hears; — my own people, my own people.
[6.23] QUICKLY. Are they so? God bless them, and make them his servants!
[6.24] FALSTAFF. Well, Mistress Ford; — what of her?
[6.25] QUICKLY. Why, sir, she's a good creature. — Lord, Lord! Your worship is a wanton! Well, heaven forgive you and all of us, I pray!
[6.26] FALSTAFF. Mistress Ford; — come, Mistress Ford—
[6.27] QUICKLY. Indeed, this is the short and the long of it: you have brought her into such a flutter as is wonderful. The best courtier of them all, when the court was at Windsor, could never have brought her to such a flutter. Yet there have been knights, and lords, and gentlemen, with their coaches; I warrant you, coach after coach, letter after letter, gift after gift; smelling so sweetly, all musk, and rustling, I warrant you, in silk and gold; and in such elegant terms; and in such wine and sugar of the best and the fairest, that would have won any woman's heart; and, I warrant you, they could never get an eye-wink from her: I had twenty angels given me this morning myself; but I defy all angels — in any such sort, as they say — except in the way of honesty: and, I warrant you, they could never get her so much as to sip on a cup with the proudest of them all: and yet there have been earls, indeed, what's more, pensioners; but, I warrant you, all is one with her.
[6.28] FALSTAFF. But what does she say to me? Be brief, my good she-Mercury.
[6.29] QUICKLY. Indeed, she has received your letter; for which she thanks you a thousand times; and she gives you notice that her husband will be away from his house between ten and eleven.
[6.30] FALSTAFF. Ten and eleven.
[6.31] QUICKLY. Yes, indeed; and then you may come and see the picture, she says, that you know about: Master Ford, her husband, will be away from home. Alas, the sweet woman leads an ill life with him! He's a very jealous man: she leads a very vexed life with him, good heart.
[6.32] FALSTAFF. Ten and eleven. Woman, commend me to her; I will not fail her.
[6.33] QUICKLY. Why, you say well. But I have another message for your worship. Mistress Page sends her hearty commendations to you, too: and let me tell you in your ear, she's as virtuous a civil modest wife — and one, I tell you, who will not miss morning or evening prayer — as any woman in Windsor, whoever the other may be: and she bade me tell your worship that her husband is seldom away from home; but, she hopes, the time will come. I never knew a woman so dote upon a man: surely, I think you have charms; yes, in truth.
[6.34] FALSTAFF. Not I, I assure you: setting aside the attraction of my good parts, I have no other charms.
[6.35] QUICKLY. Blessings on your heart for that!
[6.36] FALSTAFF. But please, tell me this: has Ford's wife and Page's wife let each other know how much they love me?
[6.37] QUICKLY. That would be a jest indeed! They have not so little grace as that, I hope: that would be a trick indeed! But Mistress Page asks you most lovingly to send her your little page: her husband has a marvellous fondness for the little page; and, truly, Master Page is an honest man. Never a wife in Windsor leads a better life than she does: do what she will, say what she will, take all, pay all, go to bed when she likes, rise when she likes, all is as she wishes: and, truly, she deserves it; for if there is a kind woman in Windsor, she is one. You must send her your page; no help for it.
[6.38] FALSTAFF. Why, I will.
[6.39] QUICKLY. No, but do so, then: and, look you, he may come and go between you both; and, in any case, have a code-word, so that you may know each other's mind, and the boy need never understand anything; for it is not good that children should know any wickedness: old folks, you know, have discretion, as they say, and know the world.
[6.40] FALSTAFF. Farewell: commend me to them both: there's my purse; I am still your debtor. Boy, go along with this woman. [Exeunt MISTRESS QUICKLY and ROBIN.] This news distracts me!
[6.41] PISTOL. This wench is one of Cupid's carriers: clap on more sails; pursue; up with your fights: give fire: she is my prize, or let the ocean overwhelm them all! [Exit.]
[6.42] FALSTAFF. Do you say so, old Jack? Go your ways; I'll make more of your old body than I've done so far. Will they still look after you? Will you, after the expense of so much money, now be a gainer? Good body, I thank you. Let them say it is grossly done; so long as it is fairly done, no matter.
[6.43] [Enter BARDOLPH.]
[6.44] BARDOLPH. Sir John, there is one Master Brook below who would gladly speak with you, and be acquainted with you; and he has sent your worship a morning draught of sack.
[6.45] FALSTAFF. Brook is his name?
[6.46] BARDOLPH. Yes, sir.
[6.47] FALSTAFF. Call him in. [Exit BARDOLPH.] Such Brooks are welcome to me, that overflow with such liquor. Aha! Mistress Ford and Mistress Page — have I cornered you? Go to; on we go!
[6.48] [Re-enter BARDOLPH, with FORD disguised.]
[6.49] FORD. Bless you, sir!
[6.50] FALSTAFF. And you, sir! Would you speak with me?
[6.51] FORD. I take the liberty of intruding on you with so little preparation.
[6.52] FALSTAFF. You're welcome. What's your wish? — Leave us, drawer. [Exit BARDOLPH.]
[6.53] FORD. Sir, I am a gentleman who has spent much; my name is Brook.
[6.54] FALSTAFF. Good Master Brook, I desire further acquaintance with you.
[6.55] FORD. Good Sir John, I sue for yours: not to be a charge on you; for I must let you understand that I think myself in better condition as a lender than you are: which has somewhat emboldened me to this untimely intrusion; for they say, if money goes before, all ways lie open.
[6.56] FALSTAFF. Money is a good soldier, sir, and will go on.
[6.57] FORD. Truly, and I have a bag of money here that troubles me: if you will help to bear it, Sir John, take all, or half, to ease me of the carriage.
[6.58] FALSTAFF. Sir, I do not know how I may deserve to be your porter.
[6.59] FORD. I will tell you, sir, if you will give me the hearing.
[6.60] FALSTAFF. Speak, good Master Brook: I shall be glad to be your servant.
[6.61] FORD. Sir, I hear you are a scholar — I will be brief with you — and you have been a man long known to me, though I never had so good means, as I had desire, to make myself acquainted with you. I shall reveal something to you, in which I must very much lay open my own imperfection: but, good Sir John, as you keep one eye upon my follies as you hear them unfolded, turn another to the register of your own; so that I may pass with a reproof more easily, since you yourself know how easy it is to be such an offender.
[6.62] FALSTAFF. Very well, sir; proceed.
[6.63] FORD. There is a gentlewoman in this town; her husband's name is Ford.
[6.64] FALSTAFF. Well, sir.
[6.65] FORD. I have long loved her, and, I protest to you, bestowed much on her; followed her with a doting attentiveness; engrossed every opportunity to meet her; paid for every slight occasion that could but stingily give me a glimpse of her; not only bought many presents to give her, but have paid handsomely to many to know what she would have given; briefly, I have pursued her as love has pursued me; which has been on the wing of every opportunity. But whatever I have merited, either in my mind or in my means, in reward, I am sure, I have received none; unless experience be a jewel that I have purchased at an infinite rate, and which has taught me to say this: 'Love like a shadow flies when substance love pursues; pursuing that which flies, and flying what pursues.'
[6.66] FALSTAFF. Have you received no promise of satisfaction at her hands?
[6.67] FORD. Never.
[6.68] FALSTAFF. Have you pressed her to that purpose?
[6.69] FORD. Never.
[6.70] FALSTAFF. Of what quality was your love, then?
[6.71] FORD. Like a fair house built on another man's ground; so that I have lost my edifice by mistaking the place where I erected it.
[6.72] FALSTAFF. To what purpose have you unfolded this to me?
[6.73] FORD. When I have told you that, I have told you everything. Some say, that although she appears honest to me, yet in other places she lets her mirth run so far that there is shrewd interpretation made of her. Now, Sir John, here is the heart of my purpose: you are a gentleman of excellent breeding, admirable discourse, great social access, authentic in your place and person, generally allowed for your many warlike, courtly, and learned accomplishments.
[6.74] FALSTAFF. O, sir!
[6.75] FORD. Believe it, for you know it. There is money; spend it, spend it; spend more; spend all I have; only give me so much of your time in exchange for it, as to lay an amorous siege to the honesty of this Ford's wife: use your art of wooing; win her to consent to you: if any man can, you can as soon as any.
[6.76] FALSTAFF. Would it suit the vehemence of your affection that I should win what you would enjoy? It seems to me you prescribe for yourself very preposterously.
[6.77] FORD. O, understand my drift. She dwells so securely on the excellence of her honour, that the folly of my soul dares not present itself: she is too bright to be looked upon. Now, if I could come to her with any evidence in my hand, my desires would have grounds and arguments to recommend themselves: I could drive her then from the defence of her purity, her reputation, her marriage-vow, and a thousand other defences of hers, which now are too, too strongly arrayed against me. What do you say to it, Sir John?
[6.78] FALSTAFF. Master Brook, I will first make bold with your money; next, give me your hand; and last, as I am a gentleman, you shall, if you will, enjoy Ford's wife.
[6.79] FORD. O good sir!
[6.80] FALSTAFF. I say you shall.
[6.81] FORD. Want no money, Sir John; you shall want none.
[6.82] FALSTAFF. Want no Mistress Ford, Master Brook; you shall want none. I shall be with her, I may tell you, by her own appointment; even as you came in to me, her go-between parted from me: I say I shall be with her between ten and eleven; for at that time the jealous rascally knave her husband will be out. Come to me tonight; you shall know how I succeed.
[6.83] FORD. I am blessed in your acquaintance. Do you know Ford, sir?
[6.84] FALSTAFF. Hang him, poor cuckoldly knave! I do not know him: — yet I wrong him to call him poor; they say the jealous wittol knave has masses of money; for which his wife seems well-favoured to me. I will use her as the key of the cuckoldly rogue's coffer; and there's my harvest-home.
[6.85] FORD. I would you knew Ford, sir, that you might avoid him, if you saw him.
[6.86] FALSTAFF. Hang him, low-born salt-butter rogue! I will stare him out of his wits; I will overawe him with my cudgel: it shall hang like a meteor over the cuckold's horns. Master Brook, you shall know I will lord it over the peasant, and you shall lie with his wife. — Come to me soon tonight. Ford's a knave, and I will pile up his titles; you, Master Brook, shall know him for knave and cuckold. Come to me soon tonight. [Exit.]
[6.87] FORD. What a damned Epicurean rascal this is! My heart is ready to crack with impatience. Who says this is foolish jealousy? My wife has sent to him; the hour is fixed; the match is made. Would any man have thought this? See the hell of having a false woman! My bed shall be abused, my coffers ransacked, my reputation gnawed at; and I shall not only receive this villainous wrong, but stand under the burden of abominable names, and by the man who does me this wrong. Names! Names! — Amaimon sounds well; Lucifer, well; Barbason, well; yet they are devils' titles, the names of fiends: but Cuckold! Wittol! — Cuckold! The devil himself has no such name. Page is an ass, a complacent ass: he will trust his wife; he will not be jealous. I would sooner trust a Fleming with my butter, Parson Hugh the Welshman with my cheese, an Irishman with my aqua-vitae bottle, or a thief to walk my ambling gelding, than my wife with herself: then she plots, then she ruminates, then she devises; and what they think in their hearts they may bring about, they will break their hearts but they will bring it about. God be praised for my jealousy! — Eleven o'clock the hour. I will prevent this, detect my wife, be revenged on Falstaff, and laugh at Page. I'll go about it; better three hours too soon than a minute too late. Fie, fie, fie! Cuckold! Cuckold! Cuckold! [Exit.]

[7.0] [Enter CAIUS and RUGBY.]

[7.31] CAIUS. Clapper-de-claw! What is that?

[7.34] HOST. And I will provoke him to it, or let him be off.
[7.35] CAIUS. Me thank you for that.

[7.40] PAGE, SHALLOW, and SLENDER. Farewell, good master doctor.

[7.47] HOST. Let us be off, then.

[10.58] FALSTAFF. I love you. — Help me away. — Let me creep in here. — I'll never—
[10.59] [Gets into the basket; they cover him with foul linen.]
[10.60] MISTRESS PAGE. Help to cover your master, boy. — Call your men, Mistress Ford. — You dissembling knight!
[10.61] MISTRESS FORD. What, John! Robert! John! [Exit ROBIN.]
[10.62] [Re-enter SERVANTS.]
[10.63] Go quickly and take up these clothes here. — Where's the cowl-staff? Look how you dawdle! — Carry them to the laundress in Datchet-mead; quickly, come.
[10.64] [Enter FORD, PAGE, CAIUS, and SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[10.65] FORD. Please, come close: if I suspect without cause, why then make sport of me; then let me be your jest; I deserve it. — How now! Where are you carrying this?
[10.66] SERVANT. To the laundress, indeed.
[10.67] MISTRESS FORD. Why, what business is it of yours where they take it? You'd best meddle with buck-washing.
[10.68] FORD. Buck! — I wish I could wash myself of the buck! — Buck, buck, buck! Yes, buck; I warrant you, buck; and in season too, it shall appear.
[10.69] [Exeunt SERVANTS with the basket.]
[10.70] Gentlemen, I have dreamed tonight; I'll tell you my dream. Here, here, here are my keys: go up to my chambers; search, seek, find out: I warrant we shall unkennel the fox. Let me block this way first. [Locking the door.] So, now uncouple the hounds.
[10.71] PAGE. Good Master Ford, be content: you wrong yourself too much.
[10.72] FORD. True, Master Page. Up, gentlemen; you shall see sport shortly: follow me, gentlemen. [Exit.]
[10.73] EVANS. This is very fantastical humours and jealousies.
[10.74] CAIUS. By God, it is not the fashion of France; one is not jealous in France.
[10.75] PAGE. No, follow him, gentlemen; see the result of his search. [Exeunt PAGE, CAIUS, and EVANS.]
[10.76] MISTRESS PAGE. Is there not a double excellence in this?
[10.77] MISTRESS FORD. I do not know which pleases me more, that my husband is deceived, or Sir John.
[10.78] MISTRESS PAGE. What a state he was in when your husband asked who was in the basket!
[10.79] MISTRESS FORD. I am half afraid he will need washing; so throwing him into the water will do him a favour.
[10.80] MISTRESS PAGE. Hang him, dishonest rascal! I wish all of the same strain were in the same distress.
[10.81] MISTRESS FORD. I think my husband has some special suspicion of Falstaff's being here; for I never saw him so gross in his jealousy until now.
[10.82] MISTRESS PAGE. I will devise a plot to test that; and we shall yet have more tricks with Falstaff: his dissolute disease will hardly obey this single medicine.
[10.83] MISTRESS FORD. Shall we send that foolish carrion, Mistress Quickly, to him, and excuse his being thrown in the water; and give him another hope, to lead him into another punishment?
[10.84] MISTRESS PAGE. We will do it: let him be sent for tomorrow, eight o'clock, to have amends.
[10.85] [Re-enter FORD, PAGE, CAIUS, and SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[10.86] FORD. I cannot find him: perhaps the knave bragged of what he could not achieve.
[10.87] MISTRESS PAGE. [Aside to MISTRESS FORD] Did you hear that?
[10.88] MISTRESS FORD. You treat me well, Master Ford, do you?
[10.89] FORD. Yes, I do.
[10.90] MISTRESS FORD. Heaven make you better than your thoughts!
[10.91] FORD. Amen!
[10.92] MISTRESS PAGE. You do yourself great wrong, Master Ford.
[10.93] FORD. Yes, yes; I must bear it.
[10.94] EVANS. If there be any body in the house, and in the chambers, and in the coffers, and in the presses, heaven forgive my sins at the day of judgement!
[10.95] CAIUS. By God, nor I either: there are no bodies.
[10.96] PAGE. Fie, fie, Master Ford! Are you not ashamed? What spirit, what devil suggests this imagination? I would not have your kind of derangement for all the wealth of Windsor Castle.
[10.97] FORD. It is my fault, Master Page: I suffer for it.
[10.98] EVANS. You suffer for a bad conscience: your wife is as honest a woman as I would desire among five thousand, and five hundred too.
[10.99] CAIUS. By God, I see she is an honest woman.
[10.100] FORD. Well, I promised you a dinner. — Come, come, let's walk in the Park: I beg you, pardon me; I will hereafter let you know why I have done this. — Come, wife; come, Mistress Page. — I beg you, pardon me; please, heartily pardon me.
[10.101] PAGE. Let's go in, gentlemen; but, trust me, we shall mock him. I invite you tomorrow morning to my house for breakfast: after which, we'll go bird-hunting together; I have a fine hawk for the bush. Shall it be so?
[10.102] FORD. Anything.
[10.103] EVANS. If there is one, I shall make two in the company.
[10.104] CAIUS. If there be one or two, I shall make-a the third.
[10.105] FORD. Please, go on, Master Page.
[10.106] EVANS. I pray you now, remember tomorrow about the lousy knave, my host.
[10.107] CAIUS. That is good; by God, with all my heart!
[10.108] EVANS. A lousy knave, to make his gibes and his mockeries! [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 11: Act 3, Scene 4 — A room in Page’s house ===
[11.0] [Enter FENTON and ANNE PAGE.]
[11.1] FENTON. I see I cannot win your father's love; therefore no more direct me to him, sweet Nan.
[11.2] ANNE. Alas, what then?
[11.3] FENTON. Why, you must be yourself. He objects that I am too high of birth; and that, my finances being strained by my spending, I seek to mend it only by his wealth: besides these, other bars he places before me — my past riots, my wild company; and tells me it's a thing impossible I should love you except as a property.
[11.4] ANNE. Maybe he tells you the truth.
[11.5] FENTON. No, heaven so prosper me in my time to come! Although I will confess your father's wealth was the first motive that I wooed you, Anne: yet, in wooing you, I found you of more value than stamped gold or sums in sealed bags; and it is the very riches of yourself that now I aim at.
[11.6] ANNE. Gentle Master Fenton, yet seek my father's love; still seek it, sir: if opportunity and humblest suit cannot attain it, why, then — listen here!
[11.7] [They converse apart.]
[11.8] [Enter SHALLOW, SLENDER, and MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[11.9] SHALLOW. Break up their talk, Mistress Quickly: my kinsman shall speak for himself.
[11.10] SLENDER. I'll take a shot at it: by God's nail, it's only venturing.
[11.11] SHALLOW. Don't be dismayed.
[11.12] SLENDER. No, she shall not dismay me: I don't mind that, but I am afraid.
[11.13] QUICKLY. Listen; Master Slender would speak a word with you.
[11.14] ANNE. I'll come to him. [Aside] This is my father's choice. O, what a world of vile ill-favoured faults looks handsome in three hundred pounds a year!
[11.15] QUICKLY. And how does good Master Fenton? Please, a word with you.
[11.16] SHALLOW. She's coming; to her, cousin. O boy, you had a father!
[11.17] SLENDER. I had a father, Mistress Anne; — my uncle can tell you good jests about him. Please, uncle, tell Mistress Anne the jest, how my father stole two geese out of a pen, good uncle.
[11.18] SHALLOW. Mistress Anne, my cousin loves you.
[11.19] SLENDER. Yes, that I do; as well as I love any woman in Gloucestershire.
[11.20] SHALLOW. He will keep you like a gentlewoman.
[11.21] SLENDER. Yes, that I will, come whatever may, as long as it's not above the degree of a squire.
[11.22] SHALLOW. He will settle a hundred and fifty pounds jointure on you.
[11.23] ANNE. Good Master Shallow, let him woo for himself.
[11.24] SHALLOW. Indeed, I thank you for it; I thank you for that good comfort. She calls for you, cousin: I'll leave you.
[11.25] ANNE. Now, Master Slender—
[11.26] SLENDER. Now, good Mistress Anne—
[11.27] ANNE. What is your will?
[11.28] SLENDER. My will! By God's little heart, that's a pretty jest indeed! I never made my will yet, I thank heaven; I am not such a sickly creature, I give heaven praise.
[11.29] ANNE. I mean, Master Slender, what would you want with me?
[11.30] SLENDER. Truly, for my own part, I would have little or nothing with you. Your father and my uncle have made proposals: if it be my luck, so; if not, may the happy man have the prize! They can tell you how things go better than I can: you may ask your father; here he comes.
[11.31] [Enter PAGE and MISTRESS PAGE.]
[11.32] PAGE. Now, Master Slender: love him, daughter Anne. — Why, how now! What is Master Fenton doing here? You wrong me, sir, to keep haunting my house like this: I told you, sir, my daughter is spoken for.
[11.33] FENTON. No, Master Page, do not be impatient.
[11.34] MISTRESS PAGE. Good Master Fenton, do not come to my child.
[11.35] PAGE. She is no match for you.
[11.36] FENTON. Sir, will you hear me?
[11.37] PAGE. No, good Master Fenton. Come, Master Shallow; come, son Slender, inside. Knowing my mind, you wrong me, Master Fenton.
[11.38] [Exeunt PAGE, SHALLOW, and SLENDER.]
[11.39] QUICKLY. Speak to Mistress Page.
[11.40] FENTON. Good Mistress Page, since I love your daughter in such a righteous fashion as I do, of necessity, against all checks, rebukes, and manners, I must advance the colours of my love, and not retreat: let me have your good will.
[11.41] ANNE. Good mother, do not marry me to that fool.
[11.42] MISTRESS PAGE. I don't intend to; I seek you a better husband.
[11.43] QUICKLY. That's my master, master doctor.
[11.44] ANNE. Alas, I had rather be set alive in the earth, and bowled to death with turnips!
[11.45] MISTRESS PAGE. Come, don't trouble yourself. Good Master Fenton, I will be neither your friend nor enemy: I will question my daughter how she loves you, and as I find her, so shall I feel. Till then farewell, sir: she must go in; her father will be angry.
[11.46] FENTON. Farewell, gentle mistress: farewell, Nan.
[11.47] [Exeunt MISTRESS PAGE and ANNE.]
[11.48] QUICKLY. This is my doing now: 'No,' said I, 'will you throw away your child on a fool and a physician? Look at Master Fenton:' this is my doing.
[11.49] FENTON. I thank you; and I beg you, sometime tonight give my sweet Nan this ring: there's something for your pains.
[11.50] QUICKLY. Now heaven send you good fortune! [Exit FENTON.] A kind heart he has: a woman would run through fire and water for such a kind heart. But yet I wish my master had Mistress Anne; or I wish Master Slender had her; or, indeed, I wish Master Fenton had her: I will do what I can for all three; for so I have promised, and I'll be as good as my word; but specially for Master Fenton. Well, I must on another errand to Sir John Falstaff from my two mistresses: what a beast I am to delay it! [Exit.]

=== Chapter 12: Act 3, Scene 5 — A room in the Garter Inn ===
[12.0] [Enter FALSTAFF and BARDOLPH.]
[12.1] FALSTAFF. Bardolph, I say—
[12.2] BARDOLPH. Here, sir.
[12.3] FALSTAFF. Go fetch me a quart of sack; put a toast in it. [Exit BARDOLPH.] Have I lived to be carried in a basket, like a barrow of butcher's offal, and to be thrown into the Thames? Well, if I am served such another trick, I'll have my brains taken out and buttered, and given to a dog for a New Year's gift. The rogues tipped me into the river with as little remorse as they would have drowned a blind bitch's puppies, fifteen in the litter: and you may know by my size that I have a kind of eagerness for sinking; if the bottom were as deep as hell, I should go down. I would have drowned, except that the shore was shelving and shallow — a death that I abhor; for water makes a man swell; and what a thing should I have been when I had been swollen! I should have been a mountain of mummy.
[12.4] [Re-enter BARDOLPH with sack.]
[12.5] BARDOLPH. Here's Mistress Quickly, sir, to speak with you.
[12.6] FALSTAFF. Come, let me pour some sack into the Thames water; for my belly is as cold as if I had swallowed snowballs as pills to cool the kidneys. Call her in.
[12.7] BARDOLPH. Come in, woman!
[12.8] [Enter MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[12.9] QUICKLY. By your leave; I cry your mercy: good morrow to your worship.
[12.10] FALSTAFF. Take away these cups. Go brew me a quart of sack thoroughly.
[12.11] BARDOLPH. With eggs, sir?
[12.12] FALSTAFF. Simple, by itself; I'll have no pullet-sperm in my brewing.
[12.13] [Exit BARDOLPH.] How now!
[12.14] QUICKLY. Indeed, sir, I come to your worship from Mistress Ford.
[12.15] FALSTAFF. Mistress Ford! I have had ford enough; I was thrown into the ford; I have my belly full of ford.
[12.16] QUICKLY. Alas the day! Good heart, that was not her fault: she does carry on with her men so; they mistook their instructions.
[12.17] FALSTAFF. So did I mine, to build upon a foolish woman's promise.
[12.18] QUICKLY. Well, she laments it, sir, so that it would grieve your heart to see it. Her husband is going bird-hunting this morning; she desires you once more to come to her between eight and nine: I must carry her word quickly: she'll make amends, I warrant you.
[12.19] FALSTAFF. Well, I will visit her: tell her so; and bid her think what a man is: let her consider his frailty, and then judge of my merit.
[12.20] QUICKLY. I will tell her.
[12.21] FALSTAFF. Do so. Between nine and ten, you say?
[12.22] QUICKLY. Eight and nine, sir.
[12.23] FALSTAFF. Well, be gone: I will not miss her.
[12.24] QUICKLY. Peace be with you, sir. [Exit.]
[12.25] FALSTAFF. I marvel I hear nothing of Master Brook; he sent me word to stay in: I like his money well. — O, here he comes.
[12.26] [Enter FORD.]
[12.27] FORD. Bless you, sir!
[12.28] FALSTAFF. Now, Master Brook — you come to learn what has passed between me and Ford's wife?
[12.29] FORD. That, indeed, Sir John, is my business.
[12.30] FALSTAFF. Master Brook, I will not lie to you: I was at her house the hour she appointed me.
[12.31] FORD. And did you succeed, sir?
[12.32] FALSTAFF. Very ill-favouredly, Master Brook.
[12.33] FORD. How so, sir? Did she change her mind?
[12.34] FALSTAFF. No, Master Brook; but the peaking cuckold her husband, Master Brook, living in a continual alarm of jealousy, comes in just at the moment of our encounter, after we had embraced, kissed, sworn vows, and, as it were, spoken the prologue of our comedy; and at his heels a rabble of his companions, thither provoked and stirred up by his obsession, and, indeed, to search his house for his wife's love.
[12.35] FORD. What, while you were there?
[12.36] FALSTAFF. While I was there.
[12.37] FORD. And did he search for you, and could not find you?
[12.38] FALSTAFF. You shall hear. As good luck would have it, in comes one Mistress Page; gives news of Ford's approach; and, by her invention and Ford's wife's panic, they conveyed me into a buck-basket.
[12.39] FORD. A buck-basket!
[12.40] FALSTAFF. By the Lord, a buck-basket! — rammed me in with foul shirts and smocks, socks, foul stockings, greasy napkins; so that, Master Brook, there was the rankest compound of villainous smell that ever offended a nostril.
[12.41] FORD. And how long did you lie there?
[12.42] FALSTAFF. No, you shall hear, Master Brook, what I have suffered to bring this woman to evil for your good. Being thus crammed in the basket, a couple of Ford's knaves, his hinds, were called forth by their mistress to carry me in the guise of foul clothes to Datchet-lane: they took me on their shoulders; met the jealous knave their master at the door, who asked them once or twice what they had in the basket: I shook with fear, lest the lunatic knave would have searched it; but fate, ordaining he should be a cuckold, held his hand. Well: on he went for a search, and away went I for foul clothes. But mark what followed, Master Brook: I suffered the pangs of three separate deaths; first, an intolerable fright, to be discovered by a jealous rotten bell-wether; next, to be folded up, like a good bilbo blade, in the circumference of a peck, hilt to point, heel to head; and then, to be stopped in, like a strong distillation, with stinking clothes that fretted in their own grease: think of that — a man of my constitution — think of that — who am as subject to heat as butter; a man in continual melting and thaw: it was a miracle to escape suffocation. And in the height of this bath, when I was more than half stewed in grease, like a Dutch dish, to be thrown into the Thames, and cooled, glowing hot, in that surge, like a horseshoe; think of that — hissing hot — think of that, Master Brook.
[12.43] FORD. In good sadness, sir, I am sorry that for my sake you have suffered all this. My suit, then, is desperate; you'll undertake her no more?
[12.44] FALSTAFF. Master Brook, I will be thrown into Etna, as I have been into the Thames, before I leave her thus. Her husband has gone bird-hunting this morning: I have received from her another appointment of meeting; between eight and nine is the hour, Master Brook.
[12.45] FORD. It is past eight already, sir.
[12.46] FALSTAFF. Is it? I will then prepare myself for my appointment. Come to me at your convenient leisure, and you shall hear how I succeed; and the conclusion shall be crowned with your enjoyment of her. Farewell. You shall have her, Master Brook; Master Brook, you shall cuckold Ford.
[12.47] [Exit.]
[12.48] FORD. Hum! Ha! Is this a vision? Is this a dream? Do I sleep? Master Ford, awake! Awake, Master Ford! There's a hole made in your best coat, Master Ford. This is what it is to be married! This is what it is to have linen and buck-baskets! Well, I will proclaim myself what I am: I will now catch the lecher; he is at my house; he cannot escape me; it is impossible he should; he cannot creep into a halfpenny purse, nor into a pepper-box: but, lest the devil that guides him should aid him, I will search impossible places. Though what I am I cannot avoid, yet to be what I would not, shall not make me tame: if I have horns to drive a man mad, let the proverb go with me — I'll be horn-mad.
[12.49] [Exit.]

=== Chapter 13: Act 4, Scene 1 — A street ===
[13.0] [Enter MISTRESS PAGE, MISTRESS QUICKLY, and WILLIAM.]
[13.1] MISTRESS PAGE. Is he at Master Ford's already, do you think?
[13.2] QUICKLY. Surely he is by now, or will be presently: but, truly, he is very courageously mad about being thrown into the water. Mistress Ford desires you to come right away.
[13.3] MISTRESS PAGE. I'll be with her shortly; I'll just bring my young man here to school. Look, where his master comes; it's a play-day, I see.
[13.4] [Enter SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[13.5] How now, Sir Hugh! No school today?
[13.6] EVANS. No; Master Slender has let the boys have leave to play.
[13.7] QUICKLY. Bless his heart!
[13.8] MISTRESS PAGE. Sir Hugh, my husband says my son makes no progress in the world at his book. Please, ask him some questions in his grammar.
[13.9] EVANS. Come here, William; hold up your head; come.
[13.10] MISTRESS PAGE. Come on, sirrah; hold up your head; answer your master, don't be afraid.
[13.11] EVANS. William, how many numbers are there in nouns?
[13.12] WILLIAM. Two.
[13.13] QUICKLY. Truly, I thought there had been one number more, because they say, 'Od's nouns.'
[13.14] EVANS. Hush your tattling! What is 'fair,' William?
[13.15] WILLIAM. Pulcher.
[13.16] QUICKLY. Polecats! There are fairer things than polecats, surely.
[13.17] EVANS. You are a very simple woman: I pray you, hush. — What is 'lapis,' William?
[13.18] WILLIAM. A stone.
[13.19] EVANS. And what is 'a stone,' William?
[13.20] WILLIAM. A pebble.
[13.21] EVANS. No, it is 'lapis:' I pray you, remember in your brain.
[13.22] WILLIAM. Lapis.
[13.23] EVANS. That is a good William. What is it, William, that lends articles?
[13.24] WILLIAM. Articles are borrowed from the pronoun, and are thus declined, Singulariter, nominativo, hic, haec, hoc.
[13.25] EVANS. Nominativo, hig, hag, hog; please, mark: genitivo, hujus. Well, what is your accusative case?
[13.26] WILLIAM. Accusativo, hinc.
[13.27] EVANS. I pray you, hold your remembrance, child; accusativo, hung, hang, hog.
[13.28] QUICKLY. 'Hang-hog' is Latin for bacon, I warrant you.
[13.29] EVANS. Leave your prattling, woman. — What is the focative case, William?
[13.30] WILLIAM. O — vocativo, O.
[13.31] EVANS. Remember, William; focative is caret.
[13.32] QUICKLY. And that's a good root.
[13.33] EVANS. Woman, forbear.
[13.34] MISTRESS PAGE. Peace!
[13.35] EVANS. What is your genitive case plural, William?
[13.36] WILLIAM. Genitive case!
[13.37] EVANS. Yes.
[13.38] WILLIAM. Genitive — horum, harum, horum.
[13.39] QUICKLY. A pox on Jenny's case! Fie on her! Never name her, child, if she be a whore.
[13.40] EVANS. For shame, woman.
[13.41] QUICKLY. You do ill to teach the child such words: — he teaches him to hick and to hack, which they'll do fast enough by themselves, and to call 'horum' — fie upon you!
[13.42] EVANS. Woman, are you lunatic? Have you no understanding of your cases, and the numbers of the genders? You are as foolish a Christian creature as I would desire.
[13.43] MISTRESS PAGE. Please, hold your peace.
[13.44] EVANS. Show me now, William, some declensions of your pronouns.
[13.45] WILLIAM. Indeed, I have forgot.
[13.46] EVANS. It is qui, quae, quod: if you forget your 'quies,' your 'quaes,' and your 'quods,' you must be flogged. Go your ways, and play; go.
[13.47] MISTRESS PAGE. He is a better scholar than I thought he was.
[13.48] EVANS. He has a good lively memory. Farewell, Mistress Page.
[13.49] MISTRESS PAGE. Farewell, good Sir Hugh. [Exit SIR HUGH.] Get yourself home, boy. Come, we stay too long. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 14: Act 4, Scene 2 — A room in Ford’s house ===
[14.0] [Enter FALSTAFF and MISTRESS FORD.]
[14.1] FALSTAFF. Mistress Ford, your sorrow has consumed my patience. I see you are obliging in your love, and I declare I shall repay you to a hair's breadth; not only, Mistress Ford, in the simple office of love, but in all the accoutrement, compliment, and ceremony of it. But are you sure of your husband now?
[14.2] MISTRESS FORD. He is bird-hunting, sweet Sir John.
[14.3] MISTRESS PAGE. [Within] What ho, gossip Ford! What, ho!
[14.4] MISTRESS FORD. Step into the chamber, Sir John.
[14.5] [Exit FALSTAFF.]
[14.6] [Enter MISTRESS PAGE.]
[14.7] MISTRESS PAGE. How now, sweetheart! Who's at home besides yourself?
[14.8] MISTRESS FORD. Why, none but my own people.
[14.9] MISTRESS PAGE. Indeed!
[14.10] MISTRESS FORD. No, certainly. [Aside to her] Speak louder.
[14.11] MISTRESS PAGE. Truly, I am so glad you have nobody here.
[14.12] MISTRESS FORD. Why?
[14.13] MISTRESS PAGE. Why, woman, your husband is in his old mad fits again: he carries on so over there with my husband; he so rails against all married mankind; he so curses all Eve's daughters, of whatever complexion; and he so buffets himself on the forehead, crying 'Peer out, peer out!' that any madness I ever yet saw seemed but tameness, civility, and patience compared to this rage he is in now: I am glad the fat knight is not here.
[14.14] MISTRESS FORD. Why, does he talk of him?
[14.15] MISTRESS PAGE. Of none but him; and swears he was carried out, the last time he searched for him, in a basket; protests to my husband he is here now; and has drawn him and the rest of their company away from their sport, to make another test of his suspicion: but I am glad the knight is not here; now he shall see his own foolishness.
[14.16] MISTRESS FORD. How near is he, Mistress Page?
[14.17] MISTRESS PAGE. Close by; at the end of the street; he will be here right away.
[14.18] MISTRESS FORD. I am undone! — the knight is here.
[14.19] MISTRESS PAGE. Why, then, you are utterly shamed, and he is but a dead man. What a woman you are! — Away with him, away with him! Better shame than murder.
[14.20] MISTRESS FORD. Which way should he go? How shall I dispose of him? Shall I put him into the basket again?
[14.21] [Re-enter FALSTAFF.]
[14.22] FALSTAFF. No, I'll come no more into the basket. May I not go out before he comes?
[14.23] MISTRESS PAGE. Alas, three of Master Ford's brothers watch the door with pistols, so that nobody shall issue out; otherwise you might slip away before he came. But what are you doing here?
[14.24] FALSTAFF. What shall I do? — I'll creep up into the chimney.
[14.25] MISTRESS FORD. They always use that place to discharge their birding-pieces. Creep into the kiln-hole.
[14.26] FALSTAFF. Where is it?
[14.27] MISTRESS FORD. He will search there, upon my word. Neither press, coffer, chest, trunk, well, vault — but he has an inventory for remembering such places, and goes to them by his list: there is no hiding you in the house.
[14.28] FALSTAFF. I'll go out, then.
[14.29] MISTRESS PAGE. If you go out in your own appearance, you die, Sir John. Unless you go out disguised—
[14.30] MISTRESS FORD. How might we disguise him?
[14.31] MISTRESS PAGE. Alas the day, I don't know! There is no woman's gown big enough for him; otherwise he might put on a hat, a muffler, and a kerchief, and so escape.
[14.32] FALSTAFF. Good hearts, devise something: any extremity rather than disaster.
[14.33] MISTRESS FORD. My maid's aunt, the fat woman of Brentford, has a gown upstairs.
[14.34] MISTRESS PAGE. Upon my word, it will fit him; she is as big as he is: and there's her thrummed hat, and her muffler too. Run up, Sir John.
[14.35] MISTRESS FORD. Go, go, sweet Sir John: Mistress Page and I will look out some linen for your head.
[14.36] MISTRESS PAGE. Quick, quick! We'll come dress you straight away: put on the gown in the meantime. [Exit FALSTAFF.]
[14.37] MISTRESS FORD. I wish my husband would meet him in this shape: he cannot abide the old woman of Brentford; he swears she's a witch; forbade her my house, and has threatened to beat her.
[14.38] MISTRESS PAGE. Heaven guide him to your husband's cudgel, and the devil guide his cudgel afterwards!
[14.39] MISTRESS FORD. But is my husband coming?
[14.40] MISTRESS PAGE. Yes, in good seriousness, he is; and talks of the basket too, however he had information.
[14.41] MISTRESS FORD. We'll test that; for I'll order my men to carry the basket again, to meet him at the door with it, as they did last time.
[14.42] MISTRESS PAGE. No, but he'll be here at once: let's go dress him like the witch of Brentford.
[14.43] MISTRESS FORD. I'll first direct my men what they shall do with the basket. Go up; I'll bring linen for him straight away.
[14.44] [Exit.]
[14.45] MISTRESS PAGE. Hang him, dishonest knave! We cannot abuse him enough. We'll leave a proof, by what we will do, that wives may be merry, and yet honest too: we do not act those things that we often jest and laugh about; it's old, but true — still swine eat all the swill. [Exit.]
[14.46] [Re-enter MISTRESS FORD with two SERVANTS.]
[14.47] MISTRESS FORD. Go, sirs, take the basket again on your shoulders: your master is right at the door; if he orders you to set it down, obey him: quickly, dispatch. [Exit.]
[14.48] FIRST SERVANT. Come, come, lift it up.
[14.49] SECOND SERVANT. Pray heaven it not be full of knight again.
[14.50] FIRST SERVANT. I hope not; I had as soon carry that much lead.
[14.51] [Enter FORD, PAGE, SHALLOW, CAIUS, and SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[14.52] FORD. Yes, but if it proves true, Master Page, have you any way then to undo my foolishness again? Set down the basket, villain! Somebody call my wife. Youth in a basket! — O you panderly rascals! There is a knot, a gang, a pack, a conspiracy against me: now shall the devil be shamed. — What, wife, I say! — Come, come forth! Behold what honest clothes you send out to bleaching!
[14.53] PAGE. Why, this goes too far, Master Ford; you are not to go loose any longer; you must be bound.
[14.54] EVANS. Why, this is lunatic! This is mad as a mad dog!
[14.55] SHALLOW. Indeed, Master Ford, this is not well, indeed.
[14.56] FORD. So say I too, sir.
[14.57] [Re-enter MISTRESS FORD.]
[14.58] Come here, Mistress Ford; Mistress Ford, the honest woman, the modest wife, the virtuous creature, who has the jealous fool for her husband! I suspect without cause, mistress, do I?
[14.59] MISTRESS FORD. Heaven be my witness you do, if you suspect me of any dishonesty.
[14.60] FORD. Well said, brazen-face! Hold it out. Come forth, sirrah! [Pulling clothes out of the basket.]
[14.61] PAGE. This is outrageous!
[14.62] MISTRESS FORD. Are you not ashamed? Leave the clothes alone.
[14.63] FORD. I shall find you shortly.
[14.64] EVANS. It is unreasonable! Will you take up your wife's clothes? Come away.
[14.65] FORD. Empty the basket, I say!
[14.66] MISTRESS FORD. Why, man, why?
[14.67] FORD. Master Page, as I am a man, there was one conveyed out of my house yesterday in this basket: why may he not be there again? In my house I am sure he is: my information is true; my jealousy is reasonable. Pull out all the linen for me.
[14.68] MISTRESS FORD. If you find a man there, he shall die a flea's death.
[14.69] PAGE. Here's no man.
[14.70] SHALLOW. By my faith, this is not well, Master Ford; this wrongs you.
[14.71] EVANS. Master Ford, you must pray, and not follow the imaginations of your own heart: this is jealousy.
[14.72] FORD. Well, he's not here whom I seek.
[14.73] PAGE. No, nor nowhere else but in your brain.
[14.74] FORD. Help to search my house this one time. If I do not find what I seek, give no excuse for my extremity; let me forever be your table-sport; let them say of me, 'As jealous as Ford, who searched a hollow walnut for his wife's lover.' Satisfy me once more; once more search with me.
[14.75] MISTRESS FORD. What, ho, Mistress Page! Come you and the old woman down; my husband will come into the chamber.
[14.76] FORD. Old woman! What old woman is that?
[14.77] MISTRESS FORD. Why, it is my maid's aunt of Brentford.
[14.78] FORD. A witch, a quean, an old cheating quean! Have I not forbidden her my house? She comes on errands, does she? We are simple men; we do not know what is brought to pass under the profession of fortune-telling. She works by charms, by spells, by the figure, and such daubery as this is, beyond our element: we know nothing. Come down, you witch, you hag, you; come down, I say!
[14.79] MISTRESS FORD. No, good, sweet husband! — Good gentlemen, do not let him strike the old woman.
[14.80] [Re-enter FALSTAFF in woman's clothes, and MISTRESS PAGE.]
[14.81] MISTRESS PAGE. Come, Mother Prat; come, give me your hand.
[14.82] FORD. I'll prat her. [Beating him] Out of my door, you witch, you hag, you baggage, you polecat, you nasty creature! Out, out! I'll conjure you, I'll fortune-tell you.
[14.83] [Exit FALSTAFF.]
[14.84] MISTRESS PAGE. Are you not ashamed? I think you have killed the poor woman.
[14.85] MISTRESS FORD. No, he will do it. It is a fine credit for you.
[14.86] FORD. Hang her, witch!
[14.87] EVANS. By yea and no, I think the woman is a witch indeed: I do not like it when a woman has a great beard; I spy a great beard under her muffler.
[14.88] FORD. Will you follow, gentlemen? I beg you, follow; just see the outcome of my jealousy: if I cry out like this on no trail, never trust me when I open my mouth again.
[14.89] PAGE. Let's humour him a little further: come, gentlemen. [Exeunt FORD, PAGE, SHALLOW, CAIUS, and EVANS.]
[14.90] MISTRESS PAGE. Trust me, he beat him most pitifully.
[14.91] MISTRESS FORD. No, by the mass, that he did not; he beat him most unpitifully, I thought.
[14.92] MISTRESS PAGE. I'll have the cudgel hallowed and hung over the altar; it has done meritorious service.
[14.93] MISTRESS FORD. What do you think? May we, with the warrant of womanhood and the witness of a good conscience, pursue him with any further revenge?
[14.94] MISTRESS PAGE. The spirit of wantonness is, surely, scared out of him: unless the devil holds him in fee-simple, with fine and recovery, he will never, I think, attempt to waste us again.
[14.95] MISTRESS FORD. Shall we tell our husbands how we have served him?
[14.96] MISTRESS PAGE. Yes, by all means; if only to scrape the figures out of your husband's brains. If they can find it in their hearts that the poor unvirtuous fat knight shall be any further afflicted, we two will still be the ministers of it.
[14.97] MISTRESS FORD. I warrant they'll have him publicly shamed: and it seems to me there would be no end to the jest unless he be publicly shamed.
[14.98] MISTRESS PAGE. Come, to the forge with it, then; shape it: I would not have things cool. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 16: Act 4, Scene 4 — A room in Ford’s house ===
[16.0] [Enter PAGE, FORD, MISTRESS PAGE, MISTRESS FORD, and SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[16.1] EVANS. This is one of the best instances of discretion in a woman that I have ever looked upon.
[16.2] PAGE. And did he send you both these letters at the same time?
[16.3] MISTRESS PAGE. Within a quarter of an hour.
[16.4] FORD. Pardon me, wife. Henceforth do what you will; I will sooner suspect the sun of cold than you of wantonness: now your honour stands, in him that was lately a heretic, as firm as faith.
[16.5] PAGE. It is well, it is well; no more: do not be as extreme in submission as in offence. But let our plot go forward: let our wives once again, to make us public sport, arrange a meeting with this old fat fellow, where we may catch him, and disgrace him for it.
[16.6] FORD. There is no better way than the one they spoke of.
[16.7] PAGE. How? To send him word they'll meet him in the Park at midnight? Fie, fie! He'll never come.
[16.8] EVANS. You say he has been thrown in the rivers, and has been grievously beaten, as an old woman: it seems to me there must be such terrors in him that he should not come; it seems to me his flesh is punished, he shall have no desires.
[16.9] PAGE. So think I too.
[16.10] MISTRESS FORD. Devise only how you'll use him when he comes, and let us two devise to bring him there.
[16.11] MISTRESS PAGE. There is an old tale that Herne the hunter, sometime a keeper here in Windsor forest, all the winter-time, in the still of midnight, walks round about an oak, with great ragged horns; and there he blasts the tree, and takes the cattle, and makes milch-cows yield blood, and shakes a chain in a most hideous and dreadful manner: you have heard of such a spirit; and well you know the superstitious idle-headed elders received, and passed on to our age, this tale of Herne the hunter for a truth.
[16.12] PAGE. Why, even now there are not a few who fear to walk by this Herne's oak in the deep of night: but what of this?
[16.13] MISTRESS FORD. Indeed, this is our scheme: that Falstaff shall meet us at that oak.
[16.14] PAGE. Well, do not doubt that he will come: and in this shape when you have brought him there, what shall be done with him? What is your plot?
[16.15] MISTRESS PAGE. That too we have thought upon, and thus: Nan Page my daughter and my little son, and three or four more of their size, we'll dress like urchins, ouphes, and fairies, green and white, with rings of waxen tapers on their heads, and rattles in their hands: on a sudden, as Falstaff, she, and I are newly met, let them rush at once from a sawpit with some confused song: upon their sight, we two in great amazement will flee: then let them all encircle him around, and, fairy-like, to-pinch the unclean knight; and ask him why, in that hour of fairy revel, he dares to tread in their sacred paths in profane shape.
[16.16] MISTRESS FORD. And till he tells the truth, let the supposed fairies pinch him soundly, and burn him with their tapers.
[16.17] MISTRESS PAGE. The truth being known, we'll all present ourselves, dis-horn the spirit, and mock him home to Windsor.
[16.18] FORD. The children must be well practised at this, or they'll never do it.
[16.19] EVANS. I will teach the children their parts; and I will be like a jackanapes also, to burn the knight with my taber.
[16.20] FORD. That will be excellent. I'll go and buy them vizards.
[16.21] MISTRESS PAGE. My Nan shall be the queen of all the fairies, finely attired in a robe of white.
[16.22] PAGE. That silk I will go buy. [Aside] And in that time shall Master Slender steal my Nan away, and marry her at Eton. Go send to Falstaff at once.
[16.23] FORD. No, I'll go to him again in the name of Brook: he'll tell me all his purpose: surely, he'll come.
[16.24] MISTRESS PAGE. Don't fear that. Go get us properties and adornment for our fairies.
[16.25] EVANS. Let us get about it: these are admirable pleasures and very honest knaveries. [Exeunt PAGE, FORD, and EVANS.]
[16.26] MISTRESS PAGE. Go, Mistress Ford, send quickly to Sir John, to know his mind.
[16.27] [Exit MISTRESS FORD.]
[16.28] I'll go to the doctor: he has my good will, and none but he, to marry Nan Page. That Slender, though well landed, is an idiot; and he my husband favours most of all. The doctor is well moneyed, and his friends powerful at court: he, none but he, shall have her, though twenty thousand worthier come to crave her. [Exit.]

=== Chapter 17: Act 4, Scene 5 — A room in the Garter Inn ===
[17.0] [Enter HOST and SIMPLE.]
[17.1] HOST. What do you want, boor? What, thick-skin? Speak, breathe, discuss; brief, short, quick, snap.
[17.2] SIMPLE. Indeed, sir, I come to speak with Sir John Falstaff from Master Slender.
[17.3] HOST. There's his chamber, his house, his castle, his standing-bed, and truckle-bed; it's painted around with the story of the Prodigal, fresh and new. Go knock and call; he'll speak like an Anthropophaginian to you: knock, I say.
[17.4] SIMPLE. There's an old woman, a fat woman, gone up into his chamber: I'll be so bold as to wait, sir, till she comes down; I come to speak with her, in fact.
[17.5] HOST. Ha! A fat woman! The knight may be robbed: I'll call. — Bully knight! Bully Sir John! Speak from your military lungs: are you there? It is your host, your Ephesian, who calls.
[17.6] FALSTAFF. [Above] How now, my host!
[17.7] HOST. Here is a Bohemian-Tartar waiting for the coming down of your fat woman. Let her descend, bully, let her descend; my chambers are honourable: fie! Privacy? Fie!
[17.8] [Enter FALSTAFF.]
[17.9] FALSTAFF. There was, my host, an old fat woman with me just now; but she's gone.
[17.10] SIMPLE. Please, sir, was it not the wise woman of Brentford?
[17.11] FALSTAFF. Yes, indeed, it was, muscle-shell: what would you with her?
[17.12] SIMPLE. My master, sir, Master Slender, sent to her, seeing her go through the streets, to know, sir, whether one Nym, sir, who beguiled him of a chain, had the chain or not.
[17.13] FALSTAFF. I spoke with the old woman about it.
[17.14] SIMPLE. And what does she say, I pray, sir?
[17.15] FALSTAFF. Indeed, she says that the very same man that beguiled Master Slender of his chain cheated him of it.
[17.16] SIMPLE. I wish I could have spoken with the woman herself; I had other things to speak with her about too, from him.
[17.17] FALSTAFF. What are they? Let us know.
[17.18] HOST. Yes, come; quick.
[17.19] SIMPLE. I may not conceal them, sir.
[17.20] HOST. Conceal them, or you die.
[17.21] SIMPLE. Why, sir, they were nothing but about Mistress Anne Page; to know whether it was my master's fortune to have her or not.
[17.22] FALSTAFF. It is, it is his fortune.
[17.23] SIMPLE. What, sir?
[17.24] FALSTAFF. To have her, or not. Go; say the woman told me so.
[17.25] SIMPLE. May I be bold to say so, sir?
[17.26] FALSTAFF. Yes, sir; as bold as any.
[17.27] SIMPLE. I thank your worship: I shall make my master glad with this news. [Exit.]
[17.28] HOST. You are scholarly, you are scholarly, Sir John. Was there a wise woman with you?
[17.29] FALSTAFF. Yes, there was, my host; one who has taught me more wit than ever I learned before in my life; and I paid nothing for it either, but was paid for my learning.
[17.30] [Enter BARDOLPH.]
[17.31] BARDOLPH. Out, alas, sir! Cozenage, mere cozenage!
[17.32] HOST. Where are my horses? Speak well of them, varlet.
[17.33] BARDOLPH. Run away with the cozeners: for as soon as I came beyond Eton, they threw me off from behind one of them, into a slough of mire; and set spurs and away, like three German devils, three Doctor Faustuses.
[17.34] HOST. They have gone but to meet the duke, villain: do not say they have fled; Germans are honest men.
[17.35] [Enter SIR HUGH EVANS.]
[17.36] EVANS. Where is my host?
[17.37] HOST. What is the matter, sir?
[17.38] EVANS. Have a care of your entertainments: there is a friend of mine come to town, who tells me there are three cousin-germans who have cozened all the hosts of Reading, of Maidenhead, of Colnbrook, of horses and money. I tell you for good will, look you: you are wise, and full of gibes and laughing-stocks, and it is not appropriate that you should be cozened. Fare you well. [Exit.]
[17.39] [Enter DOCTOR CAIUS.]
[17.40] CAIUS. Vere is mine host of the Garter?
[17.41] HOST. Here, master doctor, in perplexity and doubtful dilemma.
[17.42] CAIUS. I cannot tell vat is dat: but it is tell-a me dat you make grand preparation for a duke of Germany: by my troth, dere is no duke dat the court is know to come. I tell you for good vill: farewell. [Exit.]
[17.43] HOST. Hue and cry, villain, go! — Assist me, knight. — I am undone! — Fly, run, hue and cry, villain! — I am undone!
[17.44] [Exeunt HOST and BARDOLPH.]
[17.45] FALSTAFF. I would all the world might be cozened; for I have been cozened and beaten too. If it should come to the ear of the court, how I have been transformed, and how my transformation has been washed and cudgelled, they would melt me out of my fat drop by drop, and grease fishermen's boots with me: I warrant they would whip me with their fine wits till I were as crestfallen as a dried pear. I never prospered since I forswore myself at primero. Well, if my breath were but long enough [to say my prayers], I would repent.
[17.46] [Enter MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[17.47] Now, whence come you?
[17.48] QUICKLY. From the two parties, indeed.
[17.49] FALSTAFF. The devil take one party, and his dam the other! And so they shall be both bestowed. I have suffered more for their sakes, more than the villainous inconstancy of man's disposition is able to bear.
[17.50] QUICKLY. And have they not suffered? Yes, I warrant; specially one of them; Mistress Ford, good heart, is beaten black and blue, so that you cannot see a white spot on her.
[17.51] FALSTAFF. What do you tell me of black and blue? I was beaten myself into all the colours of the rainbow; and I was likely to be apprehended for the witch of Brentford: except that my admirable dexterity of wit, my counterfeiting the action of an old woman, delivered me, the knave constable would have set me in the stocks, in the common stocks, for a witch.
[17.52] QUICKLY. Sir, let me speak with you in your chamber: you shall hear how things go; and, I warrant, to your satisfaction. Here is a letter that will say something. Good hearts, what ado there is here to bring you together! Surely, one of you does not serve heaven well, since you are so crossed.
[17.53] FALSTAFF. Come up into my chamber. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 18: Act 4, Scene 6 — The same. Another room in the Garter Inn ===
[18.0] [Enter FENTON and HOST.]
[18.1] HOST. Master Fenton, do not talk to me; my mind is heavy: I will give up everything.
[18.2] FENTON. Yet hear me speak. Assist me in my purpose, and, as I am a gentleman, I'll give you a hundred pounds in gold more than your loss.
[18.3] HOST. I will hear you, Master Fenton; and I will at the least keep your counsel.
[18.4] FENTON. From time to time I have made known to you the dear love I bear to fair Anne Page; who has mutually answered my affection, so far as she might be her own chooser, even to my wish: I have a letter from her of such contents as you will marvel at; the mirth of which is so larded with my matter, that neither can be singly made known without showing both; fat Falstaff has a great scene: the picture of the jest I'll show you here in full. Hark, good my host. Tonight at Herne's oak, just between twelve and one, my sweet Nan must play the Fairy Queen; the reason why is here: in which disguise, while other jests are somewhat rankly underway, her father has commanded her to slip away with Slender, and immediately marry him at Eton: she has consented: now, sir, her mother, strongly against that match, and firm for Doctor Caius, has arranged that he shall likewise spirit her away, while other sports occupy their minds, and at the deanery, where a priest waits, straight away marry her: to this her mother's plot she has, seemingly obedient, likewise promised the doctor. Now, this is how it stands: her father intends she shall be all in white; and in that habit, when Slender sees his time to take her by the hand and bid her go, she shall go with him: her mother has intended, the better to mark her for the doctor — for they must all be masked and vizarded — that she shall be loosely robed in fanciful green, with ribbons hanging, flaring about her head; and when the doctor spies his moment ripe, to pinch her by the hand, and, on that signal, the maid has given consent to go with him.
[18.5] HOST. Which does she mean to deceive, father or mother?
[18.6] FENTON. Both, my good host, to go along with me: and here it rests — that you'll procure the vicar to wait for me at church between twelve and one, and, in the lawful name of marrying, to give our hearts united ceremony.
[18.7] HOST. Well, manage your scheme; I'll go to the vicar: bring you the maid, you shall not lack a priest.
[18.8] FENTON. So shall I be ever bound to you; besides, I'll make a present recompense. [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 19: Act 5, Scene 1 — A room in the Garter Inn ===
[19.0] [Enter FALSTAFF and MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[19.1] FALSTAFF. Please, no more prattling; go. I'll keep my word. This is the third time; I hope good luck lies in odd numbers. Away! Go. They say there is divinity in odd numbers, whether in birth, chance, or death. Away!
[19.2] QUICKLY. I'll provide you a chain; and I'll do what I can to get you a pair of horns.
[19.3] FALSTAFF. Away, I say; time wears on: hold up your head, and mince. [Exit MISTRESS QUICKLY.]
[19.4] [Enter FORD.]
[19.5] How now, Master Brook! Master Brook, the matter will be known tonight, or never. Be in the Park about midnight, at Herne's oak, and you shall see wonders.
[19.6] FORD. Did you not go to her yesterday, sir, as you told me you had arranged?
[19.7] FALSTAFF. I went to her, Master Brook, as you see, like a poor old man: but I came from her, Master Brook, like a poor old woman. That same knave Ford, her husband, has the finest mad devil of jealousy in him, Master Brook, that ever governed frenzy. I will tell you: — he beat me grievously, in the shape of a woman; for in the shape of man, Master Brook, I do not fear Goliath with a weaver's beam; because I know also life is a shuttle. I am in haste; come along with me: I'll tell you all, Master Brook. Since I plucked geese, played truant, and whipped top, I never knew what it was to be beaten until lately. Follow me: I'll tell you strange things of this knave Ford, on whom tonight I will be revenged, and I will deliver his wife into your hand. Follow. Strange things at hand, Master Brook! Follow. === Chapter 20: Act 5, Scene 2 — Windsor Park ===
[20.0] [Enter PAGE, SHALLOW, and SLENDER.]
[20.1] PAGE. Come, come; we'll crouch in the castle-ditch until we see the light of our fairies. Remember, son Slender, my daughter.
[20.2] SLENDER. Yes, indeed; I have spoken with her, and we have a code-word to know one another by: I come to her in white, and cry 'mum;' she cries 'budget;' and by that we know each other.

=== Chapter 21: Act 5, Scene 3 — A street leading to the Park ===

=== Chapter 22: Act 5, Scene 4 — Windsor Park ===

=== Chapter 23: Act 5, Scene 5 — Another part of the Park ===
[23.0] [Enter FALSTAFF disguised as Herne.]
[23.1] FALSTAFF. The Windsor bell has struck twelve; the minute draws near. Now, the hot-blooded gods assist me! Remember, Jove, you were a bull for your Europa; love set on your horns. O powerful love! That, in some respects, makes a beast a man; in some others, a man a beast. You were also, Jupiter, a swan for the love of Leda. O omnipotent Love! How near the god came to the complexion of a goose! — A fault first committed in the form of a beast; — O Jove, a beastly fault! And then another fault in the likeness of a fowl; — think on it, Jove; a foul fault! When gods have hot backs, what shall poor men do? For my part, I am here a Windsor stag; and the fattest, I think, in the forest. Send me a cool rutting-season, Jove, or who can blame me for pissing my tallow? — Who comes here? My doe?
[23.2] [Enter MISTRESS FORD and MISTRESS PAGE.]
[23.3] MISTRESS FORD. Sir John! Are you there, my deer? My male deer?
[23.4] FALSTAFF. My doe with the black scut! Let the sky rain potatoes; let it thunder to the tune of Green Sleeves, hail kissing-comfits, and snow eringoes; let there come a tempest of provocation, I will shelter here.
[23.5] MISTRESS FORD. Mistress Page has come with me, sweetheart.
[23.6] FALSTAFF. Divide me like a poached buck, each a haunch: I will keep my sides for myself, my shoulders for the fellow of this walk, and my horns I bequeath to your husbands. Am I a woodman, ha? Do I speak like Herne the hunter? Why, now Cupid is a child of conscience; he makes restitution. As I am a true spirit, welcome! [Noise within.]
[23.7] MISTRESS PAGE. Alas, what noise?
[23.8] MISTRESS FORD. Heaven forgive our sins!
[23.9] FALSTAFF. What should this be?
[23.10] MISTRESS FORD and MISTRESS PAGE. Away, away! [They run off.]
[23.11] FALSTAFF. I think the devil will not have me damned, lest the oil that's in me should set hell on fire; he would never else cross me thus.
[23.12] [Enter SIR HUGH EVANS, disguised as before; PISTOL, as Hobgoblin; MISTRESS QUICKLY, ANNE PAGE, and others, as Fairies, with tapers.]
[23.13] QUICKLY. Fairies, black, grey, green, and white, you moonshine revellers, and shades of night, you orphan heirs of fixed destiny, attend to your office and your quality. Crier Hobgoblin, make the fairies' call.
[23.14] PISTOL. Elves, list your names; silence, you airy creatures. Cricket, to Windsor chimneys you shall leap: where you find fires unraked and hearths unswept, there pinch the maids as blue as bilberry: our radiant queen hates sluts and sluttery.
[23.15] FALSTAFF. They are fairies; he who speaks to them shall die: I'll close my eyes and crouch: no man must look upon their works.
[23.16] [Lies down upon his face.]
[23.17] EVANS. Where's Bede? Go you, and where you find a maid who, before she sleeps, has thrice said her prayers, raise up the organs of her fantasy; let her sleep as sound as careless infancy: but for those who sleep and don't think on their sins, pinch them — arms, legs, backs, shoulders, sides, and shins.
[23.18] QUICKLY. About, about; search Windsor Castle, elves, within and out: strew good luck, ouphes, on every sacred room; that it may stand till the perpetual doom, in state as wholesome as in state it is fit, worthy the owner, and the owner it. The several chairs of order see you scour with juice of balm and every precious flower: each fair installment, coat, and several crest, with loyal blazon, evermore be blessed! And nightly, meadow-fairies, see you sing, like to the Garter's circle, in a ring: the impressure that it bears, green let it be, more fertile-fresh than all the field to see; and Honi soit qui mal y pense write in emerald tufts, flowers purple, blue, and white; like sapphire, pearl, and rich embroidery, buckled below fair knighthood's bending knee: fairies use flowers for their writing. Away; disperse: but till it is one o'clock, our customary dance around about the oak of Herne the hunter, let us not forget.
[23.19] EVANS. Please, lock hand in hand; place yourselves in order; and twenty glow-worms shall be our lanterns, to guide our measure around about the tree. — But stay; I smell a man of middle-earth.
[23.20] FALSTAFF. Heaven defend me from that Welsh fairy, lest he transform me into a piece of cheese!
[23.21] PISTOL. Vile worm, you were ill-omened even at your birth.
[23.22] QUICKLY. With trial-fire touch his fingertip: if he be chaste, the flame will descend back, and turn him to no pain; but if he starts, it is the flesh of a corrupted heart.
[23.23] PISTOL. A trial, come.
[23.24] EVANS. Come, will this wood take fire?
[23.25] [They burn him with their tapers.]
[23.26] FALSTAFF. Oh, Oh, Oh!
[23.27] QUICKLY. Corrupt, corrupt, and tainted in desire! About him, fairies; sing a scornful rhyme; and, as you trip, keep pinching him in time.
[23.28] SONG.
[23.29] Fie on sinful fantasy! Fie on lust and luxury! Lust is but a bloody fire, kindled with unchaste desire, fed in heart, whose flames aspire, as thoughts do blow them, higher and higher. Pinch him, fairies, all together; pinch him for his villainy; pinch him, and burn him, and turn him about, till candles and starlight and moonshine be out.
[23.30] [During this song they pinch FALSTAFF. DOCTOR CAIUS comes one way, and steals away a boy in green; SLENDER another way, and takes off a boy in white; and FENTON comes, and steals away MISTRESS ANNE PAGE. A noise of hunting is heard within. All the Fairies run away. FALSTAFF pulls off his buck's head, and rises.]
[23.31] [Enter PAGE, FORD, MISTRESS PAGE, and MISTRESS FORD.]
[23.32] PAGE. No, don't flee; I think we have caught you out now: will none but Herne the hunter serve your turn?
[23.33] MISTRESS PAGE. Please, come, hold up the jest no higher. Now, good Sir John, how do you like Windsor wives? Do you see these, husband? Don't these fair yokes become the forest better than the town?
[23.34] FORD. Now, sir, who's a cuckold now? Master Brook, Falstaff is a knave, a cuckoldly knave; here are his horns, Master Brook: and, Master Brook, he has enjoyed nothing of Ford's but his buck-basket, his cudgel, and twenty pounds of money, which must be paid to Master Brook; his horses are seized for it, Master Brook.
[23.35] MISTRESS FORD. Sir John, we have had ill luck; we could never meet. I will never take you for my love again; but I will always count you my deer.
[23.36] FALSTAFF. I do begin to perceive that I have been made an ass.
[23.37] FORD. Yes, and an ox too: both the proofs are evident.
[23.38] FALSTAFF. And these are not fairies? I was three or four times in the thought they were not fairies: and yet the guilt of my mind, the sudden surprise of my powers, drove the grossness of the foppery into a received belief, in defiance of all rhyme and reason, that they were fairies. See now how wit may be made a Jack-a-Lent, when it is upon ill employment!
[23.39] EVANS. Sir John Falstaff, serve God, and leave your desires, and fairies will not pinch you.
[23.40] FORD. Well said, fairy Hugh.
[23.41] EVANS. And leave your jealousies too, please.
[23.42] FORD. I will never mistrust my wife again, until you are able to woo her in good English.
[23.43] FALSTAFF. Have I laid my brain in the sun, and dried it, that it lacks substance to prevent so gross an overreaching as this? Am I ridden with a Welsh goat too? Shall I have a coxcomb of frieze? It's time I were choked with a piece of toasted cheese.
[23.44] EVANS. Cheese is not good to give butter; your belly is all butter.
[23.45] FALSTAFF. 'Cheese' and 'butter'! Have I lived to stand at the taunt of one who makes fritters of English? This is enough to be the decay of lust and late-walking throughout the realm.
[23.46] MISTRESS PAGE. Why, Sir John, do you think, though we would have thrust virtue out of our hearts by head and shoulders, and have given ourselves without scruple to hell, that ever the devil could have made you our delight?
[23.47] FORD. What, a hodge-pudding? A bag of flax?
[23.48] MISTRESS PAGE. A puffed-up man?
[23.49] PAGE. Old, cold, withered, and of intolerable entrails?
[23.50] FORD. And one that is as slanderous as Satan?
[23.51] PAGE. And as poor as Job?
[23.52] FORD. And as wicked as his wife?
[23.53] EVANS. And given to fornications, and to taverns, and sack, and wine, and metheglins, and to drinkings, and swearings, and starings, prittles and prattles?
[23.54] FALSTAFF. Well, I am your theme: you have the advantage of me; I am dejected; I am not able to answer the Welsh flannel; ignorance itself is a plummet over me: use me as you will.
[23.55] FORD. Indeed, sir, we'll bring you to Windsor, to one Master Brook, that you have cozened of money, to whom you should have been a pander: over and above what you have suffered, I think to repay that money will be a biting affliction.
[23.56] PAGE. Yet be cheerful, knight: you shall eat a hot drink tonight at my house; where I will ask you to laugh at my wife, who now laughs at you: tell her Master Slender has married her daughter.
[23.57] MISTRESS PAGE. [Aside] Doctors doubt that: if Anne Page be my daughter, she is, by this time, Doctor Caius' wife.
[23.58] [Enter SLENDER.]
[23.59] SLENDER. Whoa, ho! Ho, father Page!
[23.60] PAGE. Son, how now! How now, son! Have you dispatched?
[23.61] SLENDER. Dispatched! — I'll let the best in Gloucestershire know about it; I'll be hanged otherwise!
[23.62] PAGE. Of what, son?
[23.63] SLENDER. I came yonder at Eton to marry Mistress Anne Page, and she's a great lubberly boy. If it had not been in the church, I would have thrashed him, or he should have thrashed me. If I did not think it was Anne Page, may I never stir! — and it's a postmaster's boy.
[23.64] PAGE. Upon my life, then, you took the wrong one.
[23.65] SLENDER. What need you tell me that? I think so, when I took a boy for a girl. If I had been married to him, despite all his being in woman's apparel, I would not have had him.
[23.66] PAGE. Why, this is your own folly. Did I not tell you how you should know my daughter by her garments?
[23.67] SLENDER. I went to her in white, and cried 'mum,' and she cried 'budget,' as Anne and I had arranged; and yet it was not Anne, but a postmaster's boy.
[23.68] MISTRESS PAGE. Good George, do not be angry: I knew of your scheme; turned my daughter into green; and, indeed, she is now with the doctor at the deanery, and there married.
[23.69] [Enter CAIUS.]
[23.70] CAIUS. Vere is Mistress Page? By God, I am cozened: I have married un garçon, a boy; un paysan, by God, a boy; it is not Anne Page: by God, I am cozened.
[23.71] MISTRESS PAGE. Why, did you take her in green?
[23.72] CAIUS. Yes, by God, and it is a boy: by God, I'll rouse all Windsor. [Exit.]
[23.73] FORD. This is strange. Who has got the right Anne?
[23.74] PAGE. My heart misgives me: — here comes Master Fenton.
[23.75] [Enter FENTON and ANNE PAGE.]
[23.76] How now, Master Fenton!
[23.77] ANNE. Pardon, good father! Good my mother, pardon!
[23.78] PAGE. Now, mistress, how is it you did not go with Master Slender?
[23.79] MISTRESS PAGE. Why did you not go with master doctor, girl?
[23.80] FENTON. You are amazing her: hear the truth of it. You would have married her most shamefully, where there was no proportion held in love. The truth is, she and I, long since contracted, are now so sure that nothing can dissolve us. The offence is holy that she has committed; and this deceit loses the name of cunning, of disobedience, or undutiful title; since by it she avoids and shuns a thousand irreligious cursed hours, which forced marriage would have brought upon her.
[23.81] FORD. Don't stand amazed; there is no remedy: in love the heavens themselves do guide the situation; money buys lands, and wives are sold by fate.
[23.82] FALSTAFF. I am glad, though you have taken a special stand to shoot at me, that your arrow has glanced.
[23.83] PAGE. Well, what remedy? Fenton, heaven give you joy! What cannot be avoided must be embraced.
[23.84] FALSTAFF. When night-dogs run, all sorts of deer are chased.
[23.85] MISTRESS PAGE. Well, I will muse no further. Master Fenton, heaven give you many, many merry days! Good husband, let us every one go home, and laugh this sport over by a country fire; Sir John and all.
[23.86] FORD. Let it be so. Sir John, to Master Brook you shall yet hold your word; for he tonight shall lie with Mistress Ford. [Exeunt.]

Master Sun gave this teaching.
Raising 100,000 soldiers and moving them one hundred leagues is an undertaking that consumes 1,000 taels of the money of ordinary citizens and nobles every day. Turmoil spreads inside and outside the country. Along the army's route, porters collapse from weariness, and the livelihoods of 700,000 households are disrupted.
Armies may stand opposed for years, yet a victory gained in one day may settle the entire outcome.
To begrudge the small cost of giving spies salaries and titles, and consequently remain unaware of the opponent's circumstances, therefore shows a lack of humanity. Such a person is unworthy of command, gives his ruler no help, and possesses no mastery of success.
A discerning ruler and a thoughtful commander achieve exceptional distinction and success through their actions because they possess information in advance.
Appeals to deities and spirits cannot yield this information; neither past experience nor calculation supplies it. What we know about our opponent must come from people.
Five categories of agent are used: village spies, inner spies, converted spies, death spies, and living spies.
Use all five methods together and no one can trace how they operate. Their name is the Mysterious Thread, and they are the Lord's Treasure.
Village spies are inhabitants of the country who supply us with information.
Inner spies are officials serving our opponent whom we engage for our purposes.
Converted spies are agents of the opposing side whom we have hired ourselves.
Death spies go out to deceive our opponent and circulate fabricated accounts through agents of ours already stationed within his lines.
Living spies come back bearing their reports.
Among those connected with the armies, spies deserve the utmost kindness. When rewards are distributed, they should receive the most generous share. Everything concerning their work must remain confidential.
Employing spies requires limitless ability in the commander. Dealing with them calls for kindness and integrity. Unless they are watched with the greatest possible care, they will not yield truthful information.
The influence that spies possess is truly remarkable.
There is no situation in which they cannot serve a purpose.
If confidential business is discussed before its proper time, execute both the agent who disclosed it and the person who passed the disclosure on.
Before assaulting an army, besieging a stronghold, or killing a particular individual, learn the names of the commander responsible, his closest assistants, the people who bring visitors into the Presence, the gatekeeper, and the guards on watch. Then instruct your agents to keep these people under observation.
Find the hostile agents sent to observe us. Pay them, arrange accommodation and care for them, and bring them into our service. They enable us to recruit further agents from the opponent's villagers and officials.
An agent won over from the opponent enables us to devise a fabricated account for a death spy to take back to him.
The converted agent enables us to employ all five kinds of spy to the greatest benefit. He must therefore receive generous treatment.
In antiquity, the province of Yin gained its power through the work of Ichih, who was dispatched to the country of Hsia. Similarly, when the state of Chu was being established, Luya resided among Shang's people.
Discerning rulers and thoughtful commanders consequently select the most intelligent men for spying and consistently achieve great distinction. An army cannot do without its spy: its very movements depend upon him.

## The five most serious problems

1. **25:107 (and 25:117–119): «зарежут» rendered as "his throat will be cut".** The Russian means "he will be knifed" (killed with a knife). In 26:62 Judas is stabbed in the back and the heart, so "throat" adds a detail that the next chapter contradicts. Proposed: "he will be knifed" / "knife him".
2. **24:275: «кверху ногами» rendered as "feet first".** The Russian means upside down, matching 24:198 ("Mogarych was turned upside down"). "Feet first" changes the image.
3. **22:66–67: «пачкотня» rendered as "smearing".** In English "smearing" suggests slander. The word means messy daubing. The cat's retort in 22:67 repeats Woland's word, so both need the same fix ("daubing").
4. **22:44: "homely attire".** The edition uses US spelling, and in US English "homely" means plain or unattractive. «домашний наряд» means the clothes he wears at home. Proposed: "domestic attire". The same paragraph has "queen" in lower case, the only one in ch. 22–24 (everywhere else it is "Queen").
5. **24:270: "And what was what was this:".** The doubled "what was" reads like a typo. It is an attempt to echo «в чем тут дело» from 24:269 ("sort out what’s what"). Proposed: "And here is what was what:".

Smaller items: "bookkeeper" for бухгалтер (glossary: "accountant", 19:11); "I’m going into everything for his sake" (calque, 19:137); "opera glasses" against "opera glass" elsewhere (23:97); "it is chance" (25:112).

## Systematic pattern

- **зарезать → "cut (someone’s) throat"** across ch. 25–26. This is the only systematic defect I found. In ch. 26 (outside my range) the same rendering appears in 26:66, 88, 102, 109, 114 and 227–231. Whoever reviews ch. 26 should decide it together with 25:107–119, so the coded phrase between Pilate and Aphranius stays identical in both chapters. Where a throat is actually meant, the text should say so; elsewhere "knife" or "kill" fits the Russian.
- Otherwise the translation is very accurate. I found no omitted clauses, names or numbers, and no wrong speakers or negation errors in 961 paragraphs. The register holds: lively and colloquial in Moscow, solemn in Yershalaim (ch. 25). The motifs (19:21 = 19:93 = 24:301 = 25:1; "I’m enchanted"; "How whimsically the deck is shuffled!"; "Manuscripts don’t burn") match the glossary.

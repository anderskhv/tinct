An independent, content-only review. I read every paragraph of chapters 26–33 (806 paragraphs) against `master-and-margarita-original-ru.json`. Fixes are in `revEN-D.json`: 20 entries, each with an exact `old` substring that occurs once in its paragraph, so they can be applied mechanically. I did not edit the edition files. I did not reopen any decision Anders has approved (style, glossary, De/du, «kejseren», "mulatto"/"Black men", "Bezdomny", «?..»).

I also ran some mechanical screens on these chapters. All of them came back clean:

- **Typography:** no straight quotes, spaced dashes, `…` characters, stray Cyrillic or unbalanced curly quotes.
- **Bulgakov's «?..» / «!..»:** the counts match the Russian.
- **Possible omissions:** every paragraph flagged by the length-ratio or sentence-count screen was reread, and none is missing anything.

Overall this is a strong, faithful translation. I found no omitted clauses, names or numbers, and no invented material. The Yershalaim register in chapter 26 is solemn and concrete, as it should be. The narrator's mock-official irony in 27, 28 and 33 survives well. The motif echoes (26:243 → 27:1, the last line of 32 and 33, "pricked all over by needles", "Gods, gods!") are already identical.

## The 5 most serious problems

1. **28:8, negation inverted (major).** «порасскажу ему о вас таких вещей, что не пришлось бы вам покинуть ваш пост» is a threat: "you may well have to leave your post". The English says "that you might not have to leave your post", which is the opposite.
2. **33:41, wrong referent (major).** «старой сквалыги Пороховниковой тетки» is the aunt Porokhovnikova herself (15:107, GLOSSARY). The English "Porokhovnikov’s old skinflint of an aunt" invents a nephew called Porokhovnikov.
3. **32:9, "the demon-killer" (major).** «демон-убийца» is a murdering demon. In English "demon-killer" reads as someone who kills demons. Proposed: "the killer demon".
4. **26:66, "the two who had cut Judas’s throat" (major).** In 26:62 Judas is stabbed under the shoulder blade and through the heart. The narrator's own summary must not say his throat was cut. Proposed: "knifed".
5. **27:61, "in a state of unconsciousness had left for Leningrad" (minor, but it makes no sense).** «в беспамятстве» means in a daze, beside himself. Proposed: "in a daze".

## Systematic pattern: «зарезать» = "cut someone’s throat"

The translation renders «зарезать» as "cut [someone’s] throat" everywhere: 2:175 (sample), 25:107, 25:117–119, and in chapter 26 at 26:66, 26:88, 26:102, 26:109, 26:114, 26:227–229 and 26:231. «Зарезать» means to kill with a knife; it says nothing about the throat. Judas is in fact stabbed in the back and the heart.

- **In dialogue** the phrase is a consistent echo: Pilate's "his throat will be cut tonight" (25) is answered by Aphranius's "his throat has been cut" (26:88). Levi's threats with his bread knife also read naturally. So I have not listed those paragraphs as defects.
- **In the narration** (26:66) it is a factual error, and I flagged it.

**Recommendation:** decide the whole chain at once, across 2:175, ch. 25 and ch. 26, rather than patching single lines. A neutral rendering such as "knife" / "be knifed" / "stab to death" would work in every case. If the "throat" wording stays in the dialogue, only 26:66 needs changing.

## Smaller consistency points (all minor, in the JSON)

- **Echo wording.** 29:66 and 30:1 should use the harmonised "the darkness that came…" (25:1). In 32:18 «плохая должность» should be "a bad office", as in 26:79.
- **Glossary and established forms.** Use "O gods" (33:65). Use "tram" rather than "streetcar" (33:4, 33:30; the rest of the book has "tram"). Use "a white film" for Azazello's «бельмо» rather than "cataract" (30:44; see 10:132, 18:57).
- **Calques a reader would stumble on.** "Everything bites" (28:26), "What have you come with" (29:16), "the chemistry Candidate of Sciences Vetchinkevich" (33:18).
- **Unwanted English connotation.** "a light woman" (26:21), proposed "a light-footed woman".

## Noted, not flagged (judgement calls left as they are)

- **27:91** «Ремиз!» → "Missed the contract!". This is the card-game term (preference) and is defensible, if obscure.
- **27:60** «вампира-наводчика» → "vampire-spotter". "Lookout" or "tipster" would be closer to «наводчик». "Spotter" is acceptable.
- **31:18** «саженей на десять» → "some seventy feet". The unit is converted, which is consistent with 1:16 («ростом в сажень» → "seven feet tall").
- **32:33** «в ночь на воскресение» → "on the night before the Resurrection". This follows the printed «воскресение» (Resurrection) rather than «воскресенье» (Sunday). It is correct for this text.

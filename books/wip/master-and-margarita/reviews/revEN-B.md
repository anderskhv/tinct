Overall the translation is complete and faithful. I found no omitted clauses, names or numbers, and no wrong speakers. Sums and times are all correct (500 roubles, 21,711 roubles, 249,000 roubles, 18,000 dollars, the Yalta timetable). The narrator’s mock-official irony comes through, and Chapter 16 keeps the solemn register. Paragraph alignment is 1:1. Typography is clean: curly quotes only, unspaced em dashes, no straight quotes. The Chapter 15→16 echo and «За мной, читатель!» match the glossary.

## The 5 most serious problems

1. **13:179 (major).** «я боюсь» is rendered "…to come back alone, I’m afraid". At the end of the sentence, English readers take this as the polite hedge ("I regret"), so the master’s confession of fear is lost. Fear is the motif of his whole account (13:151–186). Fix: "…to come back alone; I am frightened."
2. **16:13.** «глаза выедал ослепительный блеск» is rendered "the eyes were eaten away by the blinding glitter". This reads as if the lion masks’ eyes had eroded, when the glare stings the onlookers’ eyes. Fix: "the blinding glitter stung the eyes".
3. **13:96.** «Нехороший цвет» is rendered "An ugly color", and «увидела она меня одного» "she saw me alone". The first turns an omen into an aesthetic judgement, which matters because the next paragraph calls it «желтому знаку». The second is ambiguous ("when I was alone").
4. **15:13 and 15:39.** «задушевно» is rendered "sincerely" both times. The interrogator and the dream compère use a false heart-to-heart warmth. "Sincerely" states the opposite of the irony.
5. **15:1.** The echo is broken. «толстяк с багровой физиономией» deliberately points back to 13:34, which reads "fat man with a purple physiognomy", but 15:1 has "the fat man with the crimson face". The callback should use the same wording.

## Systematic patterns

- **British/US drift in Chapter 17 and nearby.** "lorries" appears only at 17:104–105, against "truck" everywhere else. Other cases are "moustached" (14:7, against "mustache" elsewhere) and "THEATRE" on the 10:11 poster, against the harmonised "theater". "Queue", "pavement" and "draughts" also appear beside US forms. I flagged only the clear inconsistencies.
- **Occasional calques of idioms or bureaucratic terms**, for example "the swamp can have him" (11:18), "some type" (13:11), "a beginning and promising actress" (12:126), "writing resolutions" (17:58) and "drew itself up onto" (16:7). They are few and scattered.
- **A handful of small English grammar slips** in long periodic sentences: 12:100, 13:133, 16:33, 17:102, 18:31.
- Not a defect, noted only: 15:51 adds "or left on a doorstep" so that «подбросить» covers both "plant" and "abandon a baby". It is a justified adaptation of the pun, so no fix is proposed.

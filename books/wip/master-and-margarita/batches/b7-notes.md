## Passages I was unsure of

1. **«негры», «чернокожий», «мулат»** (ch. 23 passim, 24:97). I rendered «негры» as "Black men" / «sorte mænd» (EN capital B), «чернокожий» as "black-skinned man" / «sort mand», and kept «мулат» as "mulatto" / «mulat», matching the sample's flagged choice («темный, как мулат»). This is the same open question as the sample's item 5; Anders may want one policy for the whole book.
2. **«Какая зеленая?»** (23:46): literally "What/which green [one]?". I took it as Margarita asking who the lady with the green band is: "Who is the green one?" / «Hvem er den grønne?». Koroviev's answer introduces Tofana, which supports this reading.
3. **«так вот-с»** (23:49): the deferential -с particle. Rendered "Yes, so, if you please," / «Ja, altså,». Danish has no good equivalent, so the deference is lost there.
4. **«обрызгать стены кабинета ядом»** (23:85): whose office is not said (the historical allusion is to the Yagoda/Peshkov case). Kept it ambiguous: "the walls of the office" / «væggene i kontoret».
5. **«его хорошо отделали»** (24:128): "they've given him a good going-over" / «de har ordentlig fået ram på ham». My first Danish draft («gjort af med») meant "killed" and was corrected.
6. **«это ты... это вы...»** (24:200) and **«Котам обычно ... говорят "ты" ... брудершафта»** (24:201). Danish keeps the du/De switch exactly («er det dig... er det Dem...»; «sagt du til ... drukket dus»). English has no T/V distinction, so I used "was it you... was it you, sir..." and "cats are usually spoken to with familiarity, though no cat has ever drunk Bruderschaft with anyone". "Bruderschaft" is kept because the joke rests on it.
7. **«второй свежести»** (24:99): the cat recalls the barman's "sturgeon of the second freshness" (ch. 18). Rendered "Some of the first freshness, and the others ... of the second" / «af første friskhed ... af anden friskhed». **Check against batch b5's wording of ch. 18** so the echo matches.
8. **«ни свет ни заря»** (24:272): DA «længe før hanegal». I avoided «før fanden får sko på» because it would add a devil that Bulgakov does not have here.
9. **«Знать ничего не знаю, ведать ничего не ведаю»** (24:284): a set phrase. "I know nothing, I've seen nothing" / «Jeg ved ingenting, jeg har ikke set noget».
10. **24:301** begins the novel's own text, which opens ch. 25 («Тьма, пришедшая со Средиземного моря, накрыла ненавидимый прокуратором город...»). My wording is "The darkness that came from the Mediterranean Sea covered the city the procurator hated..." / «Mørket, der kom fra Middelhavet, dækkede den by, som prokuratoren hadede...». **Batch b8 should open ch. 25 with the same words**, or this line should be harmonised at integration.
11. **«Коньяк!»** rendered "Cognac!" / «Cognac!» (23:97), but «за коньяком» (23:85) as "over a brandy" (EN) / «over en cognac» (DA). Harmonise to "cognac" in 23:85 if preferred.

## Puns and wordplay

- **«уплочено»** (24:229): an illiterate misspelling of «уплачено» ("paid"). EN "Payed", DA «Betalet» (for «betalt»), so that each reads as a semi-literate official stamp.
- **«боров»** (24:227): EN "hog", DA «galt» (castrated boar, the exact sense).
- **«Ноблесс оближ»** (24:4): Bulgakov writes French in Cyrillic. Rendered in plain French, "Noblesse oblige", in both languages.
- **«История рассудит нас»** (24:26): "History will judge between us" / «Historien vil dømme mellem os». The "story/history" double sense works in Danish only.
- **«Ну, чего они будут хвататься!»** (24:203) echoes «хватятся» ("they'll miss me"). The play on «хвататься» ("grab") is lost. Rendered plainly: "Now why should they miss you!" / «Hvorfor skulle de dog savne Dem!».
- **«Истинным... ваше ве...»** (24:236): Varenukha starts on «Истинным Богом» and «ваше величество». Rendered "By the true... Your Maj..." / «Ved den sande... Deres maje...».
- **Devil vocabulary kept literally**: «черт тебя возьми» → "the devil take you" / «fanden tage dig»; «чертовку» → "she-devil" / «djævlekvinde»; «дьявол его знает откуда» → "the devil knows where" / «fanden ved hvorfra»; «Куда ж тебя черт несет» → "Where the devil are you rushing to" / «Hvor fanden skal du hen».

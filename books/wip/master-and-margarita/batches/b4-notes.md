## Passages I was unsure of

1. **15:51–53, «что могут подбросить?» — «Ребенка!»** *Подбросить* means both "plant (evidence) on someone" and "abandon a foundling on a doorstep". The baby joke depends on the second sense. EN: "what can be planted on somebody, or left on a doorstep?", which adds "or left on a doorstep" so the answer "A baby!" works. DA: «hvad kan man lægge på andres dørtrin?», which carries the foundling sense directly, with «smugle ind» used for the dollars elsewhere. This is the one place in the batch where I added words to carry a pun. Reviewer: please judge it.
2. **15:139, «а на нет и суда нет»** (proverb: "where there is nothing, there's no case"). EN "and where there’s none, there’s no case to answer"; DA «og hvor intet er, har selv retten tabt sin ret», which echoes the Danish proverb «hvor intet er, har selv kejseren tabt sin ret». It is idiomatic in Danish, but freer than the English.
3. **15:91, «помер к чертовой матери»**: devil vocabulary kept. EN "he dropped dead—to the devil with him—of a stroke"; DA «han døde fanden i vold», reusing the sample's «fanden i vold».
4. **15:117, «кто старое помянет...»** (the start of the proverb "whoever brings up the past loses an eye"). EN "let bygones be bygones..."; DA «glemt er glemt...». Both use the target idiom rather than the literal opening.
5. **15:84, Pushkin's *Covetous Knight* line** and **15:129, the sung couplet** «Там груды золота лежат, и мне они принадлежат!» are my own renderings. The couplet rhymes in both: "There heaps of gold lie shining bright, and all of them are mine by right!" / «Der ligger guld i dynger tæt, og det er mit med fuld ret!»
6. **15:88, «то на "вы", а то на "ты"»**: EN "now with the formal “you,” now with the familiar one"; DA »snart De, snart du«, so Danish can show the distinction directly.
7. **14:11, «Два раза ... клал руку на трубку и дважды ее снимал»**: *ее* could be the hand or the receiver. I read it as the hand ("twice he took it away again"), because the phone then rings unexpectedly, so he had not lifted the receiver.
8. **14:69, «Первую категорию ... жесткий»**: Soviet ticket classes. EN "First class ... take a hard seat"; DA «Første klasse ... tag en hård plads».
9. **14:28, «Какая там, к черту, Ялта!»**: EN "Yalta, the devil it was!"; DA «Jalta, sikken fanden!».
10. **14:10, «Тьфу ты, дьявол!»**: EN "Pah, the devil!"; DA «Fy for djævelen!» (дьявол, so «djævelen», not «fanden»).
11. **14:32, «в вытрезвителе»**: DA «i detentionen», the natural Danish police drunk-tank word, instead of a literal "sobering-up station".
12. **15:143, «скорбных главою»** (archaic, Church Slavonic flavour): EN "those afflicted in the head"; DA «de, der var plaget i hovedet». A little of the archaism is lost.
13. **15:8, «какой же я к шуту председатель!»**: *шут* (jester) is a euphemism for the devil, not *черт*, so I used the milder "what the deuce" / «hvad pokker», not "devil".
14. **16:44, «кефи»**: rendered "keffiyeh" in both languages for clarity. Bulgakov's own exotic forms are kept elsewhere (tallith/tallit, chlamys). If house style wants Bulgakov's form, use "kefi".

## Suspected source issues (Russian file not edited)

- **16:8, «а вторая кентурия наверх пропустила»** follows 16:3, «вторая когорта». This looks like Bulgakov's own inconsistency (the 1990 text has it too, as far as I can tell). It is translated as written ("the second century" / «den anden centurie»).
- **16:2, «северо-западная» ... «по северозападной дороге»**: the same word is hyphenated once and not the second time, in one paragraph. This could be an OCR or line-end de-hyphenation artifact. Translation is unaffected.
- **15:17, «положён»** carries «ё», although the README says the 1990 print uses «е» except in a handful of names. This may be a collation artifact. Worth a glance at the page image.
- **15:114, «нагробил»**: an unusual form (cf. «нагородил»). It is probably Bulgakov's colloquialism, but it is worth checking against the scan. Rendered "had blundered".
- No other suspected OCR errors found in these chapters.

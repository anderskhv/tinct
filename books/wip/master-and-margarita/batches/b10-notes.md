## Passages I was unsure of

1. **«прощенный в ночь на воскресение»** (32:33). The print has «воскресение» ("resurrection"), not «воскресенье» ("Sunday"). The night is literally Saturday into Sunday, and Bulgakov's spelling adds the Easter sense. EN: "forgiven on the night before the Resurrection"; DA: «tilgivet natten til opstandelsen». Both keep the religious sense, and the date follows from the context. A flatter "the night before Sunday" would lose the point.
2. **«Свистнуто, не спорю… свистнуто очень средне!»** (31:13). Koroviev's mock-connoisseur use of the impersonal passive. EN: "That was whistled, I don’t dispute it… it was whistled very middlingly!"; DA: «Fløjtet, det bestrider jeg ikke… fløjtet meget middelmådigt!». I kept the repetition rather than smoothing it.
3. **«Семерка под подушкой, и любое очко!»** (30:60). This refers to Azazello's shot through the playing card in ch. 24. EN: "A seven under the pillow, and any pip you like!"; DA: «Syveren under puden, og hvilket som helst øje, man vil!». "Pip" / «øje» is the spot on a card. Check that it matches how ch. 24 renders the card trick.
4. **«Придется вам, мой милый, жить с ведьмой!»** (30:12). Margarita playfully switches to «вам» with the master. DA keeps «De må nok leve med en heks, min kære!» as a teasing formality; EN cannot show the switch.
5. **«демон безводной пустыни, демон-убийца»** (32:9). EN: "the demon-killer", which is ambiguous in the way the Russian compound is ("a demon who kills"). DA: «dæmonen, der dræber», to avoid «dæmondræber», which would mean "a killer of demons".
6. **«каламбур… о свете и тьме»** (32:7) is kept as "pun" / «ordspil». «прошутить немного больше и дольше» is "go on joking a little more and a little longer" / «spøge en smule mere og en smule længere».
7. **«Двенадцать тысяч лун за одну луну когда-то»** (32:19) is "Twelve thousand moons for one moon once long ago". I added "long ago" to carry «когда-то» naturally.
8. **The Epilogue's surnames**: the joke is that Вольман, Вольпер, Володин and Волох sound like «Воланд», and Коровин, Коровкин and Караваев like «Коровьев». In EN, "Woland" is spelled with W while these citizens are transliterated with V, so the visual echo weakens a little but the sound survives. I did not respell them "Wolman" etc., because that would imply a link the Russian spelling does not make.
9. **Varenukha's quoted indirect speech** (33:43) is ungrammatical in the Russian: reported speech sits inside quotation marks («такой сволочи… он будто бы никогда не встречал…»). I kept it in both languages ("he has supposedly never in his life met…" / «han har angiveligt aldrig…»), because the comedy is the narrator's.
10. **«стопками»** is "a whole glass at a time"; «рюмка» is "glass". These are small vodka/cognac glasses. "Shot" felt too modern for Azazello.
11. **«ушел в землю и оставил по себе только туман»** (31:24) is "sunk into the earth and left only mist behind it".

## Wordplay and voice

- The devil vocabulary is kept literally throughout: «Фу-ты, черт!» "Phew, the devil!" / «Puh, for fanden!»; «черт знает что такое, черт, черт, черт!» "the devil knows what this is, the devil, the devil, the devil!" / «fanden ved… fanden, fanden, fanden!»; «ну тебя к черту» "to the devil with you" / «fanden tage dig»; «черт меня возьми» "devil take me" / «fanden tage mig»; «Какие тут к черту гипнотизеры!» "Hypnotists, the devil they are!" / «Hypnotisører, fanden heller!».
- «нечистая сила» is "the unclean powers" / «de urene magter».
- «Прошу заметить» (Azazello, 30:61) is "Kindly note" / «Læg vel mærke til», matching the sample's narrator formula.
- «?..» and «!..» are kept as in the samples, as are Danish »…« quotes with spaced en dashes, and De/Dem/Deres between strangers. Azazello, Woland and Praskovya Fyodorovna all use the formal form, as does the master with Ivan (the Russian has «вы» throughout their scene). The master and Margarita use du.

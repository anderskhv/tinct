## Hardest choices and uncertain passages

1. **«Ремиз!» (27.91).** A preference (card game) term: the declarer failed to take the tricks he bid. The cat gloats that the net-thrower has "gone down". EN "Missed the contract!" (bridge sense, readable without card knowledge); DA «Bet!», the exact Danish whist/bridge cry. A literal "Remiz!" would mean nothing to either reader.
2. **«поздоровался… не хочу, чтобы ты здравствовал» (29.18–19).** Russian «здравствуй» is literally "be healthy", so Levi's refusal to greet is a refusal to wish Woland well, and Woland's "you will have to reconcile yourself to it" answers that. EN "why did you not wish me good health… Because I do not wish you good health"; DA "hvorfor har du så ikke hilst mig med et goddag… Fordi jeg ikke ønsker dig nogen god dag" (Danish greeting carries the same wish).
3. **«Горько! Горько!» (28.45).** The Russian wedding cry ("bitter!") that makes the couple kiss; Koroviev's "it's bitter for me" turns into it, "like the best man at an old-fashioned wedding". Kept literal: EN "It's bitter for me! Bitter! Bitter!", DA «Bittert er det for mig! Bittert! Bittert!». The custom is not glossed; the simile signals it.
4. **«балык» / «балыковые бревна» (28.90, 101, 110).** Cured sturgeon back. Rendered "cured sturgeon" / "saltet stør" rather than "balyk", which neither reader would know; the closing image is "two logs of cured sturgeon" / "to blokke saltet stør".
5. **«их бы следовало разъяснить» (28.105).** Soviet officialese, "clarify" in the sense of "expose and deal with". EN "ought to be sorted out", DA "det er dem, man burde tage sig af". The bureaucratic undertone is weaker than in the Russian.
6. **«как из шайки в бане окатывают лавку» (28.50).** «шайка» is both a bathhouse washtub and a gang; the pun cannot be kept. EN "the way one sloshes a bench in a bathhouse from a washtub", DA "sådan som man i en badstue overskyller en bænk fra en balje".
7. **«Кароши?» / «Мировая!» (28.19–21).** The "foreigner's" broken Russian is rendered as broken English/German-flavoured speech: EN "Is goot?", "Goot I like, bad—no"; DA »Er gut?«, »Gut jeg lide, dårlig – nej«. The reveal in 28.49 (pure Russian) still works.
8. **«Все кусается» (28.26).** "Everything bites" / «Alting bider», kept literal (prices "bite" in both languages).
9. **«у сиреневого не не хватало чего-то в лице» (28.32).** The double «не не» is Bulgakov's (it was not that something was lacking), not an OCR error. EN "it was not that something was lacking… but rather… something extra".
10. **«вампир-наводчик» (27.60).** «наводчик» is a thieves' spotter or tipster. EN "vampire-spotter", DA "vampyr-udspejder".
11. **«покоем» (27.108).** The building is П-shaped. EN "built in the shape of a U", DA "bygget i form af et U", as in ch. 7.
12. **Title 29.** EN title case gives "The Fate of the Master and Margarita Is Decided". In running text the master stays lower case. Lower-case "the master" in a title-cased heading looked like an error, so I followed the heading convention. DA (sentence case) has "Mesterens og Margaritas skæbne er afgjort".
13. **Address in ch. 29.** Woland and Levi use «ты» with each other, so DA has *du* both ways. Levi's «вы взяли бы» and Woland's «вы не берете его к себе» are plural (Woland's party; Yeshua's side), so DA has *I/jer*. Koroviev and Behemoth address Woland as *De* + "messire". The cat addresses the police with *De* in the duel challenge.

# Accessibility Review (Final Pass) — Leviathan, Edition Ch. 2 / Hobbes Ch. 1, "Of Sense"

**Coverage:** All 5 paragraphs read in full, start to finish, then re-read paragraph by paragraph. No source text or prior review notes were consulted. Candidate file only: `candidate-sonnet.json`.

## Paragraph-by-paragraph findings

**Paragraph 0** — No issues. Clean, plain sentences; "Object" as a defined term is introduced smoothly and immediately explained ("something commonly called an Object").

**Paragraph 1** — No issues. Short, clear, causal claim stated once and restated once ("Everything else is derived from that origin") without padding.

**Paragraph 2** — No issues. Reads as ordinary authorial throat-clearing; nothing archaic or ambiguous.

**Paragraph 3** — This is the dense, technical core of the chapter, but it mostly holds together. Two spots a general reader could snag on:

- *"All these qualities, called "sensible," exist in the object that causes them as nothing but a number of different motions of matter."* — "sensible" is a false friend here: modern readers default to "sensible" = reasonable/practical, not "perceptible by the senses." Hobbes is using it in the older, technical sense, and the quotation marks signal *something* is being flagged as a term of art, but a reader could still momentarily parse this as "these qualities, [which are] reasonable" and get a beat of confusion before the rest of the sentence corrects them. Minor, but it's a real (if brief) stumble, not just a matter of taste — a single inline gloss (e.g., "these qualities, called 'sensible' — perceptible by sense —") would remove it entirely. Given this is the last documented issue after two rounds, I'd call this genuinely marginal: the sentence self-corrects within the same clause, and an adult reader will get it on the first pass without needing to backtrack to an earlier sentence. I would not hold the chapter for it.

- *"And just as pressing, rubbing, or striking the eye makes us imagine a light, and pressing the ear produces a ringing, so the bodies we see or hear likewise produce the same in us, through a strong though unnoticed action of their own."* — "produce the same in us" has a slightly loose referent. On first read it's plausible to parse "the same" as "the same bodies" rather than "the same [kind of sensation/fancy]." It resolves correctly by the end of the sentence and is confirmed by the next two sentences, but it's the one place in the paragraph that asks for a small re-read. This is the closest thing to a real comprehension stumble in the chapter — narrow in scope (one clause) and self-resolving, but worth a look if another editing pass happens. Not severe enough by itself to block acceptance.

No other sentence in this paragraph caused difficulty despite its length — the "glasses...mirrors and lenses" gloss, the echo example, and the closing summary sentence all land cleanly. The paragraph is long but not choppy; it reads as one sustained argument rather than stitched fragments.

**Paragraph 4** — No accessibility issues. The Scholastic jargon ("Visible Species," "Audible Being Seen," "Intelligible Being Seen") is exactly as opaque as it's meant to be — Hobbes is staging the jargon so he can then mock it ("speech that means nothing"), and the candidate preserves that setup faithfully, including the parenthetical glosses that let a reader follow the mockery without needing outside knowledge of Scholastic terminology. This is not a translation defect; it is the joke landing correctly.

## What reads well

The chapter's overall register is confident and even. The explanatory chain in paragraph 3 (object → pressure → nerves → brain/heart → outward-directed effort → "Fancy") is laid out as a genuine causal sequence a reader can follow start to finish, which is the hardest thing to pull off in this chapter given how compressed Hobbes's original argument is. The closing sentence of paragraph 3 ("So sense, in every case, is nothing but this original fancy...") does real work as a recap and lands cleanly. Paragraph 4's rhetorical turn — setting up the Scholastic jargon, then pivoting to "I do not say this to disparage the use of universities... one of these is how often they produce speech that means nothing" — reads with real comic timing, which is easy to lose in this kind of rendering and wasn't lost here.

## Verdict

**Substantially accessible.** After two rounds of fixes, this chapter has reached the bar. Nothing in it constitutes a real comprehension failure — no unresolved archaic constructions, no overloaded/tangled sentences, no unrecoverable ambiguous referents, no reference a general reader would be stranded by. The two items noted in paragraph 3 ("sensible" as a false-friend term, and the loose "produce the same in us" referent) are genuinely marginal, self-resolving within the sentence or paragraph, and are polish-level rather than blocking. I would accept this chapter as-is; if a future light pass happens to touch paragraph 3 anyway, those two spots are worth a glance, but I would not hold the chapter for them.

# Accessibility Review — Round 2 (Reviewer A)

**Book / chapter:** Leviathan, Part I — Of Man, Chapter 1. Of Sense

**Coverage:** Read `candidate-sonnet.json` in full — all 5 paragraphs (indices 0–4), each read completely, plus a second full pass sentence by sentence. Did not read `source.json` or any prior review `.md` file, per instructions.

## Paragraph-by-paragraph findings

**Paragraph 0** — No accessibility issues. "Taken one by one, each thought is simply a representation or appearance of some quality or other feature of a body outside us" reads cleanly for a general reader; "Object" is capitalized and immediately explained in context, so it doesn't strand the reader.

**Paragraph 1** — No accessibility issues. Short, declarative, easy to follow.

**Paragraph 2** — No accessibility issues. Reads as plain authorial throat-clearing.

**Paragraph 3** — Mostly clear despite being the chapter's longest and most conceptually loaded paragraph. One spot worth a targeted look:

- *"And just as pressing, rubbing, or striking the eye makes us imagine a light, and pressing the ear produces a ringing, so the bodies we see or hear likewise produce the same in us, through a strong though unnoticed action of their own."*
  The phrase **"produce the same in us"** has a referent a reader has to reach backward for — "the same" as what? On a first pass it's not immediately obvious whether "the same" means "the same kind of fancy/appearance" (the intended reading) or something narrower like "the same ringing." It resolves on a re-read, but it's the kind of pronoun-reference friction the reader shouldn't have to do work to untangle. Minor, but real — a candidate for a small rewrite (e.g., "produce the same kind of appearance in us") if the merge owner wants to tighten it.

Everything else in this paragraph — the sense-by-sense list ("For the eye it consists in a light... for the rest of the body, in heat, cold, hardness, softness...") and the closing sentence ("So sense, in every case, is nothing but this original fancy...") — reads unusually well for how much mechanistic causal content it's carrying. The parallel list in particular is a strong example of natural flow under density: it's long but not chopped, and the rhythm carries the reader through it.

**Paragraph 4** — No avoidable accessibility issues. The scholastic-jargon terms ("Visible Species," "Audible Species," "Audible Being Seen," "Intelligible Species," "Intelligible Being Seen") are dense and odd-sounding, but per the chapter context this is Hobbes's own intentional parody of confused Scholastic language, not a translation defect — flagging it as if it were a wording problem to fix would be flagging the argument itself. I'm noting it here only as inherent difficulty, not as an accessibility defect. The paragraph's closing line — "how often they produce speech that means nothing" — lands well and is a genuine high point of the chapter's readability.

## What reads unusually well

- The sense-by-sense catalogue in paragraph 3 (light/sound/odor/taste/heat-cold-hardness-softness) — long but rhythmic, not mechanically chopped.
- "For motion produces nothing but motion" — a crisp, almost aphoristic beat inside a dense paragraph; it gives the reader a foothold.
- The closing sentences of both paragraph 3 and paragraph 4 — each lands as a clear summary/punchline after a run of dense material.

## Overall verdict

**Substantially accessible.** This chapter has reached the point where remaining difficulty is unavoidable conceptual complexity, not avoidable wording. There is exactly one small candidate for a polish-level fix (the "produce the same in us" referent in paragraph 3), and it's minor enough that I would not call it a blocking accessibility problem on its own — a strict reader could parse it correctly on a normal read-through. The heavier apparent difficulty in the chapter — the causal chain of pressure → nerve → brain/heart → resistance → Fancy in paragraph 3, and the scholastic-jargon terms in paragraph 4 — is inherent to Hobbes's argument (mechanistic physiology in one case, intentional parody of Scholastic language in the other) and is not something a modern-English rendering should or could smooth away without changing what the chapter is doing.

**Summary:** The chapter reads as clear, purposeful modern prose. A general adult reader can follow Hobbes's argument from individual thought → Sense as the common root of all ideas → the mechanistic account of what causes sensation → the contrast with the Scholastic "species" doctrine, without getting lost in archaic syntax or unresolved constructions. The one lingering rough edge (a slightly reach-back pronoun reference in paragraph 3) is optional-fix territory; everything else that's hard is hard because Hobbes is doing something hard, not because the modern-English wording is failing him.

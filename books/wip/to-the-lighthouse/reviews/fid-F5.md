## Adjudication of renderer-flagged passages
- **32.0** Keeping "little Chinese eyes" is correct: it is Woolf's text, and the brief says to preserve it. The gloss "Brompton Road in London" is short and accurate. OK.
- **32.4** Naming "Mrs. Ramsay" as the one on the step with James is correct: she is the figure in Lily's original design. OK. (Two separate minor issues in this paragraph are listed in the JSON.)
- **32.6** "wrapped in deep silence, she watched": the rendering keeps Woolf's dangling modifier, so it stays open whether the boat or Lily is shrouded. OK.
- **33.6** Cowper gloss "a line from Cowper’s ‘The Castaway,’ about a sailor drowned alone": accurate and short, and it sits in the narration. OK.
- **33.7** The Cowper couplet is verbatim, line break included. OK. **41.13** Verbatim. OK.
- **33.9** The clouded-hill simile keeps all its parts: the cloud, the gravity, the hills pondering, pity or malicious rejoicing, "overcast". OK.
- **34.1** "her" = Minta is correct. The sentence is about his "manner to Minta" and continues "could he believe that Minta read them?", and "She dragged them about" is also Minta. "him" = Mr. Ramsay is also correct.
- **34.4** "Mrs. Ramsay would say" is correct: it is the beach memory. OK.
- **34.8** "wreathed, made up, garish" keeps "wreathed" and renders "tinted" fairly. OK.
- **34.15** The parenthesis is kept whole, with every image (star, fire of savages, winy smell, pearl brooch, signal fire). OK.
- **34.22** The careful read found the content complete and the ordering sound. The one weakness is that "she would hint it without words" settles what Woolf leaves as interrupted intention ("was going to say, or … to hint, wordlessly; when …"). Recommended fix: "she was going to hint it".
- **36.1** "lifting to her forehead a wreath of white flowers and going off with it" keeps Woolf's "with which she went" (the going/dying). OK.
- **37.3** "sovereign" → "a gold coin" is an acceptable gloss. OK.
- **37.6** "It’s going to rain" is light modernization of dialogue. OK.
- **37.10** "she had gone rigid" is left unresolved until "His mother" follows, as in the source. OK.
- **40.7** Moving the full stop inside the parenthesis and lowercasing "people" after the dash is a harmless punctuation normalization. OK.
- **40.11** "there ought to be one pair completely blind to her beauty" is a fair rendering of "must be one that was stone blind". OK.
- **40.16–17** "She" is left unresolved, as in the source. OK.
- **Refrains:** "We perished" / "each alone" (33.4), "We perished, each alone" (33.9, 41.19), "we perish, each alone" (33.11), "how we perished, each alone" (39.2), "It is finished." (42.1) and "I have had my vision." (42.2) are all word for word. "it will be fine or it won’t be fine" (40.5) and "women can’t write, women can’t paint" (32.3–4, 40.9) are kept.

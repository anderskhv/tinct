## Rulings on the passages the renderers flagged as uncertain
- **19.0 "fine gravings … drawn with steel instruments"** becomes "all the fine lines appeared, as though cut with steel tools". Accurate. Woolf's image is already a metaphor, so "as though" adds nothing new. Accept.
- **19.0 / 19.5 Scott glosses.** "old Sir Walter Scott's" and "(all in Scott's The Antiquary)" are short and correct: Mucklebackit and Steenie's drowning come from *The Antiquary*. Accept.
- **"wash-leather" → "chamois-leather".** Correct: wash-leather is soft chamois-type leather. It is used the same way throughout. Accept.
- **19.26 end of The Window.** "It's going to be wet tomorrow" matches the source word for word. Accept. One small recommendation for the closing sentence: "For once again" can be heard as "for once", which reverses the meaning.
- **22.2 "no image with semblance of serving and divine promptitude"** becomes "no image that seems to serve him with divine readiness". This is a defensible reading of a sentence that is opaque in the source too. "The hand dwindles in his hand" is kept as Woolf wrote it, which is right, since the obscurity is hers. Accept.
- **24.0 "deprecated the scorn and anger of the world" → "begged off …".** Reject. "Begged off" means asking to be excused from a commitment. Suggested replacement: "asked to be spared the scorn and anger of the world".
- **24.0 "baseborn" → "born out of wedlock".** Accurate. Accept.
- **Time Passes kept closer to Woolf's lyric rhythm.** Appropriate. The chapters still read as genuine renderings, and I found no omissions. One invented detail is flagged in 25.5 ("on a shelf behind a door").
- **25.8 implied subject made "they".** Accept. The source's dangling construction clearly means the people walking the beach from 25.7.
- **27.0 Michaelmas gloss** ("the autumn quarter day"). Accurate and short. Accept. **The "she" in the last sentence** ("Yes, she could see Mrs. Ramsay, as she came up the drive with the washing") keeps Woolf's construction. Accept. The blocking problem is the *earlier* sentence in the same paragraph: "She could see her now, coming up the drive with the washing". There the participle now attaches to Mrs. Ramsay, so she seems to be the one carrying the washing.
- **28.2 "red-hot poker".** Keep it unglossed. The flower sense (a garden plant surviving among the nettles) and the fire-iron sense both work, and Woolf leaves them both open.
- **28.3 Waverley → "Scott's Waverley novels".** Short and correct. Accept.
- **29.0 "his head crowned".** Kept, with night personified as "he". Accept. A separate issue in the same paragraph: "the house's sleep" pins down an antecedent that the source leaves as "its" (recommended fix).
- **Mrs. McNab / Mrs. Bast dialect.** Mostly well judged: "neither", "she did", "Many's the laugh". A few additions go beyond Woolf's light marking ("in them days", "it was her that was dead", "something shocking"). I flagged only "in them days" (27.4). The others are within tolerance.
- **31.11 kitchen-table symbol.** Now reads "remembering the symbol Andrew had given her when she was vague about what Mr. Ramsay actually thought about". This correctly untangles Woolf's syntax: Andrew gave Lily the image in The Window. Accept.
- **Bracketed paragraphs** (21.3, 22.3, 25.1, 25.3, 25.6, 25.9, 28.12): all are still bracketed and flat. 22.3 keeps the repeated "he stretched his arms out". Accept.

## Minor notes (no edit proposed)
- Time Passes 25 uses unspaced em dashes, while other chapters use spaced ones. The source uses unspaced dashes, so this is a consistency question for the lead, not a fidelity problem.

# Fidelity review F3 — To the Lighthouse, chapter 17 (the dinner)

**Coverage:** 17.0–17.77, all 78 paragraphs read in full as source/modern pairs with their neighbours, then the whole chapter re-read for cross-paragraph and cross-seam issues (the seam falls between 17.38 and 17.39).

**Counts:** 0 blocking, 7 recommended (see `fid-F3.json`).

**Overall:** The chapter is faithful and complete. There are no omissions of images, names or asides. Verse is verbatim, italics (`_Middlemarch_`, `_Anna Karenina_`) are intact, the ellipses at 17.18/17.32/17.37 are kept, and the "Women can't write, women can't paint" refrain and "the thing is made that remains forever after" are recognizable.

**Seam:** The two halves read consistently in register, in "Chinese eyes", "old fogies", "Lighthouse" (capitalized), "drawing room" and the "move the tree toward the middle" refrain. The one inconsistency is Lily's reminder object: "salt shaker" at 17.4/17.27 against "saltcellar" at 17.51. Fix: use "saltcellar" in all three places.

## Adjudication of renderer-flagged passages
- **17.2 near-verbatim dialogue:** Accept. It is a short line, and "put"→"leave" is enough.
- **17.10 / 17.21 "Women can't write, women can't paint" order:** Accept. The source order in ch. 17 is "write, paint", and 17.21 keeps "can't paint, can't write" as Woolf has it.
- **"chemist"→"pharmacist" (17.24):** Accept. This is the correct sense for a present-day reader.
- **"salt cellar"→"salt shaker":** Reject. The object changes, and it is inconsistent with 17.51. Use "saltcellar" throughout.
- **"bear garden"→"madhouse" (17.20):** Accept. The force matches, and it suits Mrs. Ramsay's social-manner hyperbole.
- **17.21 "Chinese eyes" kept:** Accept. It is Woolf's recurring descriptor for Lily and is also kept at 17.55.
- **17.22 "the tube"→"the Underground":** Accept. It is accurate and needs no further gloss.
- **17.30 "If at all"→"If ever":** Accept. It keeps the dry reply to "not acquire until later in life".
- **17.31 / 17.32 "Pray heaven"→"Please God":** Accept. The register is equivalent, and the phrase is consistent across both paragraphs.
- **17.37 "What had she done with it":** Accept. Keeping the question open (Rose's arrangement) is right.
- **17.55 "she exempted her from the rest":** Accept. "made a deliberate exception of Lily … did not count her with the rest" is correct: she exempts Lily from the laughing table. "(Lily had been thinking about love.)" names the correct referent.
- **17.60 "Sir Walter"→"Sir Walter Scott":** Accept. It is a minimal, accurate gloss.
- **17.62 "she" read as Minta:** Accept. The interposed "Mrs. Ramsay saw" separates this "she" from Mrs. Ramsay, and Minta ("whose instinct was fine") is the natural antecedent. Mrs. Ramsay's own wish follows in the next sentence.
- **Mile End Road gloss (17.52):** Accept. "in the East End of London" is short and accurate.
- **"boobies"→"dunces" (17.44):** Accept. It is the right sense (fond term for the not-clever) and is used consistently.
- **"vail her crest, dismount her batteries" (17.54):** Accept. "lower her crest and take down her guns" keeps both military/heraldic images.
- **"furze"→"gorse" (17.54):** Accept.
- **Luriana Lurilee verse (17.66, 17.68, 17.70, 17.73, 17.75):** Verified verbatim, with line breaks and punctuation.

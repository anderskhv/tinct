Every six months he read one of them, he said. And why should that make Charles Tansley angry? His manner told her how it was: he wanted to assert himself, and he would always be like this until he had his professorship or had married his wife, and then he would not need to keep on saying, “I—I—I.” Because that was all his criticism of poor Sir Walter Scott—or maybe it was Jane Austen—came to. “I—I—I.” She could hear from his voice, from his emphasis and his uneasiness, that he was thinking of himself and of the impression he made.

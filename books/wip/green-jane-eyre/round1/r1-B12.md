Note on the tense group (17.72, 17.79–17.81, 17.85–17.86): Brontë moves into the historic present for the gentlemen's entrance and the drawing-room tableau ("At last coffee is brought in ... I sit in the shade ... they come"). She drops back to past at "How near had I approached him" (17.81) and for 17.82–17.84, then returns to present for 17.85–17.86. The modern text had flattened all of it to past. The repairs restore the source's tense pattern and change nothing else, except in 17.85: "in a corner" was invented and has been removed, and the dropped "apparently" has been restored. These are grouped as recommended. If the edition's policy is to flatten tense, reject them together.

## Considered and rejected
- 15.4: "croquant ... (overlook the barbarism)" dropped along with the French word. The meaning ("crunching chocolate bonbons") is present, and the aside only makes sense with the French kept. Acceptable.
- 15.11: dangling "continuing his walk in silence, I ventured" is in the source too.
- 15.18: "grim paternity" and "natural claim" softened. The substance is kept.
- 15.39: "elves" becomes "spirits". Acceptable modernization.
- 15.74: "expedient" becomes "excuse". What she says next is an invented pretext, so this fits.
- 16.7: "Did no one hear him move?" becomes "hear him?". Trivial.
- 16.17: "malignant pranks" becomes "dangerous tricks". Close enough.
- 16.20: "danger or annoyance" becomes "danger". Minor.
- 16.33: Adèle's French is left untranslated, as in the source. Consistent with the chapter's handling of French.
- 16.70: "self-interest" becomes "self-preservation" and "accursed senselessness" becomes "wretched foolishness". Slightly softer, but the tone survives.
- 16.72: "oriental eye" becomes "dark eastern eyes". "Dark" is a small addition, consistent with Mrs. Fairfax's description.

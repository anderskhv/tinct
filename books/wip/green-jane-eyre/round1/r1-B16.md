Main findings:
- 23.49: "my heart was still" was rendered "my heart was full to breaking" (invented).
- 23.48: "Are you anything akin to me" became "Are you anything to me" (a different question).
- 24.2: "good or bad, they must partake of my jubilee" (deserving or not) was misread as "whether it brought them good or ill".
- 24.41: "I desire to be entreated—" became "I long to be asked, and to say yes." The added clause is invented, and the source's broken-off line was completed.
- 24.129: Jane's jab about "extensive slave-purchases" was omitted.
- 24.137: "Your regard" was raised to "Your love".
- 24.74: "put up her spectacles" (put them away) was reversed to "put on".

On the caller's warning that chapter 24 runs long: I checked specifically for embellishment. The clear invention is 24.41. 24.148 ("come closer") and 24.2 are smaller invented or misread details. Most of the extra length comes from split sentences and small connectives, which are acceptable.

## Considered and rejected
- 23.0: "seldom favour even singly" → "seldom blessed … even once in a season". Close enough in sense.
- 23.11: "sheepishly" → "meekly". A small shift in tone, acceptable.
- 23.16: the phrenological "organ of Adhesiveness" → "strong capacity for attachment". A legitimate gloss.
- 23.28 / 23.42 and other italic emphases (_are_, _you_, _never_) are not carried over. This is a typography convention of the edition, not flagged.
- 23.50: "two hundred miles or so" loses "or so". Trivial.
- 23.75: "play a farce" → "playing a joke". Acceptable.
- 23.99: "for I suffer" → "for I am in agony". A slight intensification, but it fits his state in 23.100.
- 24.1: "life in its colour" / "fount of fruition … lustrous ripple" are compressed. The images are kept in essence.
- 24.24: "ape in a harlequin's jacket" → "monkey in a clown's jacket". Acceptable modernization.
- 24.50: "Jew-usurer" → "moneylender". An editorial softening of a period slur that keeps the sense. Left for the editor to decide; not proposed.
- 24.52: "over-persuasion" → "persuasion". A minor nuance.
- 24.108 / 24.114 / 24.124 / 24.148: the French is translated rather than kept. The chapter does this consistently and the meaning is present.
- 24.131: "three-tailed bashaw" → "mighty overlord". Loses the Ottoman title but keeps the function; borderline, not proposed.
- 24.155: "the following strain" → "a sweet song". Acceptable.
- 24.179: "bathos of sentiment" → "swamp of sentiment". Acceptable.
- 24.182: "&c." is dropped. Trivial.

## Source-text notes

- 24.9: "the satin-smooth hazel hair, and the radiant hazel eyes" is authorial. Jane's aside corrects it. Not an error.

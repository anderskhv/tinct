## Considered and rejected
- 32.3 "he could not conceal it from her, try as he might": the source says "did not, because he could not"; the added "try as he might" is a light, compatible gloss.
- 32.6 "lusus naturæ" → "freak of nature": a correct translation, so the meaning is kept.
- 32.16 "Not in gloomy thought": an interpretive gloss on "in thought", which is acceptable in context. "I mistrust you still" → "I still worry about you" is acceptable.
- 32.34 quotation marks dropped around Coleridge's "burst" / "the silent sea": the words are kept, so the allusion survives.
- 32.41 "quartet sped" rendered as "quarter-hour flew by": correct (see source problems).
- 32.63 "Cui bono?" → "What would be the point?": the meaning is kept.
- 33.2 "forgot storm in music" → "music of the verse": a gloss that keeps the meaning.
- 33.52 "ultra-marine, lake, vermillion" → "blue, crimson, vermilion", and "Indian ink" → "ink": legitimate modernization.
- 33.110 "the two girls" → "the two women": this matches their age, so it is acceptable.
- 33.132 "a thousand leagues" → "a thousand miles": the hyperbole is kept.
- 33.114 "loved barrenly" → "loved without the power to give anything back": a good clarifying gloss.

## Source-text problems (recorded, not fixed)
- 32.41: "Amidst this hush the quartet sped" — "quartet" is evidently a typo for "quarter" (i.e. the quarter-hour). The modern text is correct.
- 33.92: this source paragraph duplicates 33.91 ("And I am a hard woman,—impossible to put off") without quotation marks. It looks like a Gutenberg transcription artifact, and the modern edition duplicates it too. I left the alignment alone and only kept its wording consistent with 33.91.

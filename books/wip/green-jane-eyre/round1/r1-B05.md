## Considered and rejected
- 1.13 "So did the black horned creature…": reads as "[It terrified me.] So did X". It is grammatical and faithful.
- 1.33 "reached its peak and broken through" for "passed its climax": acceptable.
- 1.37 "the maid Abbot" for "her maid Abbot": 2.2 establishes her as lady's-maid, so nothing is lost.
- 2.0 "_out_ of myself": the italic emphasis is lost, but the wording is kept.
- 2.12 "cunning" for "cover": a fair gloss of the sense.
- 2.20 "shrine" for "tabernacle", and "large" square chamber: the sacred sense is kept, and "large" is supported by "one of the largest".
- 2.30 "grudging promise" for "hard-wrung pledge": close enough.
- 3.10 "Such remarkable kindness!": the irony survives.
- 3.29 "the depth of suffering" for "morbid suffering to which I was a prey": acceptable compression.
- 3.77 "knit" for "net" (purses): a small technical inaccuracy that I left alone. I did fix "stocks" in the same paragraph.
- 3.87 "Welsh rarebit" for "Welsh rabbit": a standard modern equivalent.
- The verse (1.8, 3.22, 3.24–3.28) matches the source.

## Source-text notes
- 3.20: the source spells it "Brobdignag", which is Brontë's/Gutenberg's spelling. The modern text had "Brobdingnag", and the proposal restores the source spelling as the instructions require. If the editors prefer Swift's spelling, reject that part.
- 1.9 and 1.7: the Bewick quotations appear in the source with quotation marks. I found no transcription problems in this batch.

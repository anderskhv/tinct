## Considered and rejected
- **27.39:** "the Indian … in his canoe" became "a man … in his canoe". The canoe-on-the-rapids image survives, so I treat this as a likely editorial modernisation.
- **27.6:** A closing quote was added where the source leaves the speech open into 27.7. This is a typographic normalisation.
- **27.8:** The Nathan parable has "plate" and "arms" instead of "bread" and "bosom". The parable is intact and recognisable.
- **27.74:** "momentarily mingled" (meaning "every moment") is dropped. It is minor.
- **27.81:** "lulled and baffled" became "defeated". It is minor.
- **27.100:** "had much ado often" lost the word "often". It is minor.
- **27.150:** The moon's "she/her" became "it". The meaning is intact.
- **27.160:** "far worse than my own abandonment" has "own" added. The source's "my abandonment" is itself ambiguous, so this is acceptable.
- Italic emphasis is dropped throughout (_my wife_, _I_, _not_ and others). This is consistent with the edition style.

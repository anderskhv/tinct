### 31.20 "imposed" vs "opposed"
The round-1 reading "opposed" is right in sense and should stand. The next clause, "but since his death, I have not a legitimate obstacle to contend with", treats the father as the obstacle, which only works if he was against the missionary plan. Taken literally, "imposed the determination" says the father forced the decision on him. That contradicts the "but" clause and St. John's own story (31.19–31.20) that he reached the decision himself after "a season of darkness and struggling". The BASELINE "reinforced" was a real meaning reversal. One caveat: a web check found only Gutenberg-derived copies (e.g. Victorian Web), and they read "imposed". I could not independently confirm the first-edition or critical-edition reading. Even so, the fix is sound on internal evidence alone.

## The four required fixes

### F1 — para 1: agentless passive restored. **CONFIRMED FIXED.**

- **Source:** `and which in the Common-wealth of Rome was called Concio, as he that spake was called Ecclesiastes, and Concionator.`
- **Candidate now:** `The Roman commonwealth called this same kind of assembly Concio, and the speaker was called Ecclesiastes, and Concionator.`

Exactly the fix Review 2 specified. "The Roman commonwealth" is now the subject
of the *Concio* clause only; "the speaker was called…" is a separate agentless
passive, so the candidate no longer asserts that Rome called the speaker
*Ecclesiastes*. The cross-boundary contradiction with the paragraph's own
Latin/Greek gloss two sentences later is gone. N2 ("and", not "or") survives the
re-edit. No new problem introduced by the re-split.

### F2 — para 2: "the redeemed in heaven" gloss removed. **CONFIRMED FIXED**, but the replacement introduces a different, smaller defect — see **F5**.

- **Source:** `Sometimes also for the Elect onely; as (Ephes. 5.27.) "A Glorious Church, without spot, or wrinkle, holy, and without blemish;" which is meant of the Church Triumphant, or, Church To Come.`
- **Candidate now:** `Sometimes, too, it means only the elect, as in Eph. 5:27: 'A glorious church, without spot, or wrinkle, holy, and without blemish'—meaning what Hobbes calls the "church triumphant," or — in other words — the church still to come: the church in its final, glorified state.`

Checked specifically for the doctrinal overreach reappearing in new clothes:

- **"the redeemed in heaven"** — gone. No location is now assigned to the
  triumphant church, so the gloss no longer states as Hobbes's meaning a
  position Hobbes argues against in ch. 38.
- **"as distinct from the church still on earth"** — gone. No unstated
  counterpart (church militant) is imported.
- **"the church in its final, glorified state"** — this is the wording Review 2
  proposed. It is licensed: "glorified" is carried by the source's own
  *"A Glorious Church, without spot, or wrinkle"*, and "final" is carried by
  *"Church To Come"*. It asserts no place, no time-scheme and no institutional
  contrast. **No overreach.**
- **"or — in other words —"** — checked as a possible new interpretive move.
  Hobbes's "or" here is appositional: he equates *Church Triumphant* with
  *Church To Come*. Making that equation explicit is explicitation of a relation
  already in the source, not a new claim. Acceptable.
- **Placement of the gloss.** The gloss now sits after a colon at the end, so it
  reads most naturally as glossing "the church still to come". Since the source
  equates the two terms, glossing either glosses both. Not a defect.
- The clause still correctly reports this as the **"elect only"** sense, with
  "only" preserved, and Hobbes's compressed Eph. 5:27 is still compressed — not
  silently expanded to canonical wording.

So F2's substance is fixed. What the replacement added is an author-naming
intrusion; that is charged separately below as F5.

### F3 — para 4: "rightly" deleted. **CONFIRMED FIXED.**

- **Source:** `The Doctors of the Church, are called Pastors; so also are Civill Soveraignes:`
- **Candidate now:** `The teachers of the church are called pastors — and so are civil sovereigns.`

Exactly Review 2's fix. The evaluative endorsement is gone; the sentence is
again a statement of usage, leaving Hobbes's argument for the claim where he
puts it, in the following sentences. ("Doctors" → "teachers" is Review 1's N6,
unchanged, non-blocking.)

### F4 — para 4: "the Christian and the man" restored. **CONFIRMED FIXED.**

- **Source:** `and (which is more) in every Christian mans own brest, between the Christian, and the Man.`
- **Candidate now:** `and (what is more) in every Christian man's own breast — between the Christian and the man.`

Both interpretive additions are gone: "citizen" is back to "the man", and
"loyalty" is removed, so Hobbes again names two identities rather than two
allegiances. The clause is now word-for-word the source apart from
modernization of "which is more" → "what is more" and "brest" → "breast", and
the parallelism with the three preceding pairs is restored.

## The two new, previously unreviewed accessibility changes

### (5a) Para 0 — the sense-shift sequencing. **CLAIM CHECKS OUT; timeline is the source's own.**

- **Source:** `Sometimes (though not often) it is taken for Gods House, that is to say, for a Temple… but this is Metaphorically put, for the Congregation there assembled; and hath been since used for the Edifice it self, to distinguish between the Temples of Christians, and Idolaters.`
- **Candidate:** `This use is metaphorical: the word first stood for the congregation assembled there, and only later came to be used for the building itself, to distinguish the temples of Christians from those of idolaters.`

The brief asks whether "metaphorical-use-first, building-sense-later" is the
source's assertion or an invented sequence. **It is the source's own.** Two
independent anchors:

1. `this is Metaphorically put, for the Congregation there assembled` — in 1 Cor.
   14:34 the word, though it looks like it names a place, stands for the
   assembled congregation. The congregation sense is the one operative in the
   scriptural text.
2. `and hath been **since** used for the Edifice it self` — "since" is an
   explicit temporal marker in the source. Hobbes states that the
   building-denoting use came afterwards.

So the candidate's "first… and only later" restates the source's
metaphor-then-edifice ordering rather than inventing one. The purpose clause
("to distinguish the temples of Christians from those of idolaters") stays
attached to the later building sense, exactly as in the source — a plausible
mis-attachment (hanging it on the metaphor instead) did **not** happen.

Two small notes, neither a defect:
- "the word first **stood for**" converts Hobbes's semantic formula ("put for")
  into a temporal-priority statement. The source's "since" licenses this, so it
  is explicitation, not addition.
- "**only** later" adds a mild emphasis particle not in the source; it does not
  change what is asserted.

The rest of para 0 is unchanged and consistent with what Review 1 certified:
"though not often" preserved, `Kuriake → Kyrke → Church` derivation intact and
in order, "God's house" / "house of prayer" / "Christ's house" all present.

### (5b) Para 4 — "Spiritualists, and Temporalists". **MEANING PRESERVED IN DIRECTION, BUT THE GLOSS ADDS A CLAIM. Non-blocking; fix recommended — N7 below.**

- **Source:** `between Spiritualists, and Temporalists;`
- **Candidate:** `between those who claim spiritual authority and those who claim temporal authority`

Nothing is **lost**: the paired opposition survives, it stays in the right slot
in the four-part faction list (church/state → spiritualists/temporalists → sword
of justice/shield of faith → the Christian/the man), and the direction of the
antithesis is correct. A gloss is genuinely licensed here — "spiritualist" in
modern English means something else entirely (a medium/séance sense that would
actively mislead), so leaving the bare word would be worse.

What is **added** is the verb "claim". Hobbes's nouns name two parties — the
partisans of spiritual and of temporal government (the 17th-century
*spiritualty*/*temporalty* pairing: clergy side and lay-civil side). "Those who
claim spiritual authority" recasts party membership as an act of asserting
authority. Two consequences, both small:

- It narrows the group to those actually asserting authority, dropping lay
  partisans of the spiritual side who claim nothing themselves.
- "Claim" carries a faint whiff of pretension — a contested claim rather than a
  side one is on. Hobbes is hostile to the clerical side, but at this point in
  the sentence he is listing the *parties to a civil war*, not adjudicating
  their claims; the adjudication comes in the following sentences.

It also flattens the tight two-word antithesis into a 12-word symmetrical
construction, which weakens the list's rhythm — a style cost, not a fidelity
one.

This is a **non-blocking** note (N7), not a required fix: the meaning direction
is right and the gloss is licensed. The closer wording is
`between the spiritual party and the temporal party` or
`between churchmen and laymen`, either of which keeps the party sense and the
brevity without importing "claim".

## NEW defect (fix required)

### F5 — para 2: `what Hobbes calls` breaks the first-person voice and misattributes a standard term

- **Source:** `which is meant of the Church Triumphant, or, Church To Come.`
- **Candidate:** `—meaning what Hobbes calls the "church triumphant," or — in other words — the church still to come: the church in its final, glorified state.`
- **What's wrong (additions / actors):** Three-part problem, all from the two
  words `what Hobbes calls`.
  1. **Voice contradiction.** The edition renders Hobbes in his own first
     person — one paragraph later the same chapter says *"I define a CHURCH to
     be…"*. A text that says "I define" in para 3 cannot say "what Hobbes calls"
     in para 2; the narrator would have to be simultaneously Hobbes and someone
     describing him. This is the only third-person authorial reference anywhere
     in the chapter, so it also reads as an unsignalled editor's voice inside
     the body text.
  2. **Misattribution.** "What Hobbes calls X" implies X is Hobbes's own label.
     *Church Triumphant* is a long-standing common term he is invoking, not
     coining. The source's `which is meant of the Church Triumphant` treats it
     as the received name for a thing; the candidate makes it idiosyncratic.
  3. **Unlicensed addition.** No attribution phrase exists in the source at this
     point; the clause is a flat gloss.
- **Fix:** delete the two words. The clause becomes
  `—meaning the "church triumphant," or — in other words — the church still to come: the church in its final, glorified state.`
  This is a pure deletion; the F2 repair is untouched by it.

This defect did not exist before round 3 — it entered with the F2 rewrite.

## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH FIXES REQUIRED.**

**All four required fixes from Review 2 are correctly applied.** F1 restores the
agentless passive and removes the Rome/*Ecclesiastes* misattribution; F2 removes
"the redeemed in heaven" and "as distinct from the church still on earth" and
replaces them with a gloss ("the church in its final, glorified state") that
asserts no location, no counterpart and no doctrine the source does not carry —
**the overreach is not reintroduced in another form**; F3 deletes "rightly"; F4
restores "between the Christian and the man". Paragraph alignment is still exact
(5/5), nothing was merged, split, reordered, dropped or invented at the
paragraph level.

Of the two new accessibility changes: **para 0's sequencing claim is the
source's own** — Hobbes's "hath been **since** used for the Edifice it self"
explicitly licenses metaphor-first / building-later, and the purpose clause stays
attached to the correct sense. **Para 4's spiritualists/temporalists gloss loses
nothing and keeps the antithesis in the right slot**, but adds "claim"; that is a
recommended, non-blocking improvement (N7), not a blocker.

One fix is required before this round can be certified:

1. **F5 (para 2)** — delete `what Hobbes calls`. It breaks the chapter's
   first-person voice (para 3: "I define a CHURCH"), misattributes a standard
   term to Hobbes as his own coinage, and is unlicensed by the source. Result:
   `—meaning the "church triumphant," or — in other words — the church still to come: the church in its final, glorified state.`

This is a two-word deletion in an otherwise sound clause. No blocking defect —
no invented claim, dropped clause, flipped negation, reversed causality or broken
condition — was found anywhere in the chapter. No re-draft is needed, and a
fourth full pass is not warranted: once F5 is applied, a one-clause spot-check of
para 2 is sufficient to close this chapter.

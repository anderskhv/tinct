For although we find no time before it, since wisdom was created before all things, this is not that Wisdom which is entirely equal and coeternal with You, our God, His Father, the Wisdom through whom all things were created and in whom, as the Beginning, You created heaven and earth. It is rather the wisdom that is created, namely the intellectual nature, which becomes light by contemplating the light. For this too, though created, is called wisdom. But as great as the difference is between the Light that enlightens and the light that is enlightened, so great is the difference between the Wisdom that creates and the wisdom that is created; just as between the Righteousness that justifies and the righteousness that is brought about by justification. For we too are called Your righteousness; as a certain servant of Yours says, That we might be made the righteousness of God in Him. Since, then, a certain created wisdom was created before all things, the rational and intellectual mind of that chaste city of Yours, our mother, which is above and is free and eternal in the heavens (in what heavens, if not those that praise You, the heavens of heavens? for this is also the heaven of heaven which is the Lord's); although we find no time before it, because what was created before all things also comes before the creation of time, still the eternity of the Creator Himself is before it. From Him, when it was made, it took its beginning, not indeed a beginning in time, since time did not yet exist, but the beginning of its own creation.

@@ 19
So it comes from You, our God, in such a way that it is plainly something other than You, and not the Selfsame. For although we find time neither before it nor even in it, since it is fit always to behold Your face and is never turned away from it, so that it is not varied by any change, there is still in it a liability to change, by which it would grow dark and cold if it did not cling to You with a great love and so, like an everlasting noon, shine and glow from You. O house full of light and beauty! I have loved your beauty, and the place of the habitation of the glory of my Lord, your builder and owner. Let my pilgrimage sigh for you; and I say to Him who made you that He should take possession of me too in you, since He made me as well. I have gone astray like a lost sheep; yet on the shoulders of my Shepherd, your builder, I hope to be carried back to you.

"What do you say to me, you who contradict me, whom I was just addressing, and who nevertheless believe that Moses was the holy servant of God and that his books are the oracles of the Holy Spirit? Is not this house of God, though not coeternal with God, yet eternal after its own measure in the heavens, where you look in vain for changes of times, because you will not find them? For what it is always good to cling fast to God rises above all extension and every revolving span of time." "It is," they say. "Then of all that my heart cried out to my God when it heard inwardly the voice of His praise, which part do you claim is false? Is it that the matter was formless, and that there, because there was no form, there was no order? But where there was no order, there could be no succession of times; and yet this 'almost nothing,' insofar as it was not altogether nothing, was certainly from Him from whom is whatever is, in whatever degree it is anything at all." "This too," they say, "we do not deny."

With these, my God, I want to talk a little in Your presence: with those who grant that all these things are true which Your Truth whispers to my soul. As for those who deny them, let them bark and deafen themselves with their own noise as much as they like; I will try to persuade them to be quiet and to open in themselves a way for Your word. But if they refuse and push me away, I beg You, my God, do not be silent toward me. Speak truly in my heart, for You alone speak in that way; and I will leave them outside, blowing on the dust and raising it into their own eyes, and I will enter my chamber and sing there a song of love to You, groaning with groanings that cannot be uttered in my pilgrimage, and remembering Jerusalem, with my heart lifted up toward it: Jerusalem my homeland, Jerusalem my mother, and You who reign over it, the Enlightener, Father, Guardian, Husband, the pure and strong delight and solid joy, and all good things beyond words, all of them at once, because You are the one supreme and true Good. And I will not be turned away until You gather all that I am out of this scattered and disordered state into the peace of that dearest mother of ours, where the first fruits of my spirit already are, and from which I am made certain of these things, and until You conform and confirm it forever, my God, my Mercy. But as for those who do not call all these truths false, who honor Your holy Scripture, set forth by holy Moses, placing it, as we do, at the summit of authority to be followed, and who still contradict me on some point, to them I answer this: be the judge, our God, between my Confessions and these people's contradictions.

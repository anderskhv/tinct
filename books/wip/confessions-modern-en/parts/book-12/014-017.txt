Having considered these things, as far as You grant, my God, as far as You rouse me to knock, and as far as You open to me when I knock, I find two things that You have made outside the reach of time, though neither of them is coeternal with You. The one is so formed that, without any pause in its contemplation, without any interval of change, changeable yet never changed, it fully enjoys Your eternity and unchangeableness. The other was so formless that it had nothing that could be changed from one form into another, whether of motion or of rest, so as to become subject to time. But You did not leave this formless, because before any day, in the beginning, You created heaven and earth, the two things I was speaking of. But the earth was invisible and unformed, and darkness was upon the deep. In these words formlessness is conveyed to us, so that those who cannot conceive of a complete absence of all form which still does not come down to nothing may be led on by degrees. Out of it another heaven was to be created, together with a visible and well-formed earth, and the waters set in their various order, and whatever else in the forming of the world is recorded as having been created, not without days; and this because such things are of a nature in which the successive changes of times can take place, since they are subject to appointed changes of motions and of forms.

@@ 15
This, then, is how I understand it for now, my God, when I hear Your Scripture say, In the beginning God made heaven and earth: and the earth was invisible and unformed, and darkness was upon the deep, without mentioning on what day You created them. This is how I understand it: that it is because of the heaven of heaven — that intellectual heaven, whose understanding knows all at once, not in part, not darkly, not through a glass, but as a whole, openly, face to face; not this thing now and that thing later, but (as I said) knows all at once, without any succession of times — and because of the earth invisible and unformed, which is without any succession of times, the succession that brings "this thing now, that thing later," since where there is no form there is no distinguishing of this and that; it is, then, on account of these two, the one formed from the first and the other wholly formless, the one heaven, but the heaven of heaven, the other earth, but the earth invisible and unformed; because of these two, I understand, Your Scripture said without mention of days, In the beginning God made heaven and earth. For it immediately added which earth it meant; and also, by recording that the firmament was created on the second day and called heaven, it shows us which heaven it had spoken of before, without mention of days.

Wonderful depth of Your words! Look, their surface lies before us, coaxing the little ones; and yet they are a wonderful depth, my God, a wonderful depth! It makes one shudder to look into it: a shudder of reverence and a trembling of love. Its enemies I hate fiercely. Oh, that You would slay them with Your two-edged sword, so that they would no longer be its enemies! For this is how I love to have them slain: slain to themselves, so that they may live to You. But look, there are others, not fault-finders but admirers of the book of Genesis. "The Spirit of God," they say, "who wrote these things through His servant Moses, did not want those words to be understood this way. He did not want them understood as you say, but in another way, as we say." To them, with You Yourself, God of us all, as judge, I answer as follows.

@@ 17
"Will you say that what Truth tells me with a strong voice in my inner ear about the eternity of the Creator is false: that His substance is in no way changed by time, and that His will is not separate from His substance? It follows that He does not will one thing now and another thing later, but wills all that He wills once, and at once, and always; not again and again, not now this and now that; He does not afterward will what He did not will before, nor cease to will what He willed before, because such a will is changeable, and nothing changeable is eternal; but our God is eternal. Again, He tells me in my inner ear that the expectation of things to come becomes direct sight when they have come, and this same sight becomes memory when they have passed. Now every act of thought that varies in this way is changeable, and nothing changeable is eternal; but our God is eternal." I gather these things and put them together, and I find that my God, the eternal God, did not make any creature by some new act of will, and that His knowledge admits of nothing transitory. "What, then, will you say, you who contradict me? Are these things false?" "No," they say. "What then? Is it false that every nature already formed, or matter capable of form, exists only from Him who is supremely good because He supremely is?" "We do not deny this either," they say. "What then? Do you deny that there is a certain sublime creature, cleaving with so chaste a love to the true and truly eternal God that, although it is not coeternal with Him, it does not break away from Him or dissolve into the variety and succession of times, but rests in the truest contemplation of Him alone?" For You, God, show Yourself to the one who loves You as much as You command, and You are enough for him; and so he does not turn aside from You, nor toward himself. This is the house of God, not of earthly mold, nor of any bodily heavenly mass, but spiritual, and sharing in Your eternity, because it is without any falling away forever. For You have established it for ever and ever; You have given it a law which it shall not pass. Yet it is not coeternal with You, God, because it is not without beginning; for it was made.

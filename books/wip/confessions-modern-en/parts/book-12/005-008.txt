But as for me, Lord, if I were to confess to You with my tongue and my pen the whole of what You Yourself have taught me about that matter — I first heard its name without understanding it, when people who did not understand it themselves told me about it, and so I pictured it as having countless and varied forms, and therefore did not picture it at all. My mind turned over foul and horrible "forms" in complete disorder, but "forms" all the same; and I called it formless, not because it lacked all form, but because it had the kind of form that my mind would turn away from, as strange and jarring, if it were presented to it, and that human frailty would be disturbed by. Even so, what I pictured was formless not by being deprived of all form, but only by comparison with more beautiful forms; and true reason urged me to strip it completely of every last remnant of form whatever, if I wanted to conceive of matter that was absolutely formless. And I could not. For I could sooner imagine that something deprived of all form did not exist at all than conceive of something between form and nothing, neither formed nor nothing, a formless almost-nothing. So my mind gave up questioning my spirit about it, since my spirit was full of the images of formed bodies, changing and varying them as it pleased; and I turned my attention to bodies themselves and looked more deeply into their changeableness, by which they cease to be what they had been and begin to be what they were not. And I suspected that this same passing from form to form takes place through some formless state, not through sheer nothing; but I longed to know this, not merely to suspect it. If, then, my voice and my pen were to confess to You the whole of it, all the knots You untied for me in this question, what reader would hold out long enough to take it all in? Yet my heart will not on that account cease to give You honor and a song of praise for the things it cannot put into words. For the changeableness of changeable things is itself capable of all the forms into which changeable things are changed. And this changeableness, what is it? Is it soul? Is it body? Is it some form belonging to soul or body? If one could say "a nothing-something," or "an is-that-is-not," this is what I would call it; and yet in some way it already existed even then, since it was capable of receiving these visible and compound figures.

But where could it have had even this degree of being, except from You, from whom all things are, to whatever extent they are? Yet the more unlike You a thing is, the farther it is from You; for this is not a distance of place. So You, Lord, who are not one thing in one place and something different in another, but the Selfsame, and the Selfsame, and the Selfsame, Holy, Holy, Holy, Lord God Almighty — You, in the Beginning, which is of You, in Your Wisdom, which was born of Your own Substance, created something, and created it out of nothing. For You made heaven and earth, but not out of Yourself; if You had, they would be equal to Your Only Begotten Son, and through that to You as well, whereas it would in no way be right for anything to be equal to You that was not of You. And there was nothing else besides You out of which You could create them, O God, one Trinity and threefold Unity; and so You created heaven and earth out of nothing, a great thing and a small thing. For You are Almighty and Good, to make all things good, both the great heaven and the little earth. You were, and there was nothing else out of which You created heaven and earth: two kinds of thing, one near to You, the other near to nothing; one to which You alone would be superior, the other to which nothing would be inferior.

But that heaven of heaven was for Yourself, Lord; while the earth which You gave to the sons of men, to be seen and touched, was not such as we see and touch now. For it was invisible and unformed, and there was a deep over which there was no light; or, darkness was above the deep, that is, more than in the deep. For this deep of waters, which is now visible, has even in its depths a light proper to its own nature, perceptible in some degree to the fish and the creeping things on its bottom. But that whole deep was almost nothing, because it was still entirely without form; yet there was already something that could be formed. For You, Lord, made the world out of formless matter, which You made out of nothing as something almost nothing, so that from it You might make the great things that we sons of men marvel at. For this bodily heaven is truly wonderful, this firmament between water and water, of which You said on the second day, after the creation of light, Let it be made, and it was made. This firmament You called heaven: the heaven, that is, of this earth and sea, which You made on the third day by giving a visible shape to the formless matter which You had made before any day. For You had already made a heaven before any day, but that was the heaven of this heaven, because In the beginning You had made heaven and earth. But this earth itself which You had made was formless matter, because it was invisible and unformed, and darkness was upon the deep. Out of this invisible and unformed earth, out of this formlessness, out of this almost-nothing, You were to make all the things of which this changeable world consists and yet does not hold firm; a world in which changeableness itself shows, in that times can be perceived and counted in it. For times are produced by the changes of things, as their shapes vary and turn; and the matter of those shapes is the invisible earth spoken of above.

@@ 8
That is why the Spirit, the teacher of Your servant, when He relates that You In the Beginning created heaven and earth, says nothing about times and is silent about days. For clearly that heaven of heaven which You created in the Beginning is some intellectual creature. Although it is in no way coeternal with You, the Trinity, it still shares in Your eternity; through the sweetness of that most blessed contemplation of You it firmly holds its own changeableness in check; and, clinging to You without any fall from the moment it was created, it stands beyond all the turning succession of times. Nor is this formlessness itself, the earth invisible and unformed, counted among the days. For where there is no shape and no order, nothing comes and nothing passes away; and where this does not happen, there are certainly no days, nor any succession of spans of time.

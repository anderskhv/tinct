# Book 12, slice a (paragraphs 0-24) — notes

## Pusey kept on purpose

- 0: "Ask, and you shall receive; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you" and "If God is for us, who can be against us?" — famous scripture, only pronouns/verb forms modernized ("receive" follows Latin *accipietis* for Pusey's "have").
- 6: "Holy, Holy, Holy, Lord God Almighty" and "the Selfsame, and the Selfsame, and the Selfsame" — liturgical formula / Augustine's *idipsum* triad.
- 10, 12: refrain "let me remain soberly under Your wings" kept identical all three times, as Pusey and the Latin repeat it.
- 15: "not darkly, not through a glass, but ... face to face" — 1 Cor 13:12 cadence.
- 17: "You have given it a law which it shall not pass" — Ps 148:6.
- 18: "That we might be made the righteousness of God in Him" — 2 Cor 5:21.
- 19: "the place of the habitation of the glory of my Lord"; "I have gone astray like a lost sheep" — psalm cadence kept.
- 21: "groaning with groanings that cannot be uttered" — Rom 8:26 cadence.

## Latin consulted

- Terminology throughout: *caelum caeli* = "heaven of heaven" (lowercase, singular) everywhere, including the Psalm in 1 ("The heaven of heaven is the Lord's") where Pusey has "heaven of heavens"; *terra invisibilis et incomposita* = "invisible and unformed" everywhere (Pusey "invisible and without form"); *abyssus* = "the deep" (but "depth of abyss" in 2 for *profunditas abyssi*); *informis materia* = "formless matter"; *paene nihil* = "almost nothing". Consistent with the 25-41 slice.
- 2: Pusey "taught his soul" is a misprint; Latin *hanc animam* → "this soul". "Where then light was not" → "not yet" (*nondum*). *nimirum* → "certainly" (Pusey "now").
- 5: "that which constituteth soul or body" — Latin *species animi vel corporis* → "some form belonging to soul or body".
- 7: "which out of nothing Thou madest next to nothing" — *de nulla re paene nullam rem* → "out of nothing as something almost nothing". "consists, but subsists not" (*constat et non constat*) → "consists and yet does not hold firm".
- 8: Pusey's "It" for the Spirit rendered "He" per the style note (third-person God capitalized).
- 9: Latin is "O Truth, light of my heart, let not my darkness speak"; kept Pusey's shape ("let the Truth, the Light of my heart, speak to me, and not my own darkness").
- 10: "the motion of the will from Thee" is ambiguous in Pusey; Latin *motus voluntatis a te ... ad id quod minus est* → "movement of the will away from You ... toward what exists to a lesser degree". "first or last" → Latin *vel in primo vel in imo* → "at its highest or at its lowest".
- 11: "distracted into any times" (*distenditur*) → "drawn apart into any times". The clause "the citizens of Your city in heavenly places..." is in Pusey but not in O'Donnell's text; kept (Pusey is the base).
- 14: "waters diversly ordered" — Latin *aqua speciosa*; kept Pusey's sense ("set in their various order").
- 15: "a primitive formed, and a primitive formless" → Latin *primitus formatum et penitus informe* → "the one formed from the first and the other wholly formless".
- 17: Pusey's garbled "because such a will is and no mutable thing is eternal" → Latin *mutabilis est* → "such a will is changeable, and nothing changeable is eternal". "without defection" (*sine labe*) → "without any falling away".
- 18: *caeli caelorum* (Ps 148:4, plural) → "the heavens of heavens", distinct from *caelum caeli* → "the heaven of heaven which is the Lord's", as in the Latin.
- 20: "Holy Ghost" → "Holy Spirit" (also 22).

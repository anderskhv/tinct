O let the Truth, the Light of my heart, speak to me, and not my own darkness! I slid down into that darkness and was darkened; but even from there, even from there, I loved You. I went astray, and I remembered You. I heard Your voice behind me, calling me to come back, and I could hardly hear it for the uproar of the enemies of peace. And now, look, I am coming back, burning and panting for Your fountain. Let no one stop me! From this I will drink, and so I will live. Let me not be my own life: living from myself, I lived badly; I was death to myself; in You I come alive again. Speak to me Yourself; talk with me Yourself. I have believed Your Books, and their words are full of deep mystery.

@@ 10
You have already told me, Lord, with a strong voice in my inner ear, that You are eternal, You who alone have immortality, since You are not changed by any shape or motion, and Your will does not vary with times; for no will that becomes now one thing and now another is immortal. In Your sight this is clear to me; let it become clearer and clearer to me, I beg You, and in that revelation let me remain soberly under Your wings. You have also told me, Lord, with a strong voice in my inner ear, that You made all natures and substances which are not what You are and yet exist; and that the only thing not from You is what does not exist, together with the movement of the will away from You, who are, toward what exists to a lesser degree, because such a movement is transgression and sin; and that no one's sin either harms You or disturbs the order of Your rule, whether at its highest or at its lowest. In Your sight this is clear to me; let it become clearer and clearer to me, I beg You, and in that revelation let me remain soberly under Your wings.

@@ 11
You have also told me with a strong voice in my inner ear that not even that creature is coeternal with You whose happiness is You alone, and which, drawing its nourishment from You with a most persevering purity, never and nowhere puts forth its natural changeableness; and which, with You always present to it, to whom it holds fast with its whole affection, has no future to wait for and does not pass into the past what it remembers, and so is neither altered by any change nor drawn apart into any times. O blessed creature, if such a one exists, blessed in clinging to Your Blessedness, blessed in You, its eternal Inhabitant and its Enlightener! Nor can I find any name I would more gladly give to the heaven of heaven which is the Lord's than Your house, which contemplates Your delights without ever failing by going out to anything else: one pure mind, most harmoniously one, through the settled peace of holy spirits, the citizens of Your city in the heavenly places, far above these heavenly places that we see.

@@ 12
By this may the soul whose pilgrimage has become long and far away, by this may she understand — if she now thirsts for You, if her tears have now become her bread while they say to her every day, Where is your God? — if she now asks one thing of You and seeks after it, that she may dwell in Your house all the days of her life (and what is her life but You? and what are Your days but Your eternity, like Your years which do not fail, because You are always the same?) — by this, then, may the soul that is able understand how far above all times You are eternal, since Your house, which never went off into a far country, though it is not coeternal with You, still suffers no changeableness of times, because it clings to You continually and unfailingly. In Your sight this is clear to me; let it become clearer and clearer to me, I beg You, and in that revelation let me remain soberly under Your wings.

@@ 13
Look: there is some formlessness, I do not know what, in the changes of these last and lowest creatures. And who will tell me — unless it is someone who, through the emptiness of his own heart, wanders and tosses himself about among his own fantasies — who but such a person would tell me that if every shape were so diminished and used up that only that formlessness remained through which the thing was changed and turned from one shape into another, that formlessness could display the succession of times? For plainly it could not, because without variety of motions there are no times, and there is no variety where there is no shape.

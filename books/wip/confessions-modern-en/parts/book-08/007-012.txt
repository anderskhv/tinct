Good God! What happens in a human being that makes him rejoice more over the salvation of a soul that was given up for lost and rescued from greater danger than if there had always been hope for it, or the danger had been smaller? For You too, merciful Father, rejoice more over one who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance. And we listen with great gladness whenever we hear how joyfully the sheep that had strayed is carried back on the shepherd's shoulders, and how the coin is restored to Your treasury while the neighbours rejoice with the woman who found it. And the joy of the solemn worship in Your house moves us to tears when, in Your house, we hear read about Your younger son, that he was dead and is alive again, was lost and is found. For You rejoice in us and in Your holy angels, who are holy through holy love. For You are always the same; all things that do not stay the same or last forever, You know forever in the same way.

@@ 8
What, then, happens in the soul, that it takes more delight in finding or getting back the things it loves than if it had always had them? Other things bear witness to this too; everything is full of witnesses crying out, "That is how it is." The victorious general celebrates his triumph; yet he would not have won if he had not fought, and the greater the danger in the battle, the greater the joy in the triumph. A storm tosses the sailors and threatens shipwreck; all of them turn pale at the thought of approaching death; then sky and sea grow calm, and their joy is extreme because their fear was extreme. A friend is ill, and his pulse signals danger; all who long for him to recover are sick at heart along with him. He gets better, though he does not yet walk with his old strength; yet there is a joy now that there never was before, when he walked about sound and strong. Indeed, people come by the very pleasures of human life through hardships, not only the ones that fall on us unexpectedly and against our will, but also through troubles they choose for themselves and go looking for. Eating and drinking give no pleasure unless the pinch of hunger and thirst comes first. Drinkers eat certain salty foods to produce an uncomfortable heat, and when the drink relieves it, that gives pleasure. It is also the custom that a promised bride is not handed over at once, so that the husband will not hold cheap the woman he did not long for while she was betrothed to him.

@@ 9
This law holds in shameful and cursed joy; it holds in joy that is permitted and lawful; it holds in the very purest and most honourable friendship; it holds in him who was dead and came back to life, who was lost and was found. Everywhere the greater joy is preceded by the greater pain. What does this mean, O Lord my God, when You Yourself are eternal joy to Yourself, and some beings around You rejoice in You forever? What does it mean that this part of creation swings back and forth between loss and gain, between offence and reconciliation? Is this their appointed measure? Is this all You have given them, when from the highest heavens to the lowest depths of the earth, from the beginning of the world to the end of the ages, from the angel to the worm, from the first motion to the last, You set every kind of good thing and all Your just works each in its own place, and carry out each in its own time? Ah me! How high You are in the heights, and how deep in the depths! You never go away, and we barely find our way back to You.

@@ 10
Come, Lord, act; rouse us and call us back; set us on fire and carry us away; blaze and grow sweet to us; let us love, let us run. Do not many return to You out of a deeper hell of blindness than Victorinus, and come near, and are enlightened, receiving that Light? And those who receive it receive power from You to become Your sons. But if they are less known to the public, even those who know them rejoice less over them. For when many rejoice together, the joy of each one is richer too, because they warm and kindle one another. Then again, people known to many carry weight with many toward salvation, and they lead the way for many to follow. That is why those who went before them also rejoice greatly over them, because they are not rejoicing over them alone. Far be it that in Your tabernacle the persons of the rich should be favoured above the poor, or the well-born above the lowly, since You have instead chosen the weak things of the world to put the strong to shame, and You have chosen the lowly things of this world, and the despised things, and the things that are not, to bring to nothing the things that are. And yet that same least of Your apostles, through whose tongue You sounded out these very words, when by his campaigning the proconsul Paulus had his pride overthrown and was sent under the gentle yoke of Your Christ, and became a subject of the great King, he too chose to be called Paul instead of his former name, Saul, as a mark of so great a victory. For the enemy is more thoroughly defeated in someone he holds more tightly and through whom he holds more people. And he holds the proud more tightly through their noble rank, and through them he holds more people by their influence. So the more welcome it was to think of the heart of Victorinus, which the devil had held as a stronghold that could not be taken, and the tongue of Victorinus, the great sharp weapon with which he had killed many, the more abundantly Your sons ought to rejoice, because our King had bound the strong man, and they saw his vessels taken from him, cleansed, and made fit for Your honour, and useful to the Lord for every good work.

@@ 11
But when Your servant Simplicianus told me this about Victorinus, I burned to imitate him; that was exactly why he had told it. And when he added how, in the time of the Emperor Julian, a law was passed forbidding Christians to teach literature and oratory, and how Victorinus embraced that law and chose to abandon the school of chatter rather than Your Word, by which You make eloquent the tongues of those who cannot speak, he seemed to me not so much brave as fortunate, because he had found the chance to give his time to You alone. That was what I was sighing for, bound as I was, not by someone else's irons but by my own iron will. The enemy held my will, and out of it he had made a chain for me and bound me tight. For from a perverse will came lust; lust given in to became habit; and habit not resisted became necessity. By these links, joined one to another (which is why I called it a chain), a harsh bondage held me captive. But the new will that had begun to form in me, the will to serve You freely and to want to enjoy You, O God, the only sure delight, was not yet strong enough to overcome the old will, hardened by long years. So my two wills, one new and the other old, one of the flesh and the other of the spirit, fought with each other inside me, and by their conflict they tore my soul apart.

@@ 12
So I understood from my own experience what I had read, how the flesh lusts against the spirit and the spirit against the flesh. I myself was on both sides; but I was more myself in what I approved in myself than in what I disapproved of in myself. For in the latter it was now for the most part no longer I, since much of it I suffered unwillingly rather than did willingly. And yet it was through me that habit had gained this power to fight against me, because I had willingly arrived where I did not want to be. And who can rightly object when just punishment follows the sinner? Nor did I any longer have my old excuse, that the reason I still hesitated to rise above the world and serve You was that I had no certain grasp of the truth; for now that too was certain. But I, still tied to the earth, refused to fight in Your service, and I was as afraid of being freed from all my entanglements as one ought to be afraid of being entangled by them. So I was held down pleasantly by the baggage of this world, as if in sleep; and the thoughts in which I turned my mind to You were like the efforts of people who want to wake up but are overpowered by heavy drowsiness and sink back into it again. No one wants to sleep forever, and everyone of sound judgment agrees that being awake is better; and yet a man, feeling a heavy sluggishness in all his limbs, usually puts off shaking off sleep, and even when it is time to get up, he half-reluctantly gives in to it with pleasure. In the same way I was sure it would be much better for me to give myself up to Your love than to surrender to my own desire; but while the first convinced me and was winning, the second pleased me and kept me bound. And I had no answer to give You when You called to me, Awake, you who sleep, and rise from the dead, and Christ shall give you light. And when You showed me on every side that what You said was true, I, convicted by the truth, had nothing at all to answer except those slow and sleepy words: "Soon, yes, soon"; "In a moment"; "Just let me be a little longer." But "soon, soon" never arrived, and my "little longer" went on for a long time. In vain did I delight in Your law according to the inner man, when another law in my members fought against the law of my mind and led me captive to the law of sin that was in my members. For the law of sin is the force of habit, by which the mind is dragged along and held even against its will; and deservedly so, since it slipped into that habit willingly. Who, then, would deliver me, wretched as I was, from the body of this death, except Your grace alone, through Jesus Christ our Lord?

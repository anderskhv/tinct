For if there are as many opposing natures as there are wills in conflict, there will now be not just two but many. If someone is deciding whether to go to their meeting or to the theater, these Manichees cry out, "Look, here are two natures: a good one leading him this way, a bad one pulling him back that way. For where else would this wavering between conflicting wills come from?" But I say that both are bad, the one that leads him to them and the one that pulls him back to the theater. They, however, do not believe that the will that leads to them can be anything but good. What, then, if one of us is deciding, and wavering as his two wills quarrel within him, whether to go to the theater or to our church? Would these Manichees not be wavering too about what to answer? For either they will have to admit, which they do not want to do, that it is a good will that leads a person to our church, just as it is with those who go there because they have received its sacraments and are held by them; or they will have to suppose that two evil natures and two evil souls are fighting in one man, and then what they say will not be true, that one is good and the other bad; or they will be converted to the truth and stop denying that, when someone deliberates, a single soul is tossed about between different wills.

So let them stop saying, when they notice two wills in conflict within one person, that the conflict is between two opposed souls, made of two opposed substances and coming from two opposed principles, one good and the other bad. For You, O true God, refute them, rebuke them and convict them. Take the case where both wills are bad, as when someone is deciding whether to kill a man with poison or with a sword; whether to seize this estate belonging to someone else or that one, when he cannot take both; whether to buy pleasure through extravagance or to keep his money through greed; whether to go to the circus or to the theater, if both are showing on the same day; or, a third option, to rob someone else's house, if the chance presents itself; or, a fourth, to commit adultery, if the opportunity for that opens up at the same time. Suppose all these come together at the same moment, and all are desired equally, though they cannot all be done at once: then they tear the mind apart among four conflicting wills, or even more, given the huge number of things people desire; and yet these people do not usually claim that there are that many different substances. The same is true of good wills. For I ask them whether it is good to take delight in reading the Apostle, and whether it is good to take delight in a sober Psalm, and whether it is good to discuss the Gospel. To each they will answer, "It is good." What, then, if all of them please us equally and all at the same time? Do not different wills pull the heart in different directions while we decide which one to take up first? And all of them are good, and they compete with one another until one is chosen, so that the whole will, which was divided among many, may be carried toward that one. In the same way, when eternity delights us from above and the pleasure of a temporal good holds us back below, it is the same soul, willing neither this nor that with its whole will, and so it is torn apart by grievous distress, because truth makes it put the first ahead, while habit keeps it from putting the second aside.

So I was sick and in torment, accusing myself far more harshly than usual, rolling and twisting in my chain until it would snap completely, since by now it held me only slightly, but it still held me. And You, O Lord, pressed on me in my inmost depths with a stern mercy, redoubling the lashes of fear and shame, so that I would not give up again, and so that the thin, slight bond that was left would not stay unbroken, regain its strength and tie me down more tightly. For I kept saying to myself inside, "Let it be done now, let it be done now." And as I said it, I was already on the point of deciding. I almost did it, and yet I did not do it. Still, I did not slide back into my old state, but stood close by and caught my breath. Then I tried again, and I was a little nearer, and a little nearer still; I was all but touching it and grasping it. And yet I was not there; I did not touch it or grasp it, hesitating to die to death and to live to life. The worse, which had grown ingrained in me, had more power over me than the better, to which I was unaccustomed; and the very moment at which I was to become something different, the closer it came, the greater the horror it struck into me. Yet it did not drive me back or turn me away, but held me in suspense.

The trifles of trifles and vanities of vanities, my old loves, still held me back. They tugged at my garment of flesh and whispered softly, "Are you sending us away? From this moment will we never be with you again, forever? From this moment will this and that never be allowed to you again, forever?" And what were they suggesting by what I have called "this and that"? What were they suggesting, O my God? Let Your mercy turn it away from the soul of Your servant. What filth they suggested! What shameful things! And by now I heard them far less than half as much. They no longer came out openly to confront me, but muttered, as it were, behind my back, and plucked at me stealthily as I was leaving, to make me look back at them. Yet they did hold me back, so that I hesitated to tear myself away and shake myself free of them and leap across to where I was being called, while a violent habit said to me, "Do you think you can live without them?"

But by now it spoke very faintly. For on the side toward which I had turned my face, and where I trembled to go, the chaste dignity of Continence appeared to me, calm and cheerful but not loose, honorably coaxing me to come and not to hesitate, and stretching out her holy hands, full of crowds of good examples, to receive and embrace me. There were so many boys and girls there, a great throng of young people and of every age, dignified widows and elderly virgins; and in all of them was Continence herself, not barren, but a fruitful mother of children, of joys, born of You, O Lord, her Husband. And she smiled at me with a teasing that urged me on, as if to say, "Can you not do what these young men and these young women can? Or can they do it by themselves, and not rather in the Lord their God? The Lord their God gave me to them. Why do you stand on yourself, and so fail to stand at all? Throw yourself on Him. Do not be afraid; He will not pull away and let you fall. Throw yourself on Him without fear; He will catch you and He will heal you." And I blushed deeply, because I could still hear the muttering of those trifles, and I hung there in suspense. And again she seemed to say, "Shut your ears against those unclean members of yours on the earth, so that they may be put to death. They tell you of delights, but not such as the law of the Lord your God tells." This dispute in my heart was nothing but myself against myself. And Alypius, sitting close beside me, waited in silence to see how my unusual agitation would end.

But when deep reflection had dragged up from the hidden depths of my soul all my misery and piled it up in full view of my heart, a huge storm broke out, bringing a huge downpour of tears. So that I could pour it all out, with all its natural cries, I got up and left Alypius; being alone seemed to me better suited to the business of weeping. So I went off far enough that even his presence could not be a burden to me. That was the state I was in, and he sensed something of it; for I suppose I had said something in which the sound of my voice seemed choked with tears, and that was how I had got up. So he stayed where we had been sitting, utterly astonished. I flung myself down, I do not know how, under a fig tree, and let my tears flow freely; and the floods from my eyes burst out, a sacrifice acceptable to You. And I said many things to You, not in these exact words but to this effect: And You, O Lord, how long? How long, Lord? Will You be angry forever? Do not remember our former iniquities; for I felt that I was still held by them. I cried out these wretched words: How long, how long, "tomorrow, and tomorrow"? Why not now? Why not an end to my uncleanness this very hour?

I was saying these things and weeping in the most bitter sorrow of my heart, when suddenly I heard from a house nearby a voice, whether a boy's or a girl's I do not know, chanting and repeating over and over, "Take up and read; take up and read." At once my expression changed, and I began to think very hard whether children used to sing words like these in some kind of game, but I could not remember ever having heard anything like it. So, holding back the rush of my tears, I got up, taking it to be nothing other than a command from God to open the book and read the first passage I came upon. For I had heard about Antony, that he had happened to come in while the Gospel was being read, and took it as a warning addressed to him, as if what was being read were spoken to him: Go, sell all that you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me. And by such a sign he was at once converted to You. So I hurried back to the place where Alypius was sitting, because I had put down the volume of the Apostle there when I got up. I snatched it up, opened it, and read in silence the passage my eyes first fell on: Not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying; but put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, in concupiscence. I did not want to read any further, and there was no need. For instantly, as I reached the end of that sentence, it was as though a light of peace and certainty had been poured into my heart, and all the darkness of doubt fled away.

Then, putting my finger or some other marker in the page, I closed the book, and with a face now calm I told Alypius. And he in turn told me, in this way, what had been happening in him, which I had not known. He asked to see what I had read. I showed him, and he looked further on than I had read; I did not know what came next. What came next was this: Receive him who is weak in the faith. He applied this to himself and told me so. And he was strengthened by this admonition, and by a good decision and purpose, one very much in keeping with his character, in which he had always been far and away better than I, he joined me without any troubled delay. From there we go in to my mother and tell her; she is glad. We relate how it all happened; she leaps for joy and triumphs, and she blessed You, who are able to do more than we ask or think; for she saw that You had granted her far more concerning me than she used to beg for with her pitiful, tearful groans. For You converted me to Yourself, so that I no longer sought a wife or any hope of this world, but stood on that rule of faith where, so many years before, You had shown me to her in a vision. And You turned her mourning into joy, a joy far richer than she had wanted, and far dearer and purer than the one she once looked for in having grandchildren from my body.

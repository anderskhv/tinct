# NOTES — Book 8 (paragraphs 0-30)

Fresh rendering from Pusey (original-en) checked against O'Donnell's Latin. The served modern-en for Book 8 (Pusey) was not used.

## Pusey kept on purpose

- p17: "Give me chastity and continence, but not yet." Famous formula (*da mihi castitatem et continentiam, sed noli modo*); only "continency" modernized to "continence".
- p29: "Take up and read; take up and read." Famous formula (*tolle lege*), kept as Pusey.
- p29: Romans 13:13-14 kept in Pusey's cadence: "Not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying; but put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, in concupiscence." The only changes are "put ye on" to "put on" and "make not" to "make no". I kept "chambering" because the verse is so well known.
- p28: "tomorrow, and tomorrow" (*cras et cras*) and the Psalm phrases "How long, Lord? Will You be angry forever? Do not remember our former iniquities" stay recognizable.
- Scripture throughout (the pearl, eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake, the lost sheep, the coin and the prodigal son, "Awake, you who sleep", Romans 7, "Receive him who is weak in the faith", "above that which we ask or think") has modern pronouns and verb forms but keeps its key wording.

## Latin consulted

- p1: For "a very grievous burden it was to undergo so heavy a bondage", I followed the Latin: the worldly life was a burden now that desire no longer fired him with hopes of honour and money *ad tolerandam illam servitutem* (to make the bondage bearable).
- p5: I kept Pusey's singular "the first sacrament" for his catechumen admission and added no gloss.
- p6: *non enim erat salus quam docebat in rhetorica*. Rendered as "what he had taught in rhetoric was not salvation, and yet he had professed it publicly".
- p9: For "this portion of things thus ebbs and flows alternately displeased and reconciled", the Latin is *alternat defectu et profectu, offensionibus et conciliationibus*. Rendered as "swings back and forth between loss and gain, between offence and reconciliation". I also restored "every kind of good thing and all Your just works" (*omnia genera bonorum et omnia iusta opera tua*), which Pusey shortens to "every thing good after its kind".
- p10: "more overcome in one, of whom he hath more hold; by whom he hath hold of more" (*plus tenet et de quo plures tenet*). Clarified along the Latin.
- p12: For *illud placebat et vincebat, hoc libebat et vinciebat*, I rendered "the first convinced me and was winning, the second pleased me and kept me bound". "Anon, anon / presently / leave me but a little" (*modo, ecce modo, sine paululum*) became "Soon, yes, soon"; "In a moment"; "Just let me be a little longer".
- p13: "after the third sitting" (*post adsessionem tertiam*) became "after his third term as assessor". Pusey is obscure here, so I followed the Latin.
- p15: "those whom they style agents for the public affairs" (*agentes in rebus*) is kept as "agents for public affairs".
- p23: Pusey's "as well as theirs, who have received and are held by the mysteries of theirs" is ambiguous. The Latin (*sicut in eam pergunt qui sacramentis eius imbuti sunt atque detinentur*) refers to those who go to *our* church having received *its* sacraments. I followed the Latin.
- p24: Pusey's last sentence has "out of truth it sets this first, but out of habit sets not that aside" (*illud veritate praeponit, hoc familiaritate non ponit*). Rendered as "truth makes it put the first ahead, while habit keeps it from putting the second aside".
- p27: *serena et non dissolute hilaris* became "calm and cheerful but not loose". *fecunda mater filiorum gaudiorum de marito te* became "a fruitful mother of children, of joys, born of You, O Lord, her Husband".
- p30: The Latin shifts tense (*exultat et triumphat et benedicebat*). Fix pass: the series is now all present, as Pusey has it ("she leaps for joy and triumphs, and blesses You"); the sudden past tense read as an error in English.
- p15: *apud Treveros* — Pusey's name form "Triers" kept (STYLE-NOTE §7). *circensium spectaculo* rendered "the games in the circus" (no "chariot races" gloss). *ubertates fecundas heremi* rendered "the rich fruitfulness of the desert".
- p29: *quasi luce securitatis* — one noun, "a light of serenity" (Pusey); *amarissima contritione cordis mei* — "contrition" kept.

## Uncertainties / open issues

- p3: This is Pusey's verse quotation (Virgil, Aeneid 8). It is rendered freshly. The served text has no line breaks, so the stray verse capital "Of" was lowercased in the fix pass ("every kind of monster-god").
- p2 to p4: I kept the paragraph split through the middle of the sentence exactly as Pusey has it (p2 ends "love of", p3 is the verse, p4 continues "gods whom Rome had once conquered..."). In p4 I added "gods" at the start so the sentence can be read after the verse.
- p27: "a teasing that urged me on" renders "persuasive mockery" (*inrisione hortatoria*).
- The mother is not named in p30, as in Pusey.

But was neither the Father nor the Son borne above the waters? If this means in space, like a body, then neither was the Holy Spirit; but if it means the unchangeable supereminence of the Divinity above everything changeable, then Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit were all borne above the waters. Why, then, is this said only of Your Spirit? Why is it said of Him alone, as though He were in a place, He who is not in any place, and of whom alone it is written that He is Your gift? In Your Gift we rest; there we enjoy You. Our rest is our place. Love lifts us up to it, and Your good Spirit raises our lowliness from the gates of death. In Your good pleasure lies our peace. A body strives by its own weight toward its own place. Weight does not pull only downward; it pulls toward a thing's own place. Fire tends upward, a stone downward. They are moved by their own weights; they seek their own places. Oil poured under water rises above the water; water poured on top of oil sinks beneath the oil. They are moved by their own weights to seek their own places. When they are out of order they are restless; when they are restored to order they are at rest. My weight is my love; by it I am carried wherever I am carried. We are set on fire; by Your Gift we are kindled and carried upward; we glow within, and we go forward. We climb the ascents in our heart and sing a song of degrees; we glow within with Your fire, with Your good fire, and we go, because we are going upward to the peace of Jerusalem; for I was glad when they said to me, "We will go up to the house of the Lord." There Your good pleasure will settle us, so that we will want nothing else than to remain there forever.

Blessed is the creature which, though it is itself other than You, has known no other condition than this: that as soon as it was made, without any interval of time, it was lifted up by Your Gift, which is borne above everything changeable, lifted up by that calling in which You said, "Let there be light," and there was light. In us this happened at different times, in that we were darkness and are being made light; but of that creature it is said only what it would have been had it not been enlightened. And it is spoken of as though it had been unsettled and dark before, so that the cause by which it was made otherwise might appear: namely, that by turning to the unfailing Light it became light. Let whoever can understand this; let him ask it of You. Why should he trouble me, as if I could enlighten any man who comes into this world?

Which of us understands the Almighty Trinity? And yet which of us does not speak of It, if indeed it is It that he speaks of? Rare is the soul that knows what it is speaking of when it speaks of It. And people argue and fight, yet without peace no one sees that vision. I wish people would consider these three, which are in themselves. These three are indeed something very different from that Trinity; I am only pointing out where they may exercise themselves, and there test and feel how far off they are. The three I mean are: to be, to know, and to will. For I am, and I know, and I will: I am a knowing and willing being; I know that I am and that I will; and I will to be and to know. In these three, then, let whoever can discern how inseparable a life there is, indeed one life, one mind, and one essence; how inseparable, finally, the distinction is, and yet it is a distinction. Surely a man has this right in front of him; let him look into himself, and see, and tell me. But when he discovers something in these and can say it, let him not think on that account that he has found what is above them, the Unchangeable, which is unchangeably, and knows unchangeably, and wills unchangeably. And whether it is because of these three that there is also a Trinity in God; or whether all three are in Each, so that the three belong to Each; or whether both are true at once, in wondrous ways, simply and yet manifoldly, Itself a bound to Itself within Itself and yet unbounded, by which It is, and is known to Itself, and suffices to Itself, unchangeably the Selfsame, by the abundant greatness of Its Unity: who can easily conceive of this? Who could express it in any way? Who would in any way pronounce on it rashly?

Go on with your confession, O my faith; say to the Lord your God, "Holy, Holy, Holy, O Lord my God, in Your Name we have been baptized, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; in Your Name we baptize, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit." For among us too, in His Christ, God made heaven and earth: that is, the spiritual and the carnal people of His Church. And our earth, before it received the form of doctrine, was invisible and without form, and we were covered with the darkness of ignorance. For You chastened man for iniquity, and Your judgments were like the great deep to him. But because Your Spirit was borne above the waters, Your mercy did not abandon our misery, and You said, "Let there be light": "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand." Repent; let there be light. And because our soul was troubled within us, we remembered You, O Lord, from the land of Jordan, and from that mountain equal to You but made little for our sake; and our darkness displeased us, and we turned to You, and there was light. And see, we were once darkness, but now we are light in the Lord.

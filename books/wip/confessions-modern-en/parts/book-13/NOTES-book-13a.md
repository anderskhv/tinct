# Book 13, slice a (paragraphs 0-25) — notes

## Pusey kept on purpose

- 5: "in a glass darkly" (1 Cor 13:12) — famous scripture phrase, kept as Pusey.
- 7, 9: "supereminent" / "supereminence" — kept because Augustine's argument turns on the repeated word (supereminentem viam / scientiam; supereminens superferebatur; supereminentem requiem); any paraphrase breaks the link between the Pauline texts and the Spirit "borne above".
- 13: "deep calls to deep", "as the deer for the water brooks" — familiar Psalm cadence kept (pronouns only modernized).
- 21: "and, behold, all things have become new" (2 Cor 5:17) — "behold" kept inside the scripture quotation, as the style note allows. 17: "they always behold Your face" (Matt 18:10) echo, likewise.
- 22: "judge the fatherless, plead for the widow", "come, let us reason together, says the Lord" (Isa 1) — kept recognizable.
- Throughout: "borne above / borne over the waters", "Heaven of heavens", "firmament", "dry land", "invisible and without form", "the deep", "Let there be light", "Sacraments" (Pusey's capitalized term) kept exact.
- "Holy Ghost" rendered "Holy Spirit" throughout (STYLE-NOTE §3), including the baptismal formula in 12.

## Latin consulted

- 0: "quam praeparas ad capiendum te" — Pusey "preparest for Thee"; rendered "preparing to receive You". "colam ... quasi terram" pun kept with "cultivate".
- 4: final "beari" — Pusey "beautified"; Latin = made blessed/happy. Followed Latin: "made blessed".
- 7: "aquas quae sunt sine substantia" — Pusey "waters which yield no support"; rendered "waters that are without substance" (Latin, Ps 123/124).
- 9: "ascensiones in corde" — Pusey "Thy ways that be in our heart"; rendered "the ascents in our heart" (Latin; ties to "song of degrees"). "ibi nos conlocabit voluntas bona" is future; Pusey past ("hath placed"); rendered "will settle us". Pusey's doubled "We are inflamed, by Thy Gift we are kindled" kept (Latin has one verb) to stay with Pusey.
- 11: "in singulis" — Pusey "in Each"; kept "in Each" without adding "Person".
- 12: "montem aequalem tibi sed parvum propter nos" has no verb; Pusey "equal unto Thyself, but little for our sakes". The added gloss "made little" was removed in the fix pass: "equal to You, but little for our sake" (review #5).
- 13: "quae est illa speciei lux?" — Pusey's exclamation "O what a light of beauty" kept; Latin is a question. Not changed.
- 17: last sentence — Latin "sicuti est, domine, videre nostrum, quod nondum est nobis"; Pusey omits the final clause. Rendered "that will be our seeing, though it is not yet ours" (following Latin). "currimus" present: "we run" (Pusey "ran").
- 20: "alio fine distinctas" — Pusey "by other bounds divided"; Latin finis here = end/goal (contrast 19 "idem finis"). Rendered "set apart ... by a different end". Also added "hidden" (occulto ... fonte), which Pusey omits.
- 21: Pusey's text is garbled here ("the word of wisdom, as it were the lesser light: to another faith; to another the gift with the light of perspicuous truth..."). Followed Latin (13.18.23 "sermo sapientiae tamquam luminare maius propter eos qui perspicuae veritatis luce delectantur tamquam in principio diei"): "the word of wisdom, as it were the greater light, for the sake of those who delight in the light of clear truth, as for the rule of the day." "temporanea lux nostra" — Pusey "temporary light"; Latin = seasonable/timely (Isa 58:8); rendered "our light break forth in its season". "quo gaudet praedictus dies" — Pusey "which gladdens the forementioned day"; Latin *quo* with *gaudet* = the brightness in which the day rejoices; rendered "in which the day just mentioned rejoices" (verified correct by the reviewer; logged in the fix pass).
- 23: "non desperatorum" — Pusey "not despised"; Latin = not despaired of; followed Latin. "super omnem terram" — "over all the earth". "nondum sicut angelorum" — "not yet as the angels".
- 24: "repserunt" (crept, echoing reptilia) — "made their way, moving amid" to keep the moving-creature link.

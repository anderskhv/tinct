And yet it is still by faith and not yet by sight, for we are saved by hope; but hope that is seen is not hope. Still deep calls to deep, but now in the voice of Your waterspouts. Still, even he who says, "I could not speak to you as to spiritual people, but as to carnal," even he does not yet consider himself to have laid hold of it; he forgets what lies behind and reaches forward to what lies ahead, and groans under his burden, and his soul thirsts for the Living God, as the deer for the water brooks, and says, "When shall I come?", longing to be clothed over with his dwelling which is from heaven. And he calls to this lower deep, saying, "Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind"; and, "Do not be children in understanding, but in malice be children, so that in understanding you may be perfect"; and, "O foolish Galatians, who has bewitched you?" But now he speaks no longer in his own voice, but in Yours, who sent Your Spirit from above through Him who ascended on high and opened the floodgates of His gifts, so that the force of His streams might make glad the city of God. It is for Him that this friend of the Bridegroom sighs, already having the first fruits of the Spirit laid up with Him, yet still groaning within himself, waiting for the adoption, that is, the redemption of his body. For Him he sighs, since he is a member of the Bride; for Him he is jealous, since he is a friend of the Bridegroom; for Him he is jealous, not for himself. For it is in the voice of Your waterspouts, not in his own voice, that he calls to that other deep, over which, in his jealousy, he is afraid that, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so their minds may be corrupted from the purity that is in our Bridegroom, Your only Son. O what a light of beauty that will be, when we shall see Him as He is, and those tears have passed away which have been my bread day and night, while they say to me every day, "Where is your God now?"

See, I too say, "O my God, where are You?" See, there You are! In You I breathe a little, when I pour out my soul within me in the voice of joy and praise, the sound of one keeping festival. And yet it is sad again, because it slips back and becomes a deep, or rather feels that it is still a deep. My faith, which You have kindled to light my feet in the night, speaks to it: "Why are you sad, O my soul, and why do you trouble me? Hope in the Lord; His word is a lamp to your feet." Hope and endure, until the night, the mother of the wicked, has passed, until the wrath of the Lord has passed, whose children we too once were, we who were once darkness, whose remnants we still carry about in our body, dead because of sin; until the day breaks and the shadows flee away. Hope in the Lord: in the morning I shall stand in Your presence and contemplate You; I shall confess to You forever. In the morning I shall stand in Your presence and see the health of my countenance, my God, who will also give life to our mortal bodies by the Spirit who dwells in us, because in His mercy He was borne over our dark and fluid inner deep. From Him, in this pilgrimage, we have received a pledge that we should already be light, while we are still saved by hope, and are children of light and children of the day, not children of the night nor of the darkness, which nevertheless we once were. Between those children of darkness and us, in this still uncertain state of human knowledge, You alone draw the dividing line: You who test our hearts, and call the light day and the darkness night. For who distinguishes us but You? And what do we have that we did not receive from You? Out of the same lump vessels are made for honor, from which others too are made for dishonor.

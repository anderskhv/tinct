## Latin consulted

- 28 — "out of a reverence towards those secret signs" (in timore occultorum signorum): rendered "out of awe toward those hidden signs".
- 30 — Pusey's run-on about the "dispenser... that he might not for ever have them babes": Latin (ne semper parvulos haberet quos lacte nutriret) confirms the sense; restructured as "he did not want to keep them babies forever...".
- 31 — "brute beasts" (iumentis insensatis): kept Pusey's "brute beasts" as the psalm phrase; "Grecian / bond" rendered "Greek / slave".
- 32 — Pusey "For He judgeth" capitalizes He, but the subject is man (the spiritual man), so lowercase "he". Pusey's "consecrating" = benedicendo, rendered "blessing". Pusey's separate item "and of those things which are perceived by the senses of the body" is in the Latin part of the list "in cogitationibus piis de his quae per sensum corporis percipiuntur", so rendered "in holy meditations on those things which are perceived by the senses of the body".
- 36 — "At my first answer" (in prima mea defensione): "At my first defence".
- 37 — "For whereat he rejoiced upon that he fed" (unde autem gaudet, inde pascitur): "what he rejoiced at is what he fed on".
- 40 — Pusey has "succour Thy servant" (singular) and then "these"; Latin "pueros tuos" is plural, so rendered "Your servants" throughout the paragraph for coherent reference. "carnal men" (homines idiotae) kept as Pusey's "carnal people".
- 44 — last sentence "who Himself Is not in degree, but what He Is, Is" (non aliquo modo est, sed est est): "Him who does not exist merely in some degree, but what He Is, Is".
- 45 — Pusey "subject to the sex of her husband": Latin "masculino sexui subiceretur" = the male sex; rendered "subject to the male sex". "appetite of doing is fain to conceive" (appetitus actionis ... ad concipiendam ... sollertiam) rendered "the drive to act is made subject so as to conceive ... the skill of acting rightly".
- 46 — Pusey renders the opening as jussive ("Let Thy works praise Thee... let us love Thee"); O'Donnell's Latin is indicative ("laudant te opera tua ut amemus te, et amamus te ut laudent"). Followed the Latin: "Your works praise You, so that we may love You; and we love You, so that Your works may praise You." Reviewer may prefer restoring Pusey's jussive.
- 47 — Pusey "blessed and multiplied": Latin has only benedicerentur; kept Pusey's "and multiplied".
- 48 — Latin has three "peaces" (pacem sine vespera); kept Pusey's "the peace of the Sabbath, which has no evening".

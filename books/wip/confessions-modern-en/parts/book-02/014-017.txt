What shall I render to the Lord, that my memory can recall these things and my soul is not frightened by them? I will love You, O Lord, and thank You, and confess to Your name, because You have forgiven me these great and wicked deeds of mine. I put it down to Your grace and Your mercy that You have melted my sins away like ice. I put down to Your grace as well whatever evil I did not do; for what might I not have done, when I even loved a sin for its own sake? Yes, I confess that everything has been forgiven me, both the evil I did of my own will and the evil I did not do because You guided me. Who is there who, weighing his own weakness, dares to credit his own strength with his purity and innocence, and so loves You less, as if he had needed less of Your mercy, by which You forgive the sins of those who turn to You? For anyone who was called by You, and followed Your voice, and avoided the things he reads me recalling and confessing about myself, should not scorn me, who was sick and was cured by that Physician through whose help he himself was not sick, or rather was less sick. And for this let him love You just as much, or even more; for he sees that the One who restored me from such a deep wasting sickness of sin is the same One by whom he was kept from a wasting sickness of sin as bad.

@@ 15
What fruit did I get then, wretch that I was, from those things that I now blush to remember? Above all from that theft, in which I loved the theft itself and nothing else, when it was itself nothing, and I all the more wretched for loving it? And yet I would not have done it alone. That, as I remember, was my state of mind then: alone I would never have done it. So in it I also loved the company of the accomplices I did it with. Then it is not true that I loved nothing but the theft? No, in fact I loved nothing else, because that company was also nothing. What is the truth of it? Who can teach me, except the One who lights up my heart and uncovers its dark corners? What is it that has come into my mind to ask about, to discuss and to consider? For if I had loved the pears I stole back then, and wanted to enjoy them, I could have done it alone, if committing the theft had been enough to get me my pleasure; I would not have needed to inflame the itch of my desire by rubbing against accomplices. But since my pleasure was not in those pears, it was in the crime itself, and it was the company of fellow sinners that produced it.

@@ 16
What, then, was this state of mind? It was certainly far too foul, and woe to me for having it. But still, what was it? Who can understand his errors? It was the fun of it, which tickled our hearts, so to speak, that we were fooling people who had no idea what we were doing and would have strongly objected. Why, then, did my delight in it depend on my not doing it alone? Is it because people do not usually laugh alone? Usually no one does; yet sometimes laughter overcomes people on their own, when no one else at all is with them, if something very funny strikes their senses or their mind. Yet I would not have done this alone; alone I would never have done it. Look, my God, here before You is the living memory of my soul. On my own I would never have committed that theft, in which what pleased me was not what I stole but the fact that I stole it; on my own I would not have enjoyed doing it at all, and I would not have done it. O friendship too unfriendly! You mysterious seducer of the soul, you hunger to do harm for sport and mischief, you craving for other people's loss without any desire for my own gain or for revenge! But when someone says, "Come on, let's do it," we are ashamed not to be shameless.

@@ 17
Who can unravel that most twisted and tangled knot? It is filthy; I do not want to dwell on it, or even look at it. It is You I want, O Righteousness and Innocence, beautiful and lovely to all pure eyes, a satisfaction that never cloys. In You there is perfect rest, and a life that nothing can disturb. Whoever enters into You enters into the joy of his Lord; he shall not fear, and he shall do excellently in the All-Excellent. I slid away from You, my God, and wandered too far astray from You, my stay, in these years of my youth, and I became to myself a barren land.

For it is You, Lord, who judge me. For although no one knows the things of a man except the spirit of man that is in him, there is still something of a man that even the spirit of man within him does not know. But You, Lord, who made him, know everything about him. And I, though in Your sight I despise myself and count myself dust and ashes, still know something about You that I do not know about myself. And certainly, for now we see through a glass darkly, not yet face to face. So as long as I am a pilgrim away from You, I am more present to myself than to You; and yet I know that You cannot in any way be harmed, while I do not know which temptations I can resist and which I cannot. And there is hope, because You are faithful, who will not let us be tempted beyond what we are able, but with the temptation will also make a way of escape, so that we may be able to bear it. So I will confess what I know about myself, and I will also confess what I do not know about myself. For what I do know about myself, I know because You shine on me; and what I do not know about myself, I will go on not knowing until my darkness becomes as the noonday in Your presence.

Not with doubt, but with sure awareness, I love You, Lord. You struck my heart with Your word, and I loved You. Heaven and earth too, and everything in them, look, on every side they tell me to love You, and they never stop saying it to everyone, so that they are without excuse. But more deeply will You have mercy on whom You will have mercy, and show compassion to whom You have been compassionate; otherwise heaven and earth speak Your praises to deaf ears. But what do I love when I love You? Not the beauty of bodies, nor the fair harmony of time, nor the brightness of light, so pleasant to our eyes, nor the sweet melodies of songs of every kind, nor the fragrant smell of flowers and ointments and spices, not manna and honey, not limbs that welcome the embraces of the flesh. None of these do I love when I love my God; and yet I do love a kind of light, and melody, and fragrance, and food, and embrace when I love my God: the light, melody, fragrance, food and embrace of my inner self, where something shines into my soul that no space can contain, and something sounds that time does not carry away, and something gives off a fragrance that no wind scatters, and something has a taste that no eating lessens, and something holds fast that no fullness pulls apart. This is what I love when I love my God.

And what is this? I asked the earth, and it answered me, "I am not He"; and everything in it made the same confession. I asked the sea and the deeps and the creeping things that live, and they answered, "We are not your God; look above us." I asked the moving air, and the whole air with its inhabitants answered, "Anaximenes was mistaken; I am not God." I asked the heavens, the sun, the moon, the stars; "Nor are we the God you are looking for," they said. And I said to all the things that stand around the doors of my flesh, "You have told me about my God, that you are not He; tell me something about Him." And they cried out with a loud voice, "He made us." My questioning of them was my attention to them, and their beauty was their answer. And I turned to myself and said to myself, "Who are you?" And I answered, "A man." And look, here in me are a body and a soul, presenting themselves to me, one outside and the other within. Through which of these should I have looked for my God? I had already looked for Him through the body, from earth to heaven, as far as I could send the beams of my eyes as messengers. But the inner part is the better, for it was to this, as it presided and judged, that all the bodily messengers reported the answers of heaven and earth and everything in them, as they said, "We are not God, but He made us." My inner self knew these things through the service of the outer; I, the inner self, knew them, I, the mind, through the senses of my body. I asked the whole mass of the world about my God, and it answered me, "I am not He, but He made me."

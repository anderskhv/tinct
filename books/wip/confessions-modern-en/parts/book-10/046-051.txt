Placed, then, among these temptations, I struggle every day against concupiscence in eating and drinking. For it is not the kind of thing I can decide to cut off once and for all and never touch again, as I was able to do with sexual union. So the reins of the throat must be held with a balanced mix of slackness and tightness. And who is there, O Lord, who is not carried somewhat beyond the limits of necessity? Whoever he is, he is a great man; let him magnify Your Name. But I am not such a man, for I am a sinful man. Yet I too magnify Your Name; and He who has overcome the world intercedes with You for my sins, counting me among the weak members of His body; for Your eyes have seen what is imperfect in Him, and in Your book all shall be written.

@@ 47
The allurements of smells do not trouble me much. When they are absent, I do not look for them; when they are present, I do not refuse them; and I am always ready to do without them. So it seems to me; perhaps I am deceived. For this too is a darkness to be lamented, that the capacities within me are hidden from me, so that my mind, questioning itself about its own powers, does not think it can readily trust itself; because even what is in it is for the most part hidden, unless experience brings it to light. And no one ought to feel secure in this life, the whole of which is called a trial, as though one who could be made better from worse could not also be made worse from better. Our only hope, our only confidence, our only sure promise is Your mercy.

@@ 48
The pleasures of the ear had entangled and subdued me more tightly, but You loosened me and set me free. Now, when I hear those melodies into which Your words breathe life, sung with a sweet and skilled voice, I do rest in them a little; yet not so as to be held fast by them, but so that I can rise and leave when I wish. Yet together with the words that are their life, and by which they gain admission to me, they seek a place of some honor in my heart, and I can hardly give them a fitting one. For at times I seem to myself to give them more honor than is right, when I feel that our minds are stirred more devoutly and more fervently into a flame of devotion by the holy words themselves when they are sung in this way than when they are not; and that all the affections of our spirit, in their sweet variety, have their own proper measures in voice and song, by which they are roused through some hidden kinship. But this gratification of my flesh, to which the mind ought not to be handed over to be weakened, often deceives me, when the sense does not accompany reason in such a way as to follow patiently behind it, but, having been admitted only for reason's sake, tries even to run ahead of it and lead it. So in these things I sin without noticing, and afterwards I notice.

@@ 49
At other times, guarding too anxiously against this very deception, I err by excessive strictness, sometimes so far as to wish that the whole melody of the sweet chants to which David's Psalter is sung were banished from my ears, and from the Church's too. And the safer way seems to me the one I remember often being told about Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria, who had the reader of the psalm deliver it with so slight an inflection of the voice that it was closer to speaking than to singing. Yet when I remember the tears I shed at the singing of Your Church in the first days of my recovered faith, and how even now I am moved, not by the singing but by the things that are sung, when they are sung with a clear voice and a most fitting melody, I acknowledge once again the great usefulness of this practice. So I waver between the danger of pleasure and the proven benefit, and I am inclined rather (though I do not pronounce an irrevocable opinion) to approve the custom of singing in church, so that through the delight of the ears weaker minds may rise to a feeling of devotion. Yet when it happens that I am moved more by the singing than by what is sung, I confess that I sin in a way that deserves punishment, and then I would rather not hear the singer. See where I stand! Weep with me and weep for me, you who so govern your feelings within that good actions follow. For you who do not act, these things do not touch you. But You, O Lord my God, hear me; look and see, have mercy and heal me, You in whose sight I have become a problem to myself, and that is my sickness.

@@ 50
There remains the pleasure of these eyes of my flesh, about which I shall make my confessions in the hearing of the ears of Your temple, those brotherly and devout ears, and so conclude the temptations of the lust of the flesh, which still assail me as I groan and long to be clothed with my dwelling that is from heaven. The eyes love beautiful and varied forms, bright and pleasing colors. Let these not take hold of my soul; let God take hold of it instead, who made these things very good indeed; yet He is my good, not they. And these things touch me all day long while I am awake, and I am given no rest from them, as I am given rest from musical sounds, and sometimes, in silence, from all sounds. For light itself, the queen of colors, bathing everything we see, wherever I am during the day, glides over me in many forms and soothes me while I am busy with other things and not paying attention to it. And it works its way into me so strongly that if it is suddenly taken away, it is sought with longing, and if it is gone for long, it saddens the mind.

@@ 51
O Light, which Tobias saw when, with these eyes closed, he taught his son the way of life and went before him with the feet of love, never straying! Or which Isaac saw, when his fleshly eyes were heavy and closed with old age, and it was granted him to bless his sons, not by knowing them, but to know them by blessing them. Or which Jacob saw, when he too, blind with great age, with an illumined heart shed light on the different races of the future people, foreshadowed in his sons; and laid his hands, mystically crossed, on his grandsons by Joseph, not as their father outwardly tried to correct them, but as he himself inwardly discerned. This is the light; it is one, and all who see and love it are one. But that bodily light I was speaking of seasons the life of this world for its blind lovers with an enticing and dangerous sweetness. Yet those who know how to praise You for it too, "O God, Creator of all," take it up into Your hymn and are not taken over by it in their sleep. That is how I long to be. I resist these seductions of the eyes, so that my feet, with which I walk along Your way, may not be entangled; and I lift up my invisible eyes to You, that You may pluck my feet out of the snare. Again and again You pluck them out, for they are ensnared. You never stop plucking them out, while I often get caught in the snares laid on every side; because You who keep Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.

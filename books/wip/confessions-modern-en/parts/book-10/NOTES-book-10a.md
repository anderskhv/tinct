# Book 10, paragraphs 0-33 — notes (slice 10a)

## Pusey kept on purpose

- 6: "now we see through a glass darkly, not face to face as yet" kept as "for now we see through a glass darkly, not yet face to face" (famous 1 Cor 13:12 cadence).
- 7: "have mercy on whom You will have mercy" (Rom 9:15 cadence).
- 32: "the flesh lusts against the Spirit and the Spirit against the flesh" (Gal 5:17, Pauline "flesh" kept); "Let them walk, let them walk, so that the darkness does not overtake them" (John 12:35).
- 33: "truth breed hatred" kept in quotation marks as Pusey has it (Terence tag), modernized "generate" to "breed".
- 17: the Latin etymology words (cogo, cogito, ago, agito, facio, factito) and Pusey's parenthetical glosses kept exactly as Pusey has them.

## Latin consulted

- 6: "Thou art in no ways passible" — Latin "te novi nullo modo posse violari"; rendered "You cannot in any way be harmed".
- 7: "the fair harmony of time" (decus temporis) — kept Pusey's phrase; Latin is literally "the grace/loveliness of time".
- 8: "My questioning them was my thoughts on them" — Latin "interrogatio mea intentio mea" → "my attention to them"; "their form of beauty gave the answer" (responsio eorum species eorum) → "their beauty was their answer".
- 9: "Now to thee I speak, O my soul, thou art my better part" — Latin "iam tu melior es, tibi dico, anima"; rendered per Pusey's sense. Pusey's quotation marks around "They are a mass..." kept although the Latin has no quotation there.
- 11: "when I repeat a thing by heart" — Latin "narro memoriter" → "recount something from memory".
- 16: Touch: "If it have not size" — Latin "si corpulentum non est" → "If it has no bodily bulk".
- 18: Pusey garbles "the numbers of the things with which we number all the senses of my body"; Latin "sensi etiam numeros omnibus corporis sensibus quos numeramus" → "I have also perceived with all the senses of my body the numbers of the things we count". "therefore they indeed are" (valde sunt) → "they truly are".
- 19: Pusey's final "as if hereafter" — Latin "sicut postea... si recordabor" → "just as, if later on I recall...".
- 23: Pusey states "It is to be understood from this that..." as an assertion; Latin "an ex hoc intellegitur...?" is a tentative question, and para 24 goes on to doubt the image-theory. Rendered as a question ("Or are we to understand from this...?").
- 25: "Thou truly good and certain sweetness" (vere bone, secura suavitas) → "You who are truly good, You sure and certain sweetness". "If I find Thee without my memory" (praeter memoriam) → "outside my memory".
- 27: "maimed, as it were, by the curtailment of its ancient habit" — Latin "quasi detruncata consuetudine claudicans" → "limping as it were because its old habit had been cut short".
- 28: Pusey's "until I can say, where I ought to say it, 'It is enough'" follows a different punctuation from O'Donnell ("donec dicam, 'sat, est illic.' ubi oportet ut dicam quomodo eam quaero..."). Kept Pusey's reading, since the edition follows Pusey; flag for reviewer.
- 31: "rejoice to Thee, of Thee, for Thee" (gaudere ad te, de te, propter te) → "to rejoice toward You, in You, and for Your sake". "love Thee for Thine own sake" (gratis colunt) → "worship You for Your own sake".
- 33: "the man of Thine" (homo tuus) → "a man of Yours".

## Uncertainties / open issues

- 12: Pusey omits "smooth" from the tactile list (Latin "lene aut asperum"); followed Pusey ("rough") in the list, but his later "smooth before rugged" is kept as "smooth to rough".
- 28: textual variant on "sat, est illic" noted above.
- 25: "a fearful thing" (nescio quid horrendum) rendered "something awesome"; a reviewer may prefer "something to shudder at".
- "happy life" (vita beata) kept as the fixed term throughout 28-33.

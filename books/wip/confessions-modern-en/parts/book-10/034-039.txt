@@ 34
See how far I have ranged through my memory in search of You, O Lord, and I have not found You outside it. Nor have I found anything about You that I have not kept in memory from the time I first came to know You. For since I came to know You, I have not forgotten You. Where I found Truth, there I found my God, Truth itself, and since I came to know it, I have not forgotten it. So from the time I came to know You, You have dwelt in my memory, and there I find You whenever I call You to mind and delight in You. These are my holy delights, which You have given me in Your mercy, looking kindly on my poverty.

@@ 35
But where in my memory do You dwell, O Lord, where do You dwell there? What kind of resting place have You shaped for Yourself? What kind of sanctuary have You built for Yourself? You have given my memory this honor, that You should dwell in it; but in which part of it You dwell, that is what I am now considering. For in calling You to mind I went beyond those parts of it that the beasts also have, since I did not find You there among the images of bodily things. And I came to the parts where I have stored the affections of my mind, and I did not find You there either. And I went into the very seat of my mind, which it has in my memory, since the mind also remembers itself, and You were not there. For just as You are not a bodily image, nor the affection of a living being (such as we feel when we rejoice, grieve, desire, fear, remember, forget, and the like), so neither are You the mind itself, because You are the Lord God of the mind. All these things change, but You remain unchangeable above them all, and yet You have been pleased to dwell in my memory ever since I came to know You. And why do I now ask in which place of it You dwell, as though there really were places in it? This much is certain: You dwell in it, since I have remembered You from the time I came to know You, and I find You there whenever I call You to mind.

@@ 36
Where, then, did I find You, so that I could come to know You? For You were not in my memory before I came to know You. Where, then, did I find You, so that I could come to know You, except in Yourself, above me? There is no place at all; we move back and we move forward, and there is no place. Everywhere, O Truth, You preside over all who seek counsel from You, and You answer all of them at once, even though they ask Your counsel about many different things. You answer clearly, but not all hear clearly. All consult You about what they wish, but they do not always hear what they wish. Your best servant is the one who is intent not so much on hearing from You what he himself wants, as on wanting what he hears from You.

@@ 37
Late have I loved You, O Beauty so ancient and so new, late have I loved You! And see, You were within, and I was outside, and there I searched for You; and I, deformed, rushed headlong into those lovely things that You had made. You were with me, but I was not with You. Things held me far from You which, unless they were in You, would not exist at all. You called and cried out and broke through my deafness. You flashed, You shone, and You put my blindness to flight. You breathed Your fragrance, and I drew in my breath, and now I pant for You. I tasted, and now I hunger and thirst. You touched me, and I burned for Your peace.

@@ 38
When I cling to You with my whole self, I shall have no sorrow or toil anywhere, and my life will be truly alive, being wholly full of You. But now, since You lift up the one You fill, and I am not full of You, I am a burden to myself. Joys I should weep over contend with sorrows I should rejoice in, and which side will win I do not know. Woe is me! Lord, have pity on me. My evil sorrows contend with my good joys, and which side will win I do not know. Woe is me! Lord, have pity on me. Woe is me! See, I do not hide my wounds. You are the Physician, I am the sick one; You are merciful, I am miserable. Is not the life of man upon earth all trial? Who wants troubles and hardships? You command that they be endured, not loved. No one loves what he endures, even if he loves to endure it. For though he is glad that he endures, he would still prefer that there were nothing for him to endure. In adversity I long for prosperity; in prosperity I fear adversity. What middle ground is there between the two, where the life of man is not all trial? Woe to the prosperities of the world, once and again, because of the fear of adversity and the corruption of joy! Woe to the adversities of the world, once and again and a third time, because of the longing for prosperity, and because adversity itself is hard, and because it may shatter endurance. Is not the life of man upon earth all trial, without any respite?

@@ 39
All my hope lies nowhere but in Your exceedingly great mercy. Give what You command, and command what You will. You command us to be continent; and when I knew, as someone says, that no one can be continent unless God gives it, this too was part of wisdom, to know whose gift it is. For it is by continence that we are gathered up and brought back into the One, from whom we had scattered ourselves into the many. He loves You too little who loves anything along with You that he does not love for Your sake. O Love, ever burning and never quenched! O Charity, my God, set me on fire! You command continence: give what You command, and command what You will.

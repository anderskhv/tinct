Where were You for me then, and how far away? I was indeed wandering far from You, shut out even from the husks of the swine that I was feeding with husks. For how much better are the tales of the poets and grammarians than these traps! Verses and poems and "Medea flying" are certainly more useful than these men's five elements, dressed up in various colors to match the five caves of darkness, which do not exist at all and yet kill the one who believes in them. For verses and poems I can turn into real nourishment; and as for "Medea flying," though I sang about it, I did not claim it was true, and though I heard it sung, I did not believe it. But those other things I did believe. Woe, woe! By what steps I was led down into the depths of hell, toiling and feverish for lack of truth, because I was seeking You, my God (I confess it to You, who had mercy on me before I was yet confessing), not by the understanding of the mind, by which You willed me to be above the beasts, but by the sense of the flesh. But You were more inward to me than my innermost self, and higher than my highest. I stumbled upon that bold woman who has no sense, the one pictured in Solomon's riddle, sitting at her door and saying, Eat gladly the bread of secrecy, and drink the stolen waters, which are sweet. She seduced me, because she found my soul living outside itself, in the eye of my flesh, and chewing over the kind of food I had swallowed through that eye.

@@ 11
For I knew nothing else, nothing that truly is; and I was pushed, as if by some cleverness of mind, into siding with foolish deceivers when they asked me, "Where does evil come from?" and "Is God confined within a bodily shape, and does He have hair and nails?" and "Should those be considered righteous who had many wives at the same time, and killed people, and sacrificed animals?" In my ignorance these questions greatly troubled me, and while I was moving away from the truth I thought I was heading toward it. For I did not yet know that evil is nothing but a privation of good, until finally a thing ceases to exist altogether. How could I have seen that, when the sight of my eyes reached no further than bodies, and the sight of my mind no further than a phantasm? I did not know that God is a Spirit, not something with limbs stretched out in length and breadth, or whose being is bulk. For every bulk is smaller in a part than in its whole; and if it is infinite, it is smaller in any part marked out by a certain space than in its infinity, and so it is not wholly present everywhere, as a Spirit is, as God is. And what it is in us by which we are like God, and by which we are rightly said to be made in the image of God, I did not know at all.

@@ 12
Nor did I know that true inner righteousness which does not judge by custom but by the perfectly right law of Almighty God. By that law the customs of places and times were shaped to suit those places and times, while the law itself remains the same always and everywhere, not one thing in one place and another in another. By it Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and Moses, and David, and all those praised by the mouth of God, were righteous. Yet they were judged unrighteous by foolish people who judge by merely human standards and measure the morals of the whole human race by the small measure of their own habits. It is as if someone in an armory, not knowing what was made for which part of the body, tried to cover his head with leg armor, or to put a helmet on his foot, and then complained that they did not fit. Or as if, on a day when public business was suspended from noon onward, someone grew angry at not being allowed to keep his shop open in the afternoon, because he had been allowed to in the morning. Or as if, in a single house, he saw some servant handling something that the one who serves the cups was not permitted to touch, or something being done out of doors that was forbidden in the dining room, and was indignant that in one house and one household the same thing was not allowed everywhere and to everyone. Such are those who are upset to hear that something was once permitted to righteous men that is not permitted now; or that God, for reasons belonging to particular times, commanded one thing to those people and another to these, though both obeyed the same righteousness. And all the while they can see, in one man, and one day, and one house, that different things suit different parts, that something permitted a moment ago is not permitted an hour later, and that what is allowed or ordered in one corner is rightly forbidden and punished in another. Is justice, then, changeable or inconsistent? No; but the times over which it presides do not run evenly, because they are times. People, whose days on earth are few, cannot use their senses to connect the causes of earlier ages and other nations, which they have not experienced, with those they have experienced. Yet in one and the same body, or day, or household, they easily see what fits each part, each moment, each section and each person. So they take offense at the one and submit to the other.

@@ 13
These things I did not know then, nor did I notice them. They struck my eyes on every side, and I did not see them. I wrote verses, in which I was not free to put any foot anywhere I liked, but had to place them differently in different meters, and even within a single line could not use the same foot in every position. Yet the art by which I wrote did not have one principle here and another there; it held them all together at once. Even so, I did not see that the righteousness which good and holy men obeyed holds together at once, far more excellently and sublimely, all the things God commands, and varies in no part, even though at different times it does not prescribe everything at once but assigns and commands what is proper to each. And in my blindness I found fault with the holy Fathers, not only for the way they used present things as God commanded and inspired them, but also for the way they foretold things to come as God revealed them.

@@ 14
Can it ever, at any time or in any place, be unjust to love God with all one's heart, with all one's soul, and with all one's mind, and to love one's neighbor as oneself? So the shameful acts that are against nature must be hated and punished everywhere and always, such as those of the men of Sodom. If every nation committed them, all would be held guilty of the same crime under the law of God, which did not make human beings to use one another in that way. For the very fellowship that ought to exist between God and us is violated when that same nature of which He is the Author is polluted by perverted lust. But acts that offend against human customs are to be avoided according to the variety of those customs, so that an agreement a city or nation has made among its members, confirmed by custom or by law, may not be broken at the whim of anyone, whether citizen or foreigner. For any part that does not fit with its whole is ugly. But when God commands something against the custom or agreement of any people, even if it has never been done there before, it must be done; if it has been dropped, it must be restored; and if it was never established, it must now be established. For if a king may lawfully command, in the city he rules, something that no one before him, nor he himself, had ever commanded, and if obeying him is not against the good of that city (in fact, it would be against it not to obey, since it is a general agreement of human society to obey its rulers), how much more must we obey God without hesitation in everything He commands, the Ruler of all His creatures! For just as, among the authorities of human society, the greater authority is obeyed before the lesser, so God is obeyed above all.

# NOTES — Book 3, paragraphs 0-20

## Pusey kept on purpose

- 7: Col. 2:8-9 kept close to Pusey/KJV cadence ("Beware lest anyone spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ. For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily"); only "any man" -> "anyone", "dwelleth" -> "dwells". No quote marks added (Pusey has none).
- 9: "in whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning" (James 1:17) kept verbatim; famous scripture.
- 10: Prov. 9:17 kept recognizable: "Eat gladly the bread of secrecy, and drink the stolen waters, which are sweet."
- 20: "it is impossible that the son of these tears should perish" kept essentially as Pusey (famous line).
- 5: "Subverters" kept as Pusey's rendering of the gang name (Latin eversores); their deeds rendered "subversions".

## Latin consulted

- 0: "out of a deep-seated want, I hated myself for wanting not" -> Latin "secretiore indigentia oderam me minus indigentem": rendered "out of a need hidden deeper within me I hated myself for needing too little". "more, when I obtained to enjoy the person I loved" -> Latin "magis si et amantis corpore fruerer": rendered "sweeter still if I could also enjoy the body of the one I loved".
- 0: "circumstrepebat me undique sartago flagitiosorum amorum" — "sizzled and sang" restores the frying-pan (*sartago*/*Carthago*) noise Pusey's "cauldron … sang" loses (reviewer-noted, previously unlogged). Fix pass: "quanto felle" -> "with how much gall" (was "bitterness").
- 2: "if good will be ill willed" -> Latin "malivola benivolentia": rendered "ill-willed good will".
- 3: "feigned and personated misery" -> Latin "aliena et falsa et saltatoria": "someone else's, and invented, and acted out on a stage".
- 5: Pusey "feeding thereon their malicious birth" is a misprint; Latin "pascendo malivolas laetitias suas" -> "feeding their own malicious mirth".
- 5 (fix pass): "nomen scaevum et diabolicum" -> "ill-omened and devilish name" (was "perverse", which blurred the subverted/perverted wordplay).
- 7: "this alone checked me thus unkindled" -> Latin "hoc solum me in tanta flagrantia refrangebat": rendered "the only thing that held me back in all this blaze" (Pusey's "unkindled" contradicts the sense).
- 7 (fix pass): "amplexarer fortiter" -> "stirred me up strongly".
- 8: "lowly in access, in its recesses lofty" -> Latin "incessu humilem, successu excelsam": "humble at the entrance but lofty further in". "Yet were they such as would grow up in a little one" -> "quae cresceret cum parvulis": "grows up along with little ones".
- 9: "in looking for whom I fail, that I may become strong" -> "in quem deficio ut fortis sim": "for whom I faint so that I may become strong".
- 12: "the butler" -> Latin "qui pocula ministrat": "the one who serves the cups". "out of doors" (Latin "post praesepia", behind the stables) kept as Pusey's "out of doors". The day "when business is publicly stopped in the afternoon" = iustitium from noon; rendered "public business was suspended from noon onward".
- 15: "lust of the flesh, of the eye, or of rule" -> Latin principandi, spectandi, sentiendi; kept Pusey's order and triad ("power" for "rule"). "the three, and seven" = the two tables of the Decalogue; kept as "the three and the seven" without gloss.
- 17: "plucked by some other's, not his own, guilt" -> "alieno sane non suo scelere": "picked, of course, by someone else's guilt, not his own". "moan or groan" -> "gemendo ... atque ructando": "every sigh and belch".
- 18: "(in order to instruct, as is their wont not to be instructed)" -> "docendi, ut adsolet, non discendi gratia": "in order to teach, as such beings usually do, not to learn".
- 18 (fix pass): "fidelis tua" -> "Your faithful one" (not "servant"); "sic omnes tamquam singulos" -> "for all as though each were the only one" (parallel to *tamquam solum*).
- 20: "Go thy ways and God bless thee" -> "vade a me. ita vivas": "Go now, and God bless you".

## Uncertainties / open issues

- 11: Latin has "recte in scriptura diceremur ad imaginem dei"; Pusey omits "in Scripture", so it is omitted here too (following Pusey).
- 9: "Holy Ghost" (Pusey) rendered "Holy Spirit" per style note section 3.
- 18: "wooden rule" (regula lignea) kept literally; a reviewer may prefer "wooden ruler/measuring rod", but the Book 3 image of the "rule" of faith depends on the word.
- 0: "sizzled and sang" slightly expands Pusey's "sang" to carry the cauldron (sartago = frying pan) image; minor.

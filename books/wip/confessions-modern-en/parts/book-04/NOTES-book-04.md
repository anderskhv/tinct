- 3: "who will render to every man according to his works", "a broken and contrite heart" — familiar scripture wording.
- 13: "Blessed is the one who loves You, and his friend in You, and his enemy for Your sake" — famous formula, kept close to Pusey.
- 18: "like a bridegroom coming out of His chamber, He rejoiced like a giant to run His course" (Ps 19; pronouns capitalized in fix pass because the verse is applied to Christ); "O sons of men, how long will you be so heavy of heart?"; "set your mouth against the heavens"; "valley of tears" — scripture kept recognizable.
- 21: "they do not fall to the ground without You" — Pusey's scriptural rendering (Latin has "non minuuntur in te", "are not lessened in You"); kept Pusey's recognizable allusion.
- 24: "true light that lights every man who comes into the world"; "no variableness, nor shadow of change" — John 1:9 and James 1:17 cadence kept.
- 28: "briars and thorns", "eat my bread in the sweat of my brow" — Pusey's Genesis wording (Latin has "cum labore pervenirem ad panem meum").
- 29: "into a far country" — prodigal son.
- 30: "under/in the shelter of Your wings", "carry us even to gray hairs" (Isa 46:4).

## Latin consulted

- 1: "made sale of a loquacity to overcome by" — Latin *victoriosam loquacitatem victus cupiditate vendebam*; rendered "overcome by greed, I sold a talking skill designed to overcome others", keeping the wordplay.
- 1: "remaining faithful even to her" — Latin *servans tori fidem*; rendered "I kept faith with her bed" (reviewer-noted, previously unlogged).
- 2: "some wizard asked me" — Latin *mandasse mihi ... haruspicem* (a soothsayer sent to ask me); rendered "some soothsayer sent to ask me".
- 4: Pusey's "hanging assiduously ... on his speech (for though in simple terms, it was vivid, lively, and earnest)" — Latin *sine verborum cultu vivacitate sententiarum iucundi et graves*; rendered "plain in its words, lively, pleasant and serious in its thought".
- 4: "thou followest this of free choice" — Latin *hanc fallaciam*; rendered "you pursue this fraud" (reviewer-noted, previously unlogged).
- 5: "a youth singularly good and of a holy fear" — Latin *valde bonus et valde castus*; rendered "truly good and truly chaste".
- 8: "Trust in God" — Latin *spera in deum* (Ps 42); rendered "Hope in God", and "phantasm she was bid to trust in" as "phantom she was told to hope in".
- 10: "yet was I more unwilling to part with it than with him" — Latin *nollem tamen amittere magis quam illum*; rendered "I would have been even less willing to lose it than to lose him".
- 12: "Times lose no time" — Latin *non vacant tempora*; rendered "Time does not stand idle". "our soul, which lay itching in our ears" — *mens nostra pruriens in auribus*, rendered "our minds, which had itching ears".
- 14: Pusey's garbled "And yet they, out of Thee, and out of the soul, were not, unless they were from Thee" — Latin *tametsi figitur in pulchris extra te et extra se. quae tamen nulla essent, nisi essent abs te*; rendered "even if it is pinned to beautiful things outside You and outside itself. And yet those things would be nothing at all if they were not from You." Also "This is the law of them" (*sic est modus eorum*) → "That is the measure set for them"; "hence and hitherto" (*hinc et huc usque*) → "From here, and this far."
- 15: "where love is not forsaken, if itself forsaketh not" — *ubi non deseritur amor si ipse non deserat*; rendered "unless it abandons first". "thy mortal parts" (*fluxa tua*) → "your perishing parts".
- 16: Pusey's "Why then be perverted and follow thy flesh? Be it converted and follow thee" — Latin *ut quid perversa sequeris carnem tuam? ipsa te sequatur conversam* (the soul turned the wrong way / turned back); rendered "Why, then, turned the wrong way, do you follow your flesh? Let it follow you instead, once you have turned back." Fix pass (review MAJOR): the earlier "Let it turn around and follow you" made the flesh do the turning. *Perversa* and *conversam* both agree with the soul (*te*); the flesh (*ipsa*) is the subject only of *sequatur*. The single jussive is kept with the flesh as subject, and the turning is assigned to the soul, echoing "turned the wrong way" (*perversa*/*conversam*).
- 17: "where truth is loved" — Latin *ubi sapit veritas* (where truth is tasted/savored); rendered "wherever truth is savored". Pusey's doubled "Him let us love, Him let us love" kept (O'Donnell has it once). "through reference to Him" (*quantum est ad illum*) → "only as it relates to Him".
- 21: The horse/actor sentence — Latin *non enim sicut equus bonus amatur ... hoc et de histrione dicendum est, qui naturae nostrae socius est*; followed Pusey's reading ("it does not follow that ... the same can be said of an actor").
- 22: "I erred through a swelling pride" (*errabam typho*) → "wandering astray, puffed up with pride"; "though none joined therein" (*nullo conlaudatore mirabar*) → "even with no one to join me in praising it".
- 23: "panting soul" — *palpitantem mentem*; rendered "trembling mind". "a soul without sex" — *mentem*; rendered "a mind without sex". "anger, in deeds of violence, and in flagitiousness, lust" — *iram in facinoribus, libidinem in flagitiis*; rendered "anger in deeds of violence, lust in shameful acts". "in Thy wisdom" — Latin *in arte tua*; kept Pusey's "wisdom".
- 24: "defile the conversation" — *vitam contaminant*; rendered "defile one's life" (Pusey's "conversation" = way of life).
- 25: "a wind that passeth away" — *spiritus ambulans* (Ps 78:39); kept "a wind that passes away". "Thou resistedst my vain stiffneckedness" — *ventosae cervici meae*; "my puffed-up, stiff neck".
- 26: "corporeal fictions, buzzing in the ears of my heart" — *obstrepentia*; "clattered". Clarified that it was the ears of the heart that he was straining toward God's inward melody (*quas intendebam*).
- 28: "wonderful and unchangeable Unity" — Latin *mirabiliter simplicem*; kept Pusey's "Unity". "fictions of my misery, not the realities of Thy blessedness" — *non firmamenta*; rendered "not the firm realities".
- 30: Wordplay *aversi / perversi*, *revertamur / evertamur* kept as "turned away ... turned awry", "return ... overturned". "our mansion" (*domus nostra*) → "our home".

## Uncertainties / open issues

- 6 and 30: *caritas* rendered "love" ("the wings of love", "the fire of love" in 18) rather than Pusey's "charity", since modern "charity" suggests almsgiving. A reviewer may prefer "charity" for consistency across books.
- 6: "Holy Ghost" → "Holy Spirit" per the style note's term list.
- 3: "mathematicians" kept (Pusey's "Mathematicians", = astrologers); a reader may need the context, but no gloss was added.
- 14: "we shall be saved" (Latin *salvi erimus*) for Pusey's "we shall be whole".
- 24: "You will light my lamp" for Pusey's "Thou shalt light my candle" (Ps 18:28); modernized noun.
- 27: Pusey's "ten Predicaments" rendered "Ten Categories" and "nine categories ... the chief category of substance" (Latin *decem categorias*, *novem generibus*); "Predicaments" is archaic for the modern reader.

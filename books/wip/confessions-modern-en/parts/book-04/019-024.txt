I did not know these things then. I loved these lower beauties and was sinking into the depths, and I used to say to my friends: "Do we love anything except what is beautiful? What, then, is the beautiful? And what is beauty? What is it that draws us and attaches us to the things we love? For unless there were grace and beauty in them, they could never attract us to themselves." And I noticed and saw that in bodies themselves there is one kind of beauty that comes from their being a kind of whole, and another that comes from fitting well with something else, as a part of the body fits its whole, or a shoe fits a foot, and so on. This reflection welled up in my mind from my inmost heart, and I wrote books "On the Beautiful and the Fitting," two or three, I think. You know, O Lord, for it has slipped my memory. I no longer have them; they have strayed away from me somehow.

But what moved me, O Lord my God, to dedicate those books to Hierius, an orator in the city of Rome? I did not know him by sight, but I loved the man for his reputation for learning, which was brilliant, and I had heard some of his words, and they pleased me. But he pleased me more because he pleased others, who praised him to the skies, astonished that a Syrian, first trained in Greek eloquence, had afterward become a marvelous speaker in Latin as well, and was deeply learned in everything that belongs to the pursuit of wisdom. A man is praised, and though absent, he is loved. Does that love pass from the mouth of the one who praises into the heart of the one who hears? Not at all. Rather, one person is set on fire by another who loves. For this is why the one who is praised comes to be loved: when we believe he is being extolled by someone whose heart is not deceitful, that is, when the one praising him loves him.

@@ 21
That was how I loved people then: by human judgment, not by Yours, O my God, in whom no one is deceived. But still, why did I not love him the way people love a famous charioteer, or a fighter of wild beasts known far and wide through the crowd's enthusiasm, but in a very different and more serious way, the way I would have wanted to be praised myself? For I would not have wanted to be praised and loved as actors are, although I myself praised and loved them; I would rather have stayed unknown than be known in that way, and even be hated rather than loved in that way. Where in one soul are these weights of such various and different loves distributed? What is it that I love in another person, which, unless I hated it, I would not reject and push away from myself, since each of us is equally a man? For it does not follow that, just as a good horse is loved by someone who would not want to be that horse even if he could, the same can be said of an actor, who shares our nature. Do I then love in a man what I hate to be, though I am a man? Man himself is a great deep, whose very hairs You number, O Lord, and they do not fall to the ground without You. And yet the hairs of his head are easier to count than his feelings and the stirrings of his heart.

@@ 22
But that orator was the kind of man I loved in such a way that I wanted to be like him myself. I was wandering astray, puffed up with pride, and tossed about by every wind, and yet, very secretly, I was being steered by You. And how do I know, and how can I confess to You with certainty, that I had loved him more because of the love of those who praised him than because of the things for which he was praised? Because if those very same people had not praised him but had found fault with him, and had told the very same things about him with blame and contempt, I would never have been set on fire and stirred up about him. Yet the things themselves would not have been different, nor would the man himself; only the feelings of those telling about him. See where the weak soul lies, when it does not yet cling to the solidity of truth! As the breezes of tongues blow from the chests of those who hold opinions, so it is carried along and turned about, twisted this way and back again; the light is clouded over for it, and the truth is not seen. And yet, look, it is right in front of us. It mattered a great deal to me that my writing and my studies should become known to that man. If he approved of them, I would be all the more on fire; but if he disapproved, my vain heart, empty of Your solidity, would have been wounded. And yet that "beautiful and fitting," on which I had written to him, I gladly turned over in my mind and held before my eyes to contemplate, and I admired it even with no one to join me in praising it.

@@ 23
But I did not yet see the hinge on which so great a matter turns in Your wisdom, O Almighty, who alone do wonders. My mind moved among bodily forms. I defined and distinguished the "beautiful" as what is so in itself, and the "fitting" as what is becoming because it suits something else, and I supported this with bodily examples. Then I turned to the nature of the mind, but the false idea I held about spiritual things would not let me see the truth. The very force of truth flashed into my eyes, and yet I turned my trembling mind away from what is incorporeal toward outlines, colors and swelling sizes; and because I could not see these in the mind, I thought I could not see my mind. And since in virtue I loved peace, and in vice I hated discord, I noticed a kind of unity in the first and a kind of division in the second. In that unity I thought the rational mind, and the nature of truth and of the highest good, consisted. But in this division, wretched as I was, I imagined there was some unknown substance of irrational life, and the nature of the highest evil, which was not only a substance but truly a life, and yet did not come from You, O my God, from whom all things come. The first I called a Monad, as though it were a mind without sex; the second a Dyad: anger in deeds of violence, lust in shameful acts. I did not know what I was talking about. For I had not known or learned that evil is not a substance at all, and that our own mind is not the highest and unchangeable good.

For just as deeds of violence arise when that movement of the soul from which impulse springs is corrupted, and throws itself about insolently and wildly; and acts of lust arise when that affection of the soul by which fleshly pleasures are drunk in is out of control; so errors and false opinions defile one's life when the rational mind itself is corrupted. That was how it was in me then, for I did not know that the mind must be lit by another light in order to share in truth, since it is not itself the nature of truth. For You will light my lamp, O Lord my God; You will lighten my darkness; and of Your fullness we have all received. For You are the true light that lights every man who comes into the world, for in You there is no variableness, nor shadow of change.

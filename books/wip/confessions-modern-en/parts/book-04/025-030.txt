But I strained toward You and was pushed back from You, so that I might taste death; for You resist the proud. And what could be prouder than for me, in a strange madness, to claim that I was by nature what You are? For although I was changeable, and this much was plain to me, since my very desire to become wise was a wish to go from worse to better, I still preferred to think that You were changeable rather than that I was not what You are. So I was pushed back, and You resisted my puffed-up, stiff neck. I kept picturing bodily forms; flesh myself, I accused the flesh; and, a wind that passes away, I did not return to You, but went on and on toward things that have no being, not in You, not in me, and not in the body. Nor were they created for me by Your truth; they were made up by my vanity out of bodily things. And I used to say to Your faithful little ones, my fellow citizens, from whom I was exiled without knowing it, I used to say to them, chattering and foolish, "Why, then, does the soul that God made go wrong?" But I would not let anyone ask me, "Why, then, does God go wrong?" And I argued that Your unchangeable substance went wrong under compulsion, rather than admit that my changeable substance had strayed of its own accord and was now in error as its punishment.

@@ 26
I was about twenty-six or twenty-seven years old when I wrote those volumes, turning over within me bodily fictions that clattered in the ears of my heart. Those ears I was straining, O sweet Truth, toward Your inward melody, as I thought about the "beautiful and fitting." I longed to stand and listen to You and to rejoice greatly at the Bridegroom's voice, but I could not, because I was dragged outside by the voices of my own errors, and by the weight of my own pride I was sinking into the lowest pit. For You did not make me hear joy and gladness, nor did the bones rejoice, since they had not yet been humbled.

@@ 27
And what good did it do me that, when I was barely twenty years old, a work of Aristotle that they call the Ten Categories came into my hands, and I read it and understood it on my own? I used to hang on its very name, as on something great and divine, whenever my teacher of rhetoric at Carthage, and others who were thought learned, mouthed it with cheeks puffed out in pride. When I compared notes with others who said they had barely understood it with the help of very learned teachers, who not only explained it aloud but drew many diagrams in the sand, they could tell me nothing about it beyond what I had already learned by reading it alone. And the book seemed to me to speak quite clearly about substances, such as "man," and about what belongs to them: a man's shape, what sort it is; his height, how many feet tall; his relationships, whose brother he is; where he is placed, or when he was born; whether he is standing or sitting, whether he has shoes on or is armed; whether he is doing something or having something done to him; and all the countless things that can be found under these nine categories, of which I have given some examples, or under the chief category of substance itself.

@@ 28
What good did this do me, when it actually harmed me? For I thought that absolutely everything that exists was contained in those ten categories, and so I tried to understand even Your wonderful and unchangeable Unity, O my God, as though You were also a subject of Your own greatness or beauty, so that these existed in You as in a subject, the way they do in a body. But You Yourself are Your greatness and Your beauty, whereas a body is not great or beautiful by the fact that it is a body, since even if it were less great or less beautiful, it would still be a body. What I thought about You was falsehood, not truth; it was the fictions of my misery, not the firm realities of Your blessedness. For You had commanded, and so it was being done in me, that the earth should bring forth briars and thorns for me, and that I should eat my bread in the sweat of my brow.

@@ 29
And what good did it do me that I, at that time the worthless slave of evil desires, read on my own and understood every book I could get hold of on the arts they call liberal? I took pleasure in them, but I did not know where whatever was true and certain in them came from. For I had my back to the light and my face toward the things that are lit up, so that my face, with which I saw the things lit up, was not itself lit. Whatever there was on the art of speaking and the art of reasoning, on the measurements of figures, on music and on numbers, I understood without much difficulty and with no one to teach me. You know this, O Lord my God, because both quickness of understanding and sharpness of discernment are Your gift. Yet I did not offer sacrifice to You from them. So they served not for my good use but rather for my ruin, because I worked so hard to keep so good a portion of my substance under my own control. I did not keep my strength for You, but went away from You into a far country, to squander it on harlot desires. For what good did a good thing do me when I did not use it well? I did not realize that those arts are very hard to understand even for studious and gifted people, until I tried to explain them to such people; and the one who excelled most among them was the one who followed my explanations not too slowly.

@@ 30
But what good did this do me, when I thought that You, O Lord God, the Truth, were a vast, bright body, and that I was a chip of that body? What utter perversity! But that is what I was. Nor do I blush, O my God, to confess to You Your mercies toward me and to call on You, I who did not blush then to proclaim my blasphemies to people and to bark against You. What good, then, did my agile mind do me in those studies, and all those knotty books I untangled without the help of any human teacher, when I was going so hideously wrong, with such sacrilegious shamefulness, in the teaching of piety? Or what great harm did a much slower mind do to Your little ones, since they did not go far away from You, so that in the nest of Your Church they could safely grow their feathers and strengthen the wings of love on the food of a sound faith? O Lord our God, let us hope in the shelter of Your wings; protect us and carry us. You will carry us when we are little, and You will carry us even to gray hairs; for our firmness is firmness only when it is You, but when it is our own, it is weakness. Our good lives always with You, and because we turned away from it, we are turned awry. Let us now return, O Lord, so that we may not be overturned, for our good lives with You without any flaw, and that good is You Yourself. Nor are we afraid that there will be no place to return to because we fell from it; for while we were away, our home did not fall: Your eternity.

For this stretch of nine years, from my nineteenth year to my twenty-eighth, we lived led astray and leading others astray, deceived and deceiving, in all kinds of desires: openly, through the studies they call liberal; secretly, under the false name of religion; proud in one place, superstitious in another, empty everywhere. On one side we chased the hollowness of popular fame, all the way down to applause in the theater, prizes for poems, contests for garlands of grass, the nonsense of public shows, and unbridled desires. On the other side, wanting to be cleansed of these filthy things, we carried food to those who were called "elect" and "holy," so that out of it, in the workshop of their stomachs, they might forge for us angels and gods through whom we would be cleansed. These were the things I pursued and practiced with my friends, who were deceived through me and along with me. Let the arrogant mock me, and those who have not yet been struck down and crushed by You, O my God, for their soul's health; but I will still confess my own shame to You, in Your praise. Bear with me, I beg You, and give me grace to go back over, in my present memory, the winding paths of my past wanderings, and to offer You the sacrifice of thanksgiving. For what am I to myself without You, but a guide leading myself over a cliff? Or what am I, even at my best, but an infant sucking the milk You give, and feeding on You, the food that does not perish? And what kind of man is any man, since he is only a man? Let the strong and the mighty laugh at us now; but let us, the poor and needy, confess to You.

In those years I taught rhetoric, and, overcome by greed, I sold a talking skill designed to overcome others. Yet I preferred, Lord, as You know, to have good students, as they are usually reckoned, and without any trickery I taught them tricks: not to be used against the life of an innocent person, though sometimes to save the life of a guilty one. And You, O God, saw me from far off, stumbling on that slippery path, and through all the smoke giving off a few sparks of good faith, which I showed in my teaching of those who loved vanity and sought after lies, while I was their companion. In those years I had one woman, not joined to me in what is called lawful marriage, but one whom my restless passion, lacking all good sense, had tracked down; yet she was only one, and I kept faith with her bed. With her I learned from my own experience what a difference there is between the self-restraint of the marriage covenant, entered into for the sake of having children, and the bargain of a lustful love, in which children are born against their parents' wishes, although once they are born they compel their parents to love them.

I also remember that when I had decided to compete for a prize for theatrical verse, some soothsayer sent to ask me what fee I would pay him to make me win. But I, loathing and detesting those foul rites, answered that even if the garland were made of undying gold, I would not allow a fly to be killed to win it for me. For he was going to kill living creatures in his sacrifices, and with those offerings he seemed about to invite the demons to take my side. But I rejected this evil, too, not out of any pure love for You, O God of my heart; for I did not know how to love You, since I did not know how to think of anything beyond bodily brightness. And does not a soul that sighs after such fictions commit fornication against You, put its trust in false things, and feed the wind? Yet, of course, I would not have sacrifices offered to demons on my behalf, while I myself was offering myself to them through that superstition. For what else is feeding the wind but feeding them, that is, by going astray, becoming their delight and their laughingstock?

@@ 3
So I kept consulting, without any hesitation, those impostors called mathematicians, because they seemed to use no sacrifice and to direct no prayers to any spirit for the sake of their divinations. Even so, true Christian piety consistently rejects and condemns that art. For it is good to confess to You and to say, Have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against You; and not to abuse Your mercy as license to sin, but to remember the Lord's words: Behold, you are made whole; sin no more, lest something worse happen to you. They try to destroy all this healthy counsel when they say, "The cause of your sin is fixed in the heavens and cannot be avoided," and "Venus did this, or Saturn, or Mars." The point, of course, is that man, who is flesh and blood and proud corruption, should be blameless, while the Creator and Ordainer of heaven and the stars should bear the blame. And who is He but our God, the very sweetness and source of righteousness, You who will render to every man according to his works, and who will not despise a broken and contrite heart?

There was at that time a shrewd man, highly skilled in medicine and famous for it, who as proconsul had placed that contest garland with his own hand on my unhealthy head, though not as a physician. For that disease You alone cure, You who resist the proud and give grace to the humble. But did You fail me even through that old man, or stop healing my soul? I had become better acquainted with him, and I hung constantly and intently on his conversation, for though it was plain in its words, it was lively, pleasant and serious in its thought. When he learned from our talks that I was devoted to the books of the horoscope-casters, he advised me, kindly and like a father, to throw them away and not to waste on such empty things the care and effort that useful matters require. He told me that in his early years he had studied that art with the intention of making it the profession he would live by, and that since he understood Hippocrates, he could certainly have understood these writings too. Yet he had later given them up and taken to medicine for no other reason than that he had found them utterly false, and as a serious man he would not earn his living by deceiving people. "But you," he said, "have rhetoric to support yourself by in the world, so you pursue this fraud out of free choice, not out of need. All the more reason, then, for you to believe me about it, since I worked to learn it so thoroughly that I meant to live by it alone." When I asked him what, then, caused many true things to be foretold by it, he answered as best he could that it was the force of chance, spread everywhere throughout the order of nature. For, he said, if someone happens to consult the pages of a poet who was singing and thinking about something entirely different, a verse often comes out that fits the matter at hand astonishingly well. So it was no wonder if, from a human soul unaware of what was happening within it, some higher impulse drew out an answer, by chance and not by art, that matched the affairs and actions of the person asking.

@@ 5
This much, then, You provided for me, either from him or through him, and You traced in my memory what I would later investigate for myself. But at that time neither he nor my dearest Nebridius, a young man truly good and truly chaste, who laughed at the whole business of divination, could persuade me to throw these things aside. The authority of the writers carried more weight with me, and I had not yet found the kind of sure proof I was looking for, one that would show me beyond all doubt that the true things said by those who were consulted came from luck or chance, and not from the art of reading the stars.

In those years, when I first began to teach in the town where I was born, I had gained a friend who, through our shared studies, became far too dear to me. He was my own age, and like me he was in the first bloom of youth. He had grown up with me as a boy, and we had gone to school together and played together. But he was not yet my friend in the way he later became, and even then not in the way true friendship is; for friendship cannot be true unless You bind it together between those who cling to You, through that love which is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us. Still, it was all too sweet, ripened by the heat of our common studies. For I had also turned him away from the true faith, which as a young man he did not hold genuinely or deeply, toward those superstitious and destructive fables for which my mother wept over me. Now this man went astray in mind along with me, and my soul could not be without him. But look: You were close behind Your runaways, at once God of vengeance and Fountain of mercies, turning us to Yourself in wonderful ways. You took that man out of this life when he had barely completed one year of friendship with me, a friendship sweeter to me than all the sweetness of that life of mine.

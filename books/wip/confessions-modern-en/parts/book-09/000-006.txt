O Lord, I am Your servant; I am Your servant, and the son of Your handmaid: You have broken my bonds apart. I will offer You the sacrifice of praise. Let my heart and my tongue praise You; yes, let all my bones say, O Lord, who is like You? Let them say it, and then answer me, and say to my soul, I am your salvation. Who am I, and what sort of man am I? What evil has there not been, if not in my deeds, then in my words, and if not in my words, then in my will? But You, O Lord, are good and merciful. Your right hand looked upon the depth of my death, and drained that abyss of corruption from the bottom of my heart. And this was the whole of Your gift: that I should refuse what I had willed, and will what You willed. But where was my free will through all those years, and out of what low and deep hiding place was it called out in a moment, so that I could bow my neck to Your easy yoke and put my shoulders under Your light burden, O Christ Jesus, my Helper and my Redeemer? How sweet it suddenly became to me to be without the sweetness of those trifles! What I had been afraid to lose, I was now glad to let go. For You were driving them out of me, You who are the true and highest sweetness. You drove them out, and in their place You came in Yourself, sweeter than every pleasure, though not to flesh and blood; brighter than every light, yet more inward than any hidden depth; higher than every honor, though not to those who are high in their own eyes. Now my soul was free from the gnawing cares of seeking favor and getting gain, of wallowing in filth and scratching the itch of lust. And like a child I chattered freely to You, my brightness, my riches, my health, the Lord my God.

And I decided, in Your sight, not to tear my tongue's service out of the market of talk in a noisy way, but to withdraw it quietly. I did not want boys who were studying neither Your law nor Your peace, but lying follies and the battles of the courts, to go on buying weapons for their madness from my mouth. And very conveniently, only a very few days now remained before the Vintage Vacation. I resolved to endure them, and then to take my leave in the proper way, so that, having been bought back by You, I would never again return to be put up for sale. Our plan, then, was known to You; but it was not known to other people, apart from our own friends. For we had agreed among ourselves not to spread it around to anyone. And yet, as we climbed up out of the valley of tears and sang that song of ascents, You had given us sharp arrows and destroying coals against the deceitful tongue, the kind of tongue that opposes us as if it were giving us advice, and that devours us out of love, the way it devours its food.

@@ 2
You had pierced our hearts with Your love, and we carried Your words as though they were fixed deep inside us. And the examples of Your servants, whom You had turned from black to bright and from dead to living, were gathered together in the storehouse of our thoughts. They burned away our heavy sluggishness, so that we would not sink toward the depths, and they set us on fire so fiercely that every gust from the deceitful tongues of those who opposed us could only fan the flame higher, not put it out. Nevertheless, for the sake of Your Name, which You have made holy throughout the earth, our vow and purpose would no doubt also find some people to praise it. So it seemed like showing off not to wait for the vacation, now so close, but to walk away early from a public profession that was in front of everyone's eyes. Then everyone, turning to look at what I had done and noticing how near the vintage holiday was that I had chosen to anticipate, would say a great deal about me, as though I had wanted to seem an important person. And what good would it have done me to have people guessing and arguing about my intentions, and to have our good spoken of as evil?

@@ 3
There was more. That very summer, from too much literary work, my lungs had begun to give way. I found it hard to breathe deeply, the pains in my chest showed that they were damaged, and they would not let me speak loudly or for long. At first this had troubled me, because it was almost forcing me to lay down the burden of teaching altogether, or at least to break it off, if I could be treated and recover. But once the full desire to be at leisure, and to see that You are the Lord, had risen up in me and taken firm hold, then, my God, You know that I even began to be glad that I had this further excuse, and not a false one. It could somewhat soften the offense of those who, for their sons' sake, never wanted me to have the freedom of Your sons. So, full of this joy, I put up with that stretch of time until it ran out; I think it was perhaps twenty days. Even so, I endured them bravely; endured them, because the greed that used to carry part of this heavy business with me had gone, and I was left alone with it; I would have been crushed if patience had not taken its place. Perhaps some of Your servants, my brothers, will say that I sinned in this: that with a heart already fully set on Your service I let myself sit even one hour in the chair of lies. I will not argue. But You, most merciful Lord, have You not pardoned and forgiven this sin too, along with my other horrible and deadly sins, in the holy water?

Verecundus was worn down with worry over this happiness of ours, because he saw that the bonds that held him so tightly were going to cut him off from our company. He was not yet a Christian himself, though his wife was one of the faithful; and yet it was precisely by her, more firmly than by any other chain, that he was held back from the journey we had now set out on. For he said he did not want to be a Christian in any other way than the one that was impossible for him. All the same, he very kindly offered to let us stay at his country house for as long as we were there. You will repay him, O Lord, at the resurrection of the just, since You have already given him the lot of the righteous. For while we were away, already in Rome, he was struck by a bodily illness, and during it he became a Christian, one of the faithful, and departed this life. So You had mercy not only on him but on us too, so that we would not be tortured by unbearable grief, remembering our friend's great kindness to us and yet unable to count him among Your flock. Thanks be to You, our God! We are Yours. Your encouragements and consolations tell us so. Faithful to Your promises, You now repay Verecundus for that country house of his at Cassiacum, where we rested in You from the fever of the world, with the loveliness of Your Paradise, green forever. For You have forgiven him his sins on earth, on that rich mountain, the mountain that flows with milk, Your own mountain.

@@ 5
So at that time he was sad, but Nebridius shared our joy. For he too, before he was a Christian, had fallen into the pit of that most destructive error of believing that the flesh of Your Son was a phantom. Yet he had climbed out of it and held the same belief as we did. He had not yet received any of the Sacraments of Your Church, but he was a most passionate seeker of truth. Not long after our conversion and our rebirth through Your Baptism, when he too had become one of the Catholic faithful and was serving You in perfect chastity and continence among his own people in Africa, and his whole household had become Christian through him, You released him from the flesh. And now he lives in Abraham's bosom. Whatever it is that is meant by that bosom, that is where my Nebridius lives, my sweet friend, and Your son, O Lord, adopted after being a freedman: that is where he lives. For what other place is there for such a soul? He lives in that place about which he used to ask me, a poor untried man, so many questions. Now he no longer puts his ear to my mouth, but puts his spiritual mouth to Your fountain, and drinks as much wisdom as he can hold, as much as his thirst desires, happy without end. And I do not think he is so drunk with it that he forgets me, since You, Lord, whom he drinks, remember us. That was how things stood with us then. We comforted Verecundus, who was sad about our conversion, though without any harm to our friendship, and we urged him toward the faith suited to his condition, that is, to married life. And we were waiting for Nebridius to follow us, which, being so close, he could do, and he was on the very point of doing it. And then, look, those days finally rolled by. They had seemed long and many, because of my love for the freedom of leisure, so that I could sing to You from my inmost marrow: My heart has said to You, I have sought Your face: Your face, Lord, I will seek.

@@ 6
Then the day came when I was actually released from my Professorship of Rhetoric, from which I had already been released in thought. And it was done. You rescued my tongue, as You had already rescued my heart. And I blessed You, rejoicing, as I set out for the country house with all my people. What I did there in writing, which was now enlisted in Your service, though it was still panting from the school of pride, as if in a pause for breath, my books bear witness: both the discussions I held with those present and the ones I held with myself alone before You. What I discussed with Nebridius, who was absent, my letters bear witness. And when will I have time enough to recount all Your great kindnesses to us in that period, especially when I am hurrying on to still greater mercies? For my memory calls me back, and it is sweet to me, O Lord, to confess to You by what inner goads You tamed me, and how You leveled me by bringing low the mountains and hills of my proud thoughts, straightening what was crooked in me and smoothing my rough places. And I recall how You also brought the brother of my heart, Alypius, to submit to the name of Your Only Begotten, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, a name he at first would not allow to be put into our writings. For he would rather have had them smell of the lofty cedars of the Schools, which the Lord has now broken down, than of the healthy herbs of the Church, which are a remedy against serpents.

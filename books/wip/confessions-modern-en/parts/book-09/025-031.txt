These were the things I was saying, and if not in exactly this way or in these same words, still, Lord, You know that on that day, as we talked of these things and this world with all its delights grew worthless to us while we spoke, my mother said, "Son, for my own part, I no longer take delight in anything in this life. What I am still doing here, and why I am here, I do not know, now that my hopes in this world have been fulfilled. There was one thing for which I wanted to stay a little longer in this life: to see you a Catholic Christian before I died. My God has done this for me more abundantly, so that I now see you even despising earthly happiness and become His servant. What am I doing here?"

@@ 26
What answer I gave her to this, I do not remember. For hardly five days later, or not much more, she fell sick with a fever. During that illness she fainted one day and was for a while cut off from the things around her. We hurried to her side, but she soon came back to her senses. Looking at me and my brother standing beside her, she asked us, as if searching, "Where was I?" Then, looking steadily at us as we stood stunned with grief, she said, "Here you will bury your mother." I kept silent and held back my tears. But my brother said something to the effect that he wished, as the happier fate, that she might die not in a foreign place but in her own country. When she heard this, she gave him an anxious look, rebuking him with her eyes for still thinking of such things. Then she looked at me and said, "See what he is saying." And soon afterward she said to us both, "Lay this body anywhere. Do not let any concern about it trouble you at all. I ask only this of you: that you remember me at the altar of the Lord, wherever you may be." When she had expressed this wish in what words she could manage, she fell silent, as her illness grew worse and wore her down.

@@ 27
But I, reflecting on Your gifts, You unseen God, which You plant in the hearts of Your faithful and from which wonderful fruits grow, rejoiced and gave thanks to You. I remembered what I had known before: how careful and anxious she had always been about her burial place, which she had provided and prepared for herself beside the body of her husband. Because they had lived together in great harmony, she had also wished (so little can the human mind grasp divine things) to have this added to that happiness, and to have it remembered among people, that after her journey across the sea it had been granted that the earthly part of this united couple should be joined together under the same earth. When this emptiness had begun, through the fullness of Your goodness, to fade from her heart, I did not know; and I rejoiced, marveling at what she had revealed to me in this way. And yet even in our conversation at the window, when she said, "What am I still doing here?" there had been no sign of a desire to die in her own country. I heard afterward, too, that while we were at Ostia, one day when I was away she talked, with a mother's openness, with some of my friends about how little this life is worth and about the blessing of death. They were amazed at such courage, which You had given to a woman, and asked her whether she was not afraid to leave her body so far from her own city. She replied, "Nothing is far from God. There is no fear that at the end of the world He will not know where to raise me up from." So on the ninth day of her illness, in the fifty-sixth year of her life and the thirty-third of mine, that devout and holy soul was set free from the body.

@@ 28
I closed her eyes, and an immense sorrow came flooding into my heart and was about to overflow in tears. At the same moment my eyes, under the forceful command of my mind, drank their spring back until it was quite dry, and I was in misery in such a struggle. But when she breathed her last, the boy Adeodatus burst out into loud wailing; then, restrained by all of us, he fell silent. In the same way something childish in me, which was slipping into tears, was restrained by the grown-up voice of my heart and fell silent. For we did not think it right to mark that funeral with tearful complaints and groaning, since these are usually how people mourn the dead as if they were wretched, or as if they had been wholly destroyed. But she did not die wretched, nor did she wholly die. We were sure of this on good grounds: the evidence of her way of life and her unfeigned faith.

@@ 29
What was it, then, that hurt me so badly inside, if not a fresh wound, made by the sudden tearing apart of that very sweet and dear habit of living together? I did rejoice in her testimony when, in that last illness of hers, responding to my attentions with her own endearments, she called me a "good son," and told me with deep affection that she had never heard any harsh or reproachful word from my mouth against her. But still, O my God, who made us, what comparison is there between the honor I showed her and the way she slaved for me? So, having lost the great comfort I had in her, my soul was wounded, and my life was torn apart, as it were, because hers and mine together had been made into one.

So when the boy had been calmed from his weeping, Euodius took up the Psalter and began to sing a Psalm, with our whole household answering him: I will sing of mercy and judgment to You, O Lord. And when they heard what was happening, many brothers and devout women gathered. While those whose task it was made the arrangements for the burial, as is the custom, I withdrew to a part of the house where I could properly do so, together with those who thought I should not be left alone, and talked about something suited to the occasion. With this soothing balm of truth I eased my torment, which was known to You but not to them. They listened attentively and supposed I felt no sorrow at all. But in Your ears, where none of them could hear, I scolded the softness of my feelings and held back the flood of my grief. It gave way to me a little, but then it swept back again with force, though not so far as to burst into tears or to change my expression; still, I knew what I was pressing down in my heart. And because I was deeply displeased that these human things, which must happen in the proper order and by the lot of our condition, had such power over me, I grieved over my grief with a second grief, and so was worn down by a double sadness.

And then the body was carried out for burial. We went, and we came back, without tears. For not even during the prayers we poured out to You when the Sacrifice of our redemption was offered for her, with the body already beside the grave before being laid in it, as is the custom there, not even during those prayers did I weep. Yet all day long I was deeply sad in secret, and with a troubled mind I begged You, as well as I could, to heal my pain; but You did not. I believe You were impressing on my memory, by this single example, how strong the bond of every habit is, even on a mind that no longer feeds on a deceiving word. I also decided to go and take a bath, because I had heard that baths (balneum) got their name from the Greek Balaneion, since they drive anxiety out of the mind. And this too I confess to Your mercy, Father of the fatherless: I bathed, and I was the same after bathing as before. For the bitterness of grief could not sweat out of my heart.

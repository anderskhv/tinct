@@ 7
Oh, what words I spoke to You, my God, when I read the Psalms of David, those songs of faith, those sounds of devotion that leave no room for a swollen spirit! I was still a Catechumen, a beginner in the true love of You, resting at that country house with Alypius, also a Catechumen, and with my mother staying close to us: a woman in dress, a man in faith, with the calm of old age, a mother's love and a Christian's piety. Oh, what words I poured out to You in those Psalms, and how they set me burning toward You, and how eager they made me to recite them, if I could, to the whole world, against the pride of the human race! And yet they are sung throughout the whole world, and no one can hide himself from Your heat. With what fierce and bitter grief I was angry with the Manichees! And then again I pitied them, because they did not know those Sacraments, those medicines, and raged against the very antidote that could have cured them of their madness. How I wish they had been somewhere near me at that time, and, without my knowing they were there, could have watched my face and heard my words when I read the fourth Psalm in that time of rest, and seen what that Psalm did to me: When I called, the God of my righteousness heard me; in trouble You set me at large. Have mercy on me, O Lord, and hear my prayer. If only they could have heard what I said over these words, without my knowing whether they heard, so that they would not think I was saying it for their benefit! For in truth I would not have said the same things, or said them in the same way, if I had sensed that they were hearing and watching me. Nor, if I had said them, would they have taken them in the way I meant them when I spoke by myself and for myself before You, out of the natural feelings of my soul.

@@ 8
I trembled with fear, and then again I glowed with hope and with joy in Your mercy, O Father. And all of it came out through my eyes and my voice, when Your good Spirit turned to us and said, O sons of men, how long will you be slow of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek after lies? For I had loved vanity and sought after lies. And You, O Lord, had already magnified Your Holy One, raising Him from the dead and setting Him at Your right hand, from where, on high, He was to send His promise, the Comforter, the Spirit of truth. And He had already sent Him, but I did not know it. He had sent Him because He was now magnified, having risen again from the dead and ascended into heaven. For until then the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified. And the prophet cries out, How long, slow of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek after lies? Know this: the Lord has magnified His Holy One. He cries out, How long? He cries out, Know this. And I, for so long not knowing, had loved vanity and sought after lies. That is why I heard it and trembled, because it was spoken to people like the man I remembered I had been. For in those phantoms that I had taken for truths there was vanity and lying. And I said many things aloud, earnestly and forcefully, in the bitterness of remembering. If only they had heard them, those who still love vanity and seek after lies! Perhaps they would have been shaken and vomited it up, and You would have heard them when they cried to You. For it was by a true death in the flesh that He died for us, He who now intercedes with You for us.

@@ 9
I read on: Be angry, and do not sin. And how it moved me, O my God! I had now learned to be angry with myself for what was past, so that I would not sin in the future. Yes, to be angry with good reason, because it was not some other nature, belonging to a race of darkness, that sinned in me, as those people claim who are not angry with themselves and who store up wrath for themselves against the day of wrath and of the revelation of Your just judgment. My good things were no longer outside me, and I no longer looked for them with eyes of flesh under this earthly sun. For those who want to find their joy outside themselves easily become empty, and pour themselves out on the things that are seen and temporal, and in their starving thoughts they lick at the mere images of them. Oh, if only they would grow tired of their hunger and say, Who will show us good things? Then we would say, and they would hear, The light of Your face is sealed upon us. For we are not the light that enlightens every man; we are enlightened by You, so that we who were once darkness may be light in You. Oh, if only they could see that eternal inward thing! I had tasted it, and I was grieved that I could not show it to them, as long as they brought me their heart in their eyes, wandering outside, away from You, and asked, Who will show us good things? For it was there, where I had been angry with myself, inside, in my own chamber, where I had been pierced to the heart, where I had offered sacrifice, slaying my old self and beginning to plan a new life, putting my hope in You, it was there that You had begun to grow sweet to me and had put gladness in my heart. And I cried out as I read these words outwardly and recognized them inwardly. I did not want to be multiplied with worldly goods, wasting time and being wasted by time, when I had, in Your eternal Simple Essence, a different corn, and wine, and oil.

@@ 10
And with a loud cry of my heart I cried out at the next verse: O in peace! O in the Self-same! O what did he say? I will lie down and sleep. For who will stand against us when the saying that is written comes to pass, Death is swallowed up in victory? And You are the Self-same in the highest degree, You who do not change; and in You is the rest that forgets all toil, for there is no one else beside You, and we are not to go after those many other things that are not what You are. But You, Lord, alone have made me dwell in hope. I read and I burned, and I could not find what to do for those deaf and dead people, of whom I myself had been one: a plague, a bitter and blind barker against those writings that are sweet with the honey of heaven and bright with Your own light. And I wasted away with zeal against the enemies of this Scripture.

@@ 11
When will I call to mind everything that happened in those holidays? Still, I have not forgotten, and I will not pass over, the severity of Your scourge and the wonderful swiftness of Your mercy. At that time You tormented me with toothache. When it became so bad that I could not speak, it came into my heart to ask all my friends who were there to pray for me to You, the God of every kind of health. I wrote this on a wax tablet and gave it to them to read. As soon as we had bent our knees in humble devotion, the pain went away. But what pain was it? And how did it go away? I was terrified, O my Lord, my God, for I had never experienced anything like it since infancy. And the power of Your will was deeply impressed upon me, and rejoicing in faith I praised Your Name. And that faith did not let me be at ease about my past sins, which had not yet been forgiven me through Your baptism.

@@ 12
When the vintage vacation was over, I gave notice to the people of Milan that they should find their students another teacher to sell them words, because I had chosen to serve You, and also because my difficulty in breathing and the pain in my chest made me unfit for the Professorship. And by letter I made known to Your bishop, the holy man Ambrose, my former errors and my present desire, and asked his advice about which of Your Scriptures I should read first, to become more ready and more fit to receive so great a grace. He told me to read the Prophet Isaiah, because, I believe, he foretells the Gospel and the calling of the Gentiles more clearly than the others. But I did not understand the first part of it, and, supposing that the whole book would be like that, I put it aside, meaning to take it up again when I was better practiced in the Lord's own words.

# NOTES — Confessions Book 1, paragraphs 0-37 (modern-en revision)

Fresh sentence-by-sentence rendering from Pusey, checked against O'Donnell's Latin. The previously served modern-en (partial, You-convention) was consulted only as a draft; every paragraph was re-rendered and re-verified.

## Pusey kept on purpose

- p0 "Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised" cadence ("You are great, O Lord, and greatly to be praised"); "our heart is restless until it rests in You"; "how shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? Or how shall they believe without a preacher?"; "they that seek the Lord shall praise Him" (Ps 22 / Rom 10) — famous formulas.
- p1 "of whom are all things, by whom are all things, in whom are all things"; "Even so, Lord, even so"; "I fill heaven and earth" (Jer 23:24) — scripture.
- p4 "I am your salvation" (Ps 35:3); "Do not hide Your face from me"; "Let me die, so that I may not die" — scripture/famous paradox.
- p5 "cleanse me from my secret faults"; "if You, Lord, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could endure it?" (Ps 19, Ps 130) — scripture, lightly modernized.
- p11 "shaped in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me" (Ps 51:5).
- p20 "seeking by the sword a stroke and wound extreme" — Pusey's verse rendering of Virgil kept as a quotation.
- p21 "Creusa's shade and sad similitude" — Pusey's verse phrase for Virgil kept inside quotation marks.
- p26, p28, p29 — Pusey's verse lines from Terence (Eunuchus) lightly modernized but kept as verse quotation (p28 ~0.7 similarity is inherent to a 13-word verse quote).
- p34 "long-suffering, and plenteous in mercy and truth"; "I have sought Your face; Your face, Lord, will I seek" (Ps 27:8); "riotous living", "a far country" (Luke 15) — scripture.
- p36 "Of such is the kingdom of heaven" — scripture.

## Latin consulted

- p2: "non enim vasa quae te plena sunt stabilem te faciunt" — Pusey "uphold Thee not"; kept as "do not hold You up".
- p3: "supererogatur tibi ut debeas" — rendered "More than is due is paid to You, so that You become the debtor" (Pusey "receivest over and above, that Thou mayest owe").
- p7: last sentence — Latin "me talem fuisse magis mihi ipsi indicaverunt nescientes quam scientes nutritores mei": the unknowing infants showed me better than my knowing nurses. Rendered unambiguously (see corrections below).
- p9: "auctoritatibus etiam muliercularum" — Pusey "weak females"; rendered "on the word of simple women". "amet non inveniendo invenire potius quam inveniendo non invenire te" — rendered "be glad to find You without working it out, rather than work it out and fail to find You" (object te shared by both halves).
- p9 (fix pass): "omnia crastina ... hodie facies, hodie fecisti" — Pusey drops "hodie facies"; restored per §8 ("You will do today, You have done today"). "tu autem idem ipse es" rendered "You are still the same" (no "forever"). "non enim haberent vias transeundi" rendered "no way of passing away".
- p10: "nec vidi quemquam scientem ... bona proicere" — rendered "I have never seen anyone ... knowingly throw away what is good", following the Latin (noted by reviewer, previously unlogged).
- p10: "uberibus inhiabam plorans" — gaping/crying for the breast; Pusey "hung upon the breast and cried". Rendered "cried with my mouth wide open for the breast".
- p14: Latin "diligens eos qui haec acerbissime formidant" = "while loving those who dread them most bitterly"; Pusey has "mocking at those by whom they are feared". Followed the Latin (the point is that our parents loved us yet laughed at our torments). Reviewer may prefer Pusey's reading.
- p15: "ordinator et creator rerum omnium naturalium, peccatorum autem tantum ordinator" — Pusey "Disposer"; rendered "Creator of all things in nature and the One who orders them, but of sin only the One who orders it".
- p16: Pusey omits "aestuarem" (fever) and "in Christum" — followed Pusey, did not add.
- p32: Pusey prints "Latinum"; Latin "Italia ... avertere regem" (Virgil's Latium). Rendered "Latium".
- p14 (fix pass): "non enim aut minus ea metuebamus aut minus te de his evadendis deprecabamur" — rendered "we did not fear our torments any less, nor pray to You any less" (the earlier "than they" had no referent).
- p35: Latin "quam tu secretus es" = "How hidden You are"; Pusey "How deep are Thy ways". Followed the Latin: "How hidden You are, O God".
- p36: "magis timebam barbarismum facere quam cavebam, si facerem, non facientibus invidere" — rendered per Latin ("more afraid of committing a barbarism than I was careful, if I did commit one, not to envy those who had not"), which is what Pusey's compressed sentence means.

## Uncertainties / open issues

- Similarity to Pusey came out lower (weighted 0.43) than the 0.55-0.65 aim stated for this revision; the brief's general target is 0.4-0.6 and all paragraphs are within ratio/sim limits. All content re-verified clause by clause.
- p25 ends with a comma, as Pusey's p25 flows into the verse quotation in p26; p28 opens a quote closed in p29, and p31 ends mid-sentence ("she could not") leading into p32 — structure mirrors Pusey.
- p14 and p35 follow the Latin over Pusey (see above) — reviewer check.

### Corrections to prior draft (served modern-en)

- p7: old draft ended "they, all unconsciously, have shown me better than my nurses who knew it, that I myself was like this" — ambiguous word order; now clearly the infants (without knowing it) showed me better than my nurses (who knew).
- p9: old draft "believe much on the word of weak women" kept sense; last sentence previously "discover You by not discovering, than to not discover You by discovering" — now idiomatic.
- p14: old draft kept Pusey's "mocking those by whom they are feared" — changed per Latin (diligens).
- p35: old draft "How deep are Your ways" — changed per Latin to "How hidden You are".
- p32: old draft (and Pusey) "Latinum" — corrected to "Latium".
- Throughout: old draft retained Pusey-shaped syntax and archaisms ("unbecomingly", "playfellow", "yes, behold patiently, as You are accustomed", "wallowed", "masters", "cleaving", "endowed", "lest") — all re-expressed in present-day syntax.

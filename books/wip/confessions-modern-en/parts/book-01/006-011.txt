Still, let me speak before Your mercy, I who am dust and ashes. Let me speak all the same, since it is Your mercy I am speaking to, and not some man who would laugh at me. Perhaps You too laugh at me; yet You will turn back and have compassion on me. For what do I want to say, O Lord my God, except that I do not know where I came from into this dying life, or should I call it a living death? From the very start the comforts of Your compassion took me up, as I was told by the parents of my flesh, from whose substance You once formed me; I do not remember it myself. And so the comforts of a woman's milk received me. It was not my mother or my nurses who filled their own breasts for me; You gave me the food of my infancy through them, according to Your ordinance, by which You distribute Your riches through the hidden springs of all things. You also made me want no more than You gave, and made my nurses glad to give me what You gave them. For with an affection taught by heaven they gladly gave me what they had in abundance from You. This good that came to me from them was good for them as well. In fact it did not come from them but through them; for all good things come from You, O God, and from my God comes all my health. I learned this only later, as You proclaimed Yourself to me through these very gifts of Yours, both within me and outside me. At the time all I knew was how to suck, to settle contentedly into whatever pleased me, and to cry at whatever hurt my flesh; nothing more.

@@ 7
After that I began to smile, first in my sleep and then when awake. That is what I was told about myself, and I believed it, because we see other babies do the same; I have no memory of it myself. Then, little by little, I began to notice where I was, and I wanted to make my wishes known to those who could satisfy them, and I could not. My wishes were inside me and those people were outside, and none of their senses could reach into my spirit. So I threw my limbs about and made noises at random, the few signs I could manage, and such as I could manage, meant to resemble what I wanted, though in truth they resembled it very little. And when I was not obeyed at once, because what I wanted would have harmed me or could not be understood, I was furious with my elders for not submitting to me, and with people who owed me no service for not serving me, and I took my revenge on them with tears. This is what I have learned babies are like, from watching them; and that I was like this too, those babies, without knowing it, have shown me better than my nurses, who did know.

@@ 8
And look, my infancy died long ago, and I am alive. But You, Lord, live forever, and nothing in You dies. For before the foundation of the worlds, and before anything that can even be called "before," You are, and You are God and Lord of all that You have created. In You stand, fixed forever, the first causes of all the things that do not last; the sources of all changeable things remain unchangeable in You; and the eternal reasons of all things that are without reason and bound to time live in You. Tell me, Lord, tell Your suppliant; You who are all pity, tell me, Your pitiable one: did my infancy follow some earlier stage of my life that had died before it? Was it the time I spent in my mother's womb? I have been told something about that, and I have seen pregnant women with my own eyes. And before that life, O God my joy, what then? Was I anywhere, or anybody? I have no one to tell me this: not my father or my mother, not the experience of others, not my own memory. Are You laughing at me for asking such things, and telling me to praise You and acknowledge You for what I do know?

@@ 9
I acknowledge You, Lord of heaven and earth, and I praise You for the first beginnings of my being and for my infancy, which I do not remember at all. For You have arranged that a person should infer much about himself from others, and take much about himself on trust from the word of simple women. Even then I existed and was alive, and toward the end of my infancy I was already looking for signs by which I could make my feelings known to others. Where could such a creature come from, if not from You, Lord? Can anyone be his own craftsman? Or is there some channel, coming from anywhere else, through which being and life can flow into us, except from You, O Lord, in whom being and life are one and the same? For You Yourself are supremely Essence and Life. You are the highest, and You do not change; today does not come to its end in You, and yet it does end in You, because all such things exist in You as well. They would have no way of passing away unless You held them in being. And since Your years do not fail, Your years are a single today. How many of our years and our fathers' years have already flowed through this "today" of Yours, taking from it their measure and the shape of whatever being they had; and still others will flow through it, and take from it the shape of their own degree of being. But You are still the same, and everything belonging to tomorrow and beyond it, and everything belonging to yesterday and before it, You will do today, You have done today. What does it matter to me if someone cannot understand this? Let him rejoice too, and say, What is this? Let him rejoice even so, and be glad to find You without working it out, rather than work it out and fail to find You.

@@ 10
Hear me, O God. Alas for the sins of men! A man says this, and You have pity on him, because You made him, though You did not make the sin that is in him. Who reminds me of the sins of my infancy? For in Your sight no one is clean of sin, not even a baby who has lived only a single day on earth. Who reminds me? Does not any tiny child, in whom I see what I cannot remember of myself? What, then, was my sin back then? Was it that I cried with my mouth wide open for the breast? If I did the same now, craving food suited to my age, I would rightly be laughed at and scolded. So what I did then deserved a scolding; but since I could not understand a scolding, custom and reason both ruled it out. As we grow up we root these habits out and throw them away; and I have never seen anyone, even when pruning, knowingly throw away what is good. Or was it good, even for a time, to cry for things that would have done harm if they had been given? to be bitterly resentful that free people, and its elders, even the very people who brought it into the world, did not wait on it? that many others, wiser than itself, did not jump at its every nod? to do its best to hit and hurt them, because they did not obey orders that would have hurt it if they had been obeyed? So the innocence of infants lies in the weakness of their limbs, not in their will. I have seen and known for myself a jealous baby. It could not yet talk, but it went pale and stared bitterly at its foster-brother. Who does not know this? Mothers and nurses claim they can calm such things by some remedy or other. Unless this too is innocence: when the fountain of milk is flowing richly and abundantly, to refuse to let another share it, though that other is in desperate need and still depends on that one food for its very life. We put up with all this gently, not because it is no evil or only a slight one, but because it will disappear as the years go by; for though we tolerate them now, the very same tempers are completely intolerable when we meet them in someone older.

@@ 11
You, then, O Lord my God, gave life to me as an infant, and gave me a body which, as we see, You equipped with senses, fitted together with limbs, and shaped with beauty, and into which, for its well-being and safety as a whole, You planted all the drives of a living thing. And You command me to praise You for these things, to confess to You, and to sing to Your name, O Most High. For You are God, almighty and good, even if You had made nothing but this, which no one but You could make: You, whose Unity is the pattern of all things; who make all things beautiful out of Your own beauty; and who order all things by Your law. As for this stage of my life, then, Lord, which I do not remember, which I know only on others' word, and which I suppose I lived through by watching other infants (however sound that guess may be), I am reluctant to count it as part of the life I am living in this world. It lies hidden from me in the darkness of forgetfulness just as much as the time I spent in my mother's womb. But if I was shaped in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me, where, I beg You, my God, where, Lord, or when was I, Your servant, ever innocent? But look, I pass over that period now; what have I to do with a time of which I can recall no trace?

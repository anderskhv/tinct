You are great, O Lord, and greatly to be praised: great is Your power, and Your wisdom has no measure. And man wants to praise You. Man, only a small part of what You have created; man, who carries his mortality around with him, the evidence of his sin and the evidence that You resist the proud: even so, man wants to praise You, this small part of Your creation. You rouse us so that praising You becomes our delight, because You made us for Yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in You. Lord, let me know and understand which should come first, calling on You or praising You? And again, which comes first, knowing You or calling on You? Who can call on You without knowing You? Someone who does not know You might call on You as something other than what You are. Or is it rather that we call on You in order to come to know You? Yet how shall they call on Him in whom they have not believed? Or how shall they believe without a preacher? And they that seek the Lord shall praise Him: for those who seek will find Him, and those who find Him will praise Him. Let me seek You, Lord, by calling on You, and let me call on You as one who believes in You, since You have been preached to us. My faith calls on You, Lord: the faith You gave me, the faith You breathed into me through the Incarnation of Your Son and through the ministry of the Preacher.

And how am I to call on my God, my God and Lord, when calling on Him means calling Him into myself? What room is there in me into which my God could come? Where could God come into me, the God who made heaven and earth? Is there really anything in me, O Lord my God, that can hold You? Do heaven and earth hold You, then, the heaven and earth You made, and in which You made me? Or, because nothing that exists could exist without You, does it follow that everything that exists holds You? In that case, since I exist too, why do I ask You to come into me, when I would not exist at all unless You were in me already? Why? Because I have not gone down into hell, and yet You are there as well: for if I go down into hell, You are there. So I could not exist, my God, could not exist at all, unless You were in me; or rather, unless I were in You, of whom are all things, by whom are all things, in whom are all things. Even so, Lord, even so. Where, then, am I calling You to, when I am already in You? Or from what place could You come into me? Where could I go beyond heaven and earth, so that my God might come into me from there, He who has said, I fill heaven and earth?

@@ 2
Do heaven and earth hold You, then, since You fill them? Or do You fill them and still overflow, because they cannot hold You? And once heaven and earth are full, where do You pour out whatever is left of Yourself? Or do You need nothing at all to hold You, since You hold all things, and whatever You fill, You fill by holding it? The vessels that are full of You do not hold You up; even if they were shattered, You would not spill out. And when You are poured out upon us, You do not fall to the ground; instead You raise us up. You are not scattered; instead You gather us together. But when You fill all things, do You fill them with the whole of Yourself? Or, since all things together cannot take in the whole of You, does each take in a part of You? And do they all take in the same part at once? Or does each thing have its own part, the larger things more and the smaller things less? Is one part of You, then, greater and another smaller? Or are You entirely present everywhere, while nothing takes in the whole of You?

@@ 3
What are You, then, my God? What, I ask, but the Lord God? For who is Lord except the Lord? Or who is God except our God? Highest, best, mightiest, most all-mighty; most merciful and yet most just; most hidden and yet most present; most beautiful and yet most strong; steadfast, yet beyond our grasp; unchangeable, yet changing all things; never new and never old; making all things new, and bringing the proud into old age without their knowing it; always at work, always at rest; always gathering, yet lacking nothing; bearing up, filling and sheltering all things; creating, nourishing and bringing to maturity; seeking, though You have everything. You love, but without the heat of passion; You are jealous, but free from anxiety; You repent, but You do not grieve; You are angry, yet calm. You change Your works, but Your purpose stays the same; You take back what You find, though You never lost it. You are never in need, yet You rejoice at gain; never greedy, yet You demand interest. More than is due is paid to You, so that You become the debtor; yet who has anything that is not Yours? You pay debts, though You owe nothing; You cancel debts, and lose nothing by it. And what have I just said, my God, my life, my holy joy? Or what does anyone say when he speaks about You? Yet woe to those who say nothing, since even the most eloquent are struck dumb.

@@ 4
If only I could rest in You! If only You would come into my heart and make it drunk, so that I might forget my troubles and embrace You, my one and only good! What are You to me? Have pity on me, and teach me to put it into words. Or what am I to You, that You require me to love You, and if I do not, You are angry with me and threaten me with heavy sorrows? Is it such a small sorrow, then, not to love You? Oh, for Your mercies' sake, tell me, O Lord my God, what You are to me. Say to my soul, I am your salvation. Say it so that I can hear. Look, Lord, my heart is before You; open its ears, and say to my soul, I am your salvation. Let me run after that voice and take hold of You. Do not hide Your face from me. Let me die, so that I may not die; only let me see Your face.

@@ 5
The house of my soul is narrow; make it wider, so that You can come in. It is falling into ruin; rebuild it. There are things inside it that must offend Your eyes; I admit it, and I know it. But who will make it clean? Or to whom else should I cry out, if not to You? Lord, cleanse me from my secret faults, and spare Your servant from the power of the enemy. I believe, and that is why I speak. Lord, You know. Have I not confessed my offenses to You, speaking against myself, and have You not, my God, forgiven the wickedness of my heart? I do not argue my case against You, for You are the truth; I am afraid of deceiving myself, in case my iniquity should lie to itself. So I do not argue my case against You; for if You, Lord, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could endure it?

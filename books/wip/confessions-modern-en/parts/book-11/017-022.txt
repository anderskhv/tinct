And yet we speak of "a long time" and "a short time," though only of the past or the future. For example, we call a hundred years ago a long time past, and a hundred years from now a long time to come. A short time past, let us say, is ten days ago, and a short time to come ten days from now. But how can something be long or short when it does not exist? The past no longer exists, and the future does not exist yet. So let us not say "it is long," but say of the past "it was long," and of the future "it will be long." My Lord, my Light, will Your Truth not laugh at us humans here too? Take that past time which was long: was it long once it was already past, or while it was still present? It could be long only at the time when there was something to be long. Once past, it no longer existed, and so it could not be long, since it did not exist at all. So let us not say "the past time was long," for we will not find anything that was long, since from the moment it became past it no longer exists. Let us say instead "that present time was long," because while it was present it was long. It had not yet passed away and ceased to exist, and so there was something that could be long; but once it had passed, it stopped being long at the same moment that it stopped existing.

@@ 18
Let us see, then, human soul, whether present time can be long; for you have been given the power to perceive and to measure spans of time. What will you answer me? Is a hundred years, when present, a long time? First see whether a hundred years can be present at all. If the first of those years is under way, that year is present, but the other ninety-nine are still to come, and so they do not exist yet. If the second year is under way, one is already past, one is present, and the rest are to come. In the same way, whichever year in the middle of this hundred we take as present, all the years before it will be past and all the years after it future. So a hundred years cannot be present. But see at least whether the one year that is under way is itself present. If its first month is under way, the rest are still to come; if the second, the first has already gone and the rest do not exist yet. So not even the year now under way is present as a whole; and if it is not present as a whole, then the year is not present. For a year is twelve months, and whichever single month is under way is present, while the rest are either past or future. And yet not even the current month is present, but only one day: if it is the first day, the rest are to come; if it is the last, the rest are past; if it is one in the middle, it stands between days past and days to come.

@@ 19
Look how the present time, which we found was the only time that could be called long, has shrunk to the span of barely one day. But let us examine even that, because not even one day is present as a whole. It is made up of the twenty-four hours of night and day. The first of these has all the others still to come; the last has all of them past; and any hour in between has those before it past and those after it to come. And that single hour itself goes by in fleeting fragments: whatever part of it has flown off is past, and whatever part is left is future. If we can conceive of some instant of time that cannot be divided into even the tiniest parts of moments, that alone can be called present. Yet it flies so swiftly from future to past that it is not stretched out by even the briefest pause. For if it is stretched out, it is divided into past and future. The present has no extent. Where, then, is the time we can call long? Is it the future? We do not say of the future, "It is long," because it does not yet exist to be long; we say, "It will be long." When, then, will it be long? If, even at that point, it is still future, it will not be long, since what could be long does not exist yet. But if it will be long only when it has begun to exist, coming out of a future that does not yet exist and becoming present, so that there is something that could be long, then the present time cries out, in the words we used above, that it cannot be long.

@@ 20
And yet, Lord, we are aware of intervals of time; we compare them with one another and say that some are longer and others shorter. We even measure how much longer or shorter one time is than another, and we answer that this one is double or triple, and that one single, or just as long as the other. But it is passing times we measure, when we measure them by perceiving them. Who can measure past times, which no longer exist, or future ones, which do not exist yet? Unless someone will dare to claim that what does not exist can be measured. So while time is passing, it can be perceived and measured; but once it has passed, it cannot, because it no longer exists.

@@ 21
I am asking, Father, not asserting. O my God, govern and guide me. "Who will tell me that there are not three times, past, present and future, as we learned when we were boys and taught to boys ourselves, but only the present, because the other two do not exist? Or do they exist as well? When the future becomes present, does it come out of some hidden place, and when the present becomes past, does it withdraw into one? For where did those who foretold things to come see them, if those things do not exist yet? What does not exist cannot be seen. And those who tell of things past could not tell of them truly unless they discerned them in their mind; and if those things did not exist at all, they could not be discerned in any way. So things past and things to come do exist."

@@ 22
Let me search further, Lord, O my hope; do not let my purpose be thrown into confusion. For if past and future times exist, I want to know where they are. Even if I cannot know that yet, I do know that wherever they are, they are not there as future or past, but as present. For if they are future there too, they are not there yet; and if they are past there too, they are no longer there. So wherever they are, and whatever they are, they exist only as present. And yet when past events are recounted truly, what is brought out of the memory is not the things themselves, which have passed, but words formed from their images, which those things, as they passed through the senses, left imprinted in the mind like footprints. My childhood, for instance, which no longer exists, lies in past time, which no longer exists; but when I call up its image and tell of it, I look at that image in the present, because it is still in my memory. Whether there is a similar explanation for foretelling the future, so that images of things that do not yet exist are perceived in advance as already existing, I confess, my God, I do not know. This much I do know: we often think ahead about our future actions, and that thinking ahead is present, while the action we are thinking ahead about does not exist yet, because it is future. Once we have set about it and begun to do what we were thinking ahead about, then that action will exist, because then it will be not future but present.

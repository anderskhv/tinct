Lord, eternity is Yours: are You then unaware of what I am telling You? Or do You see in time what takes place in time? Why, then, am I setting out so many stories in order before You? Certainly not so that You can learn them from me. I do it to stir up my own devotion toward You, and the devotion of those who read this, so that all of us may say, Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised. I have said it before and I will say it again: I do this out of love for Your love. After all, we pray, even though Truth has said, Your Father knows what you need before you ask. So what we lay open before You is our own heart, as we confess to You our miseries and Your mercies toward us. We do it so that You may set us entirely free, since You have already begun, and so that we may stop being wretched in ourselves and become blessed in You. For You have called us to be poor in spirit, and meek, and mourners, and hungry and thirsty for righteousness, and merciful, and pure in heart, and peacemakers. Look: I have told You many things, as far as I was able and as far as I wished, because You first willed that I should confess to You, my Lord God. For You are good, and Your mercy endures forever.

But how could I ever be equal to setting down, with the tongue of my pen, all Your urgings and all Your terrors, Your comforts and Your guidance, by which You led me to preach Your Word and to dispense Your Sacrament to Your people? And even if I were equal to telling them in order, the drops of time are costly to me. For a long while now I have been burning to meditate on Your law, and in it to confess to You both what I know and what I do not know, the first dawn of Your light and the remnants of my darkness, until my weakness is swallowed up by strength. I do not want the hours I find free to drain away into anything else: the hours left over from the necessities of refreshing my body and the powers of my mind, and from the service we owe to other people, or the service we do not owe and give all the same.

@@ 2
O Lord my God, attend to my prayer, and let Your mercy listen to my longing. It does not burn for my sake alone; it wants to be of use to brotherly love, and You see in my heart that this is so. Let me offer You in sacrifice the service of my thought and my tongue, and give me what I am to offer You. For I am poor and needy, while You are rich toward all who call on You; You are free of all care, and yet You take care of us. Circumcise my lips, inside and out, from all rashness and all lying. Let Your Scriptures be my pure delight; let me not be deceived in them, nor deceive anyone out of them. Lord, listen and have pity, O Lord my God, Light of the blind and Strength of the weak, and Light too of those who see and Strength of the strong: listen to my soul, and hear it as it cries out of the depths. For if Your ears are not with us in the depths as well, where are we to go? Where are we to cry? The day is Yours and the night is Yours; the moments fly past at Your nod. Grant us out of that time some room for our meditations on the hidden things of Your law, and do not shut it against us when we knock. You did not have the dark secrets of so many pages written for nothing; those forests are not without their deer, which retreat into them and roam and walk about, graze, lie down and chew the cud. Bring me to completion, O Lord, and open those pages to me. See, Your voice is my joy; Your voice is beyond any abundance of pleasures. Give me what I love, for I do love, and this too is Your gift. Do not abandon Your own gifts, and do not scorn Your grass when it is thirsty. Let me confess to You whatever I find in Your books, and hear the voice of praise, and drink You in, and consider the wonderful things of Your law, from the very beginning, in which You made heaven and earth, to the everlasting reign of Your holy city with You.

@@ 3
Lord, have mercy on me and hear my longing. For I do not think it is a longing for anything of this earth: not for gold and silver and precious stones, or fine clothes, or honors and positions of power, or the pleasures of the flesh, or the things the body needs for this life of our pilgrimage. All these are added to those who seek Your kingdom and Your righteousness. Look, O Lord my God, at where my longing comes from. The wicked have told me of delights, but none like Your law, O Lord. Look, that is where my longing comes from. Look, Father; look and see and approve. May it please You, as You look on me with mercy, that I find grace before You, so that the inner depths of Your words may be opened to me as I knock. I beg You by our Lord Jesus Christ, Your Son, the Man of Your right hand, the Son of Man, whom You have established for Yourself as Your Mediator and ours. Through Him You sought us when we were not seeking You, and You sought us so that we would seek You. He is Your Word, through whom You made all things, and me among them; He is Your Only-Begotten, through whom You called the believing people to adoption, and me among them. I beg You by Him, who sits at Your right hand and intercedes with You for us, in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge lie hidden. It is these treasures I look for in Your books. Moses wrote of Him; He Himself says so; the Truth says so.

@@ 4
I want to hear and understand how "In the beginning You made heaven and earth." Moses wrote this. He wrote it and went away; he passed from here, from You to You, and he is not in front of me now. If he were, I would take hold of him and question him, and implore him by You to explain these things to me, and I would open the ears of my body to the sounds breaking from his mouth. If he spoke Hebrew, the sounds would beat on my senses in vain, and none of it would reach my mind; but if he spoke Latin, I would know what he was saying. Yet how would I know whether what he said was true? And even if I knew that too, would I know it from him? No: within me, deep within, in the dwelling place of my thought, Truth, which is neither Hebrew nor Greek nor Latin nor barbarian, and needs no organs of mouth or tongue and no sound of syllables, would say, "He is telling the truth." And at once, with full confidence, I would say to that man of Yours, "You are telling the truth." Since, then, I cannot question him, I ask You, Truth, who filled him when he spoke true things; I ask You, my God: forgive my sins. You granted Your servant to say these things; grant me too to understand them.

@@ 5
Look: heaven and earth exist. They cry out that they were made, for they change and vary. Whatever exists without having been made has nothing in it that was not there before, and that is exactly what it means to change and vary. They cry out, too, that they did not make themselves: "We exist because we were made. So we did not exist before we existed, in order to make ourselves." And the voice in which they say this is the evidence itself. So You, Lord, made them. You are beautiful, for they are beautiful; You are good, for they are good; You are, for they are. Yet they are not beautiful or good, nor do they exist, in the way that You their Creator are; compared with You they are neither beautiful nor good, nor do they exist at all. This we know, thanks be to You; and our knowledge, compared with Your knowledge, is ignorance.

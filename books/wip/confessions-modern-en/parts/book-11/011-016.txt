Look: are they not still full of their old leaven, the people who say to us, "What was God doing before He made heaven and earth? If He was at leisure," they say, "and doing nothing, why does He not stay that way from now on and forever, just as He always did in the past? For if some new motion arose in God, a new will to make a creature He had never made before, how could that be true eternity, when a will arises that was not there before? The will of God is not a creature; it comes before the creature, since nothing could be created unless the Creator's will came first. So God's will belongs to His very Substance. And if anything has arisen in God's Substance that was not there before, that Substance cannot truly be called eternal. But if it was God's will from eternity that the creature should exist, why was the creature not also from eternity?"

@@ 12
People who talk this way do not yet understand You, O Wisdom of God, Light of souls; they do not yet understand how the things are made that are made through You and in You. They try to grasp eternal things, but their heart still flutters among the movements of things past and things to come, and is still unsteady. Who will hold that heart and pin it down, so that it stands still for a little while, and for a little while catches the splendor of that eternity which is always still, and compares it with times that are never still, and sees that there is no comparison? Who will make it see that a long time becomes long only out of many movements passing by, which cannot all be stretched out at once; that in the eternal nothing passes, but the whole is present, whereas no time is ever present all at once; that all past time is driven on by the future, and all the future follows on from the past, and all past and future is created by, and flows out from, that which is always present? Who will hold the human heart still, so that it stands and sees how eternity, itself standing still, neither past nor future, utters the times past and to come? Is my hand strong enough for this? Or can the hand of my mouth, by speaking, accomplish so great a thing?

@@ 13
Here is my answer to anyone who asks, "What was God doing before He made heaven and earth?" I do not give the answer someone is said to have given as a joke, to dodge the force of the question: "He was preparing hell," he said, "for people who pry into mysteries." Answering questions is one thing; making fun of the people who ask them is another. So that is not my answer. I would much rather answer "I do not know" about what I do not know than give an answer that makes a laughingstock of someone who asks about deep things and wins praise for someone who answers with falsehoods. What I say is that You, our God, are the Creator of every creature; and if the name "heaven and earth" is taken to mean every creature, I say boldly, "Before God made heaven and earth, He did not make anything." For if He was making something, what could He have been making but a creature? I only wish I knew everything I long to know for my good as surely as I know that no creature was made before any creature was made.

@@ 14
But suppose someone's restless mind roams among images of bygone times, and marvels that You, God Almighty, Creator of all and Sustainer of all, Maker of heaven and earth, held back from so great a work for countless ages before You chose to undertake it. Let him wake up and realize that what he is marveling at is false. How could countless ages pass by that You had not made, when You are the Author and Creator of all ages? What times could there have been that were not made by You? And how could they pass by if they never existed? Since, then, You are the Maker of all times, if there was any time before You made heaven and earth, why do people say that You were refraining from work? You had made that very time, and no times could pass by before You made times. But if there was no time before heaven and earth, why do they ask what You were doing then? There was no "then" when there was no time.

@@ 15
Nor do You go before times by means of time; otherwise You would not go before all times. You go before all past times by the height of an eternity that is always present, and You stand above all future times, because they are future, and once they have come they will be past; but You are the Same, and Your years do not fail. Your years neither come nor go, whereas ours come and go, so that all of them may come. Your years stand all together, because they stand still; the years that go are not pushed out by the years that come, because Your years do not pass away. But ours will all exist only when none of them exists any longer. Your years are one day, and Your day is not one day after another, but Today, because Your Today does not give way to tomorrow, just as it does not take the place of yesterday. Your Today is Eternity. That is why You begot the Coeternal, to whom You said, "Today I have begotten You." You made all times, and before all times You are; nor was there ever a time when there was no time.

@@ 16
So there was no time at which You had not made anything, because You had made time itself. And no times are coeternal with You, because You remain, whereas if times remained they would not be times. For what is time? Who can explain it easily and briefly? Who can even grasp it in thought clearly enough to put it into words? And yet what do we mention in conversation more familiarly and knowingly than time? We certainly understand it when we speak of it, and we understand it when we hear someone else speak of it. What, then, is time? If no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it to someone who asks, I do not know. Yet I say with confidence that I know this much: if nothing passed away, there would be no past time; if nothing were coming, there would be no future time; and if nothing existed, there would be no present time. Those two times, then, the past and the future: how do they exist, when the past no longer exists and the future does not exist yet? As for the present, if it were always present and never passed into the past, it would no longer be time at all, but eternity. So if the present, in order to be time, comes into being only by passing into the past, how can we say that it exists, when the very reason it exists is that it is about to stop existing? In other words, we cannot truly say that time exists, except in the sense that it is tending toward not existing.

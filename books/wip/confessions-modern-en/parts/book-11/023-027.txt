So whatever this mysterious foreseeing of future things may be, nothing can be seen except what exists. And what exists already is not future but present. So when future things are said to be seen, what is seen is not the things themselves, which do not exist yet (that is, the things that are going to be), but perhaps their causes or signs, which already exist. These, then, are not future but present to those who see them now, and from them the future is conceived in the mind and foretold. These conceptions in turn already exist, and those who foretell the future look at them as present within themselves. Let the vast variety of things supply me with an example. I watch the dawn, and I announce that the sun is about to rise. What I am watching is present; what I announce is future. It is not the sun that is future, since the sun already exists, but its rising, which does not exist yet. And yet unless I pictured the sunrise itself in my mind, as I am doing now while I speak of it, I could not foretell it. But neither is the dawn I see in the sky the sunrise, even though it goes before it, nor is the picture in my mind. These two are seen as present, so that the sunrise, which is still to come, can be foretold. So future things do not exist yet; and if they do not exist yet, they do not exist; and if they do not exist, they cannot be seen at all. But they can be foretold from present things, which already exist and are seen.

You, then, Ruler of Your creation, how do You teach souls the things that are to come? For You did teach Your Prophets. How do You, for whom nothing is future, teach future things? Or rather, do You teach present things about the future? For what does not exist certainly cannot be taught. This way lies far beyond my sight; it is too strong for me, and I cannot reach it by myself. But I can reach it from You, when You grant it, O sweet light of my hidden eyes.

What is now clear and plain is that neither future nor past things exist. Nor is it correct to say, "There are three times: past, present and future." Perhaps it would be correct to say, "There are three times: a present of past things, a present of present things, and a present of future things." For these three do exist in some way in the soul, and I do not see them anywhere else: the present of past things is memory; the present of present things is sight; the present of future things is expectation. If we are allowed to speak this way, I see three times, and I admit that there are three. Let people go on saying, too, "There are three times: past, present and future," in the loose way we usually talk. See, I do not mind, I do not object, I do not find fault, as long as what is said is understood: that neither what is to be, nor what is past, exists now. For there are few things we say correctly and many we say incorrectly; but what we mean is understood.

I said a moment ago, then, that we measure times as they pass, so that we can say that this stretch of time is double that one, or that this one is just as long as that, and so on for any other parts of time we are able to measure. So, as I was saying, we measure times as they pass. And if someone asks me, "How do you know?" I might answer, "I know that we do measure, and we cannot measure things that do not exist; and past and future things do not exist." But how do we measure present time, when it has no extent? It is measured while it is passing; once it has passed it is not measured, because there will be nothing to measure. But while it is being measured, where does it come from, by what way does it pass, and where does it go? Where from, if not from the future? By what way, if not through the present? Where to, if not into the past? From what does not exist yet, then, through what has no extent, into what no longer exists. Yet what do we measure, if not time in some extent? For when we speak of single, double, triple and equal, or anything else of that kind in time, we are speaking of nothing but spans of time. In what extent, then, do we measure time as it passes? In the future, from which it comes? But we do not measure what does not exist yet. Or in the present, through which it passes? But we do not measure what has no extent. Or in the past, into which it passes? But we do not measure what no longer exists either.

My mind is on fire to understand this most tangled riddle. Do not shut it away, O Lord my God, good Father; I beg You through Christ, do not shut these things, so familiar and yet so hidden, away from my longing, so that it cannot get inside them; let them grow clear, Lord, as the light of Your mercy dawns on them. Whom shall I ask about these things? And to whom could I more fruitfully confess my ignorance than to You, who are not wearied by my studies, burning so fiercely toward Your Scriptures? Give me what I love; for I do love, and this You have given me. Give, Father, You who truly know how to give good gifts to Your children. Give, because I have set myself to understand, and the labor is before me until You open it. I beg You by Christ, in His name, the Holy of holies, let no one disturb me. I believed, and therefore I speak. This is my hope; for this I live: that I may contemplate the delights of the Lord. See, You have made my days old, and they pass away, and how, I do not know. And we talk about time and time, times and times: "How long did he take to say this?" "How long did he take to do this?" "How long it is since I saw that!" and "This syllable takes double the time of that short single syllable." We say these things and we hear them; we are understood and we understand. They are perfectly plain and utterly ordinary, and yet these same things are also deeply hidden, and to discover them would be something new.

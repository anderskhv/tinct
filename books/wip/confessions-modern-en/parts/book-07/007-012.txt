By this time I had also thrown out the lying predictions and godless ravings of the astrologers. Let Your own mercies confess to You for this too, O my God, from the innermost depths of my soul. For it was You, You entirely — for who else calls us back from the death of every error but the Life that does not know how to die, and the Wisdom that needs no light yet enlightens the minds that need it, the Wisdom by which the universe is governed, down to the fluttering leaves of the trees? — it was You who took care of my stubbornness. I had been arguing against Vindicianus, a shrewd old man, and against Nebridius, a young man with a remarkable mind. The first insisted forcefully, and the second said often, though with a certain hesitation, "that there was no such art of foreseeing the future; that people's guesses often worked like a lottery, and that out of the many things they said would happen, some did happen — without the speakers knowing it, since they simply stumbled on it by saying so much." So You provided me with a friend who was no idle consulter of astrologers, though not deeply versed in their writings either, but, as I said, a curious consulter of them; and yet he knew something that he said he had heard from his father, without realizing how much it could do to overturn belief in that art. This man, Firminus by name, who had a liberal education and was well trained in rhetoric, came to consult me as a very dear friend about certain affairs of his in which his worldly hopes ran high, asking what I thought of them according to his so-called constellations. I had already begun to lean toward Nebridius' opinion on the subject. I did not flatly refuse to make a guess and tell him what occurred to my wavering mind, but I added that I was by now almost convinced these things were ridiculous and empty. At that he told me that his father had been extremely curious about books of this kind and had had a friend just as devoted to them as he was. With shared enthusiasm and comparing of notes they fanned the fire of their hearts' passion for these trifles, so much so that whenever the dumb animals around their houses gave birth, they would note the moment of birth and record the position of the heavens at that time, in order to gather evidence for this so-called art. He said, then, that he had heard from his father that when his mother was pregnant with him, Firminus, a slave woman belonging to that friend of his father's was also expecting. This could not escape her master, who took the most painstaking care to know when even his dogs had their puppies. And so it came about that while the one was counting the days, hours and even the smaller fractions of hours for his wife, and the other for his slave woman, with the most careful observation, the two women gave birth at the same moment. The two men were therefore obliged to cast the very same constellations, down to the smallest detail, the one for his son and the other for his newborn slave. For as soon as the women went into labor, each had informed the other of what was happening in his own house, and each had messengers ready to send to the other the instant the child was born; and they had easily arranged for the news to be carried at once, since each was master in his own domain. And the messengers sent from each side, he said, met each other at a point exactly halfway between the two houses, so that neither man could record any different position of the stars or any difference in the smallest fractions of time. And yet Firminus, born to a high position in his family, ran along the brighter roads of the world, grew richer and rose to honors, while that slave went on serving his masters with the yoke of his condition not loosened in the least — as Firminus himself, who knew him, told me.

@@ 8
When I heard this and believed it — for the man who told it was someone I could trust — all my resistance gave way and collapsed. First I tried to draw Firminus himself away from that curiosity. I told him that if I had examined his constellations in order to foretell the truth, I ought to have seen in them parents who were leading figures among their neighbors, a family of rank in its own city, free birth, a respectable upbringing and a liberal education. But if that slave had consulted me about the same constellations, since they were his as well, then in order to tell him the truth too I ought to have seen in them a family of the lowest sort, the condition of a slave, and everything else utterly different from and far removed from the first case. So if I looked at the same data and told the truth, I would have to say different things; and if I said the same things, I would be saying something false. From this I concluded with complete certainty that whatever true things are said after consulting the constellations are said not by skill but by luck, and whatever false things are said come not from ignorance of the art but from luck turning out false.

@@ 9
Now that a way in had been opened, I went on turning over such things by myself. I did not want any of those crackpots who made their living by this trade — whom I was itching to attack, refute and hold up to ridicule — to be able to counter me by claiming that Firminus had told me a false story, or his father had told him one. So I turned my attention to twins. Most twins come out of the womb one after the other so closely that the tiny interval between them, however much force people may claim it has in the natural order, cannot be caught by human observation or recorded in any way in the figures the astrologer has to examine in order to make a true prediction. And his predictions will not be true, since looking at the same figures he would have had to say the same things about Esau and Jacob, yet the same things did not happen to both of them. So he would be speaking falsely; or, if he spoke truly, he would not be saying the same things, although he would be looking at the same figures. It is not by skill, then, but by luck that he would speak the truth. For You, Lord, the most just ruler of the universe, act by a hidden prompting, without either the consulters or the consulted knowing it, so that each person who consults hears what he ought to hear, according to the hidden merits of souls, out of the depths of Your just judgment. Let no man say to You, What is this? Why that? Let him not say it, let him not say it; for he is man.

So by now, my Helper, You had set me free from those chains, and I was asking "where evil comes from," and there was no way out. But You did not let any waves of thought sweep me away from the faith by which I believed that You exist, that Your substance is unchangeable, that You care for men and will judge them, and that in Christ, Your Son, our Lord, and in the holy Scriptures, which the authority of Your Catholic Church commended to me, You had laid down the way of man's salvation to the life that is to come after this death. With these points secure and unshakably established in my mind, I searched feverishly for "where evil comes from." What torments my heart suffered in its labor pains, what groans, my God! And even there Your ears were listening, though I did not know it. And when I searched hard in silence, the silent agonies of my soul were loud cries to Your mercy. You knew what I was going through, and no human being did. For how much of it did I actually put into words for the ears of my closest friends? Did the whole uproar of my soul reach them, when neither time nor speech was enough for it? Yet all of it reached Your hearing, all that I roared out from the groaning of my heart; my desire was before You, and the light of my eyes was not with me. For that light was within, while I was outside; and it is not in any place, but I was fixed on things contained in places, and there I found no place to rest. Those things did not take me in so that I could say, "That is enough, that is good," and yet they did not let me go back to where it would be good enough for me. For I was higher than they were, but lower than You; and You were my true joy as long as I was subject to You, and You had made subject to me the things You created beneath me. This was the right balance, the middle ground where my well-being lay: to stay in Your image, and by serving You to be master of the body. But when I rose up proudly against You and ran against the Lord with my neck, with the thick boss of my shield, even these lowest things were placed above me and weighed me down, and there was nowhere to find relief or room to breathe. When I looked, they crowded in on me from every side in heaps and masses; and when I tried to think, the images of bodies themselves stood in my way as I tried to turn back, as if they were saying, "Where do you think you are going, unworthy and filthy as you are?" And all this had grown out of my wound, for You "humbled the proud like one that is wounded"; by my own swelling I was cut off from You, and my face, puffed up with pride, shut my eyes.

@@ 11
But You, Lord, remain forever, yet You are not angry with us forever, because You have taken pity on our dust and ashes. It was pleasing in Your sight to reshape what was misshapen in me. With goads inside me You kept stirring me up, so that I could find no peace until You became certain to me by my inward sight. And so, by the hidden hand of Your healing, my swelling went down, and the troubled and darkened eyesight of my mind was healed from day to day by the stinging salve of wholesome pains.

@@ 12
And first You wanted to show me how You resist the proud, but give grace to the humble, and how great was the mercy with which You had shown men the way of humility, in that Your Word was made flesh, and dwelt among men. So, by means of a certain man swollen with the most monstrous conceit, You brought into my hands some books of the Platonists, translated from Greek into Latin. And there I read — not in these very words, but saying exactly the same thing, argued with many different kinds of reasons — that In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: the same was in the beginning with God: all things were made by Him, and without Him was nothing made: that which was made in Him is life, and the life was the light of men, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it. And I read that the soul of man, though it bears witness to the light, is not itself that light; but the Word of God, being God, is that true light which enlightens every man who comes into the world. And that He was in the world, and the world was made by Him, and the world did not know Him. But that He came to His own, and His own did not receive Him; but as many as received Him, to them He gave power to become the sons of God, as many as believed in His name — this I did not read there.

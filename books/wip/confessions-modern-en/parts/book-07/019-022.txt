There is no health in people who are displeased by anything in Your creation, just as there was none in me when many things You made displeased me. And because my soul did not dare to be displeased with my God, it preferred not to count as Yours whatever displeased it. That is how it had drifted into the opinion of two substances; and it found no rest, and talked wildly. And coming back from there, it had made itself a god that stretched through the infinite reaches of all space, and took this to be You, and set it up in its heart; and so once again it had become the temple of its own idol, a temple abominable to You. But after You had cradled my head, without my knowing it, and closed my eyes so that they would not look on vanity, I let go of myself a little, and my madness was lulled to sleep. And I woke up in You, and saw You as infinite, but in another way; and this seeing did not come from the flesh.

Then I looked back at other things, and I saw that they owe their existence to You, and that all things are bounded in You — but differently, not as if in a place, but because You hold all things in Your hand by Your Truth. All things are true insofar as they exist, and falsehood is nothing at all except when something is thought to exist that does not. And I saw that each thing fits in not only with its own place but also with its own time; and that You, who alone are eternal, did not begin to work after countless stretches of time had gone by. For no stretches of time, neither those that have passed nor those that will pass, could go or come unless You were working and abiding.

I learned by experience that it is no wonder bread which tastes pleasant to a healthy palate is torture to a sick one, and that light which is lovely to clear eyes is hateful to sore ones. Your justice displeases the wicked; much more, then, do the viper and the worm, which You created good, suited to the lower parts of Your creation. The wicked themselves are suited to those lower parts, all the more as they are unlike You; but they are suited to the higher parts as they become more like You. And I asked what wickedness was, and I found that it is not a substance, but the perversity of a will twisted away from the supreme substance, from You, O God, toward the lowest things — a will that throws away its own inmost life and swells outward.

And I was amazed that I now loved You, and not some phantom in place of You. Yet I did not stand firm in the enjoyment of my God. I was swept up to You by Your beauty, and soon torn away from You by my own weight, and I crashed back down, groaning, into these lower things. That weight was the habit of the flesh. But the memory of You stayed with me, and I had no doubt at all that there was One I should cling to, only that I was not yet able to cling to Him; for the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that thinks about many things. And I was completely certain that Your invisible things, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even Your eternal power and Godhead. For I was asking where my approval of the beauty of bodies, whether in the heavens or on earth, came from, and what I had at hand that allowed me to judge soundly about changeable things and to say, "This ought to be so, and that not" — asking, I say, where my judgment came from when I judged in this way — and I had found the unchangeable and true eternity of Truth above my changeable mind. So step by step I went up from bodies to the soul that perceives through the body; and from there to its inner power, to which the bodily senses report what is outside, which is as far as animals can go; and from there again to the reasoning power, to which whatever is taken in from the bodily senses is referred for judgment. This power, too, finding itself changeable in me, raised itself up to its own understanding. It drew my thinking away from habit and pulled itself out of the crowds of contradictory phantoms, in order to discover what light was shed on it when it cried out without any doubt that the unchangeable is to be preferred to the changeable — and how it knew the Unchangeable itself; for unless it had somehow known it, it could never have ranked it with certainty above the changeable. And so, in the flash of one trembling glance, it arrived at THAT WHICH IS. Then I saw Your invisible things, understood by the things that are made. But I could not hold my gaze there; my weakness was beaten back and I fell again into my usual ways, carrying nothing with me but a loving memory, and a longing for something I had, as it were, caught the fragrance of but could not yet eat.

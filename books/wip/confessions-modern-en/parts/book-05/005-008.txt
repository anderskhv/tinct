Still, I kept hold of many true things these men had said about the creature itself, and I could see the reasoning behind them in their calculations, in the orderly succession of the seasons and in the visible evidence of the stars. I compared them with the sayings of Manichaeus, who in his raving had written at enormous length on these subjects. But I found in him no account of the solstices or the equinoxes or the eclipses of the great lights, nor of anything else of this kind that I had learned in the books of worldly philosophy. There, though, I was ordered to believe; and what I was told did not agree with what had been established by calculation and by my own eyes. It was quite the opposite.

@@ 6
O Lord God of truth, does anyone who knows these things please You simply because of that? Surely the person who knows all these things and does not know You is unhappy, while the person who knows You is happy, even if he does not know these things. And the one who knows both You and them is not happier because of them, but happy because of You alone, provided that, knowing You, he glorifies You as God and gives thanks, and does not become futile in his thinking. Think of a man who knows how to own a tree and thanks You for the use of it, even though he does not know how many cubits tall it is or how wide it spreads. He is better off than someone who can measure it and count all its branches, but neither owns it nor knows or loves its Creator. In the same way, a believer to whom this whole world of riches belongs, and who, as having nothing, yet possesses all things by clinging to You whom all things serve, is certainly in a better state, even if he does not know so much as the circuits of the Great Bear. It would be foolish to doubt that he is better off than one who can measure the heavens, count the stars and weigh the elements, yet neglects You, who have ordered all things in number, weight and measure.

@@ 7
But then, who asked this Manichaeus to write about these things too, when skill in them was no part of piety? For You have said to man, "Behold, piety is wisdom." He might have been ignorant of that even if he had a perfect knowledge of these things; but since he did not know these things and yet had the utter impudence to teach them, he plainly could not have known piety. It is vanity to make a show of knowing these worldly things, even when one does know them; but to confess to You is piety. So this wanderer talked a great deal about these things for this very purpose: that when he was refuted by people who had truly learned them, it would become obvious what kind of understanding he had in other, more hidden matters. For he did not want to be thought little of; he tried to persuade people "that the Holy Spirit, the Comforter and Enricher of Your faithful, was personally present in him with full authority." So when he was caught teaching falsehoods about the sky and the stars and the movements of the sun and moon, even though these things have nothing to do with the teaching of religion, his sacrilegious presumption became plain enough. He was putting forward things he not only did not know but that were actually false, and doing it with such insane, prideful vanity that he tried to attribute them to himself as to a divine person.

@@ 8
When I hear some Christian brother who is ignorant of these things and mistaken about them, I can patiently watch such a man holding his opinion. I do not see how any ignorance about the position or nature of the bodily creation can harm him, as long as he does not believe anything unworthy of You, O Lord, Creator of all. But it does harm him if he imagines that it belongs to the very shape of the teaching of piety, and still insists too stubbornly on something he does not know. Yet even that kind of weakness, in the cradle of faith, is borne by our mother Charity, until the newborn grows up into a perfect man and can no longer be carried about by every wind of doctrine. But what about a man who dared to set himself up as teacher, source, guide and leader of all whom he could persuade, so that his followers thought they were following not an ordinary human being but Your Holy Spirit? Once he was proved to have taught anything false, who would not judge that such madness should be detested and utterly thrown out? But I had not yet clearly worked out whether the changes of longer and shorter days and nights, and of day and night itself, along with the eclipses of the great lights and whatever else of the sort I had read in other books, could be explained in a way consistent with his sayings. If somehow they could, it would still remain an open question for me whether it was so or not; but I could, because of his reputed holiness, rest my belief on his authority.

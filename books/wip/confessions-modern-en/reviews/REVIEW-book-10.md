## Known points judged

- **¶25 *horrendum* → "something awesome":** too colloquial. Today "awesome" mostly means "great", which flattens Augustine's shudder at how deep memory goes. Fix in finding 1 ("a fearful thing" is modern and not archaic; "something to shudder at" would also do).
- **¶45 "John" restored:** confirmed. The Latin has *Iohannem mirabili abstinentia praeditum … lucustis*, and without the name Pusey's served sentence has no subject. This restores a lost word; it does not add a name. Accept.
- **¶40 / ¶46 "sexual union" for *concubitus*:** accept. *Concubitus* means sexual intercourse, not keeping a concubine, and today Pusey's "concubinage" misleads. At ¶40, "You have commanded me to abstain from sexual union" is acceptable: *iussisti a concubitu* follows directly from *ut contineam*, and "continent" is kept in the sentence before it.
- **¶42 "corruptible":** accept. Pusey's "clothe this incorruptible" is a slip; the Latin has *corruptibile hoc* (1 Cor 15:53). "This corruptible body" adds "body" for clarity. That is acceptable, though "clothe this corruptible with an eternal incorruption" would stay closer to the scripture cadence.
- **Triad (¶40, ¶58, ¶65):** "the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the ambition of the world" follows Pusey and the Latin (*ambitione saeculi*), and "threefold concupiscence" (¶65, *cupiditate triplici*) keeps the term. Accept.
- **Mediator passage (¶66–67):** the terms are exact ("true Mediator", "Mediator between God and man, the Man Christ Jesus", "mortal with men, just with God", "wages of sin is death" / "wages of righteousness is life and peace", "as Man … Mediator; as the Word … not in the middle"). "Powers of the air" follows Latin *potestates aeris* over Pusey's "princes", which is acceptable. The only issue is finding 5.
- **¶37 "Late have I loved You":** faithful. All the sense images are present and in order (called / cried out / broke through deafness; flashed / shone / put blindness to flight; fragrance; taste; touch).
- **¶23 question form:** justified. The Latin has *an ex hoc intellegitur …?*
- **¶28:** follows Pusey's punctuation of *sat, est illic*. That is consistent with the edition's policy.

## Other sample paragraphs

¶0, 1, 11, 12, 24, 44, 45, 55, 65, 66 have no omissions, reversals or term problems. At ¶55, "forest" (Latin *silva*) replaces Pusey's "wilderness". That is Latin-correct, but it is not logged in NOTES under "Latin consulted".

**Convention skim:** No Thou/Thee/Thy forms and no archaic morphology in the book. Every lowercase "you" is addressed to people, creatures or the soul, never to God. No "concubinage" and no "pride of life". "Charity" appears 4 times (¶2 twice, ¶3 twice, ¶51, ¶58), against style note §8; see findings 2, 8–10. One lowercase pronoun for Christ appears at ¶46 (finding 7).

Verdict: PASS

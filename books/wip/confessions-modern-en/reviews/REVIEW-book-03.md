## Renderer's logged departures, checked

- ¶5 "feeding their own malicious mirth": **confirmed.** The Latin is *pascendo malivolas laetitias suas*. Pusey's "malicious birth" is a misprint.
- ¶7 "the only thing that held me back in all this blaze": **confirmed.** The Latin is *hoc solum me in tanta flagrantia refrangebat*. Pusey's "unkindled" contradicts the sense.
- ¶0 "sizzled and sang": **acceptable, not a real addition.** The Latin is *circumstrepebat me undique sartago flagitiosorum amorum*. The verb means a noise roaring or hissing all around, and *sartago* is a frying pan, a pun on *Carthago*. "Sizzled" puts back the frying-pan noise that Pusey's "cauldron … sang" loses. Pusey's own "in my ears" is not in the Latin either. No fix required.
- ¶9 "for whom I faint so that I may become strong" (*in quem deficio ut fortis sim*): confirmed. ¶18 "in order to teach … not to learn" (*docendi … non discendi gratia*): confirmed.

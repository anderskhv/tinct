## Renderer's Latin departures — verification

- **¶8 "Not even a rich thief will put up with one who steals out of need"**: correct against "quis enim fur aequo animo furem patitur? nec copiosus adactum inopia".
- **¶8 "being sick of doing right and glutted with wickedness"**: acceptable for "penuria et fastidio iustitiae et sagina iniquitatis", following Pusey's reading.
- **¶8 "because it was forbidden"**: correct against "quod eo liberet quo non liceret".
- **¶10**: the Catiline passage is complete. "we do not usually believe" correctly renders "credi non solet". "the writer says" for "inquit" is acceptable: it keeps the attribution without naming Sallust, who is not named in Pusey or the Latin. No change needed.
- **¶1 "O my joy so slow in coming"**: correct for "o tardum gaudium meum".
- **¶2 "brought order to my distress"**: correct for "modularetur aerumnam meam".
- **¶7**: the renderer's two-sentence version is clearer than Pusey, but it changes the mood of the first sentence. See finding 1.

## Renderer's logged departures, checked

- ¶14: **confirmed.** The Latin is *tametsi figitur in pulchris extra te et extra se. quae tamen nulla essent, nisi essent abs te*. The candidate's "even if it is pinned to beautiful things outside You and outside itself. And yet those things would be nothing at all if they were not from You" is correct, and it repairs Pusey's garbled sentence. "That is the measure set for them" (*sic est modus eorum*) is also correct, as is "From here, and this far" (*hinc et huc usque*). So is "we shall be saved" (*salvi erimus*, Ps 80).
- ¶16: **the departure itself is right, but the rendering assigns the turning to the wrong subject** (finding 1). The Latin is *ut quid perversa sequeris carnem tuam? ipsa te sequatur conversam*. *Perversa* and *conversam* both agree with the soul ("you"): the soul, turned the wrong way, follows the flesh, and the flesh should follow the soul once the soul has turned back. The candidate's "Let it turn around and follow you instead" makes the flesh ("it") the one that turns. That inverts Augustine's call to conversion.
- ¶17: **confirmed.** "Wherever truth is savored" (*ubi sapit veritas*) is correct, as is "only as it relates to Him" (*quantum est ad illum*). Pusey's doubled "Let us love Him" is kept, which is fine.

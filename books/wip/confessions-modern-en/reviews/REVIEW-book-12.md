## ¶32 judgment (Pusey "not because they have a divine Spirit" vs Latin)

The Latin is *qui non mihi hoc dicunt, quia divini sunt et in corde famuli tui viderunt quod dicunt, sed quia superbi sunt*. *divini* is a predicate adjective used of persons ("men of God", "inspired", in the prophetic sense). Pusey's "have a divine Spirit" paraphrases this. It does not reverse the meaning: Augustine denies that his opponents have inspired access to Moses' mind. But the candidate's capitalized "divine Spirit" reads as a claim about having the Holy Spirit, and the Latin does not make that claim. Under STYLE §6/§8 (follow the Latin where Pusey is loose and the Latin is plain), "they are divinely inspired" is closer and just as readable. Severity: MINOR (imprecision, not distortion). Fix is finding #1.

## Beginning harmonization

## Convention skim (whole book)

The book has no Thou/Thee/Thy forms, no -eth/-est verbs, no "Holy Ghost" and no archaic morphology ("art" in ¶38 means craft; "behold" in ¶19 and ¶38 is the ordinary verb). Every lowercase "you/your" was checked and addresses a human or the house of God (¶0, 12, 16, 17, 19, 20, 29, 32, 33, 38), which is correct, including the ¶33 capitals corrected per §8. Pronouns for God are capitalized throughout. There are two convention breaks: "charity" for *caritas* in six places (finding #3) and Genesis 1:1 capitalization in nine places (Beginning harmonization).

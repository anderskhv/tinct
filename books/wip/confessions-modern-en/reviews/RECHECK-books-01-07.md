## Book 4 ¶16 (the MAJOR fix) in detail

Latin: *ut quid perversa sequeris carnem tuam? ipsa te sequatur conversam.* The words *perversa* and *conversam* are both feminine singular and agree with the soul: *perversa* is the subject of *sequeris*, and *conversam* agrees with *te*. The flesh (*ipsa*) is only the subject of *sequatur*.

Current text: "Why, then, turned the wrong way, do you follow your flesh? Let it follow you instead, once you have turned back."

- "turned the wrong way" belongs to "you" (the soul) and translates *perversa*. Correct.
- "Let it follow you" makes the flesh the one that follows (*ipsa te sequatur*). Correct.
- "once you have turned back" gives the turning back to the soul (*conversam*, agreeing with *te*). Correct.
- The old error, where the flesh "turn[s] around", is gone completely, and no second imperative is added. The next sentence ("Whatever you perceive through it" = *per illam*) still refers to the flesh without ambiguity.

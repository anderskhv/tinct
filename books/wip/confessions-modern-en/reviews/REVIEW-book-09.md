### Specific checks requested

- **Vision at Ostia (¶23–24).** Recognizable and exact.
  - **¶23:** The whole sequence is present. The delight of the senses is judged unworthy even to be mentioned. They pass step by step through all bodily things and the heaven itself, go up by inward thought, talk and wonder, come to their own minds and go beyond them, and reach the "region of unfailing abundance where You feed Israel forever on the food of truth". There life is Wisdom, "through whom all these things are made". The being / having been / going to be argument is complete. "We touched her slightly, with the whole force of our heart", "left the first fruits of the Spirit bound there", the return to "a word [that] has both a beginning and an end", and "Your Word ... who remains in Himself without growing old, and makes all things new" are all kept. The doctrinal terms (Wisdom, Word, eternal, Self-same) are exact.
  - **¶24:** The rendering keeps the full list of what falls silent: the tumult of the flesh; the images of earth, water and air; the poles; the soul to itself; dreams and revelations; every tongue and sign; and whatever exists by passing away. It also keeps "We did not make ourselves; He made us who remains forever"; His Word heard without any tongue of flesh, Angel, thunder or dark riddle of a likeness; "that one moment of understanding"; "Enter into the joy of your Lord"; and "When we all rise again, though we shall not all be changed?" Pusey's single long conditional period is split into a series of "Suppose..." clauses that end in "would this not be ...?" The conditional mood and the rhetorical question are kept, and nothing is turned into a statement of fact.
- **¶25.** The mother's speech is complete. Her one reason to stay was to see her son "a Catholic Christian", and it ends with "What am I doing here?" The only issue is finding 1.
- **¶31/32 (Ambrose's hymn).** ¶31 ends "For You are the", and ¶32 continues with "Maker of all things, Lord and Ruler of the heights, who clothe the day ...". The second-person "who clothe" agrees with "You are". Every element of the hymn is present when checked against Pusey and against *Deus creator omnium*: the day in light, sleep over the night, strength restored to the limbs, weary minds lifted, and anxious sorrows loosed. The unrhymed rendering is acceptable.
- **¶18.** Following the Latin with "marks of blows" is correct and needed for the Patricius contrast. The marriage contract "as a deed of service" and "servants ... lords" are kept.
- **¶17.** The Latin rendering "the deepest currents of the torrent, the ordered turbulence of the flow of the ages" is correct. "Whoever despises small things will fall little by little" is recognizable.
- **¶9.** Psalm 4 phrases are recognizable. "Eternal Simple Essence" and "corn, and wine, and oil" are kept. "Lick at the mere images of them" follows the Latin *imagines*, which is correct.
- **¶13.** Adeodatus, *The Master*, "his sixteenth year", "I had no part in that boy except the sin" and "the same age as ourselves in grace" are all present.
- **¶16.** Euodius, Ostia and the maidservant's water-drinking rule with her speech are complete.

Human addressees are lowercase (Monnica's speeches, the maidservant, the Psalm "O sons of men"). "Monnica" appears once, as in Pusey, and "Euodius" is spelled as in Pusey. "Holy Ghost" is absent.

=== Chapter 1: Act 1, Scene 1 — An Apartment in the Duke’s Palace ===
[0] [Enter Orsino, Duke of Illyria, Curio, and other Lords; Musicians attending.]

[1] DUKE. If music be the food of love, play on. Give me an excess of it, so that, glutted, my appetite may sicken and die. That strain again — it had a dying fall. Oh, it came over my ear like the sweet sound that breathes upon a bank of violets, stealing fragrance and giving it back. Enough — no more. It is not as sweet now as it was before. O spirit of love, how quick and fresh you are! Although your capacity to receive is wide as the sea, nothing enters there, however valid or exalted, but sinks into low value within a minute. Fancy is so full of shifting shapes that it alone is supremely fantastical.

[2] CURIO. Will you go hunting, my lord?

[3] DUKE. Hunt what, Curio?

[4] CURIO. The hart.

[5] DUKE. Why, that is exactly what I am doing — the noblest hart I have. Oh, when my eyes first saw Olivia, it seemed she cleansed the air of plague. In that instant I was turned into a hart, and my desires, like savage cruel hounds, have pursued me ever since. What now? What news from her?

[6] [Enter Valentine.]

[7] VALENTINE. With your permission, my lord, I was not admitted. But her handmaid returned this answer: For seven summers’ heat, no eye, not even the sky, shall behold her face fully revealed. Like a cloistered nun she will walk veiled, watering her chamber once a day with tears that sting the eyes — all this to preserve a dead brother’s love, which she means to keep fresh and lasting in her sorrowful remembrance.

[8] DUKE. Oh, she who has a heart so finely framed that she pays this debt of love merely to a brother — how she will love when the rich golden arrow has killed off every other affection in her, when liver, brain, and heart, those sovereign thrones, are all filled and supplied with her sweet perfections under one ruling king! Lead on to fragrant beds of flowers. Thoughts of love lie richest when canopied with bowers.

[9] [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 2: Act 1, Scene 2 — The sea-coast ===
[0] [Enter Viola, a Captain, and Sailors.]

[1] VIOLA. What country is this, friends?

[2] CAPTAIN. This is Illyria, lady.

[3] VIOLA. And what should I do in Illyria? My brother is in Elysium. Perhaps he isn’t drowned. What do you think, sailors?

[4] CAPTAIN. By the same chance you yourself were saved.

[5] VIOLA. Oh my poor brother! And so perhaps he may be too.

[6] CAPTAIN. True, madam. And to comfort you with that chance, let me assure you: after our ship split apart, when you and the few rescued with you clung to our drifting lifeboat, I saw your brother — most resourceful in danger — bind himself, with courage and hope both teaching him how, to a strong mast that floated on the sea. There, like Arion on the dolphin’s back, I saw him keep company with the waves for as long as I could see him.

[7] VIOLA. For saying so, here’s gold! My own escape gives my hope reason to believe, and your account confirms it — that he too may live. Do you know this country?

[8] CAPTAIN. Yes, madam, well — I was born and raised not three hours’ journey from this very spot.

[9] VIOLA. Who governs here?

[10] CAPTAIN. A noble duke, in nature as in name.

[11] VIOLA. What is his name?

[12] CAPTAIN. Orsino.

[13] VIOLA. Orsino! I have heard my father name him. He was a bachelor then.

[14] CAPTAIN. And still is now, or was very recently. Just a month ago I left here, and then it was fresh gossip — for as you know, what great men do, lesser folk will chatter of — that he was pursuing the love of fair Olivia.

[15] VIOLA. Who is she?

[16] CAPTAIN. A virtuous maid, daughter of a count who died about a year ago, leaving her in the care of her brother, his son, who shortly afterward also died. For love of him, they say, she has sworn off the company and sight of men.

[17] VIOLA. Oh, that I might serve that lady, and not be exposed to the world until I had ripened my own moment to reveal my rank.

[18] CAPTAIN. That would be hard to arrange, because she will receive no kind of suit — no, not even the Duke’s.

[19] VIOLA. There is a noble bearing in you, Captain. And though nature often hides corruption behind a beautiful exterior, I will believe of you that your mind matches this fair outward character. I beg you — and I’ll pay you generously — conceal what I am, and help me with such disguise as may suit my purpose. I’ll serve this duke. You shall present me to him as a eunuch. It may be worth the trouble, for I can sing, and address him in many kinds of music that will make me very fit for his service. Whatever else may happen, I leave to time. Only shape your silence to fit my plan.

[20] CAPTAIN. Be you his eunuch, and I’ll be your mute. If my tongue ever blabs, then let my eyes go blind.

[21] VIOLA. I thank you. Lead the way.

[22] [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 3: Act 1, Scene 3 — A Room in Olivia’s House ===
[0] [Enter Sir Toby and Maria.]

[1] SIR TOBY. What the devil does my niece mean by taking her brother’s death this way? Worry is an enemy to life, I’m sure of it.

[2] MARIA. Honestly, Sir Toby, you must come in earlier at night. Your cousin, my lady, takes great offense at your late hours.

[3] SIR TOBY. Well, let her take offense before being offended.

[4] MARIA. Yes, but you must keep yourself within the modest limits of order.

[5] SIR TOBY. Confine myself? I’ll confine myself no more than I already am. These clothes are good enough to drink in, and so are these boots. If they’re not, let them hang themselves by their own straps.

[6] MARIA. All that swilling and drinking will be your ruin. I heard my lady speaking about it yesterday — and about a foolish knight you brought here one night as her suitor.

[7] SIR TOBY. Who? Sir Andrew Aguecheek?

[8] MARIA. Yes, him.

[9] SIR TOBY. He’s as fine a man as any in Illyria.

[10] MARIA. What’s that got to do with anything?

[11] SIR TOBY. Why, he has three thousand ducats a year.

[12] MARIA. Yes, but he’ll only have a year’s worth of all those ducats. He’s a complete fool, and a spendthrift.

[13] SIR TOBY. Shame on you for saying so! He plays the viol-da-gamba, and speaks three or four languages word for word without consulting a book, and has all the good gifts of nature.

[14] MARIA. He has indeed, but he’s practically an idiot. Besides being a fool, he’s a great quarreller. And if he didn’t have the cowardice to dampen his appetite for quarrels, the prudent say he would quickly have a grave for a gift.

[15] SIR TOBY. By this hand, anyone who says so of him is a scoundrel and a slanderer. Who are they?

[16] MARIA. The same people who add that he’s drunk every night in your company.

[17] SIR TOBY. That’s from drinking toasts to my niece. I’ll drink to her as long as there’s a passage in my throat and drink in Illyria. He’s a coward and a wretch who won’t drink to my niece till his brain spins on its toe like a parish-top. Hush, girl! _Castiliano vulgo:_ here comes Sir Andrew Agueface.

[18] [Enter Sir Andrew.]

[19] AGUECHEEK. Sir Toby Belch! How are you, Sir Toby Belch?

[20] SIR TOBY. Dear Sir Andrew!

[21] SIR ANDREW. Bless you, fair shrew.

[22] MARIA. And you too, sir.

[23] SIR TOBY. Accost her, Sir Andrew, accost.

[24] SIR ANDREW. What’s that?

[25] SIR TOBY. My niece’s chambermaid.

[26] SIR ANDREW. Good Mistress Accost, I’d like to know you better.

[27] MARIA. My name is Mary, sir.

[28] SIR ANDREW. Good Mistress Mary Accost —

[29] SIR TOBY. You’ve got it wrong, knight. ‘Accost’ means face her, board her, woo her, attack her.

[30] SIR ANDREW. Honestly, I wouldn’t undertake her in this company. Is that what ‘accost’ means?

[31] MARIA. Farewell, gentlemen.

[32] SIR TOBY. If you let her go like that, Sir Andrew, may you never draw sword again.

[33] SIR ANDREW. If you leave like that, mistress, I wish I might never draw sword again. Fair lady, do you think you have fools by the hand?

[34] MARIA. Sir, I haven’t got you by the hand.

[35] SIR ANDREW. Indeed, but you shall, and here’s my hand.

[36] MARIA. Now, sir, thought is free. Please bring your hand to the buttery bar and let it drink.

[37] SIR ANDREW. Why, sweetheart? What’s your metaphor?

[38] MARIA. It’s dry, sir.

[39] SIR ANDREW. Well, I should think so. I’m not such an ass that I can’t keep my hand dry. But what’s your jest?

[40] MARIA. A dry jest, sir.

[41] SIR ANDREW. Are you full of them?

[42] MARIA. Yes, sir — I have them at my fingertips. There, now that I let go of your hand, I’m barren.

[43] [Exit Maria.]

[44] SIR TOBY. Oh knight, you need a cup of canary wine. When did I ever see you so put down?

[45] SIR ANDREW. Never in your life, I think — unless you’ve seen canary wine put me down. I think sometimes I have no more wit than a Christian or an ordinary man. But I am a great eater of beef, and I believe that does harm to my wit.

[46] SIR TOBY. No question.

[47] SIR ANDREW. If I really thought so, I’d swear off it. I’ll ride home tomorrow, Sir Toby.

[48] SIR TOBY. _Pourquoy,_ my dear knight?

[49] SIR ANDREW. What does _pourquoy_ mean? Do, or don’t do? I wish I had spent on languages the time I’ve spent on fencing, dancing, and bear-baiting. Oh, if only I had followed the arts!

[50] SIR TOBY. Then you would have had an excellent head of hair.

[51] SIR ANDREW. Why, would that have improved my hair?

[52] SIR TOBY. Beyond question — for you can see it won’t curl naturally.

[53] SIR ANDREW. But it suits me well enough, doesn’t it?

[54] SIR TOBY. Excellent — it hangs like flax on a distaff. And I hope to see a housewife take you between her legs and spin it off.

[55] SIR ANDREW. Truly, I’ll go home tomorrow, Sir Toby. Your niece won’t be seen, and if she is, it’s four-to-one she’ll have nothing to do with me. The Count himself, right here nearby, is wooing her.

[56] SIR TOBY. She’ll have nothing to do with the Count. She’ll not match above her station — not in estate, age, or wit. I’ve heard her swear it. Come on, there’s still hope, man.

[57] SIR ANDREW. I’ll stay a month longer. I’m a fellow of the strangest mind in the world. I delight in masques and revels — sometimes altogether.

[58] SIR TOBY. Are you good at those fancy moves, knight?

[59] SIR ANDREW. As any man in Illyria, whatever his rank, below the level of my betters. Still, I won’t compare myself with an old man.

[60] SIR TOBY. What’s your excellence in a galliard, knight?

[61] SIR ANDREW. Truly, I can cut a caper.

[62] SIR TOBY. And I can cut the mutton to go with it.

[63] SIR ANDREW. And I think I’ve got the back-trick as strong as any man in Illyria.

[64] SIR TOBY. Why are these things hidden? Why have these gifts got a curtain in front of them? Are they likely to gather dust like Mistress Mall’s portrait? Why don’t you go to church in a galliard and come home in a coranto? My very walk should be a jig. I wouldn’t even make water except in a cinquepace. What do you mean by this? Is it a world for hiding virtues in? I thought, by the excellent shape of your leg, it was formed under the star of a galliard.

[65] SIR ANDREW. Yes, it’s strong, and it looks tolerably well in a flame-colored stocking. Shall we set about some revels?

[66] SIR TOBY. What else should we do? Weren’t we born under Taurus?

[67] SIR ANDREW. Taurus? That’s sides and heart.

[68] SIR TOBY. No, sir, it’s legs and thighs. Let me see you caper. Ha, higher! Ha, ha, excellent!

[69] [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 4: Act 1, Scene 4 — A Room in the Duke’s Palace ===
[0] [Enter Valentine and Viola in man’s attire.]

[1] VALENTINE. If the Duke keeps showing you favors, Cesario, you’re likely to rise high. He has known you only three days, and already you’re no stranger to him.

[2] VIOLA. You either suspect his moodiness or my carelessness, since you question whether his affection will last. Is he inconstant, sir, in his favors?

[3] VALENTINE. No, believe me.

[4] [Enter Duke, Curio, and Attendants.]

[5] VIOLA. Thank you. Here comes the Count.

[6] DUKE. Who saw Cesario? Ho!

[7] VIOLA. At your service, my lord — here.

[8] DUKE. The rest of you, stand aside a moment. — Cesario, you know everything; I have unclasped to you the very book of my secret soul. So, good youth, take yourself to her. Don’t be denied entrance. Stand at her doors and tell them your foot will take root there until you’re granted an audience.

[9] VIOLA. Surely, my noble lord, if she is as given over to her sorrow as people say, she’ll never admit me.

[10] DUKE. Then be loud, and break every civil rule, rather than come back with nothing achieved.

[11] VIOLA. Say I do speak with her, my lord — what then?

[12] DUKE. Oh, then lay out the passion of my love. Astonish her with the depth of my faithful devotion. It will suit you well to act out my sorrows. She will attend to it better from your youth than from an envoy of graver appearance.

[13] VIOLA. I don’t think so, my lord.

[14] DUKE. Dear lad, believe it. Anyone who says you’re a man would be lying about your happy years. Diana’s lip is not smoother or redder. Your small voice is like a maiden’s, shrill and clear. Everything about you resembles a woman’s part. I know your astrological sign is well-suited for this errand. Four or five of you attend him — all of you, if you wish, for I am best when in the least company. Succeed in this, and you shall live as freely as your lord, and call his fortunes your own.

[15] VIOLA. I’ll do my best to woo your lady. [Aside.] Yet what a thwarted struggle! Whoever I woo, I myself would be his wife.

[16] [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 5: Act 1, Scene 5 — A Room in Olivia’s House ===
[0] [Enter Maria and Clown.]

[1] MARIA. No — either tell me where you’ve been, or I won’t open my lips wide enough for a bristle to enter as an excuse for you. My lady will hang you for your absence.

[2] CLOWN. Let her hang me. He who is well hanged in this world needs to fear no enemy flags.

[3] MARIA. Explain that.

[4] CLOWN. He’ll see no enemies to fear.

[5] MARIA. A meager answer, fit for Lent. I can tell you where that saying ‘I fear no colours’ was born.

[6] CLOWN. Where, good Mistress Mary?

[7] MARIA. In the wars — and that you can boldly say in your foolery.

[8] CLOWN. Well, may God give wisdom to those who have it; and as for fools, let them use their talents.

[9] MARIA. Yet you’ll be hanged for being absent so long — or be dismissed; isn’t that as bad as hanging for you?

[10] CLOWN. Many a good hanging prevents a bad marriage. As for being turned away, let the summer carry me through.

[11] MARIA. You’re resolved, then?

[12] CLOWN. Not quite that, but I am resolved on two points.

[13] MARIA. That if one breaks, the other will hold; or if both break, your trousers fall.

[14] CLOWN. Apt, truly, very apt! Well, go your way. If Sir Toby would leave off drinking, you’d be as witty a piece of Eve’s flesh as any in Illyria.

[15] MARIA. Peace, you rogue, no more of that. Here comes my lady — you’d best make your excuse wisely.

[16] [Exit.]

[17] [Enter Olivia with Malvolio.]

[18] CLOWN. Wit, if it be your will, put me into good fooling! Those wits that think they have you very often turn out to be fools — and I, who am sure I lack you, may pass for a wise man. For what does Quinapalus say? Better a witty fool than a foolish wit. God bless you, lady!

[19] OLIVIA. Take the fool away.

[20] CLOWN. Do you not hear, fellows? Take away the lady.

[21] OLIVIA. Come now, you’re a dry fool. I’ll have no more of you. Besides, you grow dishonest.

[22] CLOWN. Two faults, madonna, that drink and good counsel will mend. Give the dry fool drink, and the fool is no longer dry. Tell the dishonest man to mend himself — if he mends, he is no longer dishonest; if he cannot, let the tailor mend him. Anything that’s mended is just patched. Virtue that transgresses is patched with sin, and sin that amends is patched with virtue. If this simple syllogism will serve — fine. If not, what remedy? As there is no true cuckold but disaster, so beauty is a flower. The lady bade you take the fool away, so I say again — take her away.

[23] OLIVIA. Sir, I told them to take you away.

[24] CLOWN. A misunderstanding of the highest degree! Lady, _cucullus non facit monachum_ — the hood does not make the monk. That is to say, I don’t wear the fool’s motley in my brain. Good madonna, allow me to prove that you’re a fool.

[25] OLIVIA. Can you do it?

[26] CLOWN. Skillfully, good madonna.

[27] OLIVIA. Go on, then. Prove it.

[28] CLOWN. I must catechize you for it, madonna. My good little mouse of virtue, answer me.

[29] OLIVIA. Well, sir, for lack of other amusement, I’ll endure your proof.

[30] CLOWN. Good madonna, why do you mourn?

[31] OLIVIA. Good fool, for my brother’s death.

[32] CLOWN. I think his soul is in hell, madonna.

[33] OLIVIA. I know his soul is in heaven, fool.

[34] CLOWN. The more fool you, madonna, for mourning that your brother’s soul is in heaven. Take away the fool, gentlemen.

[35] OLIVIA. What do you think of this fool, Malvolio? Doesn’t he improve?

[36] MALVOLIO. Yes — and shall continue to, till the pangs of death shake him. Infirmity, which decays the wise, always makes the fool better.

[37] CLOWN. God send you, sir, a speedy infirmity, the better to increase your folly! Sir Toby will swear that I am no fox; but he wouldn’t bet twopence that you’re no fool.

[38] OLIVIA. What do you say to that, Malvolio?

[39] MALVOLIO. I marvel that your ladyship takes pleasure in such a sterile rascal. I saw him outdone the other day by an ordinary fool with no more brain than a stone. Look — he’s already out of ammunition. Unless you laugh and give him occasion, he’s muzzled. I declare, I rate these wise men who crow at such professional fools no better than the fools’ stooges.

[40] OLIVIA. Oh, you are sick of self-love, Malvolio, and you taste with a disordered appetite. To be generous, guiltless, and open-minded is to take for harmless bird-bolts what you consider cannon balls. There is no slander in a licensed fool, even if he does nothing but rail; nor any railing in a known discreet man, though he does nothing but reprove.

[41] CLOWN. Now may Mercury give you the gift of lying, since you speak so well of fools!

[42] [Enter Maria.]

[43] MARIA. Madam, there is a young gentleman at the gate who very much wishes to speak with you.

[44] OLIVIA. From Count Orsino, is it?

[45] MARIA. I don’t know, madam. He’s a handsome young man, and well attended.

[46] OLIVIA. Which of my people is keeping him waiting?

[47] MARIA. Sir Toby, madam — your kinsman.

[48] OLIVIA. Get him away from there, please. He speaks nothing but madman’s talk. Shame on him!

[49] [Exit Maria.]

[50] Go, Malvolio. If it’s a suit from the Count, I am sick, or not at home — whatever you like to dismiss it.

[51] [Exit Malvolio.]

[52] Now you see, sir, how your fooling grows stale, and people don’t like it.

[53] CLOWN. You’ve spoken for us, madonna, as if your eldest son were to be a fool — whose skull may Jove cram with brains, because here comes one of your kin who has a very weak _pia mater_.

[54] [Enter Sir Toby.]

[55] OLIVIA. By my honor, half drunk. Who is at the gate, cousin?

[56] SIR TOBY. A gentleman.

[57] OLIVIA. A gentleman? What gentleman?

[58] SIR TOBY. There’s a gentleman here. A plague on these pickled herrings! How now, sot?

[59] CLOWN. Good Sir Toby.

[60] OLIVIA. Cousin, cousin, how have you come to this lethargy so early in the day?

[61] SIR TOBY. Lechery! I defy lechery. There’s someone at the gate.

[62] OLIVIA. Yes, indeed — who is he?

[63] SIR TOBY. Let him be the devil if he wants, I don’t care. Give me faith, I say. Well, it’s all the same.

[64] [Exit.]

[65] OLIVIA. What is a drunken man like, fool?

[66] CLOWN. Like a drowned man, a fool, and a madman. One drink above his limit makes him a fool, the second makes him mad, and a third drowns him.

[67] OLIVIA. Then go and find the coroner, and let him hold inquest on my cousin — for he’s in the third degree of drink, he’s drowned. Go, look after him.

[68] CLOWN. He’s still only mad as yet, madonna. The fool shall look after the madman.

[69] [Exit Clown.]

[70] [Enter Malvolio.]

[71] MALVOLIO. Madam, that young fellow swears he will speak with you. I told him you were sick — he claims to know that, and therefore comes to speak with you. I told him you were asleep — he seems to have foreknowledge of that too, and therefore comes to speak with you. What should I say to him, lady? He is fortified against any denial.

[72] OLIVIA. Tell him he shall not speak with me.

[73] MALVOLIO. He has been told. And he says he’ll stand at your door like a sheriff’s post, and be the support of a bench, but he’ll speak with you.

[74] OLIVIA. What kind of man is he?

[75] MALVOLIO. Why, of the human kind.

[76] OLIVIA. What manner of man?

[77] MALVOLIO. Of very ill manners. He’ll speak with you whether you will or not.

[78] OLIVIA. What appearance and age is he?

[79] MALVOLIO. Not yet old enough for a man, nor young enough for a boy — like a squash before it’s a peapod, or an unripe apple just before it’s ripe. He’s caught between the tide — between boy and man. He is very handsome, and he speaks very saucily. One would think his mother’s milk was hardly out of him yet.

[80] OLIVIA. Let him approach. Call in my gentlewoman.

[81] MALVOLIO. Gentlewoman, my lady calls.

[82] [Exit.]

[83] [Enter Maria.]

[84] OLIVIA. Give me my veil. Come, throw it over my face. We’ll hear Orsino’s embassy once more.

[85] [Enter Viola.]

[86] VIOLA. The honorable lady of the house — which is she?

[87] OLIVIA. Speak to me. I shall answer for her. What do you want?

[88] VIOLA. Most radiant, exquisite, and unmatchable beauty — please, tell me if this is the lady of the house, for I have never seen her. I would hate to waste my speech, for besides being excellently composed, I have taken great pains to memorize it. Good beauties, let me suffer no scorn — I am very sensitive, even to the slightest unkind treatment.

[89] OLIVIA. Where did you come from, sir?

[90] VIOLA. I can say little more than I have studied, and that question is outside my script. Good gentle one, give me modest assurance — if you are the lady of the house, that I may proceed with my speech.

[91] OLIVIA. Are you an actor?

[92] VIOLA. No, on my heart’s honor — and yet, by the very fangs of malice I swear, I am not what I play. Are you the lady of the house?

[93] OLIVIA. If I do not usurp myself, I am.

[94] VIOLA. Most certainly, if you are she, you do usurp yourself — for what is yours to give away is not yours to keep. But this is outside my commission. I’ll go on with my speech in your praise, and then show you the heart of my message.

[95] OLIVIA. Come to what is important. I excuse you from the praise.

[96] VIOLA. Alas, I took great pains to study it — and it is poetical.

[97] OLIVIA. All the more likely to be false. Please, keep it to yourself. I heard you were saucy at my gates, and allowed your approach more to marvel at you than to hear you. If you are mad, be gone. If you’re sane, be brief. I’m not in the mood today for such flighty dialogue.

[98] MARIA. Will you hoist sail, sir? Here lies your way.

[99] VIOLA. No, good deck-swabber, I’m to drift here a little longer. Some pacification for your giant, sweet lady. Tell me your mind. I am a messenger.

[100] OLIVIA. Surely you have some hideous matter to deliver, when the courtesy of it is so frightful. Speak your business.

[101] VIOLA. It concerns your ear alone. I bring no declaration of war, no demand for tribute. I hold the olive branch in my hand. My words are as full of peace as of substance.

[102] OLIVIA. Yet you began rudely. What are you? What do you want?

[103] VIOLA. The rudeness I’ve shown I learned from how I was received. What I am and what I want are as secret as a maiden’s virginity — to your ears, divinity; to anyone else’s, profanation.

[104] OLIVIA. Leave us alone. We’ll hear this divinity.

[105] [Exit Maria.]

[106] Now, sir, what is your text?

[107] VIOLA. Most sweet lady —

[108] OLIVIA. A comforting doctrine, and much may be said about it. Where is your text found?

[109] VIOLA. In Orsino’s bosom.

[110] OLIVIA. In his bosom? In what chapter of his bosom?

[111] VIOLA. To answer by the method, in the first chapter of his heart.

[112] OLIVIA. Oh, I have read it — it is heresy. Have you no more to say?

[113] VIOLA. Good madam, let me see your face.

[114] OLIVIA. Do you have any commission from your lord to negotiate with my face? You are now off your text. But we will draw the curtain and show you the picture. [Unveiling.] Look, sir, just like this I was at this present time. Is it not well done?

[115] VIOLA. Excellently done, if God did all of it.

[116] OLIVIA. It is fast-dyed, sir; it will endure wind and weather.

[117] VIOLA. It is beauty truly mixed, whose red and white were laid on by Nature’s own sweet and cunning hand. Lady, you are the cruellest woman alive if you would lead these graces to the grave and leave the world no copy.

[118] OLIVIA. Oh, sir, I will not be so hard-hearted. I will issue several inventories of my beauty. It shall be itemized and every particle and feature labelled by my will — as, item, two lips, fairly red; item, two grey eyes with lids on them; item, one neck, one chin, and so on. Were you sent here to praise me?

[119] VIOLA. I see what you are — you are too proud. But even if you were the devil, you are fair. My lord and master loves you. Oh, such love could only be repaid even if you were crowned the unrivalled queen of beauty!

[120] OLIVIA. How does he love me?

[121] VIOLA. With adoration, abundant tears, with groans that thunder love, with sighs of fire.

[122] OLIVIA. Your lord knows my mind — I cannot love him. Yet I think him virtuous, know him to be noble, of great estate, of fresh and stainless youth, well reported, free-spirited, learned, and brave, and in stature and physical form, a graceful person. But still I cannot love him. He might have had his answer long ago.

[123] VIOLA. If I loved you with my master’s passion, with such suffering, such a dying life, I would find no sense in your denial — I would not accept it.

[124] OLIVIA. Why, what would you do?

[125] VIOLA. Make me a willow cabin at your gate, and call upon my soul within the house. Write loyal songs of rejected love, and sing them loud even in the dead of night. Halloo your name to the echoing hills, and make the babbling gossip of the air cry out ‘Olivia!’ Oh, you should find no rest between the elements of air and earth, until you pitied me.

[126] OLIVIA. You might do much. What is your parentage?

[127] VIOLA. Above my fortunes, yet my situation is good. I am a gentleman.

[128] OLIVIA. Get yourself back to your lord. I cannot love him. Let him send no more — unless, perhaps, you come to me again to tell me how he takes it. Farewell. I thank you for your trouble. Spend this for me.

[129] VIOLA. I am no paid messenger, lady. Keep your purse. It is my master, not myself, who lacks recompense. May Love make hard as flint the heart of whomever you fall in love with, and let your passion, like my master’s, be met with contempt. Farewell, fair cruelty.

[130] [Exit.]

[131] OLIVIA. ‘What is your parentage?’ ‘Above my fortunes, yet my state is well: I am a gentleman.’ I would swear you are. Your tongue, your face, your limbs, your actions, your spirit — give you a fivefold proof of nobility. Not too fast — softly, softly! Unless the master were the man. What? Even so quickly may one catch the plague? I think I feel this youth’s perfections, with invisible and subtle stealth, creeping in through my eyes. Well, let it be. What ho, Malvolio!

[132] [Enter Malvolio.]

[133] MALVOLIO. Here, madam, at your service.

[134] OLIVIA. Run after that peevish messenger, the Count’s man. He left this ring behind, whether I wanted it or not. Tell him I’ll have none of it. Ask him not to flatter his lord with hopes, nor encourage him — I am not for him. If the youth will come this way tomorrow, I’ll give him reasons. Hurry, Malvolio.

[135] MALVOLIO. Madam, I will.

[136] [Exit.]

[137] OLIVIA. I am doing I know not what, and I fear my eye is too great a flatterer for my judgment. Fate, show your force — we do not own ourselves. What is decreed must be; and let this be so!

[138] [Exit.]

=== Chapter 6: Act 2, Scene 1 — The sea-coast ===
[0] [Enter Antonio and Sebastian.]

[1] ANTONIO. Will you stay no longer? Nor will you let me go with you?

[2] SEBASTIAN. By your leave, no. My stars shine darkly over me. The malignancy of my fate might infect yours. So I’ll ask your leave to bear my evils alone. It would be poor repayment for your love to lay any of them upon you.

[3] ANTONIO. At least tell me where you’re bound.

[4] SEBASTIAN. No, truly, sir. My intended voyage is mere wandering. But I see in you such an excellent sense of decency that you will not press from me what I am willing to keep to myself. So good manners oblige me all the more to speak openly. You must know, then, Antonio, that my name is Sebastian — which I called Roderigo. My father was that Sebastian of Messaline whom I’m sure you have heard of. He left behind him myself and a sister, both born in the same hour. If the heavens had been so pleased, would that we had ended in the same hour too! But you, sir, altered that, for an hour or so before you rescued me from the surf, my sister was drowned.

[5] ANTONIO. Alas, that wretched day!

[6] SEBASTIAN. A lady, sir — though it was said she much resembled me — was yet by many considered beautiful. Though I could not, with such enthusiastic wonder, go too far in believing it, yet this much I will boldly proclaim of her: she had a mind that even envy could only call fair. She is already drowned, sir, in salt water — though I seem to drown her memory again with more.

[7] ANTONIO. Forgive me, sir, for your poor reception.

[8] SEBASTIAN. Oh good Antonio, forgive me the trouble I’ve given you.

[9] ANTONIO. If you will not murder me for my love, let me be your servant.

[10] SEBASTIAN. If you will not undo what you have done — that is, kill the man you have rescued — do not ask for that. Farewell at once. My heart is full of kindness, and I am still so close to my mother’s ways that the slightest further occasion will make my eyes betray me. I am bound for Count Orsino’s court. Farewell.

[11] [Exit.]

[12] ANTONIO. May the gentleness of all the gods go with you! I have many enemies in Orsino’s court, otherwise I would very soon see you there. But come what may, I love you so much that danger shall seem like sport, and I will go.

[13] [Exit.]

=== Chapter 7: Act 2, Scene 2 — A street ===
[0] [Enter Viola; Malvolio at separate doors.]

[1] MALVOLIO. Weren’t you just now with Countess Olivia?

[2] VIOLA. Just now, sir. At a moderate pace I have only arrived as far as here since then.

[3] MALVOLIO. She returns this ring to you, sir. You might have spared me the trouble by taking it away yourself. She adds, moreover, that you should give your lord firm assurance she will have none of him. And one more thing — that you should not be so bold as to come again on his affairs, unless it be to report how your lord takes this news. Take it accordingly.

[4] VIOLA. She took the ring from me. I’ll have none of it.

[5] MALVOLIO. Come, sir, you peevishly threw it at her, and her wish is that it be returned the same way. If it’s worth stooping for, there it lies in plain view. If not, let it belong to whoever finds it.

[6] [Exit.]

[7] VIOLA. I left no ring with her — what does this lady mean? Heaven forbid my outward appearance has charmed her! She really did look me over — so much that I thought her eyes had silenced her tongue, for she spoke in fits and starts, distracted. She loves me, surely — the cunning of her passion invites me through this surly messenger. None of my lord’s ring? Why, he sent her none. I am the man! If that’s how it is — as it is — poor lady, she’d be better off loving a dream. Disguise, I see you are a kind of wickedness that the cunning enemy uses to great effect. How easy it is for handsome deceivers to make their imprint on women’s waxen hearts! Alas, our frailty is the cause, not we ourselves — for as we are made, so we are. How will this turn out? My master loves her dearly. And I, poor monster, am just as smitten with him. And she, mistaken, seems to dote on me. What will come of this? As I am a man, my situation is hopeless for my master’s love. As I am a woman — alas the day! — what useless sighs shall poor Olivia breathe! O time, you must untangle this, not I — it is too hard a knot for me to untie!

[8] [Exit.]

=== Chapter 8: Act 2, Scene 3 — A Room in Olivia’s House ===
[0] [Enter Sir Toby and Sir Andrew.]

[1] SIR TOBY. Come on, Sir Andrew. To not be in bed after midnight is to be up early. And _diluculo surgere_ — you know the saying.

[2] SIR ANDREW. No, honestly, I don’t. But I do know that being up late is being up late.

[3] SIR TOBY. A false conclusion. I hate it as much as an empty cup. To be up after midnight, and then go to bed, is to go to bed early. So going to bed after midnight is going to bed in good time. Don’t our lives consist of the four elements?

[4] SIR ANDREW. Truly, that’s what they say. But I think they consist more of eating and drinking.

[5] SIR TOBY. You are a scholar. Therefore, let us eat and drink. Marian, I say! A pitcher of wine!

[6] [Enter Clown.]

[7] SIR ANDREW. Here comes the fool, in faith.

[8] CLOWN. How now, my hearts? Did you never see the picture of ‘we three’?

[9] SIR TOBY. Welcome, ass. Now let’s have a round-song.

[10] SIR ANDREW. Honestly, the fool has an excellent set of lungs. I’d rather have such a leg and so sweet a voice for singing as the fool has, than have forty shillings. Truly, you were in fine form last night when you spoke of Pigrogromitus, and of the Vapians crossing the equator of Queubus. It was very good, in faith. I sent you sixpence for your sweetheart. Did you get it?

[11] CLOWN. I impeticosed your gratillity — for Malvolio’s nose is no whipstock. My lady has a white hand, and the Myrmidons are not ale-houses.

[12] SIR ANDREW. Excellent! Why, this is the best fooling, when all’s said and done. Now, a song.

[13] SIR TOBY. Come on, here’s sixpence for you. Let’s have a song.

[14] SIR ANDREW. There’s a tester from me too. If one knight gives a —

[15] CLOWN. Would you like a love-song, or a song about good living?

[16] SIR TOBY. A love-song, a love-song.

[17] SIR ANDREW. Yes, yes. I don’t care for good living.

[18] CLOWN. [Sings.] _O mistress mine, where are you roaming? O stay and hear, your true love’s coming, That can sing both high and low. Trip no further, pretty sweeting. Journeys end in lovers meeting, Every wise man’s son doth know._

[19] SIR ANDREW. Excellent, in faith.

[20] SIR TOBY. Good, good.

[21] CLOWN. _What is love? ’Tis not hereafter, Present mirth hath present laughter. What’s to come is still unsure. In delay there lies no plenty, Then come kiss me, sweet and twenty. Youth’s a stuff will not endure._

[22] SIR ANDREW. A honey-sweet voice, as I am a true knight.

[23] SIR TOBY. A contagious breath.

[24] SIR ANDREW. Very sweet and contagious, in faith.

[25] SIR TOBY. To hear it through the nose, it is sweetly contagious. But shall we make the sky dance indeed? Shall we rouse the night-owl with a round that will draw three souls out of one weaver? Shall we do that?

[26] SIR ANDREW. If you love me, let’s do it. I’m a dab hand at a round.

[27] CLOWN. By Our Lady, sir, some dogs can catch well.

[28] SIR ANDREW. Most certainly. Let our round be ‘Thou Knave.’

[29] CLOWN. ‘Hold thy peace, thou knave,’ knight? I shall be obliged to call you ‘knave,’ knight.

[30] SIR ANDREW. It’s not the first time I’ve forced someone to call me knave. Begin, fool — it begins ‘Hold thy peace.’

[31] CLOWN. I shall never begin if I hold my peace.

[32] SIR ANDREW. Good, in faith! Come, begin.

[33] [Round sung.]

[34] [Enter Maria.]

[35] MARIA. What a caterwauling you’re making in here! If my lady hasn’t called up her steward Malvolio and ordered him to turn you out of the house, then never trust me again.

[36] SIR TOBY. My lady is a Cathayan, we are statesmen, Malvolio is a Peg-a-Ramsey, and —

[37] [Sings.] _Three merry men be we._ Am I not her blood-relation? Am I not

[38] of her blood? Pish! ‘Lady’! _There dwelt a man in Babylon, Lady, Lady._

[39] CLOWN. Bless me, the knight is in fine fooling.

[40] SIR ANDREW. Yes, he does it well enough when he’s in the mood — and so do I. He does it with better grace, but I do it more naturally.

[41] SIR TOBY.

[42] [Sings.] _On the twelfth day of December —_

[43] MARIA. For the love of God, be quiet!

[44] [Enter Malvolio.]

[45] MALVOLIO. My masters, are you mad? Or what are you? Have you no wit, manners, or honesty — but to gabble like tinkers at this time of night? Do you make my lady’s house into an ale-house, that you screech out your cobblers’ rounds with no softening or restraint of voice? Have you no respect for place, persons, or time?

[46] SIR TOBY. We did keep time, sir, in our rounds. Go hang yourself!

[47] MALVOLIO. Sir Toby, I must be blunt with you. My lady asked me to tell you that, though she shelters you as her kinsman, she is in no way connected with your disorderly behavior. If you can separate yourself from your misconduct, you are welcome in the house. If not, and it would please you to take your leave of her, she is very willing to bid you farewell.

[48] SIR TOBY.

[49] [Sings.] _Farewell, dear heart, since I must needs be gone._

[50] MARIA. No, good Sir Toby.

[51] CLOWN.

[52] [Sings.] _His eyes do show his days are almost done._

[53] MALVOLIO. Is it really so?

[54] SIR TOBY.

[55] [Sings.] _But I will never die._

[56] CLOWN.

[57] [Sings.] _Sir Toby, there you lie._

[58] MALVOLIO. This is much to your credit.

[59] SIR TOBY.

[60] [Sings.] _Shall I bid him go?_

[61] CLOWN.

[62] [Sings.] _What if you do?_

[63] SIR TOBY.

[64] [Sings.] _Shall I bid him go, and not hold back?_

[65] CLOWN.

[66] [Sings.] _O, no, no, no, no, you dare not._

[67] SIR TOBY. Out of tune? Sir, you lie. Are you anything more than a steward? Do you think, because you are virtuous, there shall be no more cakes and ale?

[68] CLOWN. Yes, by Saint Anne, and ginger shall be hot in the mouth too.

[69] SIR TOBY. You’re right. Go, sir — rub your chain of office with crumbs. A pitcher of wine, Maria!

[70] MALVOLIO. Mistress Mary, if you valued my lady’s favor at anything more than contempt, you would not provide the means for this uncivil behavior. She shall hear of it, by this hand.

[71] [Exit.]

[72] MARIA. Go shake your ears.

[73] SIR ANDREW. It would be as good a deed as drinking when a man’s hungry — to challenge him to the field, and then to break the appointment and make a fool of him.

[74] SIR TOBY. Do it, knight. I’ll write the challenge for you, or I’ll deliver your indignation to him by word of mouth.

[75] MARIA. Sweet Sir Toby, be patient for tonight. Since the Count’s young man was here today with my lady, she is much disturbed. As for Monsieur Malvolio, leave him to me. If I don’t make him a byword and a common joke, don’t think I have enough wit to lie straight in my bed. I know I can do it.

[76] SIR TOBY. Tell us, tell us — tell us something about him.

[77] MARIA. Indeed, sir, sometimes he is a kind of Puritan.

[78] SIR ANDREW. Oh, if I thought so, I’d beat him like a dog.

[79] SIR TOBY. What — for being a Puritan? Your exquisite reason, dear knight?

[80] SIR ANDREW. I have no exquisite reason for it, but I have reason enough.

[81] MARIA. He’s no more a Puritan than the devil — or anything else consistently — but a time-pleaser, an affected ass who memorizes courtly behavior from books and spouts it out in great handfuls. He is so persuaded of himself, so stuffed (as he believes) with excellences, that it is the very foundation of his faith that all who look on him love him. And on that vice in him my revenge will find notable cause to work.

[82] SIR TOBY. What will you do?

[83] MARIA. I will drop in his way some mysterious love letters, in which — by the color of his beard, the shape of his leg, the way he walks, the expression of his eye, brow, and complexion — he will find himself most vividly described. I can write very like my lady, your niece. On a forgotten matter, we can hardly tell our hands apart.

[84] SIR TOBY. Excellent! I smell a scheme.

[85] SIR ANDREW. I have it in my nose too.

[86] SIR TOBY. He shall think, from the letters you’ll drop, that they come from my niece, and that she is in love with him.

[87] MARIA. My plan is indeed a horse of that color.

[88] SIR ANDREW. And your horse, now, would make him an ass.

[89] MARIA. An ass, no doubt.

[90] SIR ANDREW. Oh, it will be admirable!

[91] MARIA. Royal sport, I assure you. I know my medicine will work on him. I will plant you two — and let the fool make a third — where he shall find the letter. Watch how he interprets it. For tonight, to bed, and dream of what will come. Farewell.

[92] [Exit.]

[93] SIR TOBY. Good night, Penthesilea.

[94] SIR ANDREW. By me, she’s a good wench.

[95] SIR TOBY. She’s a true-bred beagle, and one who adores me. What of it?

[96] SIR ANDREW. I was adored once too.

[97] SIR TOBY. Let’s to bed, knight. You need to send for more money.

[98] SIR ANDREW. If I can’t win your niece, I’m in a bad way.

[99] SIR TOBY. Send for money, knight. If you don’t have her in the end, call me a gelding.

[100] SIR ANDREW. If I don’t, never trust me — take it as you will.

[101] SIR TOBY. Come, come, I’ll go warm some sack. It’s too late to go to bed now. Come, knight, come, knight.

[102] [Exeunt.]

=== Chapter 9: Act 2, Scene 4 — A Room in the Duke’s Palace ===
[0] [Enter Duke, Viola, Curio, and others.]

[1] DUKE. Give me some music. Now, good morning, friends. Now, good Cesario — that one piece of song, that old and ancient song we heard last night. I thought it relieved my passion much, more than the light tunes and contrived phrases of these brisk, giddy-paced times. Come, just one verse.

[2] CURIO. The one who should sing it, my lord, isn’t here.

[3] DUKE. Who was it?

[4] CURIO. Feste, the jester, my lord — a fool that Lady Olivia’s father took great delight in. He’s somewhere about the house.

[5] DUKE. Find him, and play the tune in the meantime.

[6] [Exit Curio. Music plays.]

[7] Come here, boy. If ever you love, in the sweet pangs of it, remember me. For all true lovers are as I am — unsettled and skittish in everything else, except in the constant image of the beloved creature. How do you like this tune?

[8] VIOLA. It echoes the very seat where love is throned.

[9] DUKE. You speak like a master. By my life, young as you are, your eye has lingered on some object it loves. Hasn’t it, boy?

[10] VIOLA. A little, by your favor.

[11] DUKE. What sort of woman is she?

[12] VIOLA. Of your complexion.

[13] DUKE. Then she’s not worth you. What age, in truth?

[14] VIOLA. About your age, my lord.

[15] DUKE. Too old, by heaven! Let a woman always take a man older than herself, so she conforms to him and remains steady in her husband’s heart. For, boy, however we praise ourselves, our affections are more giddy and unsteady, more longing, wavering, sooner lost and worn out, than women’s are.

[16] VIOLA. I think so too, my lord.

[17] DUKE. Then let your love be younger than yourself, or your affection cannot hold its bend. For women are like roses — once their fair flower is displayed, it falls that very hour.

[18] VIOLA. And so they are. Alas, that they are so — to die just when they grow to perfection!

[19] [Enter Curio and Clown.]

[20] DUKE. Oh, fellow, come — the song we had last night. Mark it, Cesario — it is old and plain. The spinsters and the knitters in the sun, and the free maids who weave their thread with bone shuttles, are accustomed to chant it. It is simple truth, and toys with the innocence of love as in the old times.

[21] CLOWN. Are you ready, sir?

[22] DUKE. Yes — please, sing.

[23] [Music.]

[24] The Clown’s song.

[25] _Come away, come away, death, and in sad cypress let me be laid. Fly away, fly away, breath; I am slain by a fair cruel maid. My shroud of white, stuck all with yew, O, prepare it! My part of death, no one so true did share it._

[26] _Not a flower, not a flower sweet, on my black coffin let there be strown. Not a friend, not a friend greet my poor corpse where my bones shall be thrown. A thousand thousand sighs to save, lay me, O, where sad true lover never find my grave, to weep there._

[27] DUKE. There’s payment for your trouble.

[28] CLOWN. No trouble, sir. I take pleasure in singing, sir.

[29] DUKE. Then I’ll pay your pleasure.

[30] CLOWN. Truly, sir — and pleasure will be paid one time or another.

[31] DUKE. Now give me leave to leave you.

[32] CLOWN. Now may the melancholy god protect you, and the tailor make your doublet of changeable taffeta, for your mind is a very opal. I would have men of such inconstancy put to sea, so their business might be everything, and their destination everywhere — for that’s what always makes a good voyage out of nothing. Farewell.

[33] [Exit Clown.]

[34] DUKE. Let all the rest withdraw.

[35] [Exeunt Curio and Attendants.]

[36] Once more, Cesario, get yourself to that same sovereign cruelty. Tell her my love, more noble than the world, doesn’t prize quantities of common land. The parts that fortune has bestowed on her, tell her I value as fickly as fortune itself. But it’s that miracle and queen of jewels which nature decks her in that draws my soul.

[37] VIOLA. But what if she cannot love you, sir?

[38] DUKE. I cannot accept such an answer.

[39] VIOLA. Truly, but you must. Say that some lady — as perhaps there is — has for love of you as great a pang of heart as you have for Olivia. You cannot love her; you tell her so. Must she not then be given her answer?

[40] DUKE. There is no woman’s body that can endure the beating of so strong a passion as love gives my heart. No woman’s heart is big enough to hold so much. They lack capacity. Alas, their love may be called appetite — no movement of the liver, only the palate, which suffers gluttony, satiation, and revolt. But mine is as hungry as the sea, and can digest as much. Make no comparison between the love a woman can bear me and what I feel for Olivia.

[41] VIOLA. Yes, but I know —

[42] DUKE. What do you know?

[43] VIOLA. Too well, what love women may bear men. In truth, they are as true of heart as we are. My father had a daughter who loved a man, as it might be perhaps, were I a woman, I should love your lordship.

[44] DUKE. And what is her history?

[45] VIOLA. A blank, my lord. She never told her love, but let concealment, like a worm in the bud, feed on her rosy cheek. She pined in her thoughts, and with a green and yellow melancholy she sat like Patience on a monument, smiling at grief. Was not this love, indeed? We men may say more, swear more — but truly, our shows are more than our will. For we always prove much in our vows, but little in our love.

[46] DUKE. But did your sister die of her love, my boy?

[47] VIOLA. I am all the daughters of my father’s house, and all the brothers too. And yet I know not. Sir, shall I go to this lady?

[48] DUKE. Yes, that’s the matter. To her in haste. Give her this jewel. Say my love will not yield ground, will tolerate no denial.

[49] [Exeunt.]

[53] [Reads.] _To the unknown beloved, this, and my good wishes._ Her very

[54] phrases! By your leave, wax. Softly! And the impression — her Lucrece, which she uses to seal with. It is my lady. Whom is this for?

[55] FABIAN. This wins him — liver, and all.

[56] MALVOLIO.

[57] [Reads.]

[58] _Jove knows I love, but who? Lips, do not move; no man must know._

[59] ‘No man must know.’ What follows? The meter altered! ‘No man must know.’ — If this should be you, Malvolio?

[60] SIR TOBY. Marry, hang you, badger!

[61] MALVOLIO. _I may command where I adore, but silence, like a Lucrece knife, with bloodless stroke my heart doth gore; M.O.A.I. doth sway my life._

[62] FABIAN. A pretentious riddle!

[63] SIR TOBY. Excellent wench, I say.

[64] MALVOLIO. ‘M.O.A.I. doth sway my life.’ — No, but first let me see, let me see, let me see.

[65] FABIAN. What a dish of poison she has dressed for him!

[66] SIR TOBY. And with what wing the kestrel stoops at it!

[67] MALVOLIO. ‘I may command where I adore.’ Why, she may command me. I serve her — she is my lady. Why, this is obvious to any normal mind. There is no obstacle here. And the end — what should that arrangement of letters signify? If I could make that resemble something in me! Softly! ‘M.O.A.I.’ —

[68] SIR TOBY. Oh, yes, make that up — he’s on a cold scent now.

[69] FABIAN. The hound will bay at it anyway, even though it’s as rank as a fox.

[70] MALVOLIO. ‘M’ — Malvolio. ‘M!’ Why, that begins my name!

[71] FABIAN. Didn’t I say he would work it out? The cur is excellent at false trails.

[72] MALVOLIO. ‘M’ — But then there’s no consistency in what follows. It fails the test: ‘A’ should follow, but ‘O’ does.

[73] FABIAN. And ‘O’ shall end, I hope.

[74] SIR TOBY. Yes — or I’ll cudgel him and make him cry ‘O!’

[75] MALVOLIO. And then ‘I’ comes behind.

[76] FABIAN. Yes, and if you had any eye behind you, you might see more disgrace at your heels than fortunes before you.

[77] MALVOLIO. ‘M.O.A.I.’ This puzzle isn’t like the first. And yet, to bend it a little, it would yield to me, for every one of these letters is in my name. Hush, here follows prose.

[78] [Reads.] _If this falls into your hand, revolve. In my stars I am above

[79] you, but be not afraid of greatness. Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them. Your fates open their hands; let your blood and spirit embrace them. And to accustom yourself to what you are likely to become, cast off your humble guise and appear fresh. Be at odds with a kinsman, surly with servants. Let your tongue ring with arguments of state; put yourself into the manner of singularity. So advises she who sighs for you. Remember who commended your yellow stockings, and wished to see you always cross-gartered. I say, remember. Go to — you are made, if you desire to be so. If not, let me see you a steward still — the fellow of servants, and not worthy to touch Fortune’s fingers. Farewell. She that would exchange roles with you, The Fortunate Unhappy._

[80] Daylight on an open plain does not reveal more! This is open. I will be proud, I will read books of policy, I will baffle Sir Toby, I will wash off my coarse acquaintances, I will be a perfect gentleman — the very man. I do not deceive myself now by letting imagination jade me — for every reason urges this: that my lady loves me. She did commend my yellow stockings recently; she did praise my leg cross-gartered; and in this she reveals herself to my love, and with a kind of injunction drives me to these habits of her liking. I thank my stars — I am happy. I will be aloof, bold, in yellow stockings, and cross-gartered — even with the speed of putting them on. Jove and my stars be praised! Here is yet a postscript. [Reads.] _You cannot but know who I am. If you accept my love, let it appear in your smiling. Your smiles become you well. Therefore, in my presence still smile, dear my sweet, I pray you._ Jove, I thank you. I will smile, I will do everything you would have me do.

[81] [Exit.]

[82] FABIAN. I wouldn’t give my share of this sport for a pension of thousands paid by the Shah of Persia.

[83] SIR TOBY. I could marry this wench for this scheme.

[84] SIR ANDREW. So could I.

[85] SIR TOBY. And ask no other dowry with her than just such another jest.

[86] [Enter Maria.]

[87] SIR ANDREW. Nor would I.

[88] FABIAN. Here comes my noble gull-catcher.

[89] SIR TOBY. Will you set your foot on my neck?

[90] SIR ANDREW. Or on mine, either?

[91] SIR TOBY. Shall I gamble my freedom at tray-trip and become your bondslave?

[92] SIR ANDREW. Honestly, or I either?

[93] SIR TOBY. Why, you have put him into such a dream, that when the vision leaves him he must run mad.

[94] MARIA. No, but tell true — does it work on him?

[95] SIR TOBY. Like aqua vitae with a midwife.

[96] MARIA. If you will see the fruits of the sport, watch his first approach before my lady. He will come to her in yellow stockings — and it is a color she abhors — and cross-gartered, a fashion she detests. And he will smile upon her, which will be so unsuitable to her disposition, being addicted to a melancholy as she is, that it cannot but turn him into notable contempt. If you want to see it, follow me.

[97] SIR TOBY. To the gates of Tartarus, you most excellent devil of wit!

[98] SIR ANDREW. I’ll make one too.

=== Chapter 11: Act 3, Scene 1 — Olivia’s garden ===
[0] [Enter Viola and Clown with a tabor.]

[1] VIOLA. God save you, friend, and your music. Do you live by your tabor?

[2] CLOWN. No, sir, I live by the church.

[3] VIOLA. Are you a churchman?

[4] CLOWN. No such thing, sir. I live by the church, because I live at my house, and my house stands by the church.

[5] VIOLA. So you may say the king lies by a beggar, if a beggar dwells near him. Or that the church stands by your tabor, if your tabor stands by the church.

[6] CLOWN. You have said it, sir. What an age this is! A sentence is just a kidskin glove to a sharp wit — how quickly the wrong side can be turned outward!

[7] VIOLA. No doubt — those who dally cleverly with words can quickly make them wanton.

[8] CLOWN. So I wish, sir, my sister had had no name.

[9] VIOLA. Why, man?

[10] CLOWN. Why, sir, her name is a word. And dallying with that word might make my sister wanton. But truly, words are great rascals, ever since promissory bonds disgraced them.

[11] VIOLA. Your reasoning, man?

[12] CLOWN. Truly, sir, I can give you none without words, and words have become so false that I am loath to argue reason with them.

[13] VIOLA. I’ll wager you are a merry fellow, and care for nothing.

[14] CLOWN. Not so, sir — I do care for something. But in my conscience, sir, I don’t care for you. If that means caring for nothing, sir, I wish it would make you invisible.

[15] VIOLA. Aren’t you Lady Olivia’s fool?

[16] CLOWN. No, indeed, sir. Lady Olivia has no folly. She’ll keep no fool, sir, till she be married — and fools are as like husbands as pilchards are to herrings: the husband is the bigger one. I am indeed not her fool, but her corrupter of words.

[17] VIOLA. I saw you recently at Count Orsino’s.

[18] CLOWN. Folly, sir, walks around the world like the sun — it shines everywhere. I would be sorry, sir, if the fool weren’t as often with your master as with my mistress. I think I saw your wisdom there.

[19] VIOLA. No — if you start mocking me, I’m done with you. Here, take this for your expenses.

[20] CLOWN. Now may Jove, in his next shipment of hair, send you a beard!

[21] VIOLA. By my troth, I’ll tell you — I’m almost sick for one, though I wouldn’t want it to grow on my chin. Is your lady within?

[22] CLOWN. Wouldn’t a pair of these have bred, sir?

[23] VIOLA. Yes, if kept together and put to use.

[24] CLOWN. I would play Lord Pandarus of Phrygia, sir, to bring a Cressida to this Troilus.

[25] VIOLA. I understand you, sir. That’s well begged.

[26] CLOWN. The matter, I hope, is not great, sir — begging just a beggar. Cressida was a beggar. My lady is within, sir. I will explain to them where you come from. Who you are and what you want are out of my sky — I might say ‘element,’ but the word is overused.

[27] [Exit.]

[28] VIOLA. This fellow is wise enough to play the fool, and to do that well requires a kind of wit. He must observe the mood of those he jests with, the quality of persons, and the time, and like the wild hawk, stoop at every feather that comes before his eye. This is a practice as full of labor as a wise man’s art. For folly, when wisely shown, fits well — but wise men, fallen into folly, completely taint their wit.

[29] [Enter Sir Toby and Sir Andrew.]

[30] SIR TOBY. God save you, gentleman.

[31] VIOLA. And you, sir.

[32] SIR ANDREW. _Dieu vous garde, monsieur._

[33] VIOLA. _Et vous aussi; votre serviteur._

[34] SIR ANDREW. I hope, sir, you are — and I am yours.

[35] SIR TOBY. Will you enter the house? My niece is desirous that you should come in, if your business is with her.

[36] VIOLA. I am bound for your niece, sir. I mean, she is the destination of my voyage.

[37] SIR TOBY. Try out your legs, sir — put them in motion.

[38] VIOLA. My legs understand me better, sir, than I understand what you mean by bidding me ‘taste my legs.’

[39] SIR TOBY. I mean, to go, sir — to enter.

[40] VIOLA. I will answer you with gait and entrance. But we are forestalled.

[41] [Enter Olivia and Maria.]

[42] Most excellent and accomplished lady, may the heavens rain fragrances upon you!

[43] SIR ANDREW. That youth is a rare courtier. ‘Rain odours’ — well done.

[44] VIOLA. My matter has no voice, lady, except for your own most pregnant and vouchsafed ear.

[45] SIR ANDREW. ‘Odours,’ ‘pregnant,’ and ‘vouchsafed’ — I’ll have all three ready.

[46] OLIVIA. Let the garden door be shut, and leave me to my hearing.

[47] [Exeunt Sir Toby, Sir Andrew, and Maria.]

[48] Give me your hand, sir.

[49] VIOLA. My duty, madam, and most humble service.

[50] OLIVIA. What is your name?

[51] VIOLA. Cesario is your servant’s name, fair princess.

[52] OLIVIA. My servant, sir? It was never a merry world since lowly pretending was called compliment. You are servant to Count Orsino, youth.

[53] VIOLA. And he is yours, and his must necessarily be yours. Your servant’s servant is your servant, madam.

[54] OLIVIA. As for him, I think not on him. As for his thoughts, would they were blank rather than filled with me!

[55] VIOLA. Madam, I come to sharpen your gentle thoughts on his behalf.

[56] OLIVIA. Oh, by your leave, please — I asked you never to speak of him again. But if you would undertake another suit, I’d rather hear you plead that than music from the spheres.

[57] VIOLA. Dear lady —

[58] OLIVIA. Give me leave, I beseech you. I did send, after the last enchantment you cast here, a ring in chase of you. So I deceived myself, my servant, and, I fear, you. I must endure your harsh judgment, for forcing on you, by shameful cunning, what you knew was none of yours. What must you have thought? Have you not set my honor on the stake, and baited it with all the unmuzzled thoughts that a tyrannical heart can think? To one of your perception, enough has been shown. A veil of gauze, not a bosom, hides my heart. So let me hear you speak.

[59] VIOLA. I pity you.

[60] OLIVIA. That is one degree toward love.

[61] VIOLA. No, not a step. For it’s a common proof that very often we pity enemies.

[62] OLIVIA. Why then, I think it is time to smile again. O world, how apt the poor are to be proud! If one must be a prey, how much the better to fall before the lion than the wolf! [Clock strikes.] The clock upbraids me with the waste of time. Be not afraid, good youth — I will not have you. And yet, when wit and youth have come to harvest, your wife is likely to reap a proper man. There lies your way, due west.

[63] VIOLA. Then westward ho! Grace and good disposition attend your ladyship! You’ll send nothing, madam, to my lord by me?

[64] OLIVIA. Stay — please tell me what you think of me.

[65] VIOLA. That you think you are not what you are.

[66] OLIVIA. If I think so, I think the same of you.

[67] VIOLA. Then you think right — I am not what I am.

[68] OLIVIA. I wish you were as I would have you be.

[69] VIOLA. Would it be better, madam, than I am? I wish it might, for now I am your fool.

[70] OLIVIA. Oh, what a deal of scorn looks beautiful in the contempt and anger of his lip! Murderous guilt does not show itself sooner than love that would seem hidden. Love’s night is noon. Cesario, by the roses of the spring, by maidenhood, honor, truth, and everything, I love you so that, despite all your pride, neither wit nor reason can hide my passion. Do not extort your reasons from this — that because I woo, you therefore have no cause to. Rather, reason with this reasoning: love sought is good, but given unsought is better.

[71] VIOLA. By innocence I swear, and by my youth, I have one heart, one bosom, and one truth — and no woman has them. Nor will any ever be mistress of them, save I alone. And so farewell, good madam. Never more will I lament my master’s tears to you.

[72] OLIVIA. Yet come again — for you may perhaps move that heart, which now abhors, to like his love.

[73] [Exeunt.]

[23] DUKE. You can fool no more money out of me at this throw. But if you’ll let your lady know I’m here to speak with her, and bring her along with you, it may rouse my bounty further.

[24] CLOWN. Indeed, sir, lullaby to your bounty till I come again. I go, sir — but I would not have you think my desire to have is the sin of covetousness. But as you say, sir, let your bounty take a nap, I will wake it shortly.

[25] [Exit Clown.]

[26] [Enter Antonio and Officers.]

[27] VIOLA. Here comes the man, sir, who rescued me.

[28] DUKE. That face of his I remember well. Yet when I saw it last, it was smeared as black as Vulcan in the smoke of war. He was captain of a worthless little vessel, of shallow draft and unremarkable bulk — and yet with it he made such a damaging grapple with the most noble ship of our fleet that even envy and the tongue of loss cried fame and honor on him. What’s the matter?

[29] FIRST OFFICER. Orsino, this is that Antonio who took the _Phoenix_ and her cargo from Candy. And this is he who boarded the _Tiger_ when your young nephew Titus lost his leg. Here in the streets, desperate with shame and station, in a private brawl we apprehended him.

[30] VIOLA. He did me kindness, sir — drew his sword on my side. But finally, he addressed me in strange terms. I don’t know what it was, but distraction.

[31] DUKE. Notable pirate, you saltwater thief — what foolish boldness brought you to the mercy of those whom, on such bloody and grievous terms, you have made your enemies?

[32] ANTONIO. Orsino, noble sir, allow me to shake off these names you give me. Antonio was never a thief or pirate, though — I confess — on basis and ground enough, an enemy of Orsino. A witchcraft drew me here: that most ungrateful boy there by your side, from the rough sea’s enraged and foamy mouth I redeemed. He was a wreck past hope. I gave him his life, and added to that my love, without restraint or reservation — all of it dedicated to him. For his sake I exposed myself, purely for his love, to the danger of this hostile town. I drew to defend him when he was beset — where, when I was apprehended, his false cunning (not wanting to share in danger with me) taught him to face me out of acquaintance, and he grew a twenty-years-removed thing in the blink of an eye. He denied me my own purse, which I had given to his use not half an hour before.

[33] VIOLA. How can this be?

[34] DUKE. When did he come to this town?

[35] ANTONIO. Today, my lord. And for three months before, without interruption, without a minute’s gap, day and night we kept company.

[36] [Enter Olivia and Attendants.]

[37] DUKE. Here comes the Countess — now heaven walks on earth. But as for you, fellow, fellow, your words are madness. For three months this youth has attended on me. But more of that later. Take him aside.

[38] OLIVIA. What does my lord want — but what he may not have — wherein Olivia may seem of service? Cesario, you do not keep your promise to me.

[39] VIOLA. Madam?

[40] DUKE. Gracious Olivia —

[41] OLIVIA. What do you say, Cesario? My good lord —

[42] VIOLA. My lord would speak; my duty silences me.

[43] OLIVIA. If it’s anything to the old tune, my lord, it is as fat and cloying to my ear as howling after music.

[44] DUKE. Still so cruel?

[45] OLIVIA. Still so constant, lord.

[46] DUKE. What — to perverseness? You uncivil lady, to whose ungrateful and inauspicious altars my soul has breathed out the most faithful offerings that ever devotion presented! What shall I do?

[47] OLIVIA. Whatever it pleases my lord that shall become him.

[48] DUKE. Why should I not — had I the heart to do it — like the Egyptian thief at the point of death, kill what I love? — a savage jealousy that sometimes savors of nobility. But hear me this: since you cast my faith into utter disregard, and I partly know the instrument that screws me out of my true place in your favor, you live the marble-breasted tyrant still. But this favorite of yours, whom I know you love, and whom by heaven I swear I tenderly cherish, him I will tear out of that cruel eye where he sits crowned in his master’s spite. — Come, boy, with me. My thoughts are ripe in mischief. I’ll sacrifice the lamb that I love, to spite a raven’s heart within a dove.

[49] VIOLA. And I, most jocund, ready, and willing, to give you peace, would die a thousand deaths.

[50] OLIVIA. Where goes Cesario?

[51] VIOLA. After him I love more than I love these eyes, more than my life — more, by all mores, than I shall ever love a wife. If I am pretending, you witnesses above, punish my life for tainting my love.

[52] OLIVIA. Ah me, detested! How am I beguiled!

[53] VIOLA. Who beguiles you? Who does you wrong?

[54] OLIVIA. Have you forgotten yourself? Is it so long ago? Call forth the holy father.

[55] [Exit an Attendant.]

[56] DUKE.

[57] [To Viola.] Come, away!

[58] OLIVIA. Where, my lord? Cesario, husband, stay.

[59] DUKE. Husband?

[60] OLIVIA. Yes, husband. Can he deny that?

[61] DUKE. Her husband, sirrah?

[62] VIOLA. No, my lord, not I.

[63] OLIVIA. Alas, it is the baseness of your fear that makes you choke down what is rightfully yours. Don’t be afraid, Cesario — claim your fortunes. Be what you know you are, and then you are as great as what you fear.

[64] [Enter Priest.]

[65] Oh, welcome, father! Father, I charge you, by your reverence, here to reveal — though lately we intended to keep in darkness what occasion now reveals before it’s ripe — what you know has newly passed between this youth and me.

[66] PRIEST. A contract of eternal bond of love, confirmed by the mutual joining of your hands, attested by the holy meeting of lips, strengthened by the exchange of your rings. And all the ceremony of this compact sealed in my office, by my testimony. Since when, by my watch, I have travelled but two hours toward my grave.

[67] DUKE. Oh you dissembling cub! What will you be when time has sown a gray on your hide? Or will not your craft so quickly grow that your own trip will be your overthrow? Farewell, and take her — but direct your feet where you and I henceforth may never meet.

[68] VIOLA. My lord, I protest —

[69] OLIVIA. Oh, do not swear. Keep a little faith, though you have too much fear.

[70] [Enter Sir Andrew.]

[71] SIR ANDREW. For the love of God, a surgeon! Send one right away to Sir Toby.

[72] OLIVIA. What’s the matter?

[73] SIR ANDREW. He has broken my head across, and has given Sir Toby a bloody coxcomb too. For the love of God, your help! I’d rather be home, even if it cost me forty pounds.

[74] OLIVIA. Who has done this, Sir Andrew?

[75] SIR ANDREW. The Count’s gentleman, one Cesario. We took him for a coward, but he’s the very devil incarnate.

[76] DUKE. My gentleman, Cesario?

[77] SIR ANDREW. By God’s little lives, here he is! — You broke my head for nothing. And what I did, I was set on to do by Sir Toby.

[78] VIOLA. Why do you speak to me? I never hurt you. You drew your sword on me without cause — but I spoke to you fairly, and did not hurt you.

[79] [Enter Sir Toby, drunk, led by the Clown.]

[80] SIR ANDREW. If a bloody coxcomb is a hurt, you have hurt me. I think you set nothing by a bloody coxcomb. Here comes Sir Toby limping — you shall hear more. But if he hadn’t been drinking, he would have tickled you in another way than he did.

[81] DUKE. How now, gentleman? How are you?

[82] SIR TOBY. That’s all one — he’s hurt me, and there’s the end of it. Sot, did you see Dick Surgeon, sot?

[83] CLOWN. Oh, he’s drunk, Sir Toby — an hour ago. His eyes were set at eight in the morning.

[84] SIR TOBY. Then he’s a rogue, and a sluggish passy-measures pavane. I hate a drunken rogue.

[85] OLIVIA. Away with him. Who has made this havoc with them?

[86] SIR ANDREW. I’ll help you, Sir Toby — because we’ll be bandaged together.

[87] SIR TOBY. Help me? An ass-head, and a coxcomb, and a knave — a thin-faced knave, a gull?

[88] OLIVIA. Get him to bed, and let his hurt be looked to.

[89] [Exeunt Clown, Fabian, Sir Toby, and Sir Andrew.]

[90] [Enter Sebastian.]

[91] SEBASTIAN. I am sorry, madam, I have hurt your kinsman. But had it been my own brother by blood, I must have done no less, in self-defense. You throw a strange look upon me, and by that I see I have offended you. Pardon me, sweet one, even for the vows we made each other just a little while ago.

[92] DUKE. One face, one voice, one habit, and two persons! A natural perspective glass — that is, and is not!

[93] SEBASTIAN. Antonio, oh my dear Antonio! How the hours have racked and tortured me since I lost you!

[94] ANTONIO. Are you Sebastian?

[95] SEBASTIAN. Do you doubt it, Antonio?

[96] ANTONIO. How have you made division of yourself? An apple split in two is not more identical than these two creatures. Which is Sebastian?

[97] OLIVIA. Most wonderful!

[98] SEBASTIAN. Is that me standing there? I never had a brother. Nor is there in my nature that divinity of being here and everywhere. I had a sister, whom the blind waves and surges have devoured. Of charity, what kin are you to me? What countryman? What name? What parentage?

[99] VIOLA. Of Messaline. Sebastian was my father. Such a Sebastian was my brother too. So he went dressed to his watery tomb. If spirits can take both form and dress, you come to frighten us.

[100] SEBASTIAN. A spirit I am indeed, but I am clothed in that physical dimension which I shared from the womb. Were you a woman, as everything else lines up, I should let my tears fall on your cheek and say, ‘Thrice welcome, drowned Viola.’

[101] VIOLA. My father had a mole upon his brow.

[102] SEBASTIAN. And so had mine.

[103] VIOLA. And he died on the day when Viola, from her birth, had numbered thirteen years.

[104] SEBASTIAN. Oh, that recollection is lively in my soul! He finished his mortal act indeed on the day that made my sister thirteen years.

[105] VIOLA. If nothing prevents our both being happy except this masculine attire I have usurped, do not embrace me till each circumstance of place, time, and fortune all cohere and confirm that I am Viola. To prove which, I’ll bring you to a captain in this town, where my maiden’s clothes are kept. By his gentle help I was preserved to serve this noble count. All the events of my fortune since have been between this lady and this lord.

[106] SEBASTIAN.

[107] [To Olivia.] So it comes about, lady, that you have been mistaken.

[108] But nature drew toward her natural inclination in that. You would have been contracted to a maid — nor are you, by my life, deceived in that. You are betrothed both to a maid and a man.

[109] DUKE. Be not amazed — his blood is right noble. If this is so — and so far the mirror seems true — I shall have a share in this most happy shipwreck.

[110] [To Viola.] Boy, you have said to me a thousand times

[111] you would never love a woman as you love me.

[112] VIOLA. And all those sayings I will swear over again — and all those swearings I will keep as true in soul as that orbed dome above holds the fire that separates day from night.

[113] DUKE. Give me your hand, and let me see you in your woman’s clothes.

[114] VIOLA. The captain who first brought me ashore has my maiden’s garments. He, on some legal action, is now in custody, on the suit of Malvolio, a gentleman and follower of my lady’s.

[115] OLIVIA. He shall release him. Fetch Malvolio here. And yet, alas, now I remember — they say, poor gentleman, he is much distracted.

[116] [Enter Clown, with a letter, and Fabian.]

[117] A most distracting frenzy of my own had clearly banished his from my memory. How is he, sirrah?

[118] CLOWN. Truly, madam, he holds Beelzebub at staff’s length as well as a man in his case can. He has written you a letter here. I should have given it to you this morning — but as a madman’s epistles are no gospels, it doesn’t matter much when they are delivered.

[119] OLIVIA. Open it, and read it.

[120] CLOWN. Look then to be well edified, when the fool delivers the madman. _By the Lord, madam —_

[121] OLIVIA. How now, are you mad?

[122] CLOWN. No, madam, I only read madness. If your ladyship wants it as it ought to be, you must allow the proper voice.

[123] OLIVIA. Please, read in your right wits.

[124] CLOWN. So I do, madonna. But to read his right wits is to read like this. So pay attention, my princess, and listen.

[125] OLIVIA.

[126] [To Fabian.] Read it, you, sirrah.

[127] FABIAN.

[128] [Reads.] _By the Lord, madam, you wrong me, and the world shall know

[129] it. Though you have put me into darkness and given your drunken cousin rule over me, yet I have the use of my senses as well as your ladyship. I have your own letter that induced me to the appearance I put on. I doubt not but I will do myself much right or you much shame by it. Think of me as you please. I leave my duty a little unthought of, and speak out of my injury. The madly-used Malvolio._

[130] OLIVIA. Did he write this?

[131] CLOWN. Yes, madam.

[132] DUKE. This does not savor much of distraction.

[133] OLIVIA. See him released, Fabian. Bring him here.

[134] [Exit Fabian.]

[135] My lord, if it please you — these things being further thought on — to think of me as much a sister as a wife, one day shall crown the alliance of it, if you please, here at my house, and at my own expense.

[136] DUKE. Madam, I am most ready to embrace your offer.

[137] [To Viola.] Your master releases you. And for your service done him,

[138] so much against the mettle of your sex, so far beneath your soft and tender upbringing — and since you called me master for so long — here is my hand. You shall from this time be your master’s mistress.

[139] OLIVIA. A sister? You are she.

[140] [Enter Fabian and Malvolio.]

[141] DUKE. Is this the madman?

[142] OLIVIA. Yes, my lord, this is he. How are you, Malvolio?

[143] MALVOLIO. Madam, you have done me wrong, notorious wrong.

[144] OLIVIA. Have I, Malvolio? No.

[145] MALVOLIO. Lady, you have. Please look at that letter. You cannot now deny it is your hand. Write differently if you can, in script or phrasing, or say it’s not your seal, not your invention. You can say none of this. Well, grant it then, and tell me, in honor’s decency, why you have given me such clear signs of favor — bidden me come smiling and cross-gartered to you, to put on yellow stockings and to frown upon Sir Toby and the lower people. And acting this in obedient hope, why have you allowed me to be imprisoned, kept in a dark house, visited by the priest, and made the most notorious dupe and gull that ever invention played upon? Tell me why?

[146] OLIVIA. Alas, Malvolio, this is not my writing — though I confess it’s much like the handwriting. But beyond question, it’s Maria’s hand. And now I remember, it was she who first told me you were mad. Then you came in smiling, and in such forms which were here prescribed for you in the letter. Please, be content. This trick has most shrewdly been played upon you. But when we know the grounds and authors of it, you shall be both the plaintiff and the judge of your own cause.

[147] FABIAN. Good madam, hear me speak. And let no quarrel, nor any brawl to come, taint the condition of this present hour, which I have marveled at. In hope it shall not, most freely I confess: I and Toby set this device against Malvolio here, on account of some stubborn and uncourteous behaviors we had conceived against him. Maria wrote the letter at Sir Toby’s great insistence, in recompense for which he has married her. How, with sportful malice, it was followed up, may rather prompt laughter than revenge, if the injuries that have passed on both sides be justly weighed.

[148] OLIVIA. Alas, poor fool, how they have tricked you!

[149] CLOWN. Why, ‘some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them.’ I was one, sir, in this interlude — one Sir Topas, sir — but that’s all one. ‘By the Lord, fool, I am not mad.’ But do you remember? ‘Madam, why do you laugh at such a barren rascal? Unless you smile, he’s gagged.’ And thus the whirligig of time brings in his revenges.

[150] MALVOLIO. I’ll be revenged on the whole pack of you.

[151] [Exit.]

[152] OLIVIA. He has been most notoriously abused.

[153] DUKE. Pursue him, and entreat him to make peace. He has not told us about the captain yet. When that is known, and golden time permits, a solemn union shall be made of our dear souls. — Meantime, sweet sister, we will not depart from here. — Cesario, come, for so you shall be while you are a man. But when in other clothes you are seen, Orsino’s mistress, and his fancy’s queen.

[154] [Exeunt.]

[155] Clown sings.

[156] _When that I was and a little tiny boy, with hey, ho, the wind and the rain, a foolish thing was but a toy, for the rain it raineth every day._

[157] _But when I came to man’s estate, with hey, ho, the wind and the rain, against knaves and thieves men shut their gate, for the rain it raineth every day._

[158] _But when I came, alas, to wive, with hey, ho, the wind and the rain, by swaggering could I never thrive, for the rain it raineth every day._

[159] _But when I came unto my beds, with hey, ho, the wind and the rain, with toss-pots still had drunken heads, for the rain it raineth every day._

[160] _A great while ago the world begun, with hey, ho, the wind and the rain, but that’s all one, our play is done, and we’ll strive to please you every day._

[161] [Exit.]

# Batch D: chapters 24–28 (Historien om Sir Pellias, kapitel 1–5)
## (a) Names and terms decided here (English = Danish)
- Faëry = feernes land (as batch E); fay, fairy lady = fe; of the fairy folk = af feernes (slægt)
- Sir Launcelot of the Lake = Sir Launcelot af Søen; Lady Nymue of the Lake = Lady Nymue af Søen
- collar / necklace (the enchanted jewel) = halssmykke, also "smykket"; a dog's or fawn's collar = halsbånd
- the lady (Lady of the Lake, Lady Ettard, Sir Brandemere's lady) = fruen; ladies in general = damer; old hag = gammel kælling
- quest = færd; adventure = eventyr; champion (a lady's own) = forkæmper; champion (great fighter) = kæmpe
- May blossoms = majblomster; May Court = majhof; holiday clothes = festtøj
- Forest of Usk = Usk-skoven; Arroy = Arroy; Castle of Grantmesnle = borgen Grantmesnle; Knight of the Seven Towers = Ridderen af De Syv Tårne; Knight of the Green Sleeves = Ridderen med De Grønne Ærmer
- Adresack, Brandemere, Ponteferet, Florian, Dinadan, Aglaval, Agravaine, Nymue = unchanged
- Sir / Messire alone = herre; Sirs, Messires = herrer; Sir Knights = herrer riddere; good sirs = gode herrer
- palfrey = ridehest; war-horse = stridshest; trappings = dækken; greyhound = mynde; doe = hind; fawn = hjortekalv
- dungeon = fangetårn (it is "very tall"; batch C has fangehul); keeper of the dungeon = fangetårnets vogter
- device, badge = mærke; on a silver field = på sølvbund; belted knight = ridder med ridderbælte; herald = herold
- sleeping-draught = sovedrik; pallet, cot = briks; casket = skrin; penance = bod; charm = tryllemiddel
- discourteous = uhøvisk; courtesy = høviskhed; bewitched = forhekset; magic = trolddom; silver tongue = sølvtunge
- abbey = kloster; abbot = abbed; black friar = sortebroder; friar of the black order = broder af den sorte orden
- knights-errant = vandrende riddere; sycamore = ahorntræ; goshawk = duehøg; carrion crow = sortkrage
- farthing = kvart penny; pleasure garden = lysthave; ante-room = forværelse; glade of young trees = lund af unge træer
## (b) Paragraphs I am unsure about
- 24:¶32 "two for a farthing" = "to for en kvart penny"; "to for en skilling" would read better but changes the coin.
- 25:¶41 the English lacks the closing quote after Pellias's words to the ladies; the Danish closes it.
- 25:¶49 "was glad to say" (original "was fain to say") = "villigt sagde".
- 26:¶9 the source lists the tents as white, green, scarlet and then calls Brandiles's "red"; kept as written.
- 26:¶16 "set his spear and lance" kept literally (spyd og lanse), odd as it is.
- 27:¶10 "You are an offense to me" = "Du er mig en torn i øjet" (idiom, not literal).
- 27:¶35 the source makes Gawaine son of Urien and Ewaine son of Lot (Pyle's own slip, reversed); kept as written.
- 28:¶20 "another enchanter" = "endnu en troldkvinde", since the one who tricked Merlin was Vivien.

# Batch A (ch. 1–9)
## (a) Names and terms decided here (English = Danish)
- Spelling variants normalised: Camiliard = Cameliard; Uriens = Urien; Ryance = Ryence; Bodwin / Bodwain = Bodwain.
- lesser king = småkonge; overlord, High King = overkonge; overlordship = overherredømmet; lords = stormænd (lords and ladies = herrer og fruer); men-at-arms = krigsmænd.
- magic = trolddom; magic arts = trolddomskunster; magic lake / sword = tryllesøen / tryllesværd; spirit of prophecy = spådomsånden.
- the Trustworthy Knight (Sir Ector) = Den Trofaste Ridder; Sable Champion = Den Sorte Kæmpe; "a Sable Knight" (indefinite) = en sort ridder.
- Seneschal = drost; Constable = marsk; Chamberlain = kammerherre; Counsellor = rådgiver.
- contest of arms = våbendyst; party of knights = flok; barriers = skrankerne; champion = kæmpe; war-horse = stridshest; palfrey = ridehest.
- boon, favor = gunst (»En gunst! En gunst!«); quest = færd, ridderfærd; knight errant = vandrende ridder; belted knight = ridder med ridderbælte.
- worthy men = hædersmænd; fellows / companions of the Round Table = fæller ved Det Runde Bord; "The Ancient and Honorable Companions of the Round Table" = »De Gamle og Hæderværdige Fæller ved Det Runde Bord«.
- Holy Grail = den hellige gral; St. George = Sankt Georg; St. Paul's = Sankt Pauls.
- King Lot of Orkney and the Isles = kong Lot af Orkney og Øerne; King Fion of Scotland = kong Fion af Skotland; Duke Clarence of Northumberland = hertug Clarence af Northumberland (Pyle's spelling here).
- Forest / Battle of Bedegraine = Bedegraine-skoven / slaget ved Bedegraine; Forests of the Usk = Usk-skovene; Arroy = Arroy.
- Sir Launcelot of the Lake = af Søen; Sir Tristram of Lyonesse = af Lyonesse; Sir Percival of Gales = af Gales; Sir Myles of the White Fountain = Sir Myles af Den Hvide Kilde.
- Kept as they are: Balamorgineas, Bedevere, Caradoc, Geharris, Nymue, Aglaval, Lamorack, Dornar, Gerlois, Ban, Bors.
- Sir Page = herr page; my lord King = herre konge; Messires = herrer; "Sir" said to a father = herre; "Ho! ho!" = »Hej! Hej!«
- surgeon = sårlæge; healer = læge; portcullis = faldgitter; brass mallet / hammer = messinghammer; bower = løvhytte; sheath, scabbard = skede; cell = celle; sanctuary = fristed.
- land of Faerie / Faëry = feernes land; the Fay, fairy = feerne, fe; land of enchantment = fortryllet land; without fear and without reproach = uden frygt og uden dadel.
- "Lady" as address: to Guinevere (unmarried) = jomfru; to the Lady of the Lake = frue. The three damsels, also where English says "ladies" = jomfruer.
## (b) Paragraphs I am unsure about
- 1:¶2 "gentle Knight" = blideste ridder, to match Den Blide Ridder; "ædel" was the alternative.
- 2:¶1 Margaise married to Urien and Morgana to Lot; 5:¶8–9 six lords named, then "the seven high lords": both kept as the English has them.
- 5:¶12 English has no closing quote after "where do they come from?"; closed it there in Danish.
- 7:¶19, 8:¶10, 9:¶30 the portcullis is "let down" and a knight rides out (Pyle's slip): kept, faldgitter sænket / faldt.
- 9:¶7 "black as jet" = sorte som gagat; a rare word for a child.
- 9:¶12 "Happily, a fairy … lived there" = Heldigvis; the original's "happily" may mean "perhaps".
- 9:¶40, ¶43 Excalibur is "he/him" in the speech: kept as han/ham there, det elsewhere.
## (c) Verse and inscriptions
- 2:¶11 sword inscription; 7:¶17 and 8:¶8 shield inscription (glossary wording). No other verse.

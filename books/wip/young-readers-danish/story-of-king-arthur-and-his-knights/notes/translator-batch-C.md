# Batch C: chapters 15 to 23
## (a) Names and terms I decided
- Sir Kay the Seneschal = drosten Sir Kay; Sir Caderes the Seneschal of Cornwall = Sir Caderes, drosten af Cornwall (ch03/04 also use "drost")
- Sir Lucan the Bottler = mundskænken Sir Lucan; Sir Ector the trustworthy = den trofaste Sir Ector
- Sir Sagramore, called the Desirous = kaldet den Higende; Sir Dodinas the Savage = den Vilde; Sir Bruin the Black = den Sorte
- of Logres / Ironside / Listinoise / Cardiff / Rouge / Leisle / Benwick = af + same spelling; Sir Baudwain of Britain = af Britannien
- North Umberland (15:¶39) and Northumberland (ch19) kept as the English spells them in each place
- The Ancient and Honorable Companions of the Round Table = De Gamle og Hæderværdige Fæller ved Det Runde Bord (same as ch06); a companion / fellow of the Round Table = fælle af Det Runde Bord
- center seat = midtersæde; covenant = pagt; Holy Court = hellige følge
- cloth of gold = guldvævet stof / klæde; tabard = våbenkjortel; trappings = dækken; litter = bærestol (Guinevere), båre (the wounded)
- Fairies, ship of Fairies, the fay, of the fairy kind = feerne, feernes skib, feernes slægt
- King of Soleyse / of the Vale / of Longtinaise = kongen af Soleyse / af Dalen / af Longtinaise; forest of Tintagalon = skoven Tintagalon
- knight-messenger = ridderen med budskabet; loadstone = magnetsten; "Ogier le Danois" kept
- charm = tryllemiddel; spells and charms = fortryllelser og trylleformler; conjuring = besværgelse; wand = tryllestav; counter-spell = modfortryllelse; magic castle / ship = trolddomsborgen / trolddomsskibet
- Master (Vivien to Merlin) = mester; Child = barn; Sir (between knights, to a king) = herre; Sirs = I herrer; Fellow = gode mand
- West country = landet mod vest (as ch10); Sir Domas, also called le Noir = som også kaldes le Noir
- champion (one who fights for another) = forkæmper (as ch24/26), not "kæmpe"
- Lady Gomyne of the Fair Hair = Lady Gomyne med det fagre hår; the Lady of the Pavilion = Teltets Frue
- hart / stag = hjort; lutes and citterns = lutter og cistre; dungeon = fangehul; priory of nuns = nonnekloster
- palfrey = ridehest (as ch07/13); courser = stridshest; pommel and cross-guard = knap og parerstang; barriers = skranker
## (b) Paragraphs I am unsure about
- 15:¶39 bynames of the knights (den Higende, drosten, mundskænken) may want one editor's eye across the whole book.
- 19:¶4 "the most powerful enchanter" said of Morgana: "den mægtigste troldkyndige", to avoid calling her troldmand.
- 19:¶33 "Now I doubt this" read as old "I fear": "Nu frygter jeg dette".
- 21:¶7 "black as jet" = "sort som beg" (gagat would not be understood).
- 22:¶7–19 Accalon says "Frue" to Lady Gomyne (mistress of the tent) while the narrator calls her "jomfruen", as the English shifts between lady and damsel.
- 22:¶44 "as a Court of Chivalry shall decide" = "sådan som et af ridderskabets hoffer afgør det" (glossary term; it is a tribunal here).
- 23:¶15 "a doddering old man" = "en affældig olding".

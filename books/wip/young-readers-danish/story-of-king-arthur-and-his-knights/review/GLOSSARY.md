# Historien om kong Arthur og hans riddere (The Story of King Arthur and His Knights, Howard Pyle, 1903): names and terms

## Titles and how they are written

- Rank before a name is lower case: kong Arthur, dronning Guinevere, hertug Mordaunt, ærkebiskoppen af Canterbury.
- `Sir` and `Lady` stay as they are, with a capital: Sir Kay, Lady Guinevere, Lady Ettard. "The Lady Ettard" is just "Lady Ettard".
- "the King", "the Queen", "the Court", "the Duke" alone: kongen, dronningen, hoffet, hertugen (lower case).
- "Sir Knight" as address: "herr ridder". "My Lord", "Lord" as address: "herre". "Lady" as address: "frue" (to a married or ruling lady) or "jomfru" (to a maiden). "Messire": "herre".
- Names of people and places keep Pyle's spelling, also where it differs from other books: Gawaine, Ewaine, Launcelot, Morgana le Fay, Uther-Pendragon, Igraine, Margaise, Leodegrance, Ryence, Pellinore, Pellias, Ettard, Vivien, Accalon, Ector, Ulfius, Kay, Griflet, Geraint, Marhaus, Mador de la Porte, Engamore of Malverat → Engamore af Malverat, Parcenet, Boisenard, Brandiles, Brandemere, Domas, Ontzlake.
- "of" in a name becomes "af": kong Lot af Orkney, kong Ryence af Nordwales, kong Urien af Gore (Pyle writes both Urien and Uriens: write Urien), Sir Ector af Bonmaison, Sir Accalon af Gallien, Sir Constantine af Cornwall, Lady Ettard af Grantmesnle. "de", "le", "de la" stay.

## The inscriptions (always these words)

```
Den, der trækker dette sværd af ambolten,
er Englands retmæssigt fødte konge.
```

```
Den, der slår på dette skjold,
gør det på eget ansvar.
```

Where the English says "at the risk of his life": "gør det med livet som indsats."

## Address

Everyone says du to one person and I to several, kings included, with "herre" or "herre konge" for respect. No De, no "Eders".

## Tone

Pyle tells it as an old storyteller would: stately, a little ceremonious, always clear. Keep the stately rhythm with plain Danish words. "Then" at the start of a sentence is often "Så" or "Da", and may be dropped where three in a row would clatter. Italic paragraphs at the head of chapters are the storyteller's summaries: keep the italics and the storyteller's voice.

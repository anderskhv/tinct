## (b) Paragraphs I am unsure about
- 2:29: `marred` left in English because the narrator explains the word; it is not in the glossary.
- 3:4–3:6: the station-master and fru Medlock say du to each other (country tone); De is also defensible.
- 3:10, 3:23, 7:10, 7:29: mile → mil as the guide says, so the distances read very long in Danish (fem mil over heden).
- 4:19: the "broad Yorkshire" line is »Kan du ikke selv komme i kludene!«, which Mary must fail to understand.
- 4:26, 4:30, 5:23, 8:7, 8:24: Martha's remarks about "blacks" are translated plainly, not softened.
- 5:28: "kommer wuthering" is my way of using the word without making a Danish verb of it.
- 7:5, 7:8: »Ikke en døjt« replaces a dialect phrase; the explanation in 7:8 is adapted to fit it.
- 8:47–8:48: "was Magic" = "var Magien" (glossary form); knob = greb, since "knop" reads as a flower bud among the ivy.
## (c) Verse
- 2:6: the rhyme, in the glossary's four lines; quoted as a name in 2:7, 2:15, 4:91.

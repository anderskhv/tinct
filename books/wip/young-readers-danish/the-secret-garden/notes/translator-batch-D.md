# Batch D: chapters 22–27
## (a) Names and terms decided here (English = Danish)
- Aligned with batches A and C: creatures = dyr (»dyr«); animal charmer = dyretæmmer; charm = tryllebinde; lad = knægt; lass = tøs; plain = ukøn; 'Lizabeth Ellen = Elizabeth Ellen; mantel = kaminhylden; ghost = spøgelse; cook = kokkepigen; "Nowt of the sort" = »Ikke en døjt«
- Jem and Bess Fettleworth, Bob Haworth, John (the footman) kept; the Blue Lion = Den Blå Løve; the Blue Cow Inn = kroen Den Blå Ko
- the Doxology = Doksologien; the Good Thing / the Big Good Thing = Det Gode / Det Store Gode; the Joy Maker = Glædens Skaber
- Scientific Discoverer = Videnskabelig Opdager; the Athlete, the Lecturer = Atleten, Foredragsholderen; Mystery and Magic = Mystik og Magi
- a Magic person / the most Magic boy = et Magisk menneske / den mest Magiske dreng (capital kept, as in 15:20)
- Eggs = Æg (capital kept); the robin's mate = rødhalsens mage; speak robin = tale rødhalsesprog; gibberish = volapyk
- play-acting = komediespil / spille komedie; poor thing = stakkel; rude = uhøflig; queer, odd = sær; half-witted = halvtosset
- canopy tree = baldakintræet; alcoves = nicher; trowel = planteske; picture gallery = billedgalleriet; the curtain = forhænget
- exercises = øvelser; champion wrestler = mester i brydning / mesterbryder; the Belt = Bæltet; prize-fighter = bokser
- clotted cream = tyk fløde; currant buns = korendeboller; cottage loaf = landbrød; roasted eggs = bagte æg; oatcakes = havrekager
- mignonette, pinks, pansies = reseda, nelliker, stedmoderblomster; rock-cress = kalkkarse; delphiniums, columbines, campanulas = riddersporer, akelejer, klokkeblomster; gentians = ensianer
- "Aye, aye, sir" (Ben as a sailor) = Javel, hr. kaptajn; "sir" to hr. Craven = hr. Craven; "ma'am" = frue; the Master of Misselthwaite = herren til Misselthwaite
- sovereign = guldsovereign; half a crown = en halv krone; Lake Como = Comosøen; the Austrian Tyrol = det østrigske Tyrol
- "bit of earth" = »lille stykke jord« (as 12:78); "live forever and ever and ever" = leve for evigt og altid og altid (as chapters 20–21)
## (b) Paragraphs I am unsure about
- 27:24–27: the letter opens with » in ¶24, but ¶27 has no closing «, because check.py rejects a paragraph that has « without ». Add « after "Susan Sowerby." if the reader needs it.
- 27:85, 27:95: fru Medlock and Ben say De to each other (not in the glossary); in 27:93 Ben says I to the house servants as a group.
- 23:33–41: "Javel, hr. kaptajn" adds "kaptajn" so the sailor's answer differs from batch C's plain "Javel, unge herre".
- 26:47, 26:52, 26:80: "dear lad" = kære knægt, to match knægt elsewhere; "kære dreng" is the softer choice.
- 26:22–26:66: Doksologien keeps the learned word so that Colin can ask what it is; "Lovsangen" is the plain alternative.
- 22:45: "go away" = "skulle herfra" both times, so the line can mean both leaving and dying, as in English.
- 22:53: Colin's "best Yorkshire" is only lightly country (jo, nok, da), as in batch C.
- 24:48: muffin kept as in 14:125; 25:5: "atrofierede" keeps the hard word the narrator explains.
- 25:26: "great, great, great, great aunts" = tip-tip-tipoldetanter (not a literal count).
- 27:39: half a crown = en halv krone, which a reader could take for Danish money.
## (c) Verse
- 26:33: the Doxology, five lines, 8 syllables per line (iambic, rhymed aabb) so it fits the tune Old Hundredth; first line quoted again in 26:35.
- 27:3: the one-line saying »Hvor du passer en rose …«, kept as prose.

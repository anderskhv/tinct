## The rhyme (chapters 2 and later; always these lines)

```
Jomfru Mary, sur og tvær,
hvordan gror din have her?
Med klokker af sølv og muslingeskal
og morgenfruer i hobetal.
```

## Yorkshire speech

Martha, Dickon, Ben Weatherstaff and fru Sowerby speak broad Yorkshire in the original. In Danish they speak warm, plain, everyday country Danish: short sentences, "jo", "da", "sådan en", "ih", "nå". No invented dialect spelling, no apostrophes for dropped letters, no Jutlandic. "Eh!" becomes "Ih!" or "Nå!", "Aye" becomes "Ja" or "Jo". Where the story says that someone speaks Yorkshire, or that Mary or Colin tries to speak it, keep that ("talte yorkshire", "på bredt yorkshire") and let the line itself sound a little more country than the rest. A chapter title in dialect is translated into plain Danish with a country turn (chapter 18: »Du må ingen tid spilde«).

## Address

- The children say du to each other and to Martha, Dickon, Ben and fru Sowerby.
- Mary and Colin say De to fru Medlock, hr. Craven and doktor Craven. Colin says du to his father once they have met in the garden.
- Martha, Dickon, Ben and fru Sowerby say du to the children. Ben says du to Colin and may call him "unge herre".
- Fru Medlock says du to Mary. Fru Medlock, the nurse, hr. Roach and doktor Craven say De to Colin.
- Servants say De to hr. Craven and to doktor Craven.

## Tone

Burnett's narrator is warm and a little amused. Keep it light and clear. Mary is sour and bossy at first: her lines are short and sharp. Colin gives orders like a small prince.

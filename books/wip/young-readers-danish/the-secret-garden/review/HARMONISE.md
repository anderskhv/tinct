# Den hemmelige have: decisions after translation (these win)

- The Sowerbys' cottage is `hytten` everywhere (chapters 1 to 8 have "det lille hus på heden": change to hytten / hytten på heden).
- Distances: the first time a chapter gives a distance in miles, write "engelske mil"; after that "mil".
- Money: half a crown = "en halv crown" (not krone); sovereign = "en sovereign"; shilling, pence, tuppence = "to pence".
- "the boy officer" (chapter 1) = "den purunge officer".
- Magic with a capital in English = Magien / Magi with a capital in Danish; lower case stays lower case. Never "trolddom" for it.
- Yorkshire words the text explains stay as the translators have them: `wuthering`, `graidely` (or its Danish stand-in "gevaldig" where the modern English has "grand"), `marred`, wick = kvik.
- A speech or letter that runs over several paragraphs: » opens each paragraph and « closes the last one only. Chapter 11 ¶65 and chapter 27 ¶27 lack the closing « (the old checker refused it): add it.
- The robin is `rødhalsen`, "han". Dickon's animals: Sod, Kaptajn, Nød og Skal, Hop.
- Colin's nurse is `sygeplejersken`; a small child's nurse (India, chapters 1 to 4 and 8) is `barnepige`.
- lad = knægt, lass = tøs in Yorkshire mouths; "dear lad" from fru Sowerby may be "kære dreng".
- Address as in the glossary. Fru Medlock and Ben Weatherstaff say De to each other; fru Medlock and the station-master say De.

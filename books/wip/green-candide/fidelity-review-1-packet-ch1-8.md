# Candide — Fidelity Review, Packet 1 (Chapters 1–8, paragraphs 1.0–8.9)

**Book / chapter:** Candide (Voltaire), Chapters 1–8
**Source edition (fidelity anchor):** `source.json` (public-domain English
translation baseline, staged from `candide-original-en.json`)
**Candidate:** `candidate.json` (staged from `candide-modern-en.json`)

## Coverage

Every paragraph in chapters 1–8 (paragraphs 1.0 through 8.9, 87 paragraphs
total) was read individually and in full, source against candidate,
side by side, in the order the chapters present them — which means every
paragraph was read with at least one paragraph of neighboring context on
each side (the preceding and following paragraph in the same continuous
read), satisfying the packeted-with-context method. No paragraph was
skimmed or skipped. This packet also serves as the context boundary for
Packet 2 (chapters 9–16), whose first paragraphs I re-checked against this
packet's last paragraph (8.9/9.0) for continuity.

## Method notes

For each paragraph I checked: actors (who does/says what), negation,
causality, certainty/hedging, conditions, omissions, additions, silent
corrections of names/facts, and unmodernized quotations, per
`books/prompts/fidelity-review-prompt.md`.

## Findings

**No blocking defects found in this packet.** Every claim, actor, negation,
causal link, condition, and quantity in the source survives in the
candidate. Proper nouns (Thunder-ten-Tronckh, Pangloss, Cunegonde, Don
Issachar, the Grand Inquisitor) and specific numbers (seventy-one/seventy-two
quarterings, three hundred and fifty pounds, thirty thousand souls, two
thousand men, etc.) are preserved exactly. No dropped clauses, no invented
detail, no meaning reversal.

### Non-blocking notes

- **[1.0]** Source: "seventy-one quarterings, the rest of his genealogical
  tree having been lost through the injuries of time." Candidate: "seventy-one
  quarterings of nobility, the rest of his family tree having been lost to
  the ravages of time." This adds the clarifying phrase "of nobility" not
  present verbatim in this sentence of the source — but the concept is fully
  licensed by context (quarterings are explicitly a nobility count
  throughout the book, e.g. [10.14]) and no meaning is changed. Not a
  defect; a legitimate accessibility clarification consistent with the
  translation mandate.
- **[3.10]** Source: "poured over him a full...." Candidate: "emptied over
  him a full…." — both preserve the source's own elision (the source text
  itself withholds the object). Verified this is not a candidate-introduced
  omission; the vagueness is original to the anchor text and correctly
  carried over.
- **[4.15]** Source: "there are about twenty thousand of them p-x-d on each
  side" (a typographically censored "poxed"). Candidate: "about twenty
  thousand cases of pox on each side." This de-censors the term into plain
  modern wording. Meaning (venereal disease casualties) is fully preserved;
  the euphemistic censoring device itself is lost, but that is a
  typographic convention of the source edition, not narrative content, and
  modernizing it is consistent with the book's satirical bluntness
  elsewhere. Non-blocking.
- **[8.0], [7.12], [10.14]** (10.14 checked in Packet 2's context window)
  — source's "ravish(ed)" is consistently rendered "rape(d)" in the
  candidate. This is a direct modern-vocabulary equivalent with no change
  in the specific act described or its severity; flagged only as a
  documented translation-policy choice, not a defect.
- **[8.6]** The "old law" / "the new [law]" phrasing (Jewish vs. Christian
  sabbath) is preserved essentially verbatim from source to candidate. This
  is a fidelity pass (nothing changed), though it was separately flagged in
  the accessibility review as potentially unclear to a reader who doesn't
  already know the reference — that's a Reviewer A note, not a fidelity
  defect here.

## Satire/tone check (Candide-specific)

Pangloss's "sufficient reason" logic-chains ([1.3], [1.4], [3.6], [4.10],
[4.20]) and the deadpan escalation of catastrophe in chapters 2–6 (forced
conscription, the earthquake, the auto-da-fé) land with the same flat,
unmoralized delivery as the source. The candidate does not editorialize,
soften, or add sympathetic commentary beyond what the source's own
characters supply (e.g., Candide's own horror is preserved as his reaction,
not narrated as authorial judgment). No instance in this packet where the
comic deadpan was smoothed into sincere-sounding prose.

## Verdict

**ACCEPT AS-IS** for chapters 1–8. No fixes required.

# Book VI — drafting notes

## Glosses added
- VI.24 "seed-principles of the universe, from which all things grow" (Long: "seminal principles"). The added words are "from which all things grow".
- VI.43 "Aesculapius, the god of healing" is added so the contrast with the Fruit-bearer, the earth, makes sense.
- VI.32 "indifferent—neither good nor bad" is glossed where the technical term first carries the argument. It is kept as "indifferent" because Marcus plays on the body being unable to perceive differences.
- Small additions to names: VI.13 "Falernian wine" (adds "wine"); VI.36 "Mount Athos".
- "sophist" (VI.30) is not glossed because Book I.7 already explains it.

## Source ambiguities / textual points
- VI.5 Long has "The reason which governs". It is not clear whether this means the reason that governs the universe or a person's ruling faculty. I rendered it neutrally as "The governing reason". Other translations read it as universal reason.
- VI.16 Long has "Wilt thou not cease to value many other things too? Then thou wilt be neither free…". I rendered this as "Will you not stop valuing…? If not, you will be neither free…" to make the implied condition explicit. The alternative is to keep a bare "Then", but that misleads listeners.
- VI.27 Long has "But it is not so." I kept it as the narrator's plain statement ("But that is not really so") and did not assign it to an objector.
- VI.32 Long has "And of these however only those which are done with reference to the present". This is elliptical: it could mean only these are in its power, or only these are not indifferent. I rendered it "only the ones that concern the present count", which covers both readings.
- VI.41 The source text reads "thou wilt **not** blame the gods". That contradicts the argument (the next clause says "and hate men too", and the conclusion is that the right judgment leaves "no reason for finding fault with God"). The baseline also drops "not". I followed the sense: "you will blame the gods". This is probably a transcription error in the source. Please check against a print copy of Long.
- VI.41 Long's bracket "[because we do not regard these things as indifferent]" is absorbed as "we treat these things as though they made a difference, instead of seeing them as neither good nor bad".
- VI.50 Long has "if the things to which thou wast moved are [not] accomplished". His bracketed "not" means the aim is achieved even if the things are not accomplished. I rendered it "even if the things you set out to do are not accomplished". The baseline's "if … are not accomplished" misleads.
- VI.49 Long's "litrae" is rendered "pounds" (a Roman pound). "amount of substance" is rendered "amount of stuff you have been given to be made of", following the standard's rule against bare "substance" for the body.

## Sections close to the baseline
- VI.1, 3, 4, 17, 37, 51, 52, 54, 55, 58, 59 are short and already clear. I changed only minimal wording or split sentences, because a stronger recast would have drifted from Long.

## Hard passages
- VI.14 is the scale of things admired: cohesion or natural growth, then "a living principle" (rendered "a principle of life"), then a rational soul. I kept all qualifications of "rational" (skilled in a craft, expert in another way, owning slaves).
- VI.15: "exhalation of the blood" (rendered "the vapor given off by the blood") and the breath-return image.
- VI.38: "active movement and mutual conspiration". I rendered this as "the active movement of things, the way they all breathe together as one", which keeps the literal image of conspiration.
- VI.42: the Chrysippus "mean and ridiculous verse" is kept as "the cheap and ridiculous line in the play" and is not explained.

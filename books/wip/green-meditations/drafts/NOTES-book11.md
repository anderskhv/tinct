# Book 11 — drafting notes

## Glosses added
- 11.2 — "pancratium—the all-in fighting contest" (a newcomer cannot picture the third example otherwise).
- 11.10 / 11.16 — "indifferent" (ἀδιάφορα) explained once at 11.16: "things that are indifferent—that is, to things that are neither good nor bad". At 11.10 Long's "middle things [things indifferent]" is rendered "the in-between things that are neither good nor bad" (standard rendering, no extra gloss).
- 11.18 — "Apollo, the leader of the Muses": Long's own bracketed supplement absorbed, not a new gloss.
- 11.23 — "Lamiae—bogeys to frighten children": Long's own explanation, kept.
- No Book II glosses repeated (ruling faculty, providence, divine spirit within, opinion, oldest city).

## Source ambiguities / apparatus decisions
- 11.3 — "not from mere obstinacy, as with the Christians": widely suspected to be a later gloss in the Greek. Followed Long and kept it.
- 11.13 — "unless indeed he only assumed it": unclear whether the pretence is Phocion's endurance or his gentleness. Rendered "unless he was only pretending" (keeps Long's vagueness). "For the interior [parts] ought to be such" → "For that is how one ought to be within".
- 11.15 — "The voice ought to be plainly written on the forehead": Long compresses the Greek (it is written on the forehead; the voice rings out so). Rendered "Your very voice ought to declare it, as plainly as if it were written on your forehead." Alternative: "It ought to be written plainly on your forehead." "the affectation of simplicity is like a crooked stick": the Greek image is disputed (a hidden knife?); followed Long.
- 11.18 (Fifth) — "done with a certain reference to circumstances" (Greek κατ' οἰκονομίαν, "by design / as a matter of management"). Rendered "done with some circumstance in view". Alternative: "done for a purpose you cannot see".
- 11.18 (Ninth) — Long marks a gap: "but either when he is alone, and if others are present..." Kept the break as " . . . " with no completion.
- 11.18 — "begin at last to be a man while thou livest": rendered "a human being" (generic ἄνθρωπος), while "manly" (ἀνδρικόν) is kept later in the section because Marcus is contrasting manliness with passion.
- 11.25 — Long names Perdiccas (the story is usually told of Archelaus). Followed Long.
- 11.26 — Long brackets "[Ephesians]" (some editors read "Epicureans"). Followed Long, bracket absorbed.
- 11.31, 11.32 — Long's locators ("Odyssey, ix. 413"; "HESIOD, Works and Days, 184") turned into attributions in words: "As Homer says in the Odyssey"; "As Hesiod says in the Works and Days". Line numbers dropped.
- 11.33, 11.35, 11.36 — Long's parenthetical "(Epictetus, iii. 24 …)" is both a locator and the only attribution. Kept the attribution in words ("So Epictetus says" / "as Epictetus says") and dropped the book and chapter numbers. The live baseline dropped these attributions altogether.
- Cross-references at 11.10, 11.12, 11.18 and 11.19 were omitted, as the standard requires.

## Sections kept close to the baseline
- 11.4, 11.7, 11.14, 11.17, 11.22, 11.24, 11.28, 11.29, 11.30, 11.38: already short and clear. I changed only wording, such as "men" to "people", and word order.

## Hard passages
- 11.1 — the long list of the rational soul's marks. I split it into short sentences, and every clause is kept, including the plants/animals aside and the "forty years old" claim.
- 11.6 — the history of tragedy and comedy. The three dramatic quotations are lightly modernised but kept as quotations. "O Cithaeron" is left without a gloss (the thought follows without it).
- 11.18 — the longest section. All nine rules and the tenth gift are kept in order. The "unsocial" in "both are unsocial" is rendered "both set us against others".
- 11.19 — "superior faculty" is rendered with the standard term "ruling faculty".
- 11.20 — the elemental physics (air and fire tending upward, earth and water downward). Rendered literally.

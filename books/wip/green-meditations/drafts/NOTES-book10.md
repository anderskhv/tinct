# Book X — drafting notes

## Glosses added (first technical use in this book)
- **10.1** "the perfect living being, **the universe**". Long gives no gloss. Without it a newcomer cannot tell the "perfect living being" is the cosmos (the Stoic view of the universe as a living being).
- **10.2** "governed by nature alone, **as a plant is**". Long's "governed by nature only" means the lowest, plant-like level of life (below "living being", which in turn is below "rational being"). Without the gloss the three-step argument can't be followed.
- **10.2** "a political being, **made for community**". This absorbs Long's "[social]" and uses the standard rendering.

## Source ambiguities / choices
- **10.6** "[a concourse of] atoms, or nature [is a system]". Absorbed as "a collection of atoms, or nature is a system".
- **10.7** "whether this at certain periods is consumed by fire". Long's "this" could refer to "the universal reason" or the universe. I took it as the universe ("whether the universe is consumed by fire at fixed periods"), which is the Stoic periodic conflagration. The alternative is "whether it (the reason of the universe)…".
- **10.7** Last sentence ("suppose that this [which thy mother brought forth] implicates thee very much with that other part, which has the peculiar quality [of change]"). This is obscure in Long and in the Greek. I followed Long's bracketed reading: "suppose that what your mother gave birth to binds you very closely to that other part, the one with its own quality of change: even so, this is no objection". The alternative takes "peculiar quality" as the individual's distinctive character rather than change.
- **10.9** "Mimi". Rendered "Farce". Long's word transliterates Greek *mimos* (mime, stage farce, play-acting). Some read it as "buffoonery / make-believe". "Astonishment" is rendered "panic" and "torpor" as "sluggishness".
- **10.13** "a good daemon [happiness]". Rendered "a good divine spirit within, which is to say, happiness". This keeps the standard term and absorbs Long's bracket. It is the *eudaimonia* wordplay.
- **10.15** "Let me see, let them know a real man". Long reads "Let me see". This is almost certainly a misprint for "Let men see" (the Greek is "let people see"). The baseline already reads "men". I rendered it "Let people see". "state [political community]" is absorbed as "as in a state, one political community".
- **10.21** "loves [is wont] to be produced". This is a pun on Greek *philei* (loves / tends to). Rendered: this or that "loves" to happen, that is, usually happens. The poet's lines have no attribution in Long, so none is given. "shower" → "rain", "solemn ether" → "solemn sky".
- **10.23** Long's translator's note "[The three last words are omitted in the translation.]" is dropped as apparatus. The omitted Greek words are "and milking the flock". I did not restore them, following Long.
- **10.33** "law [order]". Absorbed as "law, that is, order". "state" is kept as Long has it (not "city").
- **10.34** "as the poet says" is kept. Long does not name Homer.
- Cross-references dropped: 10.5 (iii. 11; iv. 26), 10.13 (vi. 32; viii. 55) and (vii. 17), 10.26 (vii. 85).

## Kept close to the baseline
- **10.4, 10.14, 10.20, 10.22, 10.35, 10.37** were already clear. I changed them only lightly (for example "a man" → "someone / a person", minor phrasing).
- **10.31**: the proper names (Satyron, Eutyches, Hymen, Euphrates, Eutychion, Silvanus, Alciphron, Tropaeophorus, Xenophon, Crito, Severus) are kept exactly as Long spells them, with no glosses. **10.27** keeps "Hadrianus", "Philippus", Croesus. **10.10** keeps "Sarmatians" with no gloss.

## Terminology notes
- **10.8**: Long's "Equanimity" and "Magnanimity" are rendered "equanimity" and "greatness of soul". The same rendering is used in 10.11 ("nothing is so well suited to produce greatness of soul").
- **10.2** "rational animal" → "rational being"; "universal nature" / "nature of the whole" → "the nature of the universe" (10.6, 10.11, 10.20); "universal reason" → "the reason that governs the universe" (10.7); "unsocial" → "sets me against others" (10.6); "ruling faculty" (10.24); "opinions" → "judgments" (10.10); "understanding" → "mind" (10.35).

## Hard passages
- **10.7**: the physics of change and the final sentence (see above).
- **10.33**: the long period on reason passing through obstacles. I broke it into many sentences and kept every member: cylinder, water, fire, the soul without reason; fire, stone, cylinder; the body as a dead thing; "except through opinion and through reason itself giving way"; citizen, state and law.
- **10.21**: the *philei* pun cannot be carried fully in English. I kept Long's approach.

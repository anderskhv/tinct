# Notes — Book V modern-en draft

## Glosses added
- **5.13** "form and matter" + "—what shapes me and what I am made of" (first technical use of the form/matter pair).
- **5.20** "act with a reservation" + "(on condition that nothing prevents it)" — Stoic *reservation* (Long: "acting conditionally").
- **5.36** "the Rostra" + ", the speakers' platform" — a name a newcomer cannot picture otherwise. Drop it if proper-name glosses are unwanted.
- No Book II glosses repeated. "Indifferent" is always rendered "neither good nor bad" (5.20, 5.36), never glossed.

## Terminology choices worth checking
- 5.3 "peculiar leading principle… peculiar movement" → "their own ruling faculty… their own impulses".
- 5.10 "my god and daemon" → "my god and the divine spirit within me" (kept as two items, as Long has them).
- 5.20 "affects and disposition" → "impulses and disposition" (Greek ὁρμή, which the standard renders as *impulse*).
- 5.26 "part of thy soul which leads and governs" → "the ruling faculty of your soul"; "that other sympathy" → "that other kind of shared feeling".
- 5.36 "good emotions" → "good impulses" (same reason as 5.20). Revert to "good emotions" if Long is to be followed to the word.
- 5.12 "prudence" → "practical wisdom"; "comic writer" → "comic poet".
- 5.35 "badness" → "wickedness".

## Source ambiguities / apparatus
- **5.8** Long's brackets: "[or suits]" dropped (the rest of the sentence carries "fits"); "fitness [harmony]" → "one fitting together, one harmony"; "necessity [destiny]" → "necessity, or destiny"; "It [necessity, destiny]" → "Destiny brought this on him"; "Zeus [the universe]" → "Zeus, that is, of the universe". I absorbed Long's supplement here because the passage is otherwise unclear. The other option is to drop it and leave "Zeus" alone.
- **5.13 / 5.32** "definite periods [of revolution]", "fixed periods [revolutions]" → "fixed cycles". I kept this neutral and did not explain the Stoic cosmic cycle.
- **5.14** "the reasoning art [philosophy]" → "the art of reasoning". The bracket is left out because the sense does not need it. It could be added back as "that is, philosophy". "Catorthoseis" is kept with Long's own explanation.
- **5.28** "[Neither tragic actor nor whore.]": Long brackets it as a detached fragment of uncertain relevance. I kept it as a bare fragment at the end: "Neither a tragic actor nor a prostitute." I did not explain it.
- **5.29** Long marks a gap with "…" after "when thou art gone out". I kept the ellipsis at the same point: "The way you intend to live when you have left this life … you can live that way here."
- **5.33** The Hesiod quotation is kept, attributed in words ("as Hesiod says"). The locator "Works, etc. v. 197" is dropped.
- **5.36** "the old man… asked back his foster-child's top": Long gives no source. I added no context such as "in the play", so the allusion stays as obscure as it is in Long.
- **5.15** "these things" has no clear antecedent in Long (external things in general). Following the standard, I kept it general.
- **5.28** "I wish thee well of thy discovery" is ironic. It is rendered "Congratulations on your discovery!" and set off as Marcus's reply to the imagined objection.

## Sections close to the baseline
None kept unchanged. 5.2, 5.17, 5.19, 5.21, 5.25 and 5.35 were already short and clear, so they changed only lightly.

## Hard passages
- 5.6: the dialogue has an unclear speaker for each turn, and the logic of "you will become one of those I spoke of first" is hard to follow. I followed Long's turn-taking exactly.
- 5.8: a long argument built on the double sense of "prescribed/fits". I broke it into short sentences and dropped no steps.
- 5.12: Menander's joke is compressed. The punchline is rendered plainly: "so rich he has no room left to relieve himself".
- 5.20: the idea of acting "with reservation" and turning obstacles into help.

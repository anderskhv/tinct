# Notes — Book VII modern-en draft

75 paragraphs; every paragraph keeps the source's "N. " prefix (checked with python3). Long's cross-references ((iii. 5), (v. 23; vi. 15), (viii. 50), (xii. 23), (viii. 29), (vii. 4), (vi. 44; ix. 28)) are left out. His bracketed supplements are absorbed without brackets.

## Glosses added
- **7.17 (§17)**: "Happiness—eudaemonia—means a good divine spirit, or a good thing." The Greek word is kept because Marcus's point is a pun on it (eu-daimon = good daemon). "daemon" is rendered "divine spirit" as the standard requires, with no further explanation.
- **7.10 (§10)**: Long's "everything formal [causal]" becomes "everything that is form and cause". In **7.29 (§29)**, "the causal [formal] and the material" becomes "what is its cause—its form—and what is its matter". This is the first technical use of the Stoic cause/matter division, and those few words are the only gloss.
- **7.13 (§13)**: melos/meros are kept in brackets, as Long has them, and "change one letter to r" is kept. Otherwise the pun cannot be followed. "member" becomes "limb", so the body image stays clear in modern English.
- No Book II gloss is repeated. The ruling faculty, divine spirit and so on appear as short terms only.

## Source ambiguities / corruptions (Long's reading followed)
- **§4**: In "in the one … to what end it refers, but in the other … the thing signified", it is unclear which of speech and action is "the one". In the Greek, the end belongs to actions and the meaning to words. I kept Long's "In the one … in the other" order without resolving it. The alternative is to state it outright: "in actions see what end they aim at; in words, what is meant".
- **§17**: "imagination" (phantasia) is kept here because Marcus speaks to it as if it were a person. In **§29**, "Wipe out the imagination" becomes "Wipe out the impression", which follows the standard (φαντασία → impression). In **§64**, "add nothing to it in imagination" is kept as "in your imagination".
- **§31**: Long's "The poet says that law rules all—" marks a gap or corruption in the text. I kept the dash: "law rules all—and it is enough to remember that law rules all." There is no attribution because Long gives none.
- **§38–42, §50, §51**: These are verse fragments that Long does not attribute (Euripides and others), so I added no attribution. Each is made into plain sentences, and the line breaks Long's layout implies are dropped. In §51 I kept Long's participle fragment ("With food and drink and cunning magic, turning the stream's course aside to escape from death."). I did not supply a subject, although the result is a sentence fragment. In §50, "This is either…" keeps Long's vague "This". I did not write "death".
- **§46**: Long's dash in "consider if this is not—a thing to be dismissed" shows a doubtful passage. I rendered it smoothly as "consider whether this is not something to put out of his thoughts".
- **§58**: Long ends with "and remember..." because the text is cut off. The ellipsis is kept ("And remember…"). I did not supply the Greek continuation.
- **§66**: The swaggering-in-the-streets doubt is kept as Long has it. Long's "we ought to inquire … if he was able …, neither … nor …" becomes a series of direct questions. Every member of the list is kept.
- **§75**: Long's "comes by way of consequence or [continuity]" becomes "follows as a consequence of that, in an unbroken chain". The either/or is kept intact: either everything follows by consequence, or even the chief things have no rational principle. "directs its own movement" becomes "impulse" (ὁρμή), which follows the standard.

## Other rendering choices worth a reviewer's eye
- **§5, §28, §55, §62**: Long's "ruling principle(s)" becomes "ruling faculty/faculties". In **§28**, "the rational principle which rules" becomes "your rational ruling faculty". In **§64**, "governing intelligence" becomes "the mind that governs you". I kept this close to Long rather than forcing "ruling faculty".
- **§55**: "motion" (κίνησις, not ὁρμή) becomes "activity/stirrings". This avoids "movement", which the standard reserves against, and avoids confusion with impulse.
- **§18**: "the wood that heats it" adds three words so the bath/firewood point can be followed. Otherwise it is a close rendering.
- **§24**: Long's unclear "it is contrary to reason" is resolved to "scowling", which is the clear antecedent.
- **§52, §67, §72**: "social" becomes "devoted to the common good" or "made for community", because the modern word "sociable" gives the wrong sense.
- **§35, §44–46**: The Plato quotations are set in quotation marks. In §46, "one who is truly a man" keeps "man" (manliness/courage in context). §44 uses "someone"/"person" for Plato's generic man.
- The poetry and quotation sections (§38–43) stay close to both Long and the baseline, since they are already short and clear. The only changes are archaisms ("nought", "corn" → "grain") and making the subject or grammar explicit.

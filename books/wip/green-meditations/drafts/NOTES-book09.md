# Book IX — drafting notes

## Glosses added
None. "Ruling faculty", "impulse", "impression", "providence" and "divine" are used without gloss (Book II glosses cover them). No new Stoic term needed a gloss.

## Source ambiguities / choices
- **9.1** (last sentence) — Long: "And I say that the universal nature employs them equally, instead of saying that they happen alike to those who are produced in continuous series and to those who come after them by virtue of a certain original movement of Providence…". Long's "instead of saying" is taken as "by this I mean": "When I say… I mean that they happen alike to the things that come into being one after another in a continuous chain, and to the things that come after them." The Greek is notoriously obscure; kept Long's structure, split into four sentences. "Movement" (ὁρμή) rendered "impulse" per the standard. Alternative: keep "instead of saying" literally.
- **9.1** "equally affected" → "equally disposed" throughout.
- **9.7** — Long: "Wipe out imagination; check desire: extinguish appetite". "Imagination" (φαντασία) → "the impression" per the standard. Long's "desire" (the Greek is ὁρμή, which the standard renders "impulse") is kept as "desire" to follow Long; "appetite" kept. Alternative: "Check impulse."
- **9.24** — Long: "poor spirits carrying about dead bodies [such is everything]; and so what is exhibited in the representation of the mansions of the dead strikes our eyes more clearly." The last clause is obscure (an allusion to the underworld scene, the Nekyia). Rendered "the picture of the dwellings of the dead strikes our eyes more clearly", with no explanation added. The "poor spirits carrying corpses" saying is Epictetus's, but Long gives no attribution, so none is added.
- **9.25** — "the quality of the form" (the Greek is closer to "the cause") → "what kind of form a thing has", following Long.
- **9.28** — "or it puts itself in motion once, and everything else comes by way of sequence in a manner" → "follows from that, in a way, as a consequence". Kept the three-way either/or and "if there is a god" / "if chance rules" as conditionals.
- **9.34** — Source typo "pool souls" read as "poor souls".
- **9.37** — "Towards the gods then, now become at last more simple and better." Rendered "So toward the gods, too, become simpler and better now, at last." The "too" is an addition, weakly supported ("toward the gods as well" is a common reading of the Greek). If you want to follow Long strictly, drop "too".
- **9.41** — The principle "of all schools" is taken to cover all three clauses (never desert philosophy / no trifling talk / be intent on what you are doing and the instrument). Long's "but to be intent…" continues the same sentence. "One unacquainted with nature" → "someone who knows nothing of nature".
- **9.42** — "whether thou didst trust… or when conferring…" is rendered as an either/or: the fault is yours in either case.

## Sections kept close to the baseline
- 9.4, 9.5, 9.17 and 9.38 are short and the baseline was already plain. I only adjusted pronouns ("whoever", "someone") and word order.

## Hard passages
- 9.1 (last sentence, see above), 9.24, and 9.9 (the elemental-fire sentence: "all the fire here below" is my reading of "all the fire which is here").

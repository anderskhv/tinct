# Book III — drafting notes

## Glosses added
- **3.11** "things that are indifferent—neither good nor bad in themselves". First technical use of "indifferent" (ἀδιάφορα); the standard says to use "indifferent" once, explained.
- No other glosses. Ruling faculty, divine spirit within, providence, opinion and the highest city are used without their Book II glosses.

## Terminology choices worth checking
- **3.6, 3.16**: Long's "appetites" rendered as **impulses**. Both translate ὁρμαί (III.6 τὰς ἰδίας ὁρμάς; III.16 ψυχῆς ὁρμαί), and the standard's rendering of ὁρμή is "impulse". If reviewers want Long's word kept, use "desires" in 3.6 and "appetites" in 3.16.
- **3.1**: Long's "appetite", in the list of faculties that outlast dotage, is kept as "appetite" because it sits beside bodily functions (perspiring, nourishment). The Greek there may also be the ὁρμ- root. Reviewers may want "impulse" for consistency.
- **3.4, 3.6, 3.16**: "[deity] which is planted within him", "the Deity which is planted in thee" and "the divinity which is planted in his breast" all rendered as **the divine spirit** (planted within him / in you / in his breast). This follows the standard.
- **3.6**: temperance and fortitude → "self-control and courage". **3.11**: manliness and fidelity → "manly courage" and "faithfulness". This keeps 3.11's manliness distinct from 3.6's fortitude.
- **3.3**: "Chaldaei" → "the Chaldaeans" (standard English form, as in the baseline). **3.14**: "Hellenes" → "Greeks". Both depart slightly from "keep Long's spelling" to help a newcomer. "Pompeius" and "Caius Caesar" are kept as Long spells them.
- **3.3**: "the vessel" → "the vessel, the body". Long's vessel is plainly the body that the mind serves. Without the two added words, the "as much inferior as that which serves it is superior" clause is opaque.
- **3.7**: "needs walls and curtains" → "needs walls and curtains to hide it". The added words make explicit the sense of hiding that the image already carries.

## Source ambiguities
- **3.6**: Long's "rationally and politically [or, practically] good" is rendered "good for a rational being and a member of the community", following Long's primary reading "politically". The alternative "practically" would give "good in reason and in action".
- **3.6**: Long's "adapt themselves [to the better things] in a small degree" is rendered "fit in with the better things to a small degree", absorbing his supplement.
- **3.7**: Long's "without either pursuing or flying from [death]". The Greek has no expressed object, and some translators understand "life" or "solitude/company". This draft follows Long: "without either chasing death or running from it".
- **3.8**: Long's supplements "too closely bound [to other things], nor yet detached [from other things]" are absorbed.
- **3.3**: "other lice killed Socrates" is kept literal, with no explanation. Long (and the tradition) take it to mean bad men.
- **3.15**: "this is not effected by the eyes" — "this" is taken as the last item, "seeing what ought to be done", and possibly the whole list. It is kept as a general "this".

## Baseline kept
- No section was kept unchanged. Several short sentences stay close to the baseline because it was already clear:
  - 3.9: structure only
  - 3.10: most of the sentence order
  - 3.14: the list of books

## Hard passages
- **3.2**: "things which follow after the things which are produced according to nature" is rendered as "side effects of what nature produces". The long period is split into several sentences, and every image is kept (bread, figs, olives, ears of grain, lion's brow, boar's foam, beasts' jaws, the old, the young).
- **3.15**: cryptic in the Greek itself. It is kept as short and literal as Long.
- **3.16**: "To receive the impressions of forms by means of appearances" is rendered "to receive the stamp of impressions from the way things appear". It is a technical Stoic phrase, and the draft keeps it only lightly clarified.

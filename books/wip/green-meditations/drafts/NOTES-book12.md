# Book XII draft notes

## Glosses added
- 12.9: "the all-in fighter, the pancratiast" (a few words so the contrast with the gladiator is followable; not a Stoic term).
- No Stoic-term glosses added. Book II glosses (ruling faculty, providence, divine spirit within, opinion) not repeated.

## Source ambiguities / apparatus
- 12.2: Long "minds [ruling principles]" → "ruling faculties" (his bracket taken as the technical term).
- 12.3: Long's "own daemon [to the god that is within thee]" → "the divine spirit within you". Empedocles verse kept as a quotation, lightly modernised: "round on every side, resting in its joyful rest" (Long: "All round and in its joyous rest reposing"). Long's cross-reference dropped.
- 12.8: "formative principles [forms]" → "the forms of things".
- 12.14: "a providence which allows itself to be propitiated" → "a providence that can be won over". Alternative: "that can be moved by prayer" (rejected as adding content).
- 12.16: "this is like tearing his own face" kept as Long reads it (self-condemnation compared to tearing one's own face). "If then thou art irritable, cure this man's disposition" kept literally ("irritable"); the Greek is ironic (δριμύς, "sharp") but Long's reading followed.
- 12.17: Long's bracketed "[For let thy efforts be—]" is a broken-off fragment; kept as "For let your effort be—".
- 12.23: last sentence ("he is moved by the Deity who is moved in the same manner with the Deity...") is compressed and obscure; rendered "this, too, is how a person is moved by God: when he moves the same way God moves, and in his own mind moves toward the same ends." "the peculiar nature of man" → "a person's own nature".
- 12.27: source.json reads "Stertinius at Briae" — a transcription error for Long's "Baiae"; rendered Baiae (as the live baseline). Long's alternative "Velius Rufus [or Rufus at Velia]" — kept "Velius Rufus" only; the alternative is editorial apparatus. "Capreae" kept as Long spells it.
- 12.30: "individual circumscriptions [or individuals]" → "separate individuals". "the gravitation towards the same" → "the weight that draws them toward the same place" (the physical tendency of heavy things).
- 12.31: Long's "movement" here renders ὁρμᾶν; per the standard's impulse rule → "to have impulses".
- 12.36: "[the world]" and "[or three]" absorbed. "praetor" kept (context makes the role clear; no historical gloss).
- Cross-references dropped at 12.3, 12.12, 12.14, 12.19, 12.21, 12.35.

## Baseline kept (largely unchanged)
- 12.10 and 12.11: baseline/Long already plain; only small adjustments ("a human being", "gives him").
- 12.17: first two sentences essentially Long's own plain wording.

## Hard passages
- 12.3: one enormous conditional period; split into several "Suppose…" sentences, then Long's "If, I say, …" resumption kept so the whole stays conditional, leading to the "then" clause.
- 12.5: argument with nested "if" clauses ("if in fact it is not so"); all conditionals kept; the opening kept as a question.
- 12.23: logic of "not bad because not shameful…; good because seasonable" kept in order.
- 12.30: dense physics of the one soul / one substance.

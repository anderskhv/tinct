# Source ambiguities and deliberate readings — Meditations modern-en

Where Long's text (or the Greek behind it) admits more than one reading and the
candidate could not resolve it with confidence, the candidate follows Long's
own reading, or keeps Long's openness, and the case is recorded here. Ids are
`book.index` (0-based index; the section number in the text is index + 1).

| Id | Long | Candidate | Alternatives / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.12 | "to be ready to speak well of teachers, as it is reported of Domitius and Athenodotus" | "to be ready to speak well of teachers, as is reported of Domitius and Athenodotus" | Domitius and Athenodotus may be the ones who praised their teachers, or the teachers whom Catulus praised. Long's openness kept. |
| 1.15 | "He was most ready to give without envy to those who possessed any particular faculty" | "to give way to people who had some special ability" | Long's "give" has no object; "give way" (Greek παραχωρεῖν) fits the following "he gave them his help". Alternative: give credit/rewards. |
| 1.15 | "His dress came from Lorium, his villa on the coast, and from Lanuvium generally." | "His clothes came from Lorium, his villa on the coast, and from Lanuvium generally." | Corrupt Greek. Could mean his dress came from Lorium while his things in general came from Lanuvium. Long's word order kept; no reading forced. |
| 1.15 | "or to attend him of necessity when he went abroad" | "or to travel with him when he went away" | "abroad" = out of doors or on a journey; the Greek (συναποδημεῖν) points to travel. |
| 1.16 / 2.16 | "Among the Quadi at the Granua." / "This in Carnuntum." | "Written among the Quadi, on the Granua." / "Written at Carnuntum." | "Written" is the standard understanding of these place notes; kept so that a listener does not hear the note as part of the preceding list. |
| 2.0 | "not [only] of the same blood or seed, but …" | "not only because we share the same blood or seed, but because …" | Long supplies "[only]"; the Greek reads a plain "not … but". Long followed. |
| 2.5 | "Every man's life is sufficient." | "Each person's life is enough;" | Corrupt Greek. Editors read "life is short for each", "each has but one life", or (Long) "each life suffices". Long's reading only. |
| 2.14 | "what was said by the Cynic Monimus is manifest" | "For what the Cynic Monimus said is plain" | The Greek may mean words said *to* or *against* Monimus. Long's "by" followed. |

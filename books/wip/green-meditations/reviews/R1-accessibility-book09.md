# Accessibility review — Book IX (9.0–9.41)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 9.0, 9.1, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4, 9.5, 9.6, 9.7, 9.8, 9.9, 9.10, 9.11, 9.12, 9.13, 9.14, 9.15, 9.16, 9.17, 9.18, 9.19, 9.20, 9.21, 9.22, 9.23, 9.24, 9.25, 9.26, 9.27, 9.28, 9.29, 9.30, 9.31, 9.32, 9.33, 9.34, 9.35, 9.36, 9.37, 9.38, 9.39, 9.40, 9.41 (all 42).

Note on terms across this packet: "ruling faculty" (9.6, 9.14, 9.17, 9.21, 9.25, 9.33, 9.38), "impression" (9.6), "form"/"matter" (9.13, 9.24, 9.35, 9.36) and "providence" (9.0) are never glossed inside Book IX. 9.14 does let a reader infer that the ruling faculty is the part of us that judges, but it comes after the first use in 9.6. If earlier books have already glossed these terms, the MINOR items tied to them can be downgraded.

## Findings

### 9.0 — BLOCKING
1. "For the nature of the universe is the nature of the things that exist, and the things that exist are related to everything that comes into being." "Related to" is too vague. A listener cannot tell what the relation is, or why it shows that lying is impious. The next sentence ("this nature ... is called truth") only works if this link is clear.
   Proposed: "For the nature of the universe is the nature of everything that exists, and everything that exists is bound up with everything that comes into being."
2. "He had received from nature the powers to tell falsehood from truth, and because he neglected them he can no longer do so." The past perfect "had received" loses the thread about the present liar. Proposed: "Nature gave him the power to tell falsehood from truth, but he neglected it, and now he can no longer do so."
3. "Now there are things toward which the nature of the universe is equally disposed; for it would not have made both of them unless it were equally disposed toward both." "Equally disposed" is abstract, and "both of them" comes before we learn which pairs are meant (they arrive two sentences later). Proposed: "Now there are pairs of opposites toward which the nature of the universe is equally well disposed; for it would not have made both halves of each pair unless it were equally well disposed toward both."
4. The last three sentences ("When I say ... they happen alike to the things that come into being one after another in a continuous chain, and to the things that come after them. They happen by virtue of a certain original impulse of providence. By that impulse providence set out, from a certain beginning, on this present ordering of things. It conceived certain principles of all that was to be, and it fixed the powers that would produce beings, and changes, and successions of that kind.") are the part a first-time listener would lose. The phrases "the things that come into being one after another ... and to the things that come after them" say nearly the same thing twice. "Original impulse of providence" and "successions of that kind" are unexplained. "It" is ambiguous between providence and the ordering.
   Proposed: "When I say that the nature of the universe makes use of them equally, I mean that they happen, without distinction, to everything that comes into being in the unbroken chain of events and to everything that follows it. They happen because of a single first impulse of providence, the divine plan that governs the world. With that impulse, providence set out from a starting point to arrange the world as it now is. Providence decided in advance the principles of everything that was to come, and fixed the powers that would produce new beings, their changes, and the way each follows the last."

### 9.1 — BLOCKING
1. "The next best voyage, as the saying goes": the proverb is obscure. A listener hears "voyage" with no sea context. Proposed: "The next best course, as the saying goes, is to breathe out your life once you have had your fill of these things."
2. "That corruption is a plague to animals insofar as they are animals; but this one is a plague to human beings insofar as they are human." The pronouns point the wrong way on first hearing. The last corruption mentioned is the corruption of the mind, so a listener takes "that corruption" to mean the mind. The contrast is reversed and the thought is lost.
   Proposed: "The foulness in the air is a plague to animals insofar as they are animals; but corruption of the mind is a plague to human beings insofar as they are human."

### 9.6 — MINOR
"Wipe out the impression. ... Put out appetite." Both "impression" (a technical term for what the mind is presented with) and "ruling faculty" are used here for the first time with no help. "Put out appetite" reads like a translation. Proposed: "Wipe out the impression that has formed in your mind. Check desire. Quench appetite. Keep your ruling faculty, the part of you that judges, in its own control."

### 9.8 — MINOR
1. "Fire does move upward, drawn by the elemental fire above" is obscure. Proposed: "Fire does move upward, drawn toward the great mass of fire that fills the heights."
2. "because it has less mixed into it of what keeps things from burning" is inverted and awkward. Proposed: "because less of what resists burning is mixed into it."
3. "and, in a way, loves" is unclear (loves of what?). Proposed: "and something like love between mates."
4. "the power that draws them together is seen working in them more strongly": more strongly than what? Proposed: "the power that draws them together is seen working in them more strongly than in things without souls, in a way never observed in plants or stones or trees."
5. "See, then, what happens now." The transition is vague. Proposed: "Now see what actually happens."

### 9.9 — MINOR
"if ordinary usage applies such words especially to the vine": "such words" has no clear antecedent. Proposed: "And if ordinary speech uses the phrase 'bearing fruit' mostly of the vine and the like, that does not matter."

### 9.10 — MINOR
"It is in your power too": the referent of "it" (being kind like the gods) is unclear. Proposed: "You too have the power to be this kind; or tell me, who is stopping you?"

### 9.13 — MINOR
"worthless in their matter": "matter" in the technical sense (the stuff things are made of) will be misread as "importance". Proposed: "made of worthless stuff."

### 9.17 — MINOR
"and what kind of judges they are of themselves" is compressed. Proposed: "and how poorly they judge even themselves." (If the neutral sense is meant: "and what sort of judges they are when they judge themselves.")

### 9.23 — BLOCKING
"poor little souls carrying corpses around" and "And so the picture of the dwellings of the dead strikes our eyes more clearly." A newcomer will not see that the "corpses" are our own bodies, or what "the dwellings of the dead" are. The "And so" gives no link a listener can follow. The thought is not reachable.
Proposed: "Children's quarrels and their games, and poor little souls carrying their own dead bodies around: that is what everything is. So the old picture of the land of the dead seems all the more vivid and true to life."

### 9.24 — MINOR
"what kind of form a thing has. Separate it completely from its material": "form" vs "material" is a technical pair. "the length of time—the longest—" is awkward. Proposed: "Look closely at what makes a thing the kind of thing it is, its form. Separate that completely from the material it is made of, and consider it by itself. Then work out the longest time that a thing of that kind is naturally made to last."

### 9.27 — MINOR
"Or indivisible elements are the origin of all things." The third option comes with no instruction after it. The listener expects "and if so, ..." and does not get it. The term also needs help (it means atoms, as 9.38 later says). Proposed: "Or else the origin of all things is indivisible particles, atoms, and nothing more."

### 9.28 — MINOR
1. "The cause of the universe is like a winter torrent" is an unfamiliar image. Proposed: "The cause of the universe is like a river in winter flood: it sweeps everything along."
2. "Do not lead me astray into arrogance and pride." It is unclear who is being addressed (the reader? those three men? an imagined critic?). Proposed: "Let no one lead me astray into arrogance and pride."
3. "They themselves will judge whether they understood..." reads like a translation. Proposed: "It is for them to show whether they understood what the common nature required and trained themselves accordingly."

### 9.30 — MINOR
"the cause outside you / the cause within you" is abstract. Proposed: "Toward what happens to you from causes outside you, keep free from disturbance. In what you do from the cause within you, your own choice, be just."

### 9.34 — MINOR
"What, then, are you saying?" The imagined objector is not signalled, so the question seems to come from nowhere. Proposed: "What, then, would you say instead? That all things have been bad and always will be bad? ..."

### 9.35 — BLOCKING
1. "And what is made of breath is another thing of the same kind, changing from this into that." A newcomer will not know that "what is made of breath" means the living spirit or soul. The sentence's point, that even this is the same perishable stuff, cannot be reached. Proposed: "And the breath of life within us, our spirit, is more of the same stuff, always changing from one thing into another."
2. "marble, the calluses of the earth; gold and silver, its sediments" is understandable but compressed. Proposed: "marble is just the hardened skin of the earth; gold and silver, its dregs." (MINOR within this section.)

### 9.36 — MINOR
"It is all the same whether we study these things for a hundred years or for three." The link to what came before is unclear. Proposed: "Whether we look into these things for a hundred years or for three, we will find the same thing."

### 9.40 — MINOR
1. Three "he says" inserts break up the quotation, and a listener cannot hear where Epicurus stops and Marcus starts. Proposed: open with "Epicurus writes:", drop the inner "he says" tags, and begin the last part with "So, Marcus, do what he did."
2. "these movements of the poor flesh" is obscure. Proposed: "while it shares in what the poor body goes through."
3. "the instrument you are doing it with" is unclear (it means the mind or ruling faculty). Proposed: "and on the instrument you are doing it with, your own mind."

### 9.41 — MINOR
"you did not do it without conditions, and not in such a way that the act itself gave you all the benefit." The stacked negatives are hard to decode by ear. Proposed: "or, when you did him a kindness, you attached conditions to it, instead of doing it so that the act itself was all the reward you needed."

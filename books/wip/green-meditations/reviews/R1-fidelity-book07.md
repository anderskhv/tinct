# Fidelity review — Book VII (7.0–7.74)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 7.0–7.74, all 75 paragraphs checked sentence by sentence against Long (1862) and against STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 7.1 — MINOR
- Source: "How can our principles become dead … Let this be the state of thy affects, and thou standest erect."
- Candidate: "How can our principles die … Let this be your state of mind, and you stand upright."
- Problem: (a) Terminology. "principles" here are Marcus's δόγματα. The style standard renders "fixed opinions"/principles as "settled judgments". (b) "affects" (feelings, what you undergo) has been narrowed to "state of mind". This is a small imprecision.
- Proposed: "How can our settled judgments die, unless the impressions—the thoughts—that go with them are put out? … Let this be the state of your feelings, and you stand upright."

### 7.3 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "In discourse thou must attend to what is said, and in every movement thou must observe what is doing. And in the one thou shouldst see immediately to what end it refers, but in the other watch carefully what is the thing signified."
- Candidate: "… In the one, see at once what end it is aimed at; in the other, watch carefully what is meant."
- Note: The candidate keeps Long's "the one / the other", and "movement" becomes "impulse", as the standard requires for ὁρμή. Both choices are faithful to Long. Long's order suggests that "the one" is speech. The Greek pairs the reverse way: you look for the end of the *impulse* and for the meaning of the *words*. There are two ways to resolve this. Leave it as it is, which follows Long. Or make it explicit: "In every impulse, see at once what end it is aimed at; in what is said, watch carefully what is meant." Log the choice in AMBIGUITIES.md.

### 7.6 — MINOR
- Source: "Be not ashamed to be helped; for it is thy business to do thy duty like a soldier in the assault on a town."
- Candidate: "Do not be ashamed to be helped. Your task is to do your duty, like a soldier storming a city wall."
- Problem: The causal "for" is dropped. The second sentence is the *reason* you need not be ashamed, and the lame-soldier question depends on that link.
- Proposed: "Do not be ashamed to be helped. For your task is to do your duty, like a soldier storming a city wall."

### 7.12 — MINOR
- Source: "I am a member (Greek: melos) of the system of rational beings."
- Candidate: "\"I am a limb (melos) of the body that rational beings make up.\""
- Problem: Long's "system" becomes "body". The limb image does make the melos/meros wordplay clear. But "body" makes the organism metaphor more explicit than Long does in this clause, although the opening sentence already sets up the comparison.
- Proposed: "\"I am a limb (melos) of the whole that rational beings make up.\""

### 7.14 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "Whatever any one does or says, I must be good; just as if the gold, or the emerald, or the purple, were always saying this. Whatever any one does or says, I must be emerald and keep my color."
- Candidate: "Whatever anyone does or says, I must be good—just as if gold, or emerald, or purple were always saying: \"Whatever anyone does or says, I must be emerald and keep my color.\""
- Note: Long ends the first sentence at "saying this" and gives the emerald sentence on its own. The candidate makes the second sentence the words that gold, emerald and purple speak. This matches the Greek (a colon after ἔλεγεν), and no content is lost. The alternative, closer to Long's punctuation: "…just as if gold, or emerald, or purple were always saying this. Whatever anyone does or says, I must be emerald and keep my color." Either reading is defensible. Log it in AMBIGUITIES.md.

### 7.15 — MINOR
- Source: "But if any one else can frighten or pain it, let him do so. For the faculty itself will not by its own opinion turn itself into such ways."
- Candidate: "If someone else can frighten it or cause it pain, let him. It will not turn itself that way by its own judgment."
- Problem: The causal "For" is dropped. The second sentence explains why outside frightening is allowed, and the style standard asks that causal links be kept.
- Proposed: "If someone else can frighten it or cause it pain, let him. For it will not turn itself that way by its own judgment."

### 7.16 — MINOR
- Source: "What then art thou doing here, O imagination?"
- Candidate: "So what are you doing here, imagination?"
- Problem: Terminology inconsistency. This is φαντασία, and the standard renders it as "impression". The candidate uses "impression" for the same word in 7.28 ("Wipe out the impression") but "imagination" here. The two sections are paired: here Marcus speaks to the intruding impression, and in 7.28 he wipes it out.
- Proposed: "So what are you doing here, impression? Go away, I beg you by the gods, the way you came. …"

### 7.30 — MINOR
- Source: "Adorn thyself with simplicity and modesty …"
- Candidate: "Clothe yourself in simplicity and modesty …"
- Problem: The image changes. "Adorn" (make bright, beautify) becomes "clothe" (cover, dress).
- Proposed: "Adorn yourself with simplicity and modesty, and with indifference toward the things that lie between virtue and vice."

### 7.41 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "For the good is with me, and the just."
- Candidate: "For the good is on my side, and the just."
- Note: "With me" can mean "on my side" (the gods favour me) or "in my possession" (I have the good and the just in me). The candidate chose the first reading, which suits the quotation's context after 7.40. To keep both readings open: "For the good is with me, and the just."

### 7.43 — MINOR
- Source: "But I would make this man a sufficient answer, which is this: …"
- Candidate: "\"But I would give this man a fair answer, and it is this: …\""
- Problem: Long's "sufficient" (an adequate answer) becomes "fair" (a just answer). "Fair" is closer to the Greek δίκαιον, but it departs from the anchor translation.
- Proposed: "\"But I would give this man a sufficient answer, and it is this: …\""

### 7.59 — MINOR
- Source: "For what the mind shows in the face by maintaining in it the expression of intelligence and propriety, that ought to be required also in the whole body."
- Candidate: "What the mind achieves in the face, by keeping it intelligent and composed, should be required of the whole body as well."
- Problem: "shows" (what the face displays) becomes "achieves". "For" is also dropped, although this sentence gives the reason for the body rule before it.
- Proposed: "For what the mind shows in the face, by keeping it intelligent and composed, should be required of the whole body as well."

### 7.60 — MINOR
- Source: "… it should stand ready and firm to meet onsets which are sudden and unexpected."
- Candidate: "… it must stand ready and firm to meet blows that come suddenly and without warning."
- Problem: "onsets" means attacks or rushes, which fits wrestling. "blows" narrows the image to strikes, which fits boxing more than wrestling. "should" has also become "must", which is a slight change of modality.
- Proposed: "…in this respect: it should stand ready and firm to meet attacks that come suddenly and without warning."

### 7.69 — MINOR
- Source: "… because during so long a time they must tolerate continually men such as they are …"
- Candidate: "… do not resent having to put up, for so long, with people such as they are …"
- Problem: "continually" is omitted. It adds to "so long a time" and strengthens the contrast with "you, who will so soon come to an end".
- Proposed: "The gods, who are immortal, do not resent having to put up, for so long and without a break, with people such as they are, so many of them bad; …"

### 7.74 — MINOR
- Source: "The nature of the All moved to make the universe. But now either everything that takes place comes by way of consequence or [continuity]; or even the chief things … are governed by no rational principle."
- Candidate: "The nature of the whole set out to make the universe. So now, either everything that happens follows as a consequence of that, in an unbroken chain; or even the most important things … are governed by no rational principle."
- Problem: Long's "But now" becomes "So now". This turns a shift of time and topic into an inference, as if both alternatives *followed* from the first sentence. The second alternative clearly does not follow from it. "of that" is a reasonable reading of Long's bare "by way of consequence", and the either/or is kept intact. (This passage is also a known crux: the second limb is often read as an absurd alternative that Marcus sets up to reject. The candidate follows Long's wording, which is correct.)
- Proposed: "The nature of the whole set out to make the universe. But now, either everything that happens follows as a consequence of that, in an unbroken chain; or even the most important things, toward which the ruling power of the universe directs its own impulse, are governed by no rational principle."

Coverage: every changed id. That is 8.0, 8.1, 8.2, 8.6, 8.7, 8.10, 8.14, 8.16, 8.19, 8.20, 8.24, 8.28, 8.30, 8.31, 8.34, 8.35, 8.36, 8.40, 8.43, 8.46, 8.47, 8.48, 8.49, 8.55, 8.56 and 8.59 (26 ids).

Method: I diffed BEFORE against AFTER word by word with a script, then read each AFTER against Long and the neighbouring sections. The section number and opening words are unchanged in every paragraph.

## Per paragraph

### 8.0 — CLEAN
Two changes: "tried many wanderings," became "wandered down many paths", and "feelings" became "impulses". Both are faithful: Long has "experience of many wanderings", and the style standard fixes ὁρμή as "impulse". The fidelity finding is resolved and the English now sounds natural.

### 8.1 — CLEAN
"How does this stand with me?" became "How does this affect me?". This is faithful to Long's "How is this with respect to me?", and "Will I regret it?" follows on naturally.

### 8.2 — CLEAN
"their matter" became "the matter they are made of". Nothing is added. "They" points to "things". In principle a listener could attach it to "these men", but the list ("things, and their causes, and …") makes the reference clear. The rejected finding about "ruling faculty" is correctly rejected: the term is glossed at II.2 ("the reasoning mind that governs the rest of me"), and I checked that in the candidate.

### 8.6 — CLEAN
Three changes. "assents" became "agrees", which is the standard's plainer form. "it" became "that common nature". Long's list was moved ahead of "in equal shares and according to the worth of each", and "times" became "span of time" and "what befalls it" became "what happens to it".
- The antecedent is correct. In Long, the thing that "gives to everything" is the unimpeded nature of which man's nature is a part, and that is the common nature.
- All five members of the list are kept, and both "equal portions" and "according to its worth" are kept. Long's "since" survives as "For".
- Long's "[form]" after "cause" was already missing before this change, so it is not a regression.
- The blocking finding is resolved: the reference is fixed and the list is now concrete. The remaining tension between "equal" and "worth" belongs to Long, and the next sentence addresses it.

### 8.7 — CLEAN
"are not free / are free" (four times) became "have no time / have time". This follows Long's main reading ("leisure") and keeps the repetition. The permission misreading is gone. "[or ability]" is lost, and the disposition says it was logged. See the process note under the verdict.

### 8.10 — CLEAN
"cause—its form" became "causal nature, or form". This is Long's wording, with his bracketed term offered as an alternative. It no longer sounds like an equation, and it is acceptable aloud.

### 8.14 — CLEAN
The sentence now gives each profession its own case: "for a doctor to be surprised … or for a ship's pilot to be surprised when the wind is against him". Long's pairing is kept, and "him" is fine for a helmsman. The finding is resolved.

### 8.16 — CLEAN
"If something you find fault with is in your own power, why do you do it?" The addition is supported by the same section: "of what use is it to thee to find fault?". It also matches the standard reading of the section, which is about blaming and complaining. The question now makes sense and sets up the contrast with "whom do you blame". The blocking finding is resolved.

### 8.19 — CLEAN
"the lasting" became "the time it lasts", which is faithful to Long's "continuance" and reads naturally.

### 8.20 — CLEAN
"people do not all agree with one another—no, no one even agrees with himself" is faithful to Long's "not even here do all agree, no, not any one with himself". The grammar is fixed.

### 8.24 — CLEAN
"or else it must be moved and placed somewhere else": the added "it" is the breath, which is Long's subject for "be removed". All three alternatives are kept. The partial rejections are right: "compound" and "breath" are Long's words, and "little compound that is you" can be followed.

### 8.28 — CLEAN
"Instead, I look at all things, see … and use" became "Rather, looking at all things, I see … and I use". This is Long's construction ("but looking at all things I see"), and the connective now reads correctly. Rejecting "that crowd into your mind" is right. The saying that follows makes clear which impressions are meant, and V.2 has already used "wipe out every impression that is troubling", so a listener can follow the thought.

### 8.30 — CLEAN
"such as the Pompeii" became "such as the family of the Pompeii". This is lexical only and stops the listener hearing the buried city. Keeping "Then turn to the others" is right, because it comes straight after "the whole court is dead", which makes it clear.

### 8.31 — CLEAN
"by accepting … you are at once given …—one that" became "if you accept … and are content …, another opportunity … is at once put before you …. And it is one that …". Long's means ("by acquiescing") becomes a condition, which is equivalent here and not an imperative. "put before" is Long's own wording. The long sentence is now two, and nothing is lost. Keeping "does its duty" is right: it is Long's personification, and the phrase is easy to follow.

### 8.34 — DEFECT (minor, English)
The rewrite is faithful. "the following power" keeps Long's forward-pointing "this power", "turns … around" renders "converts", and the purpose that "to its own ends" added is gone, so the fidelity finding is resolved. The "For as … so also" analogy is kept as "… In the same way, …".

One problem is new. "takes everything that stands in its way and opposes it: it turns **that obstacle** around". "That obstacle" is singular and points back to "everything", so it has no clear antecedent, which is a grammar slip that a listener hears.

Proposed replacement for the second sentence: "The nature of the universe takes everything that stands in its way and opposes it: it turns each such obstacle around, fixes it in its destined place, and makes it part of itself."

### 8.35 — CLEAN
"and scold your mind if it cannot" became "and if you scold your mind when it cannot". This is correct: Long and the Greek (ἐὰν … καὶ ἀπελέγχῃς) make scolding a second condition, not a command, so the finding is resolved faithfully. "When" for Long's inner "if" is acceptable English, because it is conditional in force, and it avoids "if … if".

### 8.36 — CLEAN
The redundant and unsourced "still" is removed. "Pergamus" is kept as a corrected misprint, which the style standard's proper-names rule allows.

### 8.40 — CLEAN
"The senses will see to that" became "That is for the senses to look to", which is Long's "will look to that" and no longer suggests a fix. "it" became "the understanding" in the sphere sentence, and that is Long's antecedent. Keeping "without any reservation" (Long's own bracket) is right. The next sentence, "if you took into account how things usually go", makes the contrast clear enough to follow.

### 8.43 — CLEAN
The causal "For" is restored, which resolves the fidelity finding. "make" became "utter" ("utter this or that sound"), which is Long's word and is clearly about speech.

### 8.46 — CLEAN
"and at peace, too, with the things that stood in his way" became "—and leave it well pleased, too, with the things that stand in your way". This is the fidelity reviewer's proposal. It is supported by the Greek, where ἵλεως agrees with the subject of ἄπιθι, and it restores the strength of "well pleased". It reads clearly. Rejecting "the opinion that lies behind it" is right: the section has just set up "your own judgment about it … wipe that judgment out", so a listener can follow "correcting your opinion".

### 8.47 — CLEAN
Changes: "if" became "as long as", "—even if" became ", even when", "fortress. A" became "fortress: a", and "taken" became "captured".
- The causal link is restored, which resolves the fidelity finding.
- The condition is kept ("as long as" = provided that).
- "Captured" is right for "inexpugnable".
- "even when" is concessive, the same as Long's "even if", and it does not change the meaning.
- Rejecting a "passions" gloss is right: it is glossed at I.8 ("free from passion—from disturbing emotion"), and I checked that.

### 8.48 — CLEAN
"someone who knows everything" became "someone familiar with everything". This fits the Greek παρακολουθῶν ("one who follows / understands") and removes the impression of an all-knowing person without dropping "everything".

### 8.49 — CLEAN
"her art" became "nature's art". The antecedent ("the nature of the universe") is in the sentence just before. The later uses of "she" and "her" now read clearly.

### 8.55 — CLEAN
"so that my unhappiness should not depend" became "because he did not want my unhappiness to depend". This is Long's purpose clause ("in order that"), with the result misreading removed.

### 8.56 — CLEAN
"(in Greek, apo tou ekteinesthai)" became "in Greek the name comes from their being stretched out, apo tou ekteinesthai". This is faithful to Long's "because they are extended (Greek: apo tou ekteinesthai)". The Greek phrase is now introduced by "in Greek", so a listener can follow the derivation. The quotation marks are straight ASCII.

### 8.59 — CLEAN
"no less, and toward its target" became "all the same, toward its target". Long's "not the less" means "nonetheless", so the editor's reading is right and the reviewer's "no less than the arrow does" would have been a misreading. Long's "and to its object" is absorbed into "toward its target" without losing any meaning.

## Rejected accessibility findings: can a first-time listener still follow?
I read every rejection and partial rejection in context. None leaves the thought unfollowable, so none is a DEFECT.
- **8.2** ruling faculty, **8.44** divine spirit within, and **8.47** passions: each is glossed earlier in reading order (II.2, II.13 and I.8 respectively), and I checked this in `candidate.json`.
- **8.5** "the way things are shared out": the section opens with "The nature of the universe has this work to do", which supplies the agent. Long names no distributor.
- **8.12** "impression": the term has been used in plain senses since Book I and in the technical sense since V.2. "every impression that comes to your soul" can be followed as plain English.
- **8.27** "within … no evil climbs up that high": this is Long's image. The body-versus-soul contrast earlier in the section supplies the "height", and the gist (pain cannot reach the soul's own acts) is clear.
- **8.46** "correcting your opinion": this follows directly from "your own judgment about it … wipe that judgment out".
- The partial rejections at 8.10, 8.24, 8.28, 8.30, 8.31 and 8.40 are covered in the per-paragraph notes above. All can be followed.
- **8.35** (imperative) and **8.59** ("no less than the arrow"): these were rightly rejected, because the reviewer's rewrites would have changed Long's meaning.

# Accessibility review — Book II (2.0–2.16)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 2.0, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 2.10, 2.11, 2.12, 2.13, 2.14, 2.15, 2.16 (all 17 sections).

Terms tracked across the packet: "ruling faculty" is glossed at first use (2.1: "the reasoning mind that governs the rest of me"). "Providence" is glossed at first use (2.2: "the gods' care and foresight"). "Opinion" is glossed (2.14: "what we judge things to be"). "Divine spirit within" first appears in 2.12 with no gloss, and 2.16 uses it again. "Nature of the universe" / "nature of the whole" is never glossed, but the context makes it usable.

## Findings

### 2.1 — MINOR
Problem words: "The third part, then, is the ruling faculty. Think of it this way: you are an old man. Do not let this part be a slave any longer."
Why: "Think of it this way" sounds as if a description of the ruling faculty is coming. Instead the listener gets "you are an old man," which is really the reason for the commands that follow. A first-time listener may not connect old age with "no longer letting it be a slave."
Rewrite: "The third part, then, is the ruling faculty. Consider this: you are an old man, so do not let this part be a slave any longer."

### 2.2 — MINOR
Problem words: (a) "Everything flows from there." (b) "Necessity has its part too, and so does what benefits the whole universe, of which you are a part." (c) "so that you do not die grumbling, but cheerfully, truly, and grateful to the gods"
Why: (a) "there" has no clear antecedent: is it chance, nature, or providence? (b) "has its part": a part in what? The listener can't tell what necessity and "what benefits the whole" contribute to. (c) "truly" in a list of manners of dying reads like translationese, since it's unclear what dying "truly" means.
Rewrite: (a) "Everything flows from providence." (b) "Necessity plays its part in this too, and so does what benefits the whole universe, of which you are a part." (c) "so that you do not die grumbling, but cheerfully, sincerely, and grateful to the gods from your heart."

### 2.3 — MINOR
Problem words: "how long you have been putting these things off"
Why: Nothing in the section says what "these things" are. A listener can work it out from the next sentence (understanding the universe and its governor), but only after the fact.
Rewrite: "Remember how long you have been putting off these matters, understanding your place in the universe, and how many times the gods have given you an opportunity that you have not used."

### 2.4 — MINOR
Problem words: "with complete and unaffected dignity"
Why: Here "unaffected" means "without affectation / not put on." Modern listeners usually hear it as "not influenced" or "unmoved," which gives the opposite sense next to "warmth." The opening sentence is also long: seven qualities plus a second goal ("and on freeing yourself…") hang from "set your mind firmly."
Rewrite: "Every moment, set your mind firmly, as a Roman and a man, on doing the task in front of you with complete and unpretended dignity, with warmth, freedom and justice. And set it on freeing yourself from every other thought."

### 2.5 — MINOR
Problem words: "Go on, wrong yourself, wrong yourself, my soul! … still your soul does not respect itself"
Why: Marcus is addressing his soul ("my soul"), and then speaks of "your soul" as if it were a separate thing. The listener loses track of who "you" is. The sarcastic imperative "Go on, wrong yourself" can also be heard as a real instruction on a single hearing.
Rewrite: "Go on, keep wronging yourself, my soul, keep wronging yourself! But you will not have the chance to honor yourself much longer. Each person has one life, and it is enough; but yours is nearly over, and still you do not respect yourself, but make your happiness depend on the souls of others."

### 2.10 — MINOR
Problem words: (a) "why should I want to live" (b) "The nature of the universe could not have let such things slip past it"
Why: (a) The section addresses "you" and then switches to "I" mid-argument. This is minor, but it jars in a spoken passage. (b) "such things" is the weak link in a long chain of reasoning. The listener can't tell whether it means bad things in general, things outside our power, or bad and good falling on people without distinction. The conclusion is still reachable, but the step that gets there is not.
Rewrite: (a) "why should you want to live in a universe without gods, or without providence?" (b) "The nature of the universe could not have let such a flaw slip past it (truly bad things lying outside our power), whether from ignorance, or from knowing about it but lacking the power to prevent or correct it."

### 2.11 — MINOR
Problem words: (a) "How quickly everything vanishes: in the universe, the bodies themselves; in time, even the memory of them." (b) "Observe, too, how a human being comes near to God, and through what part of himself, and in what condition that part is when it does."
Why: (a) The compressed "in X, Y; in Z, W" pattern is hard to take in by ear. (b) "when it does" hangs loosely. Does "it" mean the part, or the human being coming near? The listener has to rebuild the sentence.
Rewrite: (a) "How quickly everything vanishes: the bodies themselves vanish into the universe, and even the memory of them vanishes into time." (b) "Observe, too, how a human being comes near to God, through what part of himself he does so, and in what condition that part is when he does."

### 2.12 — MINOR
Problem words: (a) "goes around and around everything" (b) "the divine spirit within him"
Why: (a) The image of circling is vague; a listener won't know it means restless, aimless inquiry into everything. (b) This is the first use of "divine spirit within" in the packet, and it has no gloss. A newcomer can't tell whether it is the "ruling faculty" of 2.1 or something else. (The "portion of the divine" in 2.0 hints at it but is not connected.)
Rewrite: (a) "Nothing is more wretched than a person who restlessly circles around everything, and pries into the things beneath the earth, as the poet says…" (b) "…it is enough to attend to the divine spirit within him (his own guiding mind, his portion of the divine) and to honor it sincerely."

### 2.13 — MINOR
Problem words: "For the present is the same for everyone, even though what passes away is not the same; and so what is lost turns out to be a mere moment."
Why: "what passes away is not the same" is too abstract to follow by ear. The listener needs to hear that the *amount* of time already gone differs from person to person, while the present moment is equal for all.
Rewrite: "For the present moment is equal for everyone, even though the amount of time that has passed away is not; and so what anyone loses turns out to be a mere moment."

### 2.14 — MINOR
Problem words: "What the Cynic Monimus said makes this plain; and the use of his saying is plain too, if you take from it what it has to offer, as far as it is true."
Why: The saying itself is never given, so "makes this plain" points to something the listener hasn't heard. "the use of his saying is plain too" is also a knotted phrase: "use" could mean "usefulness" or "application." (Note for the editor: I can't tell whether the source quotes the saying. If it doesn't, don't add it. The phrasing can still be untangled.)
Rewrite: "What the Cynic Monimus said makes this plain; and his saying is plainly useful too, if you take from it the part worth taking, as far as it is true."

### 2.15 — BLOCKING
Problem words: (a) "For to resent anything that happens is to break away from nature, and some part of nature holds the natures of all other things." (b) "Fourth, when it plays a part" (c) "the reason and the law of the oldest city and commonwealth of all"
Why: (a) A first-time listener cannot connect "some part of nature holds the natures of all other things" either to resentment or to the abscess image. What is this "part," and why does it matter? The reasoning that makes resentment a tumor on the universe is lost. (b) "plays a part" comes right after talk of parts of nature, so a listener may hear "is a part" rather than "acts a role / pretends." (c) "the oldest city and commonwealth" is a technical idea (the universe as a city) that a newcomer can't recognize here without a hint.
Rewrite: (a) "For to resent anything that happens is to break away from nature, the nature of the whole, within which the natures of all other things are held, each in its part." (b) "Fourth, when it plays a role, putting on an act, and does or says anything falsely or insincerely." (c) "…the purpose of rational beings is to follow the reason and the law of the oldest city and commonwealth of all, the universe itself."

### 2.16 — MINOR
Problem words: (a) "our substance is always flowing" (b) "fame is without judgment" (c) "accepting everything that happens and everything that is given to him as coming from the same source, wherever it is, from which he himself came"
Why: (a) "substance" can be heard as "wealth" or "essence." The meaning here is the physical stuff we are made of. (b) "without judgment" is ambiguous: fame is undiscerning, or fame is unjudged? (c) The clause is nested ("the same source, wherever it is, from which he himself came") inside a very long list, so the listener loses the thread. The list is also long enough (six items after "philosophy means") that repeating the verb frame would help.
Rewrite: (a) "our bodily substance is always flowing" (b) "fame does not discriminate" (c) "accepting everything that happens and everything that is given to him as coming from the same source he himself came from, wherever that source may be"

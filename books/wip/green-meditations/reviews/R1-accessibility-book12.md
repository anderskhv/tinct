# Accessibility review — Book XII (12.0–12.35)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 12.0, 12.1, 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 12.5, 12.6, 12.7, 12.8, 12.9, 12.10, 12.11, 12.12, 12.13, 12.14, 12.15, 12.16, 12.17, 12.18, 12.19, 12.20, 12.21, 12.22, 12.23, 12.24, 12.25, 12.26, 12.27, 12.28, 12.29, 12.30, 12.31, 12.32, 12.33, 12.34, 12.35 (all 36).

## Findings

### 12.0 — MINOR
Problem words (1): "And that means this: if you pay no attention to all that is past, entrust the future to providence, and direct the present only by piety and justice."
Why: "that means this: if..." opens a condition whose result is never stated. The listener waits for a "then" that never comes, and has to work out that the missing result is "you can have them now".
Rewrite: "You can have them if you pay no attention to all that is past, entrust the future to providence, and direct the present only by piety and justice."

Problem words (2): "let the part that suffers them see to that."
Why: "them" (the sensations) and "the part" (the flesh) are two referents in a row, and a listener cannot easily match them. "See to that" is vague.
Rewrite: "let the flesh that feels those sensations deal with them."

Problem words (3): "Whenever the time comes for you to depart, suppose you let everything else go … Suppose you are afraid, not because … Then you will be a man worthy …"
Why: Two "Suppose" sentences in a row read as translated conditionals. The listener hears them as invitations to imagine something, not as the conditions for the "Then" that follows. "Ruling faculty" and "the divine spirit within you" also appear here for the first time in the packet, with no gloss.
Rewrite: "When the time comes for you to depart, if you have let everything else go and honored only your ruling faculty — the reasoning mind that governs you — and the divine spirit within you; and if what you fear is not that you must one day stop living, but that you have never begun to live according to nature; then you will be a man worthy of the universe that brought you into being."

Problem words (4): "stop hanging on this thing or that."
Why: "Hanging on" can mean "waiting eagerly for" or "clinging to", so it is ambiguous.
Rewrite: "and stop depending on this thing or that."

### 12.1 — MINOR
Problem words: "For with his own mind alone he touches only the intelligence that has flowed from himself into these bodies. If you train yourself to do the same …"
Why: The "For" does not clearly explain the sentence before it. "Do the same" makes the listener look back to work out what God does.
Rewrite: "For he uses his mind alone, and it touches only the intelligence that has flowed from him into these bodies. If you train yourself to look at people the same way, seeing only their ruling faculty, you will free yourself from a great deal of trouble."

### 12.2 — BLOCKING
Problem words: "Suppose, then, that you separate from yourself … Suppose you separate too whatever troubles you … and whatever belongs to the body … and clings to you without your choosing; and whatever the whirlpool rushing round you from outside sweeps along. Suppose you do this so that … If, I say, you separate …"
Why: The section runs three "Suppose" sentences, and then an "If, I say" sentence restarts the same list before the result finally arrives. Each list is very long and made of stacked "whatever" clauses, so a listener loses the thread well before "then you will be able to live". It is also unclear what "clings to you without your choosing" goes with (the body, the breath, or both). "The whirlpool … sweeps along" is obscure.
Rewrite (keeps every item): "So separate from yourself, that is, from your understanding, these things: whatever others do or say; whatever you yourself have done or said; whatever troubles you about the future because it might happen; whatever belongs to the body that wraps you, or to the breath that nature joined to it, since these cling to you without your choosing; and whatever the swirling world outside you carries along with it. Do this so that the power of your mind, set free from what fate controls, can live pure and free by itself, doing what is just, accepting what happens, and speaking the truth. If you separate from this ruling faculty everything that has clung to it through the impressions of the senses, and everything in the future and in the past; if you make yourself like the sphere of Empedocles, 'round on every side, resting in its joyful rest'; and if you try to live only the life that is truly yours, which is the present — then you will be able to live the rest of your life, until you die, free from disturbance, nobly, and obedient to the divine spirit within you."

### 12.4 — MINOR
Problem words: "So because it is not so—if in fact it is not so—be convinced that it ought not to have been so." Also: "And we would not argue this way with the gods unless they were supremely good and supremely just."
Why: Four uses of "so" close together blur the logic. The second sentence skips a step: the point is that raising a question of justice with the gods assumes they are just.
Rewrite: "So if it is not the case — if in fact it is not — be convinced that it ought not to have been. … And we would not challenge the gods about justice unless they were supremely good and supremely just."

### 12.7 — MINOR
Problem words: "Consider who is the cause of his own unrest"; "everything is opinion."
Why: "Who is the cause of his own unrest" sounds like a question about some particular person, when the point is that each person causes his own unrest. "Everything is opinion" appears here for the first time with no gloss, and a newcomer may take it to mean "nothing is real" rather than "everything depends on how you judge it". The phrase comes back in 12.21, 12.24 and 12.25.
Rewrite: "Consider that each person is the cause of his own unrest; that no one is ever hindered by anyone else; and that everything is opinion, that is, everything depends on how we judge it."

### 12.15 — MINOR
Problem words: "So if you are irritable, cure this man's character."
Why: The step from "you are irritable" to "cure his character" is abrupt. A listener may hear "irritable" as a fixed trait of yours rather than your annoyance at him.
Rewrite: "So if it irritates you so much, cure this man's character."

### 12.16 — MINOR
Problem words: "For let your effort be—"
Why: The sentence breaks off. Heard aloud, it sounds like a reading error, not a gap in the text. "Let your effort be" is also old-fashioned phrasing.
Rewrite: keep the break but mark it plainly in the edition's usual style, e.g. "For your effort should be — [the rest of this sentence is lost]."

### 12.21 — MINOR
Problem words: "So take away your opinion whenever you choose."
Why: If "opinion" has not been explained (see 12.7), "take away your opinion" sounds like "stop having views". What it means is dropping the judgment you have added to a thing.
Rewrite: "So take away your opinion about a thing, your judgment of it, whenever you choose." (Not needed if 12.7 is glossed.)

### 12.22 — BLOCKING
Problem words: "For this, too, is how a person is moved by God: when he moves the same way God moves, and in his own mind moves toward the same ends."
Why: "For" suggests a reason, but this sentence does not explain the one before it. "This" has no clear referent. "Moved by God" sits next to "moves the same way God moves", so the three uses of "move" run together. A first-time listener will not get the thought, which is that accepting death in its season means moving in step with God.
Rewrite: "And this, too, is what it means to be carried along by God: to move the same way God moves, and to direct one's own mind toward the same ends."

### 12.23 — MINOR
Problem words: "in the air and in the ether."
Why: "Ether" is obscure to most readers today (or makes them think of the anaesthetic).
Rewrite: "in the air and in the upper heavens."

### 12.26 — MINOR
Problem words: "In short, think of all the eager chasing after things, joined with pride."
Why: "Joined with pride" is loosely attached, so it is unclear whether the pride belongs to the chasing or to the people doing it.
Rewrite: "In short, think of all the proud, eager chasing after things."

### 12.29 — MINOR
Problem words: "Now, among the things just mentioned, all the other parts, such as air and matter, have no sensation …"
Why: Air was not among "the things just mentioned", so the listener goes looking for a referent that is not there. "The other parts" is also unclear: other than what?
Rewrite: "Now, among all these, the parts other than minds, such as air and matter, have no sensation and no fellowship with one another."

### 12.35 — MINOR
Problem words: "It is as if a praetor who had hired an actor were to dismiss him from the stage."
Why: "Praetor" means nothing to most modern listeners, and the image depends on knowing that he is the official who hired the actor.
Rewrite: "It is as if a magistrate who had hired an actor were to dismiss him from the stage."

## Summary

- MINOR: 11 (12.0, 12.1, 12.4, 12.7, 12.15, 12.16, 12.21, 12.23, 12.26, 12.29, 12.35)
- Clear: 23

Verdict: **FAIL**. There are two blocking sections: 12.2, where the long "Suppose … If, I say" build-up loses the listener, and 12.22, whose closing "moved by God" sentence has no clear link to what comes before. Across the book, "ruling faculty" (12.0) and "opinion" (12.7) are never glossed, and a single short gloss at the first use of each would make the later sections easier to follow.

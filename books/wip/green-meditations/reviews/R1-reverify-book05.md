# Re-verification — Book V R1 changes
Coverage: every changed id: 5.0, 5.2, 5.4, 5.5, 5.7, 5.8, 5.9, 5.11, 5.14, 5.15, 5.16, 5.18, 5.19, 5.20, 5.25, 5.27, 5.28, 5.31, 5.32, 5.33, 5.35 (21). AFTER texts in the packet were confirmed identical to `candidate.json`. I also checked the rejected accessibility findings on unchanged sections 5.12, 5.13 and 5.34, and every rejected item inside the changed sections. The test was whether leaving the text as it is leaves a first-time listener unable to follow the thought.

Section numbers and openings: unchanged in all 21. Each paragraph still opens with its source's first sentence. The openings of 5.2, 5.11, 5.14 and 5.28 are reworded but keep the same content.

## Per paragraph

### 5.0 — CLEAN
Changes: "those" became "these" limits; "; while in your actions it is not so: there you stop" became ", while in your actions you do not: you stop"; "stop working at" became "stop working to perfect". Long's "perfect" is restored. The contrast now reads clearly. The rejected "in resting" is a sound rejection: Long's "these bounds" is open, and the listener can still follow the rest/work contrast.

### 5.2 — CLEAN
Change: the opening word order became "Judge worthy of you every word and every act…". This is faithful to Long's "judge … to be fit for thee" and is clear aloud. The rejected "ruling faculty" gloss is a sound rejection: the gloss is present at II.2 ("the reasoning mind that governs the rest of me").

### 5.4 — CLEAN
Changes: the excuse is now quoted in first person ("People cannot admire me…"); "mean" became "stingy" (Long's word); "Only, if" became "But if". All ten qualities and seven vices are intact. No change of meaning.

### 5.5 — CLEAN
Changes: "you have misunderstood what is being said now" became "you misunderstand the point now being made"; "first" became "earlier"; "what is being said" became "what that point means". This is faithful to Long's "what is now said" and "spoke before". The rejected "You ask:" label and the reviewer's "the ones who count up favors" are sound rejections. The exchange is marked by dashes, "it may be said" and "What you say is true", and the listener can follow the gist: the objection is true, but it is misapplied.

### 5.7 — DEFECT
Changes: "in the whole" became "in all" (fine). The bodies/causes sentence became "all bodies together make the universe the body it is, so all existing causes together make necessity, or destiny, the cause it is" (faithful and clear). "happened to you" became "was done for you" (fine). The "Let … be, in your judgment" sentence became "Judge … to be…". That last change is faithful to Long's "judged by thee", but heard once it is a garden path. "what the nature of the universe judges good to be the same kind of thing as your own health" parses at first as the nature of the universe judging good things *to be* like your health. The second "judge" is the one that attaches to "to be".
Proposed replacement: "Judge that the completion and fulfillment of what the nature of the universe judges good is the same kind of thing as your own health."
The rejected "Nothing's nature" item is a sound rejection: the current sentence is clear.

### 5.8 — CLEAN
Changes: Long's three states are restored ("disgusted, or discouraged, or dissatisfied"). The objection is now marked with Long's own "it may be objected", and "this, the thing I am doing" became "what I am doing". It is faithful and clear. The "sponge and egg" rejection is sound.

### 5.9 — CLEAN
Changes: "all our assent is liable to change" became "whatever we assent to, we may change our minds about"; "no one can force me" became "there is no one who will force me". This is faithful to Long's "changeable" and "will". The rejections are sound: "breaking up" is the standard rendering, and the divine spirit is glossed at II.13.

### 5.11 — CLEAN
Changes: "Even from this" became "Even from the following". The comic poet's line is now identified at its first mention, and "the comic poet's line" became "that line of the comic poet" at the end. The line now appears twice, but it is the same line from Long, so nothing new is added. The forward-reference problem is resolved.

### 5.14 — CLEAN
Changes: the opening was restructured to "None of those things that do not belong to a person as a human being should be called his own"; "goes without" became "deprives himself of"; the "if" clause was moved forward. Long's contrast between voluntary deprivation and loss imposed by others is restored, and his conditional is kept. The antecedent is missing in Long too, and it is logged. The sentence now parses on one hearing.

### 5.15 — DEFECT
Change: an em dash now marks the palace objection. The content is fine, and the dash is right. The defect is typographic: there is a stray space before the dash ("live well. —But"). The whole edition uses unspaced dashes after end punctuation ("warm?—But", "such things.—But"), and this is the only spaced occurrence in `candidate.json`.
Proposed replacement (the affected span only): "wherever a person can live, he can also live well.—But he must live in a palace.—Very well, then he can also live well in a palace."
The rejection of the "tautology" finding is sound: the repetition is Long's.

### 5.16 — DEFECT
Change: "it is impossible that bad people should not do things of this kind" became "bad people cannot help doing things of this kind". This fixes the double negative but drops the repeated "impossible". In Long the repetition carries the argument: "To seek what is impossible is madness: and it is impossible that the bad should not…". Expecting bad people not to act badly is itself pursuing the impossible. The style standard says to keep repetitions that carry meaning. With "cannot help", the listener has to supply the link.
Proposed replacement: "17. To pursue the impossible is madness; and it is impossible for bad people not to do things of this kind."
This keeps the link and uses a simpler negative than the old "that … should not".

### 5.18 — CLEAN
Change: "—for itself" was appended, which restores Long's "for itself". Faithful. It is slightly loose aloud, but still clear. An optional smoother placement (not required): "it makes the things that come before it, for itself, into whatever those judgments say."

### 5.19 — CLEAN
Change: "act with a reservation (on condition that nothing prevents it)" became "act conditionally". This is Long's plain wording, and the added apparatus is removed. It resolves both the fidelity finding and the accessibility finding.

### 5.20 — CLEAN

### 5.25 — CLEAN
Change: the sentence was split. Long's "sympathy" is restored with the gloss "the shared feeling", and "when these feelings rise up" became "these feelings also rise up… When they do". The assertion is implicit in Long's temporal "when", and no route is invented. "Other" is kept, as it is in Long. With "sympathy … in a body which is all one" stated plainly, the listener can follow the thought (the feelings reach the mind because the body is one), so the rejected fuller explanation is not needed.

### 5.27 — CLEAN
Changes: "correct" became "admonish" (Long's word); "… " now comes before the fragment. The rejection of a supplied verb or gloss is sound. The anger argument is complete and followable before the break. The fragment is Long's own detached, bracketed note, and its meaning is disputed. Any verb would choose a reading. The ellipsis marks it as detached, and it is logged in `ambiguities-book05.md`.

### 5.28 — DEFECT
Change: "when you have left this life" became "once you have departed". The disposition and the ambiguities log both call "departed" neutral between "leaving life" and "leaving court or public life". In modern English it is not neutral. "Departed" is the standard euphemism for dying ("the departed"), so the change keeps the death reading and loses the retreat reading. It also does not answer the accessibility concern that the line "sounds like life after death".
Proposed replacement: "29. The way you intend to live once you have left … you can live that way here."
"Left" is as open as Long's "gone out". It also echoes the next sentence ("then leave life"), which is the evidence for the death reading, without settling it. Update the 5.28 row in `ambiguities-book05.md` to match.
The rejection of "as the saying goes" is sound: the smoky-house line follows the thought.

### 5.31 — CLEAN
Change: "a soul that has" became "the one who has". This is Long's "him". Clear.

### 5.32 — CLEAN
Changes: the three assertions became three "if" clauses, which restores Long's dependency; "only" was dropped; "it is to be put out or moved" became "that end is extinction or a move". Faithful ("extinction" is Long's). The sentence is long but is carried by the repeated "if".

### 5.33 — CLEAN
Change: "first," and "and second," were added. These are counting words only; Long announces "two things". No content was added.

### 5.35 — CLEAN
Changes: the objection became "—Yes, but these things matter a great deal to these people.—", and the reply became "Will you, then, be made a fool over these things too?". This is faithful to Long's "for these things" and "wilt thou". The rejection of the reviewer's additions is sound. The listener can follow the thought as Long gives it: losses in things neither good nor bad are like a child's top, and you should not be made a fool over them. The added moral and source labels would interpret an anecdote that Long leaves bare.

### Rejected findings on unchanged sections
- **5.12:** "For nothing prevents us from saying this, even if the universe is governed in fixed cycles." The concession is followable, and "repeating" would add doctrine. Not a defect.
- **5.13:** "Catorthoseis, or right acts: the word means…". The word is explained at once. Not a defect.
- **5.34:** the open "this" is anchored by "why am I troubled about it". The final question is Long's. Not a defect.

## Verdict
3 DEFECTS in content and 1 in typography. Everything else is VERIFIED CLEAN.
1. **5.7:** "Judge … to be the same kind of thing" is a garden path when heard. Use "Judge that the completion and fulfillment of what the nature of the universe judges good is the same kind of thing as your own health."
2. **5.15 (typography):** remove the stray space. Use "live well.—But he must live in a palace.—Very well".
3. **5.16:** "cannot help" drops Long's argument-bearing repeat of "impossible". Use "and it is impossible for bad people not to do things of this kind."
4. **5.28:** "departed" is not neutral; it means died. Use "once you have left …" and update the ambiguities log.

No rejected accessibility finding leaves a first-time listener unable to follow the thought.

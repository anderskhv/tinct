# Accessibility review — Book III (3.0–3.15)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 3.0, 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 3.6, 3.7, 3.8, 3.9, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12, 3.13, 3.14, 3.15.

## Findings

### 3.2 — BLOCKING
**Problem words:** "The vessel is as much inferior as the part that serves it is superior: for the one is intelligence and the divine; the other is earth and decay."
**Why a listener stumbles:** A listener hearing this once cannot sort it out. "The part that serves it" sounds as if the mind serves the body, which seems to contradict the sentence just before ("stop being a slave to the vessel"). "The one ... the other" then asks the listener to map two labels back onto a comparison they have already lost track of. Most people would miss the point: the body you are enslaved to is worth much less than the part of you that is enslaved to it.
**Proposed rewrite:** "The vessel you serve is as far beneath you as the part of you that serves it is above it: the part that serves is intelligence and the divine; the vessel is earth and decay."

**Secondary (minor) in the same section:**
- "If it is to another life, there is no lack of gods, not even there." "It" points back to "come to shore", which is a long way off, and "not even there" is a clumsy double negative. Proposed: "If you are landing in another life, there are gods there too, as there are everywhere."
- "Lice destroyed Democritus; and other lice killed Socrates." A newcomer will take the second "lice" literally and be puzzled. The figurative sense (vile men) is the thought. Proposed: "Lice destroyed Democritus; and lice of another kind, men, killed Socrates."

### 3.0 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "But the power to make use of ourselves, to fulfil the full measure of our duty, to sort out clearly everything that appears to us, to consider whether it is now time for a person to leave life, and whatever else of that kind absolutely requires a trained reason—all this has already gone out."
**Why a listener stumbles:** The sentence holds five items before its verb arrives, so a listener has forgotten the subject by the time they hear it. "Make use of ourselves" reads like a translation. "Gone out" (like a lamp) could be heard as "left the room". The last clause, "whatever else of that kind absolutely requires a trained reason", is also hard to parse.
**Proposed rewrite:** "But other powers have already gone out, like a lamp: the power to direct ourselves, to fulfil the full measure of our duty, to sort out clearly everything that appears to us, to judge whether it is now time for a person to leave life, and every other task of that kind that absolutely requires a trained reason."

### 3.1 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "Still, because they follow from things that nature forms, they help to adorn them and they please the mind."
**Why a listener stumbles:** The first "they" means the by-products, and "them" means the things nature forms. Heard aloud, the two pronouns blur together. Separately, "look at ... with chaste eyes" is slightly old-fashioned but still understandable.
**Proposed rewrite:** "Still, because these by-products follow from things that nature forms, they help to adorn those things, and they please the mind."

### 3.3 — MINOR
Several separate snags in a very long section:
- "make us wander away from watching over our own ruling faculty." This is the first appearance of "ruling faculty" in the packet, and nothing here says what it is. Proposed: "make us wander away from watching over our own ruling faculty, the mind that governs us."
- "we ought to check everything that is aimless and useless." "Check" can be heard as "inspect" when the sense here is "stop". Proposed: "we ought to hold back everything that is aimless and useless, and most of all whatever is over-curious or malicious."
- "For the lot assigned to each person is carried along with him, and carries him along with it." The idea is not stated clearly: it is hard to tell what carrying and being carried mean here. Proposed: "For the lot assigned to each person travels along with him through life, and at the same time carries him along with it."
- "Yet a person should hold on to the opinion not of everyone." "Hold on to the opinion" sounds like "adopt the belief" when the sense is "value the approval". Proposed: "Yet a person should care about the good opinion not of everyone, but only of those who clearly live in keeping with nature."

### 3.4 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "ready to go, and needing neither an oath nor anyone's testimony."
**Why a listener stumbles:** Without context, "oath" and "testimony" are puzzling here. A listener has to work out that the point is that his readiness needs no one to bind or vouch for it.
**Proposed rewrite:** "ready to go, needing no oath to bind him and no one's testimony to vouch for him."

### 3.5 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "But if it is useful to you only as an animal, say so, and keep to your judgment without arrogance."
**Why a listener stumbles:** "Say so" and "keep to your judgment" leave the listener unsure what is being said and which judgment is meant. The logic of the whole exchange is lost at the point where it lands. Also, "carefully examines every impression" uses the technical word "impression" for the first time, with no hint of what it means. Given the context, this is tolerable.
**Proposed rewrite:** "But if it is useful to you only as an animal, say so plainly, and hold to your judgment that the better thing comes first, without arrogance." *(If the reviewer's reading of "your judgment" is not what the source means, at minimum make clear which judgment is meant.)*

### 3.6 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "his own intelligence and divine spirit, and the worship of its excellence"
**Why a listener stumbles:** It is unclear whether "its" refers to the intelligence or the divine spirit, or to both, which are two things.
**Proposed rewrite:** "his own intelligence and divine spirit, and the worship of that spirit's excellence".

### 3.7 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "nothing too tightly bound to other things, nor yet cut off from them"
**Why a listener stumbles:** "Nor yet" is archaic, and a modern ear may hear "yet" as "so far".
**Proposed rewrite:** "nothing too tightly bound to other things, and nothing cut off from them".

### 3.8 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "Honor the faculty that forms opinions. It depends entirely on this faculty whether your ruling faculty will hold any opinion..."
**Why a listener stumbles:** This short section contains two different "faculties", and the listener cannot tell whether they are one thing or two. "Ruling faculty" is still unexplained from 3.3.
**Proposed rewrite:** "Honor your power of forming opinions. It depends entirely on this power whether your ruling faculty, the mind that governs you, will hold any opinion that is out of keeping with nature and with the makeup of a rational being."

### 3.10 — MINOR
- "Tell yourself its proper name, and the names of the things it has been put together from and into which it will break up again." The stacked prepositions are hard to follow when heard. Proposed: "Tell yourself its proper name, and the names of the parts it was made from and will break back into."
- "and how long it is in its nature to last—this thing that is now making an impression on me—and what virtue I need". The insertion between the dashes breaks up an already long list, and the switch from "a person" to "me" comes without warning. Proposed: "It means seeing, of this thing that is now making an impression on me, what it is, what it is made of, how long it is in its nature to last, and what virtue I need in dealing with it: ..."
- "by coincidence and chance of that kind." "Of that kind" has no clear antecedent. Proposed: "and by the coincidences and chances that destiny brings."

### 3.11 — MINOR
**Problem words:** The whole of the first sentence, which has two stacked "if" clauses and several embedded participles before "then you will live happily".
**Why a listener stumbles:** The main clause arrives so late that a listener loses track of the conditions.
**Proposed rewrite:** "Do the work in front of you by following right reason seriously, energetically and calmly, not letting anything else distract you, and keeping your divine part pure, as if you had to give it back at once. Hold to this, expecting nothing and fearing nothing, content with acting now in keeping with nature and with speaking heroic truth in every word and sound you utter. If you do, you will live happily."

### 3.12 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "for doing everything, even the smallest thing, with the bond that joins the divine and the human to each other in mind."
**Why a listener stumbles:** "In mind" comes at the end of a long phrase, so the listener first hears "doing everything ... with the bond" as if the bond were a tool.
**Proposed rewrite:** "and for doing everything, even the smallest thing, mindful of the bond that joins the divine and the human to each other."

### 3.14 — MINOR
**Problem words:** "how many things are meant by the words ..." and "For this is not done with the eyes".
**Why a listener stumbles:** "This" could point to the whole list or only to the last item. "How many things are meant" is slightly odd English. The section is cryptic by nature, which is acceptable, but the reference should at least be clear.
**Proposed rewrite:** "People do not know how many different things are meant by the words 'stealing,' 'sowing,' 'buying,' 'keeping quiet,' and 'seeing what ought to be done.' For that last kind of seeing is not done with the eyes, but with another kind of sight."

### 3.15 — MINOR
- "to men who have made themselves into women". A modern listener is likely to misread this as a reference to gender transition, and the old sense (men who have made themselves unmanly or soft) is lost. Proposed: "to men who have made themselves effeminate".
- "what remains that is special to the good person is this: to be pleased ... ; not to defile ... , but to keep it at peace, following it obediently as a god, saying nothing ... and doing nothing ...". This is a very long run of infinitives and participles. Proposed: split it after "spun for him": "... is this: to be pleased and content with what happens and with the thread that is spun for him. It is also not to defile the divine spirit planted in his breast, or disturb it with a crowd of images, but to keep it at peace, following it obediently as a god, saying nothing contrary to the truth and doing nothing contrary to justice."

## Sections that read clearly
3.9, 3.13

## Summary
- BLOCKING: 1 (3.2)
- MINOR: 13 (3.0, 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 3.6, 3.7, 3.8, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12, 3.14, 3.15)
- Clear: 2 (3.9, 3.13)

Verdict: **FAIL** (one blocking issue: the vessel/servant comparison at the end of 3.2). Otherwise the edition is mostly understandable when heard once. The recurring weaknesses are over-long sentences that delay the main verb (3.0, 3.11, 3.15), unclear pronouns (3.1, 3.6, 3.14), and "ruling faculty", which is never explained (3.3, 3.8).

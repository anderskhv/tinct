# Re-verification — Book II R2 changes
Coverage: every changed id: 2.2, 2.14, 2.16 (3 paragraphs). For each one I diffed BEFORE and AFTER word by word, checked the AFTER against Long and the neighbouring sections, compared it with the R1 re-verification defect it answers (`R1-reverify-book02.md`) and the disposition (`R1-applied-book02.md`), and checked it against `STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md`. In all three the section number ("3.", "15.", "17.") and the opening words are unchanged. No content comes in from a neighbouring section.

## Per paragraph

### 2.2 — CLEAN
Diff: in the final sentence, "cheerfully, and truly grateful to the gods from your heart" became "cheerfully, sincerely, and grateful to the gods from your heart". Nothing else changed.
- Long's three-part series ("cheerfully, truly, and from thy heart thankful"; Greek ἵλεως, ἀληθῶς, καὶ ἀπὸ καρδίας εὐχάριστος) is fully restored. "Sincerely" is a fair and clear rendering of ἀληθῶς, and "truly … from your heart" no longer says the same thing twice.
- The text is exactly the replacement proposed in R1, so the R1 defect is resolved.
- The series mixes adverbs with an adjective ("cheerfully, sincerely, and grateful"). The BEFORE did the same, and it mirrors Long's structure. It reads naturally aloud after "die grumbling". The earlier edits (fortune, providence, "plays its part", "settled judgments", and the providence gloss) are untouched and still follow the standard.

### 2.14 — CLEAN
Diff: "For " was added at the start of the second sentence ("For what the Cynic Monimus said is plain;"). Nothing else changed.
- This restores Long's connective "For what was said by the Cynic Monimus is manifest". The listener now hears why Monimus comes up: his saying is given as the ground for "everything is opinion". The "plain … plain too" pairing still matches Long's "manifest … manifest too". The qualification "as far as it is true" is kept, and the one-time gloss "what we judge things to be" matches the standard.
- The text is exactly the replacement proposed in R1, so the R1 defect is resolved.

### 2.16 — DEFECT (minor)
Diff: in the first sentence, "the whole body is made of stuff that rots" became "the whole body, as it is put together, is bound to rot". Nothing else changed.
- The echo that R1 flagged ("the stuff we are made of … made of stuff that rots") is gone. "As it is put together" renders Long's "composition" (σύγκρισις) and keeps it distinct from the flux of substance in the previous clause, so the finding is resolved in substance.
- **Defect:** the editor did not use R1's proposed "is subject to rot" and wrote "is bound to rot" instead. That changes the modality. Long's "subject to putrefaction" (Greek εὔσηπτος, "easily decaying, prone to rot") names a liability or tendency. "Bound to" states that the rot is certain. The shift is small, and it holds true of bodies. Even so, the standard asks us to keep Long's logic and modality exactly ("'no longer' stays 'no longer'"), and nothing in the source or the disposition supports making this stronger. "Prone to rot" is as easy to hear as "bound to rot" and stays faithful.
- Proposed replacement for the opening sentence: "17. In human life, time is a point; the stuff we are made of is always flowing; our perception is dim; the whole body, as it is put together, is prone to rot; the soul is a whirl; fortune is hard to predict; and fame has no judgment."

## Verdict
Not clean. There is 1 minor defect and no blocking ones. 2.2 and 2.14 are clean, and both match exactly the replacements proposed in R1.

1. **2.16**: "is bound to rot" makes Long's "subject to putrefaction" (εὔσηπτος) stronger than the source. Replace with "the whole body, as it is put together, is prone to rot" (or R1's "is subject to rot").

# Accessibility review — Book X (10.0–10.37)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 10.0, 10.1, 10.2, 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 10.6, 10.7, 10.8, 10.9, 10.10, 10.11, 10.12, 10.13, 10.14, 10.15, 10.16, 10.17, 10.18, 10.19, 10.20, 10.21, 10.22, 10.23, 10.24, 10.25, 10.26, 10.27, 10.28, 10.29, 10.30, 10.31, 10.32, 10.33, 10.34, 10.35, 10.36, 10.37 (all 38).

## Findings

### 10.0 — BLOCKING
"Will you never be full, lacking nothing, longing for nothing more, wanting nothing, **whether living or lifeless**, for the sake of enjoying pleasures?" A listener will hear "whether living or lifeless" as describing *you* ("whether you are alive or dead"), which makes no sense. It must refer to the *things* one might crave (living creatures or objects). The phrase also sits far from "wanting", so the link is lost.
Proposed: "Will you never be full, lacking nothing, longing for nothing more, wanting nothing, neither living creatures nor lifeless things, to enjoy as pleasures?"
Secondary (minor): "whatever pleases them and whatever they will give for the preservation of the perfect living being, the universe" is long and packs "them" (the gods) and "the perfect living being" into one clause. Proposed: "...that everything is well with you and will be well: whatever pleases the gods, and whatever they will give to preserve that perfect living being, the universe."

### 10.1 — MINOR
"Then do it and **accept it**" — "accept it" has no clear object (accept what your nature requires? accept the result?). Proposed: "Then do it, and welcome what it requires, provided that your nature as a living creature will not be made worse by it."

### 10.2 — MINOR
"do not complain either; for **it will perish once it has consumed you**." Two "it"s in quick succession; a listener may not realise "it" is the unbearable event, nor why this is a comfort. Proposed: "do not complain either; for once the thing has destroyed you, it will itself come to an end."
Also: "by thinking that it is either in your interest or your duty **to do so**" — "do so" (bear it) is far from its antecedent. Proposed: "...everything that your own opinion can make bearable and tolerable, once you think that bearing it is either in your interest or your duty."

### 10.5 — MINOR
"Whether the universe is a collection of atoms, **or nature is a system**" — the second half is not a clear alternative to the first for a newcomer. Proposed: "Whether the universe is a random collection of atoms or an ordered system of nature, let this be settled first..."
"All natures, indeed, have **this principle** in common" — "this principle" reaches back two sentences to "The whole contains nothing that is not to its own benefit"; a listener loses it. Proposed: "Every nature, indeed, shares this principle, that it contains nothing harmful to itself; but the nature of the universe has a further one..."

### 10.6 — BLOCKING
Final sentence: "And suppose that what your mother gave birth to **binds you very closely to that other part, the one with its own quality of change**: even so, this is no objection at all to what has been said." A first-time listener cannot tell what "that other part" is or what the objection being answered is. The preceding "So it is this, what came in, that changes, not what your mother gave birth to" is also hard to parse.
Proposed: "So what changes is this newly taken-in material, not what your mother gave birth to. And even suppose that what you were born with is bound very closely to that newer, ever-changing material: even so, this is no objection at all to what has been said."
Secondary (minor): "these parts are taken back into **the reason that governs the universe**" — a technical idea arriving unannounced. It is understandable only loosely; consider "taken back into the governing reason of the universe" with no further change. Also "Now if **this** were by nature both an evil and a necessity" — "this" = perishing/change; proposed "Now if this changing were, by nature, both an evil and a necessity for the parts...".

### 10.7 — MINOR
"go bravely into some corner where you will keep hold of them" — "corner" in this sense (a place of retreat) is odd but reachable; acceptable. The real snag is "To help you remember these names, however, **it will help you greatly** to remember the gods" — double "help/remember" is awkward. Proposed: "It will greatly help you to remember these names if you remember the gods: that they do not want to be flattered, but want all rational beings to become like them."
"If you are able to stay in them, stay there" — "in them" (in these names) is a little abstract; proposed "If you are able to live up to them, stay there, as if..."

### 10.8 — BLOCKING
"Farce, war, panic, sluggishness, slavery: day by day these will wipe out those sacred principles of yours. **How many things do you picture to yourself without studying nature, and how many do you let slip?**" The list is unexplained but tolerable; the second sentence is not reachable: "picture to yourself without studying nature" and "let slip" (forget? neglect?) give the listener no clear thought.
Proposed: "How many things do you form ideas about without studying their nature, and how many do you let slip from your mind?"
Secondary (minor): the next sentence nests three results plus "kept up, not shown off, and yet not hidden either". Proposed split: "But you ought to look at everything and do everything so that, at one and the same time, your power of dealing with circumstances is perfected and your power of contemplation is exercised. And keep up the confidence that comes from knowing each individual thing: do not show it off, yet do not hide it either."

### 10.9 — MINOR
"another bears, another **Sarmatians**" — the point (the last hunter is catching *people*, i.e. a war of conquest) is lost if the listener does not know Sarmatians are a people. Proposed: "another bears, another Sarmatian warriors." Also "If you examine their judgments" — "judgments" = their guiding principles; proposed "If you examine the principles they act on, are they not all robbers?"

### 10.10 — MINOR
"**Such a person** has stripped off the body." The antecedent is implied (one who has gained greatness of soul), and "stripped off the body" arrives abruptly. Proposed: "A person who has this greatness of soul has, in effect, stripped off the body."

### 10.11 — MINOR
"What need is there for **anxious suspicion**" — "suspicion" in the old sense of anxious guessing; a modern ear hears distrust of people. Proposed: "What need is there for anxious guessing, when it is in your power to find out what ought to be done?"
"For it is best to reach **this goal**" — which goal? Proposed: "For it is best to succeed in acting justly; and if you do fail, let your failure be in the attempt."

### 10.12 — BLOCKING
"As soon as you wake from sleep, ask yourself whether it will make any difference to you if someone else does what is just and right. It will make no difference." A first-time listener cannot see what the question is about or how it connects to the next sentence about people who "hand out praise or blame". The implied thought (that it should not matter to you whether others praise or blame right action, given what those judges are like) is not reachable as written.
Proposed (keeping every element, only making the link explicit): "As soon as you wake from sleep, ask yourself whether it will make any difference to you if someone else [criticises / takes credit for — check source] what is just and right. It will make no difference. For you have not forgotten, I suppose, what they are like in bed and at table, these people who put on arrogant airs as they hand out praise or blame..." (Flag for the source-checker: confirm which reading is intended before rewording.)
Secondary (minor): "a good divine spirit within, which is to say, happiness" — first appearance in this packet of "divine spirit within"; the gloss "which is to say, happiness" helps but the idea is compressed. Acceptable if the term has been introduced in earlier books.

### 10.14 — MINOR
"if everywhere he lives in the world **as in a state, one political community**" — "state" in the sense of city-state is not how a modern listener hears it. Proposed: "if everywhere he lives in the world as a citizen of one city, one political community."

### 10.16 — MINOR
"the whole of **substance**" / "every individual thing, **in substance**, is a fig seed" — "substance" as the philosophical term for all matter. Proposed: "Constantly keep in view the whole of time and the whole of matter, and consider that every individual thing, measured against all matter, is a fig seed, and measured against all time, a single turn of a hand drill."

### 10.18 — MINOR
"Yet a short time ago **they were slaves to how many, and for what things!**" — translationese exclamation; unclear "how many" what? Proposed: "Yet only a short time ago, how many masters they were slaving for, and for what petty ends!"

### 10.20 — MINOR
"'the solemn sky loves'" — the quoted fragment has no object, so the listener asks "loves what?". The closing wordplay ("'loves' to happen, that is, usually happens") depends on an idiom a modern listener will not recognise. Proposed for the last sentence: "And is this not also what we mean when we say that this or that 'loves' to happen, meaning that it tends to happen?"

### 10.21 — MINOR
"or you are **going away**, and that was your own will" — ambiguous: leaving this place, or leaving life? Since the next clause is "or you are dying", "going away" probably means leaving by choice. Proposed: "or you are leaving of your own accord, and that was your own will" (or "choosing to leave life, and that was your own will" if the source means that — check).

### 10.22 — MINOR
"that **this piece of land** is like any other" — "this" has no referent. And "For you will find just what Plato says: living within the walls of a city as in a shepherd's fold on a mountain" — the quote is a fragment and does not say what it proves. Proposed: "Always keep it clear to yourself that the place where you are is like any other... For you will find it just as Plato says: living within the walls of a city is like living in a shepherd's fold on a mountain."

### 10.23 — MINOR
"What is my **ruling faculty** to me now?" — technical term, first appearance in this packet, unglossed; and "What is X to me" is an awkward construction. Proposed: "What is my ruling faculty, the mind that governs me, doing for me now?"

### 10.24 — MINOR
The third sentence is long and nested ("dissatisfied because something has happened, or is happening, or will happen, among the things appointed by the one who rules all things; and he is Law, and assigns..."). Proposed split: "And whoever is grieved, or angry, or afraid is dissatisfied with something that has happened, is happening, or will happen. But these things are appointed by the one who rules all things; he is Law, and he assigns to each person what is fitting."

### 10.26 — MINOR
"the whole court of **Hadrianus**... of **Philippus**" — Latinized forms read as translation; a modern listener knows "Hadrian" and "Philip". Proposed: "the whole court of Hadrian, the whole court of Antoninus, and the whole court of Philip, of Alexander, of Croesus."

### 10.27 — MINOR
"silently laments **the bonds that hold us**" — which bonds? (the constraints of our lot/fate). Proposed: "silently laments the bonds of fate that hold us."

### 10.29 — MINOR
"For if you attend to this, you will quickly forget your anger, **if you also add this thought**" — two "if"s tangle the sentence; and "he is under compulsion" — compelled by what? Proposed: "If you attend to this, and also add the thought that he is under compulsion (for what else could he do, believing what he believes?), you will quickly forget your anger." (If "believing what he believes" is not in the source, drop it and keep the rest.)

### 10.30 — MINOR
The name-pairs are content and must stay, but the listener is never told *why* each pair goes together (they resemble one another). Hearing seven obscure names in a row, the thought is lost until "where are those men now?". Proposed opening: "When you have seen Satyron the Socratic, think of someone like him from the past, Eutyches or Hymen; ..." — or, minimally, move "Do the same in the case of everyone" to follow a short framing phrase. Also "What material and what opportunity for your own activity are you running away from?" is fine.

### 10.32 — BLOCKING
"or else they do not crush us or do any harm of any kind, except through opinion and through reason itself giving way. **For if they did, the one who felt it would at once become bad.**" "they did" (did what — harm us?), "the one who felt it" (felt what?), and why this proves the point are all unclear; the argument (real harm would make the victim worse, but humans can become better through misfortune) is lost on one hearing.
Proposed: "For if they really did harm us, whoever suffered them would at once be made bad."
Secondary (minor): the opening "What is it, in this material we have to work with, our life, that can be done or said..." is awkwardly interrupted. Proposed: "In the material we have to work with, our life, what can be done or said in the way most in keeping with reason?" Also "the body, which is a dead thing" is fine.

### 10.34 — MINOR
"The healthy stomach **ought to be toward all food as a mill is toward** everything it is made to grind" — translationese. Proposed: "The healthy stomach ought to take all food as a mill takes everything it is made to grind." And "teeth looking for soft things" introduces teeth without prior mention; acceptable, but could be "or healthy teeth that ask only for soft things".

### 10.35 — MINOR
"I am parted from them as from my kin, **not dragged away resisting, but without being forced**" — the two halves say the same thing, so the "not... but" contrast confuses. Proposed: "I am parted from them as from my kin, not dragged away resisting, but going freely."

### 10.37 — MINOR
"That is **the power of persuasion**" — a first-time listener cannot see why the hidden inner thing is "persuasion" (the inner power that speaks and moves us). Proposed: "That is the power that speaks and persuades; that is life..." (check source). "the vessel that surrounds you and the instruments attached to it" is reachable (body and limbs); optional: "the body that encloses you and the limbs attached to it".

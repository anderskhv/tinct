# Fidelity review — Book V (5.0–5.35)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 5.0–5.35, all 36 paragraphs, each compared sentence by sentence against Long (1862) and checked against STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 5.0 — MINOR
- Source: "choose neither to eat nor to sleep rather than to perfect the things which they care for."
- Candidate: "they would rather go without food and sleep than stop working at what they care about."
- Problem: Long's "perfect" (bring to completion, advance) becomes "stop working at", which drops the idea that they are trying to *perfect* their work.
- Proposed: "When such people are gripped by a passion for something, they would rather go without food and sleep than fail to perfect what they care about."

### 5.5 — MINOR
- Source: "thou wilt become one of those of whom I spoke before"
- Candidate: "you will become one of those I spoke of first"
- Problem: "before" (earlier) means the people described earlier, and that plausibly includes both the first and the second type. "first" narrows it to the first type only.
- Proposed: "and because of that you will become one of those I spoke of earlier; for they too are led astray by something that looks like reason."

### 5.7 — MINOR
- Source: "the one, because it was done for thee and prescribed for thee"
- Candidate: "The first is that it happened to you, was prescribed for you"
- Problem: "done for thee" means it happened *for your sake*, not just to you. That sense matters to the argument, which says the event was arranged with reference to you. "happened to you" weakens it.
- Proposed: "The first is that it was done for you, was prescribed for you, and was in some way related to you, spun into your destiny from the beginning out of the most ancient causes."

### 5.7 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "For there is altogether one fitness [harmony]."
- Candidate: "For in the whole there is only one fitting together, one harmony."
- Note: Long's "altogether" is an adverb ("in all, taken all together": Greek ὅλως). The candidate reads it as "in the whole (universe)". Both readings give the same Stoic point, so this is acceptable. A closer option: "For in all there is only one fitting together, one harmony."

### 5.8 — MINOR
- Source: "Be not disgusted, nor discouraged, nor dissatisfied"
- Candidate: "Do not be disgusted, or give up, or lose heart"
- Problem: "discouraged" becomes "give up", which names an action rather than a feeling. "dissatisfied" (vexed, impatient) becomes "lose heart", which is really a synonym for discouraged. As a result the third member, dissatisfaction, is lost.
- Proposed: "Do not be disgusted, or discouraged, or impatient with yourself, if you do not manage to do everything according to right principles."

### 5.9 — MINOR
- Source: "for there is no man who will compel me to this."
- Candidate: "for no one can force me to do that."
- Problem: the modality changes from "will" (there is no one who will compel) to "can". The meaning is close, but it is not Long's.
- Proposed: "for there is no one who will force me to do that."

### 5.14 — MINOR
- Source: "the more of these things a man deprives himself of, or of other things like them, or even when he is deprived of any of them, the more patiently he endures the loss"
- Candidate: "the more of these things, or of others like them, a person goes without, or the more patiently he bears being deprived of any of them"
- Problem: Long contrasts voluntary self-deprivation ("deprives himself of") with loss imposed by others ("is deprived of"). "goes without" blurs the voluntary side of that contrast.
- Proposed: "But as it is, the more of these things, or of others like them, a person strips himself of, or the more patiently he bears being deprived of any of them, the better a person he is, in exactly that degree."

### 5.18 — MINOR
- Source: "such it makes for itself the things which present themselves to it."
- Candidate: "it makes the things that come before it into whatever those judgments say."
- Problem: "for itself" is dropped. It carries the point that things take on that character *for the soul*, not in themselves.
- Proposed: "and whatever judgments it sees fit to make, it makes the things that come before it into what those judgments say, for itself."

### 5.19 — MINOR
- Source: "which have the power of acting conditionally and changing"
- Candidate: "which can act with a reservation (on condition that nothing prevents it) and can change course."
- Problem: the parenthesis is an interpretive gloss on the Stoic "reservation" that the style guide does not prescribe. It is accurate, but it is added apparatus in parenthetical form.
- Proposed: "but they do not hinder my impulses or my disposition, which can act conditionally and can change course."

### 5.27 — MINOR
- Source: "show him his error, admonish him."
- Candidate: "show him his mistake, correct him."
- Problem: "admonish" (Greek ὑπομίμνησκε: remind, put him in mind) becomes "correct", which suggests setting him right rather than warning or reminding him.
- Proposed: "Use your reason to stir up his; show him his mistake, remind him of it."

### 5.28 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "As thou intendest to live when thou art gone out,... so it is in thy power to live here."
- Candidate: "The way you intend to live when you have left this life … you can live that way here."
- Note: Long's "gone out" is unspecified, and the text is lacunose (Long's ellipsis). "left this life" is one reading. The other is "gone away" from here, to a retreat away from court and public life, which fits the "here" contrast. The following sentence ("get away out of life") favours the candidate's reading but does not settle it. Log this in AMBIGUITIES.md. A neutral option: "The way you intend to live once you have gone away from here … you can live that way here."

### 5.31 — MINOR
- Source: "Why do unskilled and ignorant souls disturb him who has skill and knowledge?"
- Candidate: "Why do unskilled and ignorant souls disturb a soul that has skill and knowledge?"
- Problem: Long's "him" (the skilled person) becomes "a soul". The change fits the next question, but it is still a change of who is disturbed.
- Proposed: "Why do unskilled and ignorant souls disturb the one who has skill and knowledge?"

### 5.32 — MINOR
- Source: "What then is there which still detains thee here, if the objects of sense are easily changed and never stand still, and the organs of perception are dull … and the poor soul itself is an exhalation from blood?"
- Candidate: "What is it, then, that still keeps you here? The things the senses perceive are easily changed … and the poor soul itself is only a vapor rising from the blood."
- Problem: the "if" (Greek εἴγε, "if indeed / seeing that") becomes three free-standing assertions. That breaks the logical dependency: in the source, the question is asked *given* these facts. The added "only" also sharpens "is an exhalation" slightly.
- Proposed: "What is it, then, that still keeps you here, if the things the senses perceive are easily changed and never stand still, if the organs of perception are dim and easily take in false impressions, and if the poor soul itself is a vapor rising from the blood?"

### 5.35 — MINOR
- Source: "wilt thou too then be made a fool for these things?"
- Candidate: "Must you, then, be made a fool for their sake too?"
- Problem: "for these things" (the trivial objects) becomes "for their sake" (the people), which changes what the fool is made a fool over. "wilt thou" also becomes "Must you".
- Proposed: "Will you, then, be made a fool over these things too?"

Notes on checks that passed:
- 5.2 renders "leading principle" and "movement" as "ruling faculty" and "impulses", and "common nature" as "nature of the universe", as the style guide requires.
- 5.3's "air" and "last breath" make explicit what Long's "that element" plainly refers to.
- 5.4 keeps all ten qualities and all seven vices.
- 5.12's short form/matter gloss is accurate.
- 5.30 keeps all nine relations.
- 5.35's "Rostra, the speakers' platform" gloss is allowed under Proper names. "good impulses" for Long's "good emotions" follows the ὁρμή rule, so it is not flagged under 5.35.

# Re-verification — Book II R1 changes
Coverage: every changed id: 2.0, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.10, 2.11, 2.12, 2.13, 2.14, 2.15, 2.16 (13 paragraphs). For each one I diffed BEFORE and AFTER word by word, checked the AFTER against Long and the neighbouring sections, compared it with the finding it answers in `R1-applied-book02.md`, and checked it against `STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md`. All 13 AFTER texts keep their section number (1–17 as before) and open with content equivalent to the source's opening. No content comes in from a neighbouring section. The AFTER texts match `candidate.json`.

## Per paragraph

### 2.0 — CLEAN
Change: the only edit is the added word "only" ("not only because … but because"). This follows Long's "[only]" and resolves the fidelity ambiguity. "Not only … but" with no "also" is idiomatic.

### 2.1 — CLEAN
Changes: "Think of it this way:" became "Consider this:", and "man. Do" became "man, so do". "Consider this" is Long's "consider thus". The added "so" states the link from old age to the commands, which Long's run of clauses after "Thou art an old man;" already implies. This resolves the accessibility finding.

### 2.2 — DEFECT (minor)
Changes: "chance" became "fortune"; "from there" became "from providence"; "has its part" became "plays its part"; "convictions" became "judgments"; "cheerfully, truly, and grateful" became "cheerfully, and truly grateful".
- The changes to fortune, plays, and judgments are correct and consistent with 2.16 and with the opinion/judgment row of the standard.
- "Everything flows from providence" is acceptable. Long's "thence" most naturally points to providence, the order that fortune is interwoven with. The disposition calls providence "the only antecedent", but that is inaccurate: "nature" and "the gods" also stand in Long's sentences. This choice resolves a reference that is genuinely open, and it is defensible. It is not a defect.
- **Defect:** Long's closing is a three-part series of how to die: "cheerfully, truly, and from thy heart thankful" (Greek ἵλεως, ἀληθῶς, καὶ ἀπὸ καρδίας εὐχάριστος). The AFTER folds "truly" into "grateful". That drops a member of the series, which the standard forbids ("rather than drop any member"). It also makes "truly … from your heart" say the same thing twice, and the comma in "cheerfully, and truly grateful" is odd when heard aloud. The accessibility reviewer's actual concern was that "truly" was unclear on its own. That is solved by giving the word a clear rendering, not by merging it.
- Proposed replacement for the last sentence: "But throw away your thirst for books, so that you do not die grumbling, but cheerfully, sincerely, and grateful to the gods from your heart."

### 2.3 — CLEAN
Change: "putting these things off" became "putting all this off". Long's "these things" points back to the earlier sections, so the edit invents no antecedent. "All this" is natural as a general reference and reads well aloud.

### 2.4 — CLEAN
Changes: "unaffected" became "unpretentious" (Long's "simple", Greek ἄπλαστος, "unfeigned"). The long sentence is split with "And set it on freeing yourself…", and nothing is lost in the split. The sufficient condition is restored ("whoever holds on to them is able to live…"), which exactly resolves the fidelity finding.

### 2.5 — CLEAN
Changes: the opening became "Go on, keep wronging yourself, my soul, keep wronging yourself!", which keeps the doubled imperative and the address. "You will no longer have the chance" restores Long's flat modality. "Each person's life is enough" is Long's reading only, as the ambiguity note requires. "Still you do not respect yourself, but make your happiness depend…" is consistent because the soul is the one being addressed ("thy soul reverences not itself"), and it removes the my-soul/your-soul confusion. Both findings are resolved.

### 2.10 — CLEAN
Change: "If such things were bad, the nature of the universe could not have let them slip past it". The conditional is supported. Long's sentence is counterfactual (Greek παρεῖδεν ἄν), and it continues directly from "if there was anything evil, they would have provided for this also". The clause makes explicit a step the source implies and does not add content. It fixes the vague "such things". The rejection of the I/you finding is correct, because Long has "what is it to me".

### 2.11 — CLEAN
Changes: the opening is now "the bodies themselves vanish into the universe, and even the memory of them vanishes into time". This keeps Long's parallel ("in the universe … in time") and is easier to follow by ear. "Even" was already in the BEFORE. The last item is now "And it should observe how … through what part of himself he does so, and in what condition that part is when he does". This restores the list of tasks for "the intellectual faculty" and settles the loose "it". Both findings are resolved.

### 2.12 — CLEAN
Changes: "goes around and around" became "runs around in circles after everything". This is Long's "traverses everything in a round", and it gives the sense of restless, aimless prying. The added gloss is "—the guiding mind that is his share of the divine—". Marcus's own definition at V.27 supports it: the daemon "which Zeus hath given to every man for his guardian and guide, a portion of himself. And this is every man's understanding and reason." It also echoes "portion of the divine" in 2.0. Note for the editor: the style table says the definition is followed "at V.27". This gloss now comes at the first use in II.13, so the table row should say that the gloss is given once here and that V.27 follows Marcus's own wording. When later books render the term, they should not re-gloss it with a different phrase, and in particular not in a way that competes with "ruling faculty".

### 2.13 — CLEAN
Changes: "present" became "present moment", and "what passes away is not the same" became "the time that has passed away is not the same for all". Long's "that which perish[es] is not the same" is obscure. Reading it as the amount of time already gone, which differs from person to person, is the reading the argument needs: what is lost at death is the equal present moment. This narrows an obscure phrase without adding a claim. It resolves the accessibility finding.

### 2.14 — DEFECT (minor)
Changes: "makes this plain" became "is plain", and "the use of his saying is plain too" became "plain too is how useful his saying is". The "manifest … manifest too" repetition and Long's logic are restored, so the fidelity finding is resolved.
- **Defect:** the edit drops Long's connective "For" ("For what was said by the Cynic Monimus is manifest"), which the fidelity reviewer's proposal kept. "For" is the only word that ties Monimus's saying to "everything is opinion". Without it, a first-time listener hears "What the Cynic Monimus said is plain" with no idea why Monimus comes up. This is a small omission, and it partly recreates the accessibility problem.
- Proposed replacement: "15. Remember that everything is opinion—what we judge things to be. For what the Cynic Monimus said is plain; and plain too is how useful his saying is, if you take from it what it has to offer, as far as it is true."

### 2.15 — DEFECT (minor, standard conflict)
Changes: "and some part of nature holds…" became "nature, which holds within it, each in its part, the natures of all other things". This restores Long's relative clause and the Greek sense (ἧς ἐν μέρει … περιέχονται). It makes the reasoning from resentment to separation audible, and it resolves the BLOCKING accessibility finding and the fidelity finding. "Plays a part" became "puts on an act", which is correct (ὑποκρίνηται) and resolves the mishearing. These edits are clean.
- **Defect:** the added gloss "—the universe itself" is supported by Marcus's own words (IV.4: "the world is in a manner a state"; III.11, "the highest city"). However, it directly contradicts the standard's row for "the city and polity (the cosmos)": "kept as Marcus's image … Not glossed where he does not gloss it." Here he does not gloss it. The text and the standard have to agree before later books meet this image again.
- Proposed resolution (preferred, because it keeps the accessibility fix): keep the AFTER text unchanged and amend the standard's row to read: "'the oldest city and commonwealth' — kept as Marcus's image; glossed once at first use (II.16) as '—the universe itself' (Marcus's own identification, IV.4, III.11); not glossed elsewhere." Alternative if the standard stands: revert the last clause to "…the reason and the law of the oldest city and commonwealth of all."

### 2.16 — DEFECT (minor)
Changes: "our substance" became "the stuff we are made of". "Fame is without judgment" became "fame has no judgment", which is Long's "devoid of judgment" (ἄκριτος) and resolves the ambiguity. The nested source clause became "the same source he himself came from, wherever that source may be". That keeps "wherever it is" and is clearer. These edits are correct.
- **Defect (introduced by the change):** the new first clause now sits two clauses before the unchanged "the whole body is made of stuff that rots". The listener hears "the stuff we are made of is always flowing; … the whole body is made of stuff that rots". The same words are repeated in close succession. That sounds clumsy, and it blurs Long's two distinct items: the flux of substance (οὐσία) and the perishable composition of the body (σύγκρισις). Fix the second clause so that the accessibility gain in the first is kept.
- Proposed replacement for the opening sentence: "17. In human life, time is a point; the stuff we are made of is always flowing; our perception is dim; the whole body, as it is put together, is subject to rot; the soul is a whirl; fortune is hard to predict; and fame has no judgment."

## Verdict
Not clean. There are 4 minor defects and no blocking ones. 9 of the 13 changed paragraphs are clean (2.0, 2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.10, 2.11, 2.12, 2.13). Every finding the editor claimed to resolve is resolved in substance, including the BLOCKING accessibility finding at 2.15.

1. **2.2**: folding "truly" into "grateful" drops a member of Long's three-part series. Replace with "…but cheerfully, sincerely, and grateful to the gods from your heart."
2. **2.14**: Long's connective "For" is dropped. Replace with "For what the Cynic Monimus said is plain; …"
3. **2.15**: the gloss "—the universe itself" contradicts the standard's "not glossed" rule for the city/commonwealth image. Amend the standard row (preferred) or revert the gloss.
4. **2.16**: the new "the stuff we are made of" echoes "made of stuff that rots". Replace the latter with "the whole body, as it is put together, is subject to rot".

Housekeeping (not a text defect): update the "divine spirit within" row of the standard so it records the one-time gloss at II.13 (see 2.12).

| 10.0 | "the conservation of the perfect living being, the good and just and beautiful" | "the preservation of the perfect living being, the universe? That being is good and just and beautiful." | Long does not name the "perfect living being"; the short gloss "the universe" follows Marcus's own "one living being" (IV.40) and Long's "generates and holds together all things". Alternative: no gloss. |
| 10.5 | "Whether the universe is [a concourse of] atoms, or nature [is a system]" | "Whether the universe is a collection of atoms, or nature is a system" | Long's supplements absorbed; no stronger reading (e.g. "random" / "ordered") imposed. |
| 10.6 | "taken back into the universal reason, whether this at certain periods is consumed by fire or renewed by eternal changes" | "whether the universe is consumed by fire at fixed periods or renewed by everlasting changes" | "this" may be the universal reason or the universe; the universe taken (Stoic periodic conflagration). Alternative: "whether that reason is consumed by fire…". |
| 10.6 | "suppose that this [which thy mother brought forth] implicates thee very much with that other part, which has the peculiar quality [of change]" | "suppose that what your mother gave birth to does bind you very closely to that other part, the incoming one with its own quality of change" | Obscure in Long and the Greek. Long's brackets followed ("that other part" = the incoming, changing part). Alternative: "peculiar quality" = the individual's distinctive character, not change. |
| 10.8 | "Mimi, war, astonishment, torpor, slavery" | "Farce, war, panic, sluggishness, slavery" | Greek μῖμος: mime-shows / farce, or play-acting / sham; some editors emend to μῖσος ("hatred"). "Astonishment" (πτοία, fluttering alarm): "panic" slightly stronger; "alarm" is closer. |
| 10.12 | "a good daemon [happiness]" | "a good divine spirit within, which is to say, happiness" | Eudaimonia wordplay; Long's bracket absorbed with the standard term. |
| 10.14 | "Let me see, let them know a real man who lives according to nature." | "Let people see, let them come to know, a true human being who lives in keeping with nature." | Source misprint: Long prints "Let me see"; the Greek (ἰδέτωσαν … οἱ ἄνθρωποι) and Long's own "let them know" / "If they cannot endure him" require "Let men see". Corrected. |
| 10.14 | "as in a state [political community]" | "as in one city, one political community" | Long's bracket absorbed; "city" (polis) for "state" so that the cosmic-city image is heard. |
| 10.20 | "this or that loves [is wont] to be produced" | "this or that 'loves' to happen, that is, usually happens" | Pun on Greek φιλεῖ (loves / tends to); cannot be carried fully in English. The poet's lines are unattributed in Long; none given. |
| 10.21 | "or thou art going away, and this was thy own will" | "or you are going away, and that was your own will" | Open whether Marcus means leaving this place or withdrawing from life by choice; Long's openness kept. |
| 10.22 | "Dwelling within the walls of a city as in a shepherd's fold on a mountain. | 10.32 | "harm law [order]" | "harms law, that is, order" | Long's bracket absorbed; "state" kept as Long has it. |

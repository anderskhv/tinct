# Re-verification — Book I R1 changes
Coverage: every changed id: 1.3, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 1.9, 1.10, 1.11, 1.12, 1.13, 1.14, 1.15, 1.16 (14 paragraphs). For each one I diffed BEFORE and AFTER word by word and checked the AFTER against Long's section and its neighbours. I also compared each change with the finding it answers in `R1-applied-book01.md` and checked it against `STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md`. All 14 AFTER texts keep their section number (4–17) and their opening words. No content comes in from a neighbouring section. Each AFTER text matches `candidate.json` and `drafts/book01.json`.

## Per paragraph

### 1.3 — CLEAN
Changes: "gone" became "been sent", "public schools" became "public schools open to everyone", and "things like this" became "an education like this". The meaning is still Long's ("not to have frequented public schools … on such things … spend liberally"). "Such things" clearly refers back to the teaching at home. The brief gloss removes the British "public school" misreading. This resolves the accessibility finding.

### 1.4 — CLEAN
Change: the lexical gloss "the light-shield and heavy-shield fighters" was added after Parmularius/Scutarius. The gloss is accurate: *parma* is a small shield and *scutum* the large one. It only says what the two names mean and does not interpret them, and it keeps the parallel with the Green/Blue racing factions. This resolves the accessibility finding.

### 1.5 — CLEAN
Changes: "evil spirits" became "spirits"; "keep" became "breed"; "and" was added before "to put up with"; and the perfect-infinitive list was recast as "It was through him, too, that I became … and heard …; that I wrote …; and that I wanted …". Every Long item is still there: Bacchius, Tandasis, Marcianus, dialogues, plank bed and skin, and the Greek discipline. "Through him" is a fair reading of the "From Diognetus" frame. This resolves the fidelity finding (both parts) and the accessibility finding.

### 1.6 — CLEAN
Changes: "idea" became "impression", in line with the standard and with "impressions" in 1.16. The gloss "a showman of clever speeches" was added after "sophist". The long sentence was split with "I learned from him to keep away…". The gloss gives only the word's meaning, and the same term recurs in 1.15 and 1.16. Every list member is kept. This resolves both findings.

### 1.7 — CLEAN
Changes: "to look to nothing but reason, not even for a moment" became "never, even for a moment, to look to anything but reason", and "lowered" became "humbled", which is Long's word. The meaning is exact, and the negation is now easy to follow by ear.

### 1.8 — CLEAN
Changes: the gloss "that is, as nature made a human being to live" was added, and "free from passion" became "free from passion—from disturbing emotion—". The "disturbing emotion" gloss matches the standard's passion/disturbance row. The nature gloss is supported by Marcus's own words ("In doing what man's nature requires", VIII.1; "the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world", V.1), and the phrase "made a human being" also takes in universal nature. Advisory, not a defect: the disposition does not cite this support. I suggest adding the V.1/VIII.1 citation to the log. This resolves the accessibility finding.

### 1.9 — CLEAN
Change: "discussing" became "joining them in looking into". This restores Long's "joining in an inquiry" and the shared act the fidelity finding pointed to. It reads naturally.

### 1.10 — CLEAN
Changes: "how much … there is" became "what … are like", and "fatherly affection" became "natural, fatherly affection". This resolves the BLOCKING fidelity finding, because it now describes the kind of vice and not its amount. "Natural" matches the Greek *astorgoteroi*/*philostorgia* (natural affection) and does not narrow Long's "paternal". The historical gloss on Patricians was not added, which fits the "no historical explanation" rule. This partly addresses the accessibility concern, and that is acceptable.

### 1.11 — DEFECT (minor)
Changes: the clause was reordered to "not to say often to anyone, or write in a letter, without real need, that I have no time".
- Problem: Long gives two separate prohibitions over both verbs, "not frequently nor without necessity to say … or to write". In the AFTER, "often" now attaches only to "say". A listener can also take "often … without real need" as one combined condition (saying it often *and* needlessly), which loses Long's either/or. The object "that I have no time" is still split from its verb by two inserted phrases, so the accessibility finding is only partly resolved.
- Proposed replacement: "12. From Alexander the Platonist: not to say to anyone, or write in a letter, that I have no time, either often or without real need; and not to keep excusing myself from the duties we owe to the people we live with by pleading urgent business."

### 1.12 — CLEAN
Changes: "finds fault with me" became "finds fault"; "my teachers" became "teachers"; and "as Domitius and Athenodotus are said to have done" became "as is reported of Domitius and Athenodotus". This is now Long's neutral wording, and both readings stay open, as the ambiguity note asked. "Shrug it off" still clearly refers to the fault-finding.

### 1.13 — CLEAN
Change: "keep up good hopes" became "stay hopeful". This faithfully renders Long's "cherish good hopes", and it is idiomatic.

### 1.14 — DEFECT (minor)
Changes: "seemed like" became "gave the impression of"; "rather than one who had needed correcting" became "not of a man who had had to be set right".
- Problem: Long says "the appearance of a man who could not be diverted from right, **rather than** of a man who had been improved" (Greek μᾶλλον … ἤ). This is a comparison, and "not of" turns it into a flat denial, which drops the qualification. "Had had to be" also sounds clumsy when read aloud.
- Proposed replacement for the sentence: "He gave the impression of a man who could not be turned from what was right, rather than of a man who had needed to be set right."

### 1.15 — CLEAN
Changes:
- "go with him when he went out" became "travel with him when he went away". This matches *synapodēmein*, and "any obligation" still covers "of necessity".
- "found him the same" became "found him unchanged toward them".
- "mean" became "petty or base".
- "flippant house-born slave" became "pert, flippant slave raised in the house". "Pert" is a near-synonym that makes the tone clear and adds no new content.
- "institutions" became "ancestral customs", which is correct for πάτρια.
- "and mostly from Lanuvium" became "and from Lanuvium generally". This is Long's exact wording, it is logged in `AMBIGUITIES.md`, and no reading is chosen.
- "nothing, as they say, pushed to the point of a sweat" became "nothing forced, nothing, as they say, carried to the point of sweating". "Nothing forced" restates the meaning of the idiom beside it. It is not a new claim.
- The Socrates comparison was recast as two parallel clauses: "hold out when one lacks such things … stay sober when one has them". Long's single set of things stays intact through "such things", and "the one … the other" now maps correctly onto going without and having plenty.

This resolves the BLOCKING accessibility finding and the other accessibility and fidelity items. Nothing is lost: Lorium, Lanuvium, Tusculum, Maximus and Socrates are all present.

### 1.16 — CLEAN
Changes:
- "any of them" became "any of the gods", which is Long's referent.
- "meaner" became "less noble".
- "the things a ruler must do for the public good" became "doing what must be done for the public good in the way that befits a ruler". This restores Long's manner clause.
- "for giving me such a brother" became "that I had such a brother".
- "since" became "on the grounds that", Long's "because", now clearly read as the excuse Marcus did not use.
- "I thank the gods, too," was inserted before "that my body…". It only repeats the governing verb and adds no content.
- "unaffected" became "unpretentious", as the standard requires.
- "for other things" became "both for other ailments", which follows Long's "both others".
- "working out syllogisms" became "solving logical syllogisms".
- "appearances in the sky" became "phenomena of the sky". This renders μετέωρα, a different word from φαντασία, so the "impression" rule does not apply.

"Written among the Quadi" was kept, and the rejection is justified: it matches "Written at Carnuntum." at II.17 in `candidate.json`. This resolves the findings.

## Verdict
Not clean. There are two minor defects, both one-line fixes:
1. **1.11**: the qualifier scope has changed ("often" now attaches only to "say", and the either/or is blurred). Use the replacement above.
2. **1.14**: "not of" turns Long's "rather than" into a flat denial. Use the replacement above.

The other 12 changed paragraphs are verified clean. That includes both BLOCKING fixes (1.10 and 1.15).

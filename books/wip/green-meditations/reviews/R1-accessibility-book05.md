# Accessibility review — Book V (5.0–5.35)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 5.0, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6, 5.7, 5.8, 5.9, 5.10, 5.11, 5.12, 5.13, 5.14, 5.15, 5.16, 5.17, 5.18, 5.19, 5.20, 5.21, 5.22, 5.23, 5.24, 5.25, 5.26, 5.27, 5.28, 5.29, 5.30, 5.31, 5.32, 5.33, 5.34, 5.35 (all 36).

Note on audio: several sections (5.0, 5.5, 5.8, 5.15, 5.27, 5.35) use em-dash dialogue with an imagined objector. On the page this works; heard once, the switch of speaker is often invisible. Where it causes real confusion it is flagged below.

## Findings

### 5.0 — MINOR
"Yet you go beyond those limits, beyond what is enough; while in your actions it is not so: there you stop short of what you could do." — "those limits" must be traced back to rest (not eating and drinking, which were mentioned last), and "it is not so: there" is translation-shaped; the contrast between resting too much and working too little blurs on one hearing.
Rewrite: "Yet in resting you go beyond those limits, beyond what is enough, while in your actions you do the opposite: you stop short of what you could do."

### 5.2 — MINOR
"Those people have their own ruling faculty and follow their own impulses." — first use of "ruling faculty" in this packet (it is only half-glossed later, in 5.10), so a newcomer does not know what it is. Also the opening "Judge every word and every act that is in keeping with nature to be worthy of you" puts the verb's object far from its complement.
Rewrite: "Consider every word and act that is in keeping with nature to be worthy of you." / "Those people have their own ruling faculty, the reasoning part that directs each of us, and they follow their own impulses."

### 5.4 — MINOR
"You say: People cannot admire you for the sharpness of your wits." — unclear who says what to whom (the self is quoting its own excuse, but it sounds like an outside accusation). Also "to be mean" (stingy or cruel?) and the archaic connective "Only, if in truth you can be charged with…".
Rewrite: "You say: 'People cannot admire me for the sharpness of my wits.'" … "to be petty" (or "stingy", whichever is meant) … "But if you really can be accused of being somewhat slow and dull to understand, then you must work at that too, neither neglecting it nor taking pleasure in your dullness."

### 5.5 — BLOCKING
"But you have misunderstood what is being said now, and because of that you will become one of those I spoke of first; for they too are led astray by something that looks like reason. But if you choose to understand what is being said, do not fear…" — "what is being said now" has no clear referent, and "those I spoke of first" requires the listener to recall the first of three types from a long passage ago. Combined with the unmarked back-and-forth ("—Yes.—But surely…—What you say is true."), a first-time listener loses who is objecting and what the misunderstanding is.
Rewrite: "What you say is true. But you have misunderstood my point, and because of that you will become one of the first kind of people I described, the ones who count up favors owed; for they too are misled by something that looks like reasoning. But if you choose to understand my point, do not fear that it will make you neglect any act that serves the community." (Also consider "—Must a person, then, …?" → "You ask: must a person, then, …?")

### 5.7 — BLOCKING
"And just as the universe is made up out of all bodies to be the kind of body it is, so necessity, or destiny, is made up out of all existing causes to be the kind of cause it is." — the "made up out of … to be the kind of … it is" construction is opaque; a listener cannot extract the idea (all bodies together form one body; all causes together form one cause).
Rewrite: "Just as all bodies together make up the universe, which is itself one body, so all existing causes together make up necessity, or destiny, which is itself one cause."
Also MINOR in the same section:
- "Let the completion and fulfillment of what the nature of the universe judges good be, in your judgment, the same kind of thing as your own health." → "Treat the carrying out and fulfillment of what the nature of the universe judges good as you would treat your own health."
- "Nor does the nature of anything, whatever it may be, bring about anything that does not suit what it governs." → "Nothing's nature, whatever that thing may be, brings about anything that does not suit what it governs."

### 5.8 — MINOR
"—Yes, but what could be more pleasant than this, the thing I am doing?" — "this" points to the unnamed un-natural thing just mentioned; heard aloud, the objection is not clearly marked as the self's reply. "their bit of sponge and egg" is an obscure remedy, though "sore eyes" gives enough context.
Rewrite: "—Yes, you say, but what could be more pleasant than what I am doing now?"

### 5.9 — MINOR
"And all our assent is liable to change" — "assent" is a technical term (agreeing to a judgment) not explained here; "wait for his natural breaking up" is an old way of saying death/dissolution; "the divine spirit within me" appears here for the first time without explanation (it is explained later, in 5.26).
Rewrite: "And every judgment we give our agreement to is liable to change" … "wait for his natural dissolution" … "it is in my power never to act against my god, the divine spirit within me".

### 5.11 — BLOCKING
"Even from this we can learn … he will listen to the line of the comic poet, and gladly accept it as right on target … Otherwise the joke would not offend us…" — "this", "the line" and "the joke" all refer to a line that is only quoted in the last sentence. A listener hears an argument about a joke they have not been told, and cannot follow why it would "clash" with true goods.
Rewrite (same content, joke moved to where it is first referred to): "Even from the following we can learn what sort of things the many take to be good. … But if someone has first thought of the things the many take to be good, he will hear the comic poet's line — that a certain man is so rich he has no room left to relieve himself — and gladly accept it as right on target. … Go on, then, and ask whether we should value and think good those things which, once they come to mind, invite that line of the comic poet about their owner."

### 5.12 — MINOR
"For nothing prevents us from saying this, even if the universe is governed in fixed cycles." — the listener cannot see why cycles might have prevented it; the "For" makes it sound like a reason when it is a concession.
Rewrite: "Nothing stops us from saying this, even if the universe is governed in fixed, repeating cycles."

### 5.13 — MINOR
"Catorthoseis" — an unfamiliar Greek word, hard to catch aloud; and "So they start from…" presents as a consequence what is really a description.
Rewrite: "They start from a first principle of their own and make their way to the goal set before them. That is why such acts are called 'right acts' (in Greek, katorthōseis): the word means that they go by the right road."

### 5.14 — BLOCKING
"None of these things should be called a person's own that do not belong to a person as a human being." — "these things" has no antecedent, and the relative clause is split from its noun, so the listener does not know what category is being discussed; every later "these things" inherits the confusion. The final "—if these things were really good" is also tacked on after the point it qualifies.
Rewrite: "Nothing that does not belong to a person as a human being should be called his own." … "Nor would someone deserve praise for showing that he does not need them; nor, if these things were really good, would someone who went without any of them be good."

### 5.15 — MINOR
"But he must live in a palace." — the objection is unmarked and its sense (court life is the obstacle) is easy to miss. "whatever each thing was made for, that is what it was made for" sounds like an empty tautology.
Rewrite: "—But I must live in a palace.—Very well, then one can also live well in a palace." / "And again: each thing is made for some purpose, and it moves toward that purpose."

### 5.16 — MINOR
"it is impossible that bad people should not do things of this kind" — double negative plus "of this kind" with no stated kind.
Rewrite: "and bad people cannot possibly help doing such bad things."

### 5.19 — MINOR
"they do not hinder my impulses or my disposition, which can act with a reservation (on condition that nothing prevents it) and can change course" — dense; "act with a reservation" is a technical idea and the gloss in brackets is easy to lose aloud.
Rewrite: "they do not hinder my impulses or my disposition, because I can always act with a reservation — 'provided nothing prevents it' — and can change course."

### 5.20 — MINOR
"it is of the same kind as that" — "that" must be traced back across a sentence.
Rewrite: "it is of the same kind as what is best in the universe."

### 5.25 — BLOCKING
"But when these feelings rise up to the mind through that other kind of shared feeling that naturally exists in a body which is all one…" — "that other kind of shared feeling" refers to an idea (the body's sympathy between its parts) never introduced; a newcomer cannot tell what this route is or what "other" contrasts with.
Rewrite: "But when these feelings reach the mind by the other route — the natural sympathy by which every part of a single, unified body shares what the other parts feel — then you must not try to resist the sensation, for it is natural."

### 5.27 — BLOCKING
"Neither a tragic actor nor a prostitute." — a verbless fragment with no stated link to the anger passage; a listener has no idea what is being said.
Rewrite (keeps the content, supplies the verb): "Be neither a tragic actor nor a prostitute: neither rant nor fawn." (If the reviewers of source fidelity reject the gloss, at minimum: "Do not play the tragic actor, and do not play the prostitute.")

### 5.28 — MINOR
"The way you intend to live when you have left this life … you can live that way here." — the ellipsis and "when you have left this life" make it sound like life after death; the quoted "The house is smoky, and I am leaving it" is unattributed, so aloud it is unclear who speaks.
Rewrite: "However you plan to live once you have got away from all this, you can live that way here." / "Say, as the saying goes: 'The house is smoky, and I am leaving it.'"

### 5.32 — MINOR
"whether it is to be put out or moved to another place" — "it" is ambiguous (your end? you?), and "put out" (like a flame) may be misheard.
Rewrite: "whether you are to be extinguished or moved to another place".

### 5.33 — MINOR
"These two things are shared by …: not to be hindered by anyone else; and to hold that the good lies in a just disposition and in just action, and to let your desire go no further than that." — announced as two things, but a listener hears three clauses and loses count.
Rewrite: "…two things: first, not to be hindered by anyone else; second, to hold that the good lies in a just disposition and just action, and to let your desire go no further than that."

### 5.34 — MINOR
"If this is not my own wickedness … And what harm is there to the common good?" — "this" has no referent (whatever is troubling me), and the final question sounds like a request for information rather than "how could it be harmed?".
Rewrite: "If what is troubling me is not my own wickedness, nor the result of it, and the common good is not harmed, why am I troubled? And how could the common good be harmed by it?"

### 5.35 — BLOCKING
"Instead, be like the old man who, when he left, asked for his foster child's spinning top back, knowing all the while that it was only a top. Do the same in this case too. When you are holding forth on the Rostra… have you forgotten, man, what these things really are?—Yes; but these people care about them a great deal.—Must you, then, be made a fool for their sake too? 'Once I was a fortunate man, but I lost it, I do not know how.'" — the anecdote is too compressed to see its point (sympathise with the loss while knowing it is trivial); "Do the same in this case too" and "these things" have unclear referents; three voices (Marcus, the objector, the lamenting man) follow one another with no markers. A first-time listener cannot reconstruct the thought.
Rewrite: "Instead, be like the old man in the story who, as he was leaving, asked for his foster child's spinning top back, as if it mattered, while knowing all along that it was only a top. Do the same here: help, but do not mistake the loss for real harm. When you are speaking on the Rostra, the speakers' platform, have you forgotten, man, what these external things really are?—Yes, you reply, but these people care about them a great deal.—Must you, then, become a fool for their sake as well? Someone laments: 'Once I was a fortunate man, but I lost my good fortune, I do not know how.'—But being fortunate means giving yourself a good fortune; and a good fortune is a good disposition of the soul, good impulses, good actions."

Verdict: FAIL (blocking issues present). Most blocking items are structural (referents that come after their pronouns, unmarked dialogue voices, one unglossed Stoic concept in 5.25) and fixable without dropping content. The 5.27 and 5.35 rewrites add a brief interpretive gloss; a source-aware reviewer should confirm those glosses.

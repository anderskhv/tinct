# Accessibility review — Book IV (4.0–4.50)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 4.0, 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4, 4.5, 4.6, 4.7, 4.8, 4.9, 4.10, 4.11, 4.12, 4.13, 4.14, 4.15, 4.16, 4.17, 4.18, 4.19, 4.20, 4.21, 4.22, 4.23, 4.24, 4.25, 4.26, 4.27, 4.28, 4.29, 4.30, 4.31, 4.32, 4.33, 4.34, 4.35, 4.36, 4.37, 4.38, 4.39, 4.40, 4.41, 4.42, 4.43, 4.44, 4.45, 4.46, 4.47, 4.48, 4.49, 4.50 (all 51).

## Findings

### 4.0 — BLOCKING
- **"When the ruling faculty is in keeping with nature"**: this is the first time "ruling faculty" appears in the packet, and it opens the book. A newcomer hearing it once doesn't know it means the mind's governing part, so every "it" that follows in the section has nothing concrete to attach to.
- **"It moves toward its purpose, though only on certain conditions"**: "on certain conditions" is opaque. The listener can't tell what the conditions are or why they matter here. The thought seems to be that the mind pursues its aims while allowing that they may not work out, but the English doesn't carry that.
- Proposed rewrite: "When the ruling faculty, the part of us that governs everything else, is in keeping with nature, it meets whatever happens by adapting itself easily to what is possible and to what it is given. For it does not demand any fixed material to work on. It moves toward its aims, but always with a reservation about how things may turn out; and it turns whatever stands in its way into material for itself."

### 4.1 — MINOR
- **"the principles that bring an art to perfection"**: "art" is used in the old sense of *craft or skill*. A modern listener hears painting and music.
- Rewrite: "Let nothing you do be done without a purpose, or in any way other than by the principles that bring a craft to perfection."

### 4.2 — BLOCKING
- **"the mind, once it has drawn itself apart and recognized its own power, does not mix with the breath, whether the breath moves gently or roughly"**: "the breath" is used in a technical sense (the body's life-breath, its physical sensations). A first-time listener takes it literally as breathing and loses the point entirely, which is that bodily sensations, pleasant or harsh, don't reach the mind.
  - Rewrite: "Then consider further that the mind, once it has drawn itself apart and recognized its own power, does not mix with the breath of life that moves the body, whether that breath moves gently or roughly."
- **"either there is providence, or there are only atoms—things thrown together by chance"** (MINOR within this section): "providence" appears here for the first time with no gloss. The listener also isn't told why either alternative should end the discontent. The sentence works as a reminder of an argument the listener has never heard.
  - Rewrite: "Then call to mind this alternative: either the universe is guided by providence, a caring order, or there are only atoms, things thrown together by chance."
- **"they stand outside it and do not move"** (MINOR): "do not move" is ambiguous. It could mean the things stay still, or that they cannot move you.
  - Rewrite: "things do not touch the soul: they stand outside it, motionless, and our disturbances come only from the opinion within."
- **"And among the things closest to hand, which you will turn to, let there be these two."** (MINOR): the nested relative clause makes it awkward to follow by ear.
  - Rewrite: "And among the principles you keep closest to hand and turn to, let there be these two."
- **"look at things as a man, as a human being"** (MINOR): heard aloud, "a man" and "a human being" sound like the same word said twice. If "man" is meant as *a grown man*, say so.
  - Rewrite: "look at things as a grown man, as a human being, as a citizen, as a mortal."

### 4.3 — MINOR
- **"the earthy part of me is a portion given to me from some earth"**: "from some earth" reads like a translation, and "earthy" invites the modern sense of *coarse*.
- Rewrite: "For just as the earthly part of me is a portion drawn from earth somewhere, and the watery part from another element, and the hot and fiery part from some source of its own ... so the thinking part too comes from some source."

### 4.4 — MINOR
- **"not against the reason by which we are made"**: "reason" can be heard as the faculty of thinking or as the cause. The intended sense (the rational plan of our makeup) isn't clear on one hearing.
- Rewrite: "For it is not against the nature of a rational being, and not against the rational plan on which we are made."

### 4.5 — MINOR
- **"Anyone who will not have it so will not allow the fig tree to have its juice."**: "will not have it so" is archaic. The fig-tree image is also hard to take in because "juice" hides the point that sap is simply what the tree naturally has. "he" in "you and he" has no antecedent (only "people like this"), so the listener has to guess that it means the offender.
- Rewrite: "Anyone who refuses to accept this is refusing to let the fig tree have its sap. But above all remember this: in a very short time both you and the man who wrongs you will be dead, and soon not even your names will be left behind."

### 4.8 — BLOCKING
- **"The nature of what is useful to all has been compelled to do this."**: "the nature of what is useful to all" is a noun phrase a listener can't parse, and "compelled" makes it sound as if something forced nature. There's nothing a newcomer could restate.
- Rewrite: "The nature that works for the good of all had no choice but to bring this about."

### 4.9 — MINOR
- **"do it together with this: being good"**: "together with this" is stilted, and the listener has to rebuild "do it while being good".
- Rewrite: "And whatever you do, do it while being good, good in the true sense in which a person is understood to be good."

### 4.11 — MINOR
- **"to do only what the reason of the ruling and law-giving faculty suggests"**: a stack of four abstract nouns ("reason of the ruling and law-giving faculty"), which is hard to hold by ear.
- Rewrite: "The first is to do only what reason suggests, the reason of the part of you that rules and gives law, for the good of human beings."

### 4.13 — MINOR
- **"You have existed as a part."** A part of what? Then **"its generative principle"**: "its" could point to "change" or to "what produced you".
- Rewrite: "You have existed as a part of the whole. You will vanish into what produced you; or rather, you will be taken back by change into the whole's generative principle, the seed of reason from which things grow."

### 4.18 — BLOCKING
- **"For now you are rejecting nature's gift at the wrong time, and clinging to something else...."**: "rejecting nature's gift at the wrong time" can't be followed on one hearing. The listener doesn't know what the gift is or why the timing is wrong, and the ellipsis leaves them stranded. If the ellipsis marks a real gap in the text, the words before it still need to be understandable.
- Rewrite: "For as it is, you are neglecting nature's gift, what is in front of you now, out of season, and clinging to something else...."

### 4.19 — MINOR
- **"Or gold, ivory, purple, a lyre"**: "purple" used as a noun (purple-dyed cloth) will be heard as a colour, which makes the list feel broken.
- Rewrite: "Or gold, ivory, purple cloth, a lyre, a little knife, a flower, a shrub?"

### 4.20 — MINOR
- **"how does the air hold them all from eternity?"**: "from eternity" is an old idiom. A listener doesn't hear that it means *all the souls that have gathered since time without beginning*.
- Rewrite: "If souls go on existing, how has the air held all of them from the beginning of time?"

### 4.21 — MINOR
- **"Do not be spun around."**: an odd, literal-sounding phrase. **"with every impression"**: "impression" appears for the first time as a technical term, and a newcomer hears it as *a general feeling*.
- Rewrite: "Do not let yourself be tossed about. In every impulse, hold to what is just; and with every impression, every image that presents itself to your mind, keep hold of your power to grasp and understand it."

### 4.22 — MINOR
- **"'Dear city of Cecrops'; and will you not say, 'Dear city of Zeus'?"**: the contrast is the whole point, but a listener who doesn't know that "city of Cecrops" means Athens can't hear it. This is a problem of reference, not a request for history, so a two-word appositive is enough.
- Rewrite: "The poet says of Athens, 'Dear city of Cecrops'; and will you not say of the universe, 'Dear city of Zeus'?"

### 4.23 — MINOR
- **"whatever the reason of a being made by nature for community requires, and in the way it requires"**: this is nested, and "it" is ambiguous (reason? the being?).
- Rewrite: "Do what is necessary, and whatever reason requires of a being that nature made for community, and in the way reason requires it."

### 4.24 — MINOR
- **"Try how the life of the good person suits you"**: "try how" is an archaic construction.
- Rewrite: "Try living the life of the good person and see how it suits you: the life of someone who is content with his share of the whole, and content with his own just acts and kindly disposition."

### 4.26 — BLOCKING
- **"And that, too, when all things are so distinct from one another, and so spread through everything, and so in sympathy with one another?"**: heard once, the three "so ..." clauses sound contradictory (distinct, yet spread through everything?). "In sympathy" will be heard as *feeling pity* rather than *linked, affecting one another*. The listener can't reconstruct the argument that a connected whole can't be disordered while you are ordered.
- Rewrite: "And that, too, when all things, distinct as they are from one another, are so spread through the whole and so bound up with one another, each affecting the rest?"

### 4.28 — MINOR
- **"He is a runaway who flees ... He is blind who shuts ... He is poor who needs ... He is an abscess on the universe who ... He is a piece torn off ... who tears"**: the old "He is X who does Y" inversion makes the listener wait for the "who" clause before knowing who "he" is. It happens five times in a row.
- Rewrite (pattern, applied to each): "Whoever flees from the reason that makes us a community is a runaway. Whoever shuts the eyes of his understanding is blind. Whoever needs someone else, and does not have within himself everything that is useful for life, is poor. Whoever is displeased with what happens, and so withdraws and cuts himself off from the reason of our common nature, is an abscess on the universe, for the same nature that brings this about brought you about too. Whoever tears his own soul away from the soul of rational beings, which is one, is a piece torn off from the state."

### 4.29 — BLOCKING
- **"'I have no bread,' he says, 'and I hold fast to reason'—and I get no living from my learning, and I hold fast to my reason."**: after the quotation closes, the speaker switches to Marcus with no signal. Heard aloud, it sounds like the half-naked philosopher is still talking, and the parallel between them is lost.
- Rewrite: "'I have no bread,' he says, 'and still I hold fast to reason.' And I, for my part, get no living from my learning, and still I hold fast to my reason."

### 4.30 — MINOR
- **"Love the art you have learned"**: the same old sense of "art" as in 4.1.
- Rewrite: "Love the craft you have learned, however humble it may be, and be content with it."

### 4.31 — MINOR
- **"the attention given to each thing has its own proper value and proportion"**: abstract and slightly off. The listener can't tell that it means *attention should match each thing's worth*.
- Rewrite: "And here you must remember that the attention you give to each thing should have its proper value, in proportion to the thing's worth."

### 4.32 — MINOR
- **"what is even everlasting remembrance?"**: the word order stumbles. **"as flowing from a source and origin of the same kind"**: the same kind as what? The comparison has no second term.
- Rewrite: "And, to sum up, what is everlasting remembrance, even that? Nothing at all. ... and a disposition that gladly accepts everything that happens as necessary, as familiar, as flowing from the same kind of source and origin as everything else."

### 4.36 — MINOR
- **"nor do you yet place wisdom in acting justly"**: "place wisdom in" is a translated idiom.
- Rewrite: "nor do you yet believe that wisdom lies in acting justly, and in that alone."

### 4.38 — MINOR
- **"nor in any turn or change of the body that covers you"**: "turn" in this sense (alteration) is obscure.
- Rewrite: "nor in any shift or change in the body that covers you."

### 4.39 — MINOR
- **"how everything is referred to one perception"**: "referred to" in this sense (brought back to, registered by) is technical, and a listener hears *mentioned*.
- Rewrite: "Notice how everything is taken in by one perception, the perception of this one living being."

### 4.42 — MINOR
- **"Time is like a river made of the things that happen, and a violent current."**: "and a violent current" dangles. It's unclear whether time is the current or the river has one.
- Rewrite: "Time is like a river made of the things that happen, and its current is violent."

### 4.46 — MINOR
- **"think it no great thing to die after as many years as you can name rather than tomorrow"**: "think it no great thing" is archaic, and the comparison is back-to-front for a listener.
- Rewrite: "In the same way, do not think it matters much whether you die tomorrow or after as many years as you can name."

### 4.48 — BLOCKING
- **"Why, then, call that a misfortune rather than this a good fortune?"**: "that" and "this" have no clear referents on one hearing. The listener has to work out that "that" is the event and "this" is having stayed free from pain. The next sentence's **"a departure from human nature"** is also abstract, and without the first sentence the argument is lost.
- Rewrite: "Why, then, call what happened a misfortune, rather than calling your having stayed free from pain a good fortune? Do you really call something a person's misfortune when it does not make him fall short of human nature? And does something seem to you to make a person fall short of human nature when it is not against what human nature wills?"

### 4.49 — MINOR
- **"think with how much trouble, in the company of what sort of people, and in how feeble a body it is laboriously lived through"**: an inverted exclamatory structure where "it" (the span of life) arrives last. A listener loses the thread.
- Rewrite: "and think how much trouble it takes to live that span through, in the company of what sort of people, and in how feeble a body."

2. "Art" is used in the old sense of craft (4.1, 4.30).

4. Old inverted constructions: 4.28, 4.46, 4.49.

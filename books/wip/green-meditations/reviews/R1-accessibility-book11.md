# Accessibility review — Book XI (11.0–11.38)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 11.0, 11.1, 11.2, 11.3, 11.4, 11.5, 11.6, 11.7, 11.8, 11.9, 11.10, 11.11, 11.12, 11.13, 11.14, 11.15, 11.16, 11.17, 11.18, 11.19, 11.20, 11.21, 11.22, 11.23, 11.24, 11.25, 11.26, 11.27, 11.28, 11.29, 11.30, 11.31, 11.32, 11.33, 11.34, 11.35, 11.36, 11.37, 11.38 (all 39).

## Findings

### 11.0 — BLOCKING
- "and valuing nothing more than itself—which is also a mark of law. So right reason and the reason of justice do not differ at all." A listener cannot tell what "which" refers to (valuing nothing above itself? all three traits?), why that is "a mark of law", or how the "So" conclusion follows. "The reason of justice" also sounds like "the motive for justice" rather than "the reason that guides justice." The section's closing thought is lost.
  Proposed: "This too is a mark of the rational soul: love of one's neighbor, truthfulness and modesty, and valuing nothing more highly than itself. Valuing nothing above itself is also a mark of law. So right reason and the reason that guides justice are not different at all; they are the same."
- Minor, same section: "valuing nothing more than itself" can be heard as "valuing only itself" (selfishness). The rewrite above ("more highly than itself") helps.

### 11.1 — MINOR
- "You will be too ashamed to admit it." It is unclear what "it" is (that a single note overpowers you). Proposed: "You will be ashamed to answer yes."
- "remember to go to the separate parts" is not natural English. Proposed: "In everything, then, except virtue and the acts that come from virtue, remember to break things down into their separate parts, and by dividing them in this way come to think little of them."

### 11.5 — MINOR
- "even those who cry out 'O Cithaeron!' have to bear them." A newcomer will not know who cries this; the point (even tragic heroes must bear their fate) is reachable only if the speaker is identified. Proposed: "even the tragic heroes who cry out 'O Cithaeron!' have to bear them."
- First sentence is long and nested ("to remind people... that it is according to nature... and that if what is shown on the stage delights you, you should not be distressed..."). Proposed split: "At first, tragedies were put on the stage to remind people of the things that happen to them, and that it is according to nature for things to happen this way. They also showed that if what happens on the stage delights you, you should not be distressed when the same things happen on the larger stage of life."
- "But as for the Middle Comedy that came next, consider what it was" — the listener is not told what to conclude; this may be the original's own ellipsis, but the sentence reads as a half-thought. Proposed: "But as for the Middle Comedy that came next, consider what it was like; and then consider for what purpose the New Comedy was brought in, which little by little sank into mere skill at imitation."

### 11.7 — MINOR
- "The grafted branch is something like what gardeners mean when they say that it grows along with the rest of the tree, but is not of one mind with it." The sentence structure ("is something like what gardeners mean when they say") is tangled, and it is unclear whether "it" is the grafted branch. Proposed: "Gardeners say of a grafted branch that it grows along with the rest of the tree but is not of one mind with it."

### 11.9 — BLOCKING
- "No nature is inferior to art, for the arts imitate the natures of things." A modern listener hears "art" as painting or music, not as craft or skill; the argument then fails to make sense. Proposed: "No nature is inferior to craft, for the crafts imitate the natures of things. If this is so, then the nature that is the most perfect of all natures, and the one that contains them all, cannot fall short of any craftsman's skill. Now all the crafts make the lesser things for the sake of the higher; so the nature of the universe does this too."
- "And this is where justice has its origin" — "this" is ambiguous (nature? the ordering of lesser for higher?). Proposed: "And this ordering of lesser things for the sake of higher ones is where justice has its origin, and the other virtues have their foundation in justice."

### 11.10 — MINOR
- "If the things whose pursuit or avoidance disturbs you do not come to you, still, in a way, it is you who go to them." Nested relative clause plus a conditional with "still"; a listener loses who moves toward whom. Proposed: "The things that disturb you, the things you chase or run from, do not come to you; in a way, it is you who go to them."

### 11.12 — MINOR
- "For that is how one ought to be within" — "that" has no clear referent (the gentle, honest attitude just described). Proposed: "For that gentleness and honesty is how one ought to be within: a person should be seen by the gods neither discontented about anything nor complaining."
- Final sentence is a long rhetorical question with an appositive tacked on ("—you, a human being placed at your post..."); a listener loses the thread. Proposed: "For what harm can come to you, if you are now doing what suits your own nature, and are content with what at this moment suits the nature of the universe? You are a human being placed at your post so that the common good may be achieved in one way or another."

### 11.14 — MINOR
- "But a show of simplicity is like a crooked stick." and "The good, the simple and the kind" — modern readers hear "simple" as "unsophisticated" or "unintelligent", not "straightforward, sincere"; "crooked stick" then seems random. Proposed: "But a show of straightforwardness is like a crooked stick pretending to be straight." and "The good, the straightforward and the kind show all this in their eyes, and there is no mistaking it."

### 11.15 — MINOR
- "if it looks at each of these things both separately and as a whole" — unclear what looking at them "separately" means (breaking them into parts). Proposed: "if it looks at each of these things both broken into its parts and as a whole".
- "that this care will be needed only for a short time" — "this care" has no clear antecedent. Proposed: "that this watchfulness will be needed only for a short time".
- "If these things are in keeping with nature" — "these things" could mean the indifferent things or the judgments. Proposed: "If the things that happen to you are in keeping with nature, take pleasure in them..."

### 11.17 — BLOCKING
- "For unless what is shameful is the only thing that is bad, you too must inevitably do many wrongs, and become a robber and everything else." Double negative plus an unexplained leap; a first-time listener will not get the thought. Proposed: "For if anything other than what is shameful counts as bad, then you too will inevitably do many wrongs, and become a robber and everything else."
- "since those acts are rooted in their own ruling faculties" — first appearance in this packet of "ruling faculty" (it reappears in 11.18 and 11.19); a newcomer will not know it means the governing mind. Proposed: "since those acts are rooted in their own ruling faculties—the minds that govern them".
- "if all things are not mere atoms" — ambiguous scope ("not all things are atoms" vs. "the universe is not just atoms"). Proposed: "if the universe is not mere atoms, then it is nature that orders all things."
- "for many things are done with some circumstance in view" — obscure phrasing. Proposed: "for many things are done with some purpose that depends on circumstances you cannot see."
- "That is why people are pained when they are called unjust..." — the link to the previous sentence (they do wrong unwillingly, so they resent the label) is hard to follow. Proposed: "That is why, since no one does wrong willingly, people are pained when they are called unjust, ungrateful, greedy—in a word, wrongdoers toward their neighbors."
- "but either when he is alone, and if others are present . . ." — "either... and" is ungrammatical and the break-off is jarring aloud. Proposed: "but either when he is alone or, if others are present, . . ."

### 11.18 — MINOR
- Four ways are announced, but the first three are given only as quoted sayings with no numbering, and the aside "and you should count it among the most absurd things..." runs on inside the third. A listener cannot match sayings to faults. Proposed: "...wipe them out, saying to the first: 'This thought is not necessary'; to the second: 'This one tends to break the bonds of community'; to the third: 'What you are about to say does not come from your real thoughts'—and count it among the most absurd things for a person not to speak from his real thoughts. The fourth is when you reproach yourself for anything."

### 11.19 — MINOR
- "and indeed they come before acts of justice." — "come before" can be heard as timing rather than priority. Proposed: "and indeed they rank even higher than acts of justice."
- "These qualities, too" — refers to piety and reverence; make explicit. Proposed: "Piety and reverence, too, come under the general heading of contentment with the way things are ordered."

### 11.20 — BLOCKING
- "People do not all hold the same opinion about everything that most of them regard, in one way or another, as good; they agree only about certain things, namely the things that concern the common interest." Heavily nested; a listener cannot reconstruct the comparison that the next sentence ("In the same way") depends on. Proposed: "Most people regard many things as good in one way or another, but they do not all agree about all of them; they agree only about certain things, namely those that concern the common interest."

### 11.23 — MINOR
- "The Lacedaemonians" — archaic name most listeners will not recognize. Proposed: "The Lacedaemonians—the Spartans—at their public shows, used to set out seats in the shade for strangers, but sat down anywhere themselves."

### 11.32 — MINOR
- "when that is no longer allowed" — sounds like a matter of permission or custody; the meaning (when the child is no longer granted to him, i.e. is gone) is missed. Proposed: "and the person who looks for his child when the child is no longer granted to him is just like him."

### 11.33 — MINOR
- '"But those are words of ill omen."' — unattributed turn; aloud, it sounds like Epictetus contradicting himself. Proposed: '"But," someone objects, "those are words of ill omen."'

### 11.36 — BLOCKING
- "a person must find an art, a set of rules, for giving assent." "Giving assent" is a technical term that has not come up earlier in this packet. A newcomer cannot tell what is being assented to, so the first half of the thought is lost. "Art" is also the old sense of the word (method, skill). Proposed: "Epictetus also said that a person must find a method, a set of rules, for giving assent—that is, for deciding which impressions to accept as true."
- "that they take account of the worth of their object": "their object" is abstract. Proposed: "and that they are in proportion to the worth of the thing they aim at."

### 11.37 — MINOR
- "the dispute" — no antecedent; the listener does not know what dispute is meant. Proposed: "So, he said, the struggle here is not about some ordinary matter, but about whether we are to be mad or not."

### 11.38 — MINOR
- Rapid unattributed exchange; aloud, the listener cannot tell who asks and who answers, especially at "Because we have them." Proposed: 'Socrates used to ask: "What do you want: the souls of rational beings, or of irrational ones?" They answered: "Of rational beings." "Rational beings of what kind: sound or unsound?" "Sound." "Then why do you not seek them?" "Because we have them." "Then why do you fight and quarrel?"'

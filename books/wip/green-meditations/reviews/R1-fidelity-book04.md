# Fidelity review — Book IV (4.0–4.50)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 4.0–4.50, all 51 paragraphs checked sentence by sentence against Long (1862) and STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 4.2 — MINOR
- Source: "consider how many already, after mutual enmity, suspicion, hatred, and fighting, have been stretched dead"
- Candidate: "And think how many people, after hostility, suspicion, hatred and fighting, have been laid out dead and turned to ashes."
- Problem: two small omissions: "already" (the point that this has happened many times before) and "mutual" (the enmity was on both sides, which is the point after "rational beings exist for one another").
- Proposed: "And think how many people already, after mutual hostility, suspicion, hatred and fighting, have been laid out dead and turned to ashes."

### 4.2 — MINOR
- Source: "the narrowness of the space within which it is circumscribed" (the subject is "the thing called fame")
- Candidate: "and at how narrow the space is within which praise is confined."
- Problem: the subject of this list is fame; the candidate switches it to praise. The two are close, but Long's list is about the fame the passage began with.
- Proposed: "and at how narrow the space is within which fame is confined."

### 4.2 / 4.48 — MINOR (terminology consistency)
- Source (4.2): "free from all discontent with the things to which thou returnest. For with what art thou discontented?"; (4.48): "every occasion which leads thee to vexation"
- Candidate: "free of all resentment toward the things…", "what is it you resent?"; "whenever something leads you toward resentment"
- Problem: "discontent" and "vexation" mean being annoyed or dissatisfied. "Resentment" is narrower and stronger: it suggests a grudge against a person. In 4.2 the same passage goes on to "discontented with what the universe has allotted you", so the candidate uses two words for the one idea.
- Proposed (4.2): "…and to send you back free of all discontent with the things you are returning to. For what are you discontented with?" (4.48): "Remember too, whenever something leads you toward vexation, to apply this principle…"

### 4.3 — MINOR
- Source: "common also is the reason which commands us what to do, and what not to do"
- Candidate: "then the reason that tells us what to do and what not to do is also common."
- Problem: "commands" is prescriptive, and the next step ("there is a common law") depends on that. "Tells" weakens it.
- Proposed: "If so, then the reason that commands us what to do and what not to do is also common."

### 4.13 — MINOR (addition)
- Source: "thou shalt be received back into its seminal principle by transmutation."
- Candidate: "you will be taken back by change into its generative principle—the seed of reason from which things grow."
- Problem: the gloss "the seed of reason from which things grow" is not in Long, and the style guide does not prescribe it. It is doctrinally accurate (λόγος σπερματικός), but it is interpretive apparatus. It is also inconsistent with 4.20, where "seminal intelligence" appears without a gloss.
- Proposed: "or rather, you will be taken back by change into its generative principle, the seed from which it produces things." (or simply end at "its generative principle.")

### 4.17 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "or, as Agathon says, look not round at the depraved morals of others"
- Candidate: "Or, as Agathon says, do not look around at the corrupt ways of others"
- Note: the Greek (κατὰ τὸν ἀγαθὸν) can be read either as "as Agathon says" (Long) or as "as befits the good man" / "the good man does not look around". The candidate follows Long, as the guide requires. Log this in AMBIGUITIES.md.

### 4.18 — MINOR
- Source: "What is praise, except indeed so far as it has a certain utility?"
- Candidate: "What is praise, except perhaps insofar as it has some practical use?"
- Problem: Long's "indeed" grants the exception. "Perhaps" turns that grant into doubt.
- Proposed: "What is praise, except, of course, insofar as it has some practical use?"

### 4.29 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "Bread I have not, he says, and I abide by reason—and I do not get the means of living out of my learning, and I abide [by my reason]."
- Candidate: "\"I have no bread,\" he says, \"and I hold fast to reason\"—and I get no living from my learning, and I hold fast to my reason."
- Note: Long uses no quotation marks, so it is unclear whether the second clause belongs to the half-naked philosopher or is Marcus speaking of himself. By closing the quotation before it, the candidate makes it Marcus's own contrast. That reading is supported by the Greek ἐγὼ δέ, but it is a choice, so log it.

### 4.31 — MINOR
- Source: "those whom thou hast thyself known distracting themselves about idle things"
- Candidate: "the people you yourself have known who wore themselves out over empty things"
- Problem: this changes the image from being pulled in many directions (distraction) to exhaustion.
- Proposed: "think of the people you yourself have known who let themselves be pulled this way and that by empty things, and neglected…"

### 4.31 — MINOR
- Source: "For thus thou wilt not be dissatisfied, if thou appliest thyself to smaller matters no further than is fit."
- Candidate: "For then you will not lose heart, as long as you give smaller matters no more attention than is fitting."
- Problem: "lose heart" (discouragement) is defensible from the Greek but departs from Long's "dissatisfied".
- Proposed: "For then you will not become dissatisfied, as long as you give smaller matters no more attention than is fitting."

### 4.45 — MINOR (terminology/voice)
- Source: "And think too of him who forgets whither the way leads"
- Candidate: "Think too of the man who forgets where the road leads."
- Problem: this "him" is generic (Heraclitus's saying about any person), so under the style guide it should not be "the man".
- Proposed: "Think too of the person who forgets where the road leads."

### 4.48 — MINOR
- Source: "And dost thou in all cases call that a man's misfortune which is not a deviation from man's nature?"
- Candidate: "Do you really call something a person's misfortune when it is not a departure from human nature?"
- Problem: "in all cases" (the generalizing force) has become "really". The point of the question is that he does this universally.
- Proposed: "Do you, in general, call something a person's misfortune when it is not a departure from human nature?"

### 4.50 — MINOR
- Source: "Always run to the short way … For such a purpose frees a man from trouble, and warfare, and all artifice and ostentatious display."
- Candidate: "Always take the short road … frees a person from trouble and strife, and from all scheming and showy display."
- Problem: "run" (urgency, τρέχε) is weakened to "take". "Warfare" (στρατεία, campaigning) is generalized to "strife".
- Proposed: "Always run by the short road; and the short road is the natural one. … For a purpose like this frees a person from trouble and warfare, and from all scheming and showy display."

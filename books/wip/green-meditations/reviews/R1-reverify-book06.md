# Re-verification — Book VI R1 changes

Coverage: every changed id. That is 6.0, 6.3, 6.4, 6.7, 6.8, 6.9, 6.10, 6.13, 6.14, 6.15, 6.21, 6.23, 6.24, 6.25, 6.26, 6.32, 6.38, 6.39, 6.40, 6.41, 6.43, 6.47, 6.49 and 6.54 (24 paragraphs). For each one, the AFTER text in the packet was confirmed to match `drafts/book06.json` exactly. The opening section number is id + 1 and is unchanged in all 24.

I also checked every accessibility finding the editor rejected. The question for each was whether leaving it unfixed stops a first-time listener from following the thought. I read the rejected items against the current drafts and the earlier glosses the editor cited: I.7 "sophist", II.2 "ruling faculty", II.3 "providence", IV.14 "generative principle" and V.20 "reservation".

## Per paragraph

### 6.0 — CLEAN
The only change is "has no cause in itself for doing evil" → "has nothing in itself that would cause it to do evil". The meaning of Long's "has in itself no cause for doing evil" is unchanged, and it now sounds like natural English.

### 6.3 — CLEAN
The condition moved: "Either—if indeed all substance is one—they will be turned into vapor, or they will be scattered." It now clearly attaches to the vapor option, as in Long, and both alternatives are kept. Rejecting "single thing" and "or else" loses nothing.

### 6.4 — CLEAN
"what its own disposition is" → "what state it is in". This is a fair rendering of Long's "what its own disposition is" (Greek πῶς διάκειται) and is plainer. The rejected gloss "Your own governing reason…" is correctly rejected. The Greek subject is ὁ διοικῶν λόγος, the same phrase used in VI.1 for the reason that governs the universe. So a listener who hears it as the cosmic reason is on a legitimate reading, and the listener is not left unable to follow.

### 6.7 — CLEAN
"turns itself around" → "directs itself". This resolves the fidelity finding: Long's "turns itself" (τρέπον ἑαυτό) no longer carries the sense of reversal. The ruling-faculty gloss was correctly rejected, because it already exists at II.2 ("the reasoning mind that governs the rest of me").

### 6.8 — CLEAN
The new wording is "a nature that encloses this nature of the universe from outside, or one contained within it, or one that stands outside it and is independent of it". "Encloses" renders Long's "externally comprehends" more closely than "surrounds". The first and third options are now audibly different, and all three alternatives are kept. "This nature of the universe" is slightly stiff but clear.

### 6.9 — CLEAN
"I revere" → "I feel reverence". Nothing is added, and the reviewer's added object ("it") was correctly rejected because Long's object is "him who governs".

### 6.10 — CLEAN
"forced you to be thrown off balance" → "forced you off balance". Long's idea of compulsion ("compelled by circumstances") is kept, and the double passive is gone.

### 6.13 — CLEAN
One long sentence is now split into three. Every member of Long's clause is still there:
- the rational soul
- "not however a universal soul"
- skill in some art
- expertise in some other way
- "simply" possessing a number of slaves

"Rational so far as it is…" has become "value… only for…". That is a small shift from describing the soul to naming why people value it, but it keeps Long's contrast and limitation. The link between "rational soul" and people comes through "skilled in some craft" and "slaves", so rejecting "that is, human beings" does not leave the listener lost. This resolves the BLOCKING finding.

### 6.14 — CLEAN
The breath comparison is now spelled out: one breath given back is "just the same" as giving back the whole power of breathing, which was received at birth and returns to its element. All of Long's parts are present, and "yesterday or the day before" is kept. "And you give it back" puts Long's "to give it back" in the present tense. This is acceptable, but "and you will give it back" would be slightly closer if the lead wants it.

### 6.15 — CLEAN
Two changes:
- The gloss "—the way you are made" is a lexical gloss on "constitution". It is the first use of the word in Books I–VI, since a search of books 1–6 finds no earlier "constitution", so it counts as a first-use gloss. It adds no interpretation.
- "any of these things" → "any of those things". This matches the earlier "those things" and follows Long.

The rejected finding (b), "aim at something", does not block a listener: "aim at something too" follows directly after the crafts' stated aim, so the listener can carry that aim across.

### 6.21 — CLEAN
The new text is "For those other things are either lifeless, or without reason, or things that have wandered off…", following Long's "things". Rejecting "beings" is acceptable, because "wandered off and do not know the way" makes clear the third group can go astray.

### 6.23 — CLEAN
Two changes:
- "stable boy" → "stable hand". This drops the youth that "boy" added, matching Long's "groom".
- "seed-principles… from which all things grow" → "generative principles", with no gloss. This is the same term as IV.14, and both fidelity findings are resolved.

Note for the lead: the disposition says this is "as at IV.14", but IV.14's gloss was itself removed in Book IV R1 (R1-applied-book04 4.13). So "generative principle" is now unglossed everywhere, consistently. The either/or with "scattered… among the atoms" still lets a listener follow the thought, so this is not a defect.

### 6.24 — CLEAN
The new text is "in the one whole that is everything, which we call the Cosmos". This renders Long's "that which is the one and all" and removes the idiom trap.

### 6.25 — CLEAN
"count out" → "name each letter in turn". Long's "number every letter" (going through the letters in sequence) is kept by "in turn", and the new wording avoids the clash with "shout out".

### 6.26 — CLEAN
The new text is "But what they suppose is not really so. Teach them, then, and show them, without being angry." The referent now matches Long's "But it is not so": they suppose the things are suitable and profitable. "Then" now reads as an inference ("so"), and Long's imperative is kept.

### 6.32 — CLEAN
The new text is "as long as that labor does a human being's work". Long's subject (the labor, "so long as it does the things of a man") is kept, and the pronoun tangle is gone.

### 6.38 — CLEAN
The new text is "Fit yourself to the things that have been allotted to you". This is a fair plain rendering of "the things with which thy lot has been cast" (οἷς συγκεκλήρωσαι), and it matches "given your place" in the second half.

### 6.39 — CLEAN
Clause order is reversed: "With every instrument, tool or vessel, the one who made it is not there; yet it is doing well if it does what it was made for." Long's own "and yet" concession is kept, nothing is added, and the maker outside vs. the power inside now lines up with the next sentence. The rejected "outside it and no longer" is not needed for a listener, because "inside them and stays there" follows at once.

### 6.40 — CLEAN
The new sentence is "If you suppose… then it is inevitable that… you will blame the gods and hate people too: those who caused…". The broken syntax is fixed, and "inevitable" now governs both consequences, as in Long and in the fidelity sub-point. The lead's ruling ("you will blame", logged in the ambiguities file) is kept, and nothing is lost.

### 6.41 — CLEAN
The new text is "as one part among the fellow workers". It renders Long's "among some part of the co-operators" and still sets up "do not be that kind of part". The indefiniteness is carried by "what kind of workers you are placing yourself among". The Chrysippus allusion is Long's own, and the point of the line ("cheap and ridiculous") is audible.

### 6.43 — CLEAN
Two changes:
- "or what would the whole gain—the whole that is the special object of their providence?" This fills in the ellipsis in Long's "or to the whole" without adding anything.
- "these two cities" is accurate, because Rome and the world are named in the sentence before.

The providence gloss was correctly rejected, because it is already given at II.3.

### 6.47 — CLEAN
The new text is "and appear there in as great abundance as possible". This renders Long's "present themselves in abundance, as far as is possible" (Greek ὅση δυνατόν), and the tacked-on qualifier is now attached to what it limits.

### 6.49 — DEFECT
The other changes are correct:
- "Let us try to persuade people" absorbs Long's "[men]".
- The dash dialogue "What, then, did you want?—To make an attempt of just this kind.—" keeps Long's form and makes the point audible.
- Long's bracketed "[not]" reading is kept and logged.

The problem is the editor's rejection of BLOCKING item (b), which rests on a false premise. The disposition says "reservation" is "glossed at V.20, 'act with a reservation (on condition that nothing prevents it)'". That gloss was removed in Book V R1 (R1-applied-book05, 5.19). V.20 now reads only "which can act conditionally and can change course", and "reservation" appears nowhere else in Books I–VI. So 6.49 introduces the Stoic "reservation" unglossed, and a modern listener hears it as doubt or a booking. The whole argument of the section depends on it: you wanted only a conditional attempt, so you succeed even when the outcome fails. The colon clause after it does not replace the missing idea of a condition.

Long's own text gives the faithful fix. He writes "with a reservation [conditionally]", and "conditionally" is the rendering Book V settled on at V.20.

Proposed replacement for the sentence: "And remember that you made your attempt with a reservation, that is, conditionally: you did not want to do what was impossible."

The rest of the paragraph stays unchanged.

### 6.54 — CLEAN
"would they listen to anyone else?" → "whom else would they listen to?". This matches the sense of Long's "would they listen to anybody else?" (Greek ἄλλου τινὸς ἂν προσεῖχον;) and removes the reversed reading. Long's "Or" is kept.

## Verdict
There is one defect, so this is not VERIFIED CLEAN:
- **6.49**: "reservation" is unglossed because the V.20 gloss the disposition relies on was removed in Book V R1. Replace the sentence with "And remember that you made your attempt with a reservation, that is, conditionally: you did not want to do what was impossible." ("conditionally" is Long's own bracket and matches V.20.)

The other 23 changed paragraphs are CLEAN.

# Accessibility review — Book VIII (8.0–8.60)
Reviewer: candidate-only accessibility reviewer (source not seen). Coverage: 8.0, 8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4, 8.5, 8.6, 8.7, 8.8, 8.9, 8.10, 8.11, 8.12, 8.13, 8.14, 8.15, 8.16, 8.17, 8.18, 8.19, 8.20, 8.21, 8.22, 8.23, 8.24, 8.25, 8.26, 8.27, 8.28, 8.29, 8.30, 8.31, 8.32, 8.33, 8.34, 8.35, 8.36, 8.37, 8.38, 8.39, 8.40, 8.41, 8.42, 8.43, 8.44, 8.45, 8.46, 8.47, 8.48, 8.49, 8.50, 8.51, 8.52, 8.53, 8.54, 8.55, 8.56, 8.57, 8.58, 8.59, 8.60 (all 61).

## Findings

### 8.6 — BLOCKING
Problem words: "For it gives to everything, in equal shares and according to its worth, its times, its substance, its cause, its activity and what befalls it."
Why: "it" could be the human nature, the plant, or the common nature. The list of abstract nouns ("its times, its substance, its cause") gives a listener nothing to hold on to, and "in equal shares and according to its worth" sounds like a contradiction: equal, or by worth? The next two sentences ("do not look for any one thing to be equal…") only make sense once this sentence has been understood, so the whole second half of the section is lost. Also minor: "assents to nothing false or uncertain" uses "assents" in its technical sense with no gloss (first use in the packet).
Proposed rewrite: "For the common nature gives each thing its fair and equal share, measured by what that thing is worth: its span of time, its substance, its cause, its activity, and what happens to it." Optionally, earlier: "when, in its thinking, it agrees to (assents to) nothing false or uncertain".

### 8.16 — BLOCKING
Problem words: "If something is in your own power, why do you do it?"
Why: As written it asks why you would do something that is within your power. That sounds like nonsense. The listener cannot tell that "it" means a wrong act or fault, so the contrast with "whom do you blame" in the next sentence falls apart.
Proposed rewrite: "If the wrong thing is in your own power, why do you do it? And if it is in someone else's power, whom do you blame—the atoms, that is, chance, or the gods?"

### 8.34 — BLOCKING
Problem words: "so we have received this power from it too" / "turns to its own ends everything that stands in its way and opposes it, fixes it in its destined place, and makes it part of itself"
Why: "this power" points forward to something not yet named, so on first hearing it refers to nothing. In the second sentence "its" and "it" switch back and forth between the nature of the universe and the obstacle, five times in one clause. A listener loses track of what is being turned, fixed and absorbed.
Proposed rewrite: "Just as the nature of the universe has given each rational being all its other powers, so we have also received from it the following power. The nature of the universe takes whatever stands in its way and opposes it, turns that obstacle to its own ends, fixes it in its destined place, and makes it part of itself; in the same way a rational being can make every obstacle into material for itself, and use it for whatever purpose it set out to achieve."

### 8.0 — MINOR
Problem words: "For you have tried many wanderings"
Why: This reads like a translation. People don't "try wanderings" in English.
Proposed rewrite: "For you have wandered down many paths and found happiness nowhere—not in logical arguments, not in wealth, not in reputation, not in enjoyment, not anywhere."

### 8.1 — MINOR
Problem words: "How does this stand with me?"
Why: The idiom is old-fashioned and vague. A listener may not hear it as "How does this affect me?"
Proposed rewrite: "How does this affect me? Will I regret it?"

### 8.2 — MINOR
Problem words: "and their matter; and their ruling faculties were in keeping with what they pursued"
Why: "matter" has its old philosophical sense here (the stuff things are made of), and a listener may hear "their matter" as "their business". "Ruling faculty" appears here for the first time in the packet and is never explained. It comes back in 8.42, 8.47, 8.55 and 8.60, so the first use should carry a brief anchor.
Proposed rewrite: "For these men understood things, their causes, and the material they are made of; and their ruling faculties—the minds that guided them—were in keeping with what they pursued."

### 8.5 — MINOR
Problem words: "and the way things are shared out still remains the same"
Why: It isn't clear what is being "shared out" or by whom. The sentence doesn't link back to "the nature of the universe" at the start.
Proposed rewrite: "Everything is familiar to us; and the way the universe shares things out still remains the same as ever."

### 8.7 — MINOR
Problem words: "You are not free to read."
Why: On first hearing this sounds like a prohibition, as if reading were forbidden. The point is that he has no time for reading, while the other freedoms are always open to him.
Proposed rewrite: "You have no leisure to read. But you are free to curb arrogance; …"

### 8.10 — MINOR
Problem words: "What is its cause—its form?"
Why: For a newcomer, "cause" and "form" are not the same thing, so the dash makes an equation they can't follow.
Proposed rewrite: "What is its cause—that is, its form, what makes it the thing it is?"

### 8.12 — MINOR
Problem words: "with every impression that comes to your soul"
Why: "Impression" is used technically for the first time in the packet (it recurs in 8.25, 8.28 and 8.48), and it's not clear that it means anything that appears to or strikes the mind.
Proposed rewrite: "Constantly, and if possible with every impression that comes to your soul—everything that presents itself to your mind—apply to it the principles of physics (the study of nature), of ethics, and of logic."

### 8.14 — MINOR
Problem words: "when someone has a fever, or when the wind is against them"
Why: "them" could mean the doctor and pilot together, or the ship. Each example belongs to one profession, and the plural blurs that.
Proposed rewrite: "And it is shameful for a doctor to be surprised when someone has a fever, or for a ship's pilot to be surprised when the wind is against him."

### 8.19 — MINOR
Problem words: "for the ending no less than for the beginning and the lasting"
Why: "the lasting" is not natural English as a noun and a listener may miss that it means the time in between.
Proposed rewrite: "In everything, nature has taken thought for the ending no less than for the beginning and for the time it lasts, just like someone who throws a ball into the air."

### 8.20 — MINOR
Problem words: "and even here not everyone agrees—no, not even anyone with himself"
Why: The grammar is broken ("not even anyone with himself"), and "agrees" has no object: agrees about what, with whom?
Proposed rewrite: "and even here people do not all agree with one another—no, no one even agrees with himself."

### 8.24 — MINOR
Problem words: "this little compound that is you must either be broken up, or your poor breath must be put out, or else moved and placed somewhere else"
Why: "compound" is used in its old sense (a thing made of parts). "Breath" stands for the life-spirit, and "moved and placed somewhere else" has no subject that a listener can hold on to.
Proposed rewrite: "Remember this, then: the little compound of parts that is you must either be scattered, or else your poor breath of life must either be put out or be moved and placed somewhere else."

### 8.27 — MINOR
Problem words: "is within, and no evil climbs up that high"
Why: The spatial metaphor switches mid-sentence from "within" (inward) to "that high" (upward), which leaves the listener with no picture of where the soul's judgments sit relative to the pain.
Proposed rewrite: "For every judgment, every impulse, every desire and every aversion is within, in a place so high that no evil climbs up to it."

### 8.28 — MINOR
Problem words: "Wipe out the impressions in your mind" / "Instead, I look at all things"
Why: 8.12 and 8.25 have just told the reader to examine and judge impressions, so "wipe them out" sounds like a contradiction when nothing marks these as the disturbing ones. "Instead" after a list of things to keep out doesn't connect clearly.
Proposed rewrite: "Wipe out the impressions that crowd into your mind by often saying to yourself: 'It is in my power now to keep this soul free of all badness, of desire, and of any disturbance at all. Rather than being disturbed, I look at all things, see what their nature is, and use each one according to its worth.'"

### 8.30 — MINOR
Problem words: "such as the Pompeii" / "Then turn to the others."
Why: When heard, "the Pompeii" will be taken for the buried city, not a family. "The others" (others than whom?) is vague.
Proposed rewrite: "Then turn to the others who are dead. Consider not the death of a single person but the death of a whole family, such as the family of the Pompeii; …"

### 8.31 — MINOR
Problem words: "if each act does its duty as far as possible" / final sentence
Why: Saying acts "do their duty" is a strange personification. The last sentence is long and nested (by accepting… and by being content… you are at once given… one that will fit…), and a listener loses the main clause.
Proposed rewrite: "…and if each act achieves what it should, as far as possible, be content. … Well, accept the hindrance calmly, and be content to turn your efforts to what is allowed. By doing so, you are at once given another opportunity for action in place of the one that was hindered—one that will fit into the ordering of life we are speaking of."

### 8.35 — MINOR
Problem words: "And the present shrinks to very little, if you only mark off its limits, and scold your mind if it cannot hold out against even this much."
Why: "and scold your mind" is tacked onto the "if" clause, so a listener cannot tell whether it is a second condition or a command.
Proposed rewrite: "And the present shrinks to very little if you only mark off its limits. If your mind cannot hold out against even this much, scold it."

### 8.40 — MINOR
Problem words: "The senses will see to that." / "without any reservation" / "Once it has become a sphere, it stays a sphere."
Why: "see to that" might mean the senses will fix the pain; the point is that the pain is the senses' concern, not the mind's. "Reservation" is used in a technical sense (a mental "if nothing prevents it" condition), which the next sentence only half explains. "it" in the last sentence could be the understanding or the obstacle.
Proposed rewrite: "The senses must deal with that; it is their business." … "If you were striving for it unconditionally, without silently adding 'if nothing prevents me', then this obstacle really is bad for you as a rational being." … "Once the understanding has become a sphere, it stays a sphere."

### 8.43 — MINOR
Problem words: "if these later people make this or that sound"
Why: "make this or that sound" is odd for speech, and a listener may not realize it means what they say.
Proposed rewrite: "And what is it to you, in any way, if these later people make this or that noise about you, or hold this or that opinion of you?"

### 8.44 — MINOR
Problem words: "the divine spirit within me"
Why: This technical term appears here for the first time in the packet with no anchor. It's also not clear that it is the same thing the text elsewhere calls the ruling faculty or the soul.
Proposed rewrite: "For there I shall keep the divine spirit within me—my inner guide—calm, that is, content, so long as it can feel and act in keeping with its own proper makeup."

### 8.46 — MINOR
Problem words: "But if something in your own character pains you, who is stopping you from correcting your opinion?"
Why: It jumps from "character" to "opinion" without saying that the fault in character comes from an opinion. A listener wonders which opinion is meant.
Proposed rewrite: "But if something in your own character pains you, who is stopping you from correcting the opinion that lies behind it?"

### 8.47 — MINOR
Problem words: "cannot be conquered when it gathers itself together and is satisfied with itself, if it does nothing it does not choose to do—even if its resistance is mere stubbornness" / "a mind free from passions" / "where from then on he cannot be taken"
Why: Stacking "when… and… if… even if" loses a listener. "Passions" is used in its old sense (disturbing emotions), and "taken" means captured, which is easy to miss.
Proposed rewrite: "Remember that the ruling faculty cannot be conquered when it gathers itself together and is satisfied with itself, refusing to do anything it does not choose to do—even when that refusal is mere stubbornness. … That is why a mind free from passions—from disturbing emotions—is a fortress. A human being has nothing more secure to run to for refuge, where from then on he cannot be captured."

### 8.48 — MINOR
Problem words: "add it as someone who knows everything that happens in the world"
Why: It sounds as if you must be all-knowing. The sense is someone who knows well what kinds of things happen in the world.
Proposed rewrite: "Or rather, do add something—but add it as someone who is familiar with everything that happens in the world."

### 8.49 — MINOR
Problem words: "The wonderful thing about her art"
Why: The sudden feminine "her/she" has no clear antecedent. The last noun was "the nature of the universe", with no personification before it, so a listener may not link the two.
Proposed rewrite: "The wonderful thing about nature's art is that, although she has set limits around herself, …"

### 8.55 — MINOR
Problem words: "and God has not willed this, so that my unhappiness should not depend on someone else"
Why: "so that" can be heard as the result of God's not willing, rather than the reason, which muddles the logic.
Proposed rewrite: "Otherwise my neighbor's wickedness would be my harm; but God has not willed this, because he did not want my unhappiness to depend on someone else."

### 8.56 — MINOR
Problem words: "(in Greek, apo tou ekteinesthai)"
Why: A listener hears a string of untranslated Greek in a parenthesis with no signal of what it is doing. The connection between aktines and ekteinesthai has to be spelled out.
Proposed rewrite: "That is why its rays are called aktines, 'extensions': the Greek name comes from ekteinesthai, 'to be stretched out' (apo tou ekteinesthai)."

### 8.59 — MINOR
Problem words: "moves straight ahead no less, and toward its target"
Why: "no less" has no stated comparison, and "and toward its target" trails off. A listener may not realize the mind is being compared with the arrow.
Proposed rewrite: "Yet the mind, both when it is being cautious and when it is busy examining something, moves straight ahead toward its target no less than the arrow does."

# Fidelity review — Book XII (12.0–12.35)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 12.0–12.35, all 36 paragraphs checked sentence by sentence against Long (1862) and STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 12.22 — BLOCKING
Source: "Therefore the termination of life for every man is no evil, because neither is it shameful, since it is both independent of the will and not opposed to the general interest, but it is good, since it is seasonable, and profitable to and congruent with the universal."
Candidate: "So the end of life is not bad for anyone. It is not shameful, since it does not depend on our choice and is not against the common good. And it is good, since it comes in its season, benefits the whole, and fits in with it."
Problem: Long's "because" is the key step of the argument: death is no evil *because* it is not shameful (only the shameful is evil). The candidate splits the sentences and drops the connective, so "not shameful" reads as a separate, parallel remark instead of the reason death is not bad. The style guide says to keep Long's logic exactly and to keep reason-giving links causal.
Proposed replacement: "So the end of life is not bad for anyone, because it is not shameful: it does not depend on our choice, and it is not against the common good. And it is good, since it comes in its season, benefits the whole, and fits in with it."

### 12.22 — MINOR
Source: "but always the universal nature, by the change of whose parts the whole universe continues ever young and perfect."
Candidate: "but always by the nature of the universe. For it is by the changing of its parts that the whole universe stays forever young and complete."
Problem: Long's descriptive relative clause becomes a "For …" explanation. That suggests the universe staying young is the *reason* nature sets the limit, which the source does not say. (It is the reverse of the style-guide rule, where causal relatives must stay causal. Here a non-causal relative has been made causal.)
Proposed replacement: "but always by the nature of the universe, through whose changing parts the whole universe stays forever young and complete."

### 12.0 — MINOR
Source: "for the passive part will look to this."
Candidate: "let the part that suffers them see to that."
Problem: Long's future statement ("will look to this") becomes an imperative. The Greek (προσοψέσθω) is in fact imperative, so the candidate is defensible. Against the Long anchor, though, it is a modality change.
Proposed replacement (if following Long): "the part that suffers them will see to that."

### 12.0 — MINOR
Source: "do the things which are agreeable to law and according to the worth of each."
Candidate: "do what the law requires and what each person deserves."
Problem: "agreeable to law" (in keeping with law) is narrowed to "what the law requires". "according to the worth of each" may refer to each thing as well as each person (κατ' ἀξίαν).
Proposed replacement: "and do what is in keeping with the law and with the worth of each."

### 12.10 — MINOR
Source: "and to accept all that God may give him."
Candidate: "and to accept everything that God gives him."
Problem: "may give" (whatever God may assign) becomes a flat "gives", and the open-ended modality is lost.
Proposed replacement: "and to accept everything that God may give him."

### 12.12 — MINOR
Source: "How ridiculous and what a stranger he is who is surprised at anything which happens in life."
Candidate: "How ridiculous, and what a stranger to the world, is the person who is surprised at anything that happens in life!"
Problem: "to the world" is added. It is well supported by Marcus's own "stranger in the universe" (IV.29) and 12.0's "stranger in your own native land", so this is borderline. Still, Long says only "a stranger".
Proposed replacement: "How ridiculous, and what a stranger, is the person who is surprised at anything that happens in life!" Alternatively, keep "to the world" and log it as an interpretive gloss.

### 12.23 — MINOR
Source: "thou wouldst see the same things, sameness of form and shortness of duration."
Candidate: "you would see the same things: the same forms, and the same short lives."
Problem: "shortness of duration" applies to everything seen. "the same short lives" narrows it to living beings and adds a second "same".
Proposed replacement: "you would see the same things: sameness of form and shortness of duration." A plainer version: "the same forms, and the same short span of existence."

### 12.27 — MINOR
Source: "in the first place, they may be seen even with the eyes"
Candidate: "first, they can in fact be seen, even with the eyes."
Problem: "in fact" is an added intensifier. It makes the claim sound more emphatic than Long's plain "may be seen".
Proposed replacement: "first, they can be seen, even with the eyes."

### 12.26 — AMBIGUITY
Source: "and Velius Rufus [or Rufus at Velia]"
Candidate: "and Velius Rufus."
Problem: Long prints two readings. One is a personal name, Velius Rufus. The other is a name plus a place, "Rufus at Velia", which would match the list's pattern of person plus place (Stertinius at Baiae, Tiberius at Capreae). The candidate silently picks the first reading and drops the alternative. This is Long's textual alternative, not a supplement that can be absorbed silently. The choice is legitimate because it is Long's main text. The candidate should log it in AMBIGUITIES.md. Alternatively, it can keep both readings: "and Velius Rufus (or Rufus at Velia)". Omitting a whole list member is not at issue, since both readings refer to one person.

### 12.26 — AMBIGUITY
Source: "and Stertinius at Briae"
Candidate: "and Stertinius at Baiae"
Problem: none. "Briae" is a misprint in Long for Baiae (Greek Βαΐαις), and the correction is right. It also matches the current style standard, which says obvious misprints in names are corrected and logged, with "Baiae for 'Briae'" as its own example. Just make sure the AMBIGUITIES.md entry exists. No change to the text is proposed.

### 12.16 — AMBIGUITY
Source: "If it is not right, do not do it: if it is not true, do not say it. [For let thy efforts be—]"
Candidate: "If it is not right, do not do it. If it is not true, do not say it. For let your effort be—"
Problem: Long's bracket marks a broken or incomplete text. In the Greek, ἡ γὰρ ὁρμὴ ἐπὶ σοὶ ἔστω roughly means "for let your impulse be in your own power", and some editors join it to §18. The candidate keeps Long's dangling fragment, which is faithful to the anchor. If completed, the standard term would be "impulse" (ὁρμή), not "effort". Log the choice. Keeping the fragment is acceptable.

Notes on the paragraphs above: every section number, opening sentence and boundary is correct. The terminology follows the standard: ruling faculty, divine spirit within, impression (12.17), impulse for ὁρμή (12.30), the common good for "social end" (12.19), and "flowed from God" for efflux (12.25). Marcus's conditionals and "if in fact it is not so" (12.4), the "either … or" triad (12.13) and the "you have forgotten" repetitions (12.25) are all preserved.

- MINOR: 7 (12.22, 12.0 ×2, 12.10, 12.12, 12.23, 12.27)
- AMBIGUITY: 3 (12.26 Velius Rufus / Rufus at Velia; 12.26 Briae → Baiae; 12.16 fragment)

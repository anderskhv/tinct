# Re-verification — Book XI R1 changes
Coverage: every changed id: 11.0, 11.1, 11.5, 11.7, 11.9, 11.10, 11.12, 11.14, 11.15, 11.17, 11.19, 11.20, 11.23, 11.24, 11.32, 11.33, 11.36, 11.38 (18). I checked each AFTER against `candidate.json` and all 18 match the packet exactly. Every section-number prefix is unchanged. I also checked the accessibility findings the editor rejected (11.5, 11.12, 11.17 ×4, 11.18, 11.37) against the current candidate text.

## Per paragraph

### 11.0 — CLEAN
Changes: "what it set out to do" became "what was set before it". "…valuing nothing more than itself—which is also a mark of law" is now two sentences: "…valuing nothing more highly than itself. Valuing nothing more highly than itself is also a mark of law." "the reason of justice" became "the reason that belongs to justice". The first change restores Long's "what has been set before it". The referent follows Long's "which" (ὅπερ, which picks up "to value nothing more than itself"). "More highly" fixes the "valuing only itself" mishearing. The genitive is rendered plainly. This resolves the fidelity MINOR and the accessibility BLOCKING finding. Nothing is added or omitted.

### 11.1 — CLEAN
Changes: "admit it" became "admit that it does". "go to the separate parts, and by dividing things" became "break things down into their separate parts, and by dividing them". "That it does" clearly means that the note overpowers you. "Break things down… dividing them" is faithful to Long's "apply thyself to their several parts… by this division".

### 11.5 — CLEAN
Changes: the first sentence is split into "…of the things that happen to them. They reminded people that it is according to nature…, and that if…". "consider what it was" became "consider what it was like". Both "that" clauses stay under "remind", as in Long. "What it was like" is a natural rendering of "observe what it was" and adds no content. Rejected finding ("tragic heroes"): a listener still follows the thought. In a passage about tragedy, "even those who cry out 'O Cithaeron!'" is heard as a cry of anguish that people must nonetheless bear. Not a defect.

### 11.7 — CLEAN
Changes: "In short" became "Finally", which restores Long. "The grafted branch is something like what gardeners mean when they say that it grows…" became "Gardeners say something like this of a grafted branch: that it grows along with the rest of the tree, but is not of one mind with it." Long's hedge ("something like") is kept, and "it" is now clearly the grafted branch. Faithful and clearer.

### 11.9 — CLEAN
Changes: "art"/"arts" became "craft"/"crafts"; "the skill of art" became "the skill of any craft"; "this is where" became "this ordering is where". "Craft" for τέχνη matches 11.4 ("your craft") and IV.2, IV.31 and VI.15, so it is consistent. "This ordering" resolves Long's "hence" to the sentence just before it (lesser things for the sake of higher), which is the only available antecedent. This resolves the accessibility BLOCKING finding.

### 11.10 — CLEAN
Changes: the relative clause "the things whose pursuit or avoidance disturbs you" is lifted into its own sentence ("Your chasing after certain things, or running from them, disturbs you."), and the conditional follows. Long's "if" and "still" are kept. What was presupposed in Long's defining clause is now stated, which adds no new claim. It is easy to follow aloud.

### 11.12 — CLEAN
Change: the trailing appositive became a separate sentence: "…of the universe? For you are a human being placed at your post…". Long's causal "since" is kept as "For", and the rhetorical question stays a question. Rejected finding ("that is how one ought to be within"): the colon immediately supplies the content ("a person should be seen by the gods neither discontented… nor complaining"), and "that" naturally takes in the attitude just described. A listener can follow. Not a defect.

### 11.14 — CLEAN
Changes: "Your very voice ought to declare it, as plainly as if it were written on your forehead" became "What your voice says ought to be plainly written on your forehead". "must notice it" became "must smell it". "simplicity/simple" became "straightforwardness/straightforward". The smell image is restored. The forehead sentence drops the added "declare" and the simile, and takes one of the two readings the fidelity reviewer allowed; the ambiguity is logged. "Straightforwardness" suits the crooked-stick image and fixes the "simple = unintelligent" mishearing. Note, not a defect: ἁπλότης is "simplicity" everywhere else in the candidate (III.11, V.9, VII.31, VIII.51, X.9, XII.27). This local exception is justified by the image, but it could be recorded in the standard so a later consistency pass does not "fix" it back.

### 11.15 — CLEAN
Changes: the repeated gloss "—that is, to things that are neither good nor bad" is removed. "both separately and as a whole" became "by itself and at all of them together". "that this care will be needed" became "that we need to give things such attention". "If these things" became "If the things themselves". The gloss exists at first use (III.11, `candidate.json` ch. 3 para 10), so removing it is correct under the standard. "By itself… all of them together" matches Long's "separately and all together". "Such attention" now points back to the looking and remembering just described, and Long's word "attention" is used. "The things themselves" correctly picks up the external things that "remain immovable".

### 11.17 — CLEAN
Changes: "if all things are not mere atoms" became "if it is not true that all things are mere atoms". "do not even know for certain whether they are doing wrong" became "do not even understand whether they are doing wrong or not". "with some circumstance in view" became "with an eye to circumstances". "For unless what is shameful is the only thing that is bad" became "For if anything besides what is shameful is bad, then". Each change is faithful. "Unless P, Q" equals "if not-P, Q", and not-P ("the shameful is not the only bad thing") equals "something besides the shameful is bad". The logic is equivalent and the double negative is gone. "Or not" is restored and "for certain" removed. "With an eye to circumstances" renders "with a certain reference to circumstances" without the reviewer's added "you cannot see". All ten rules, the lacuna and the tenth gift are intact.

Rejected findings: all four leave the thought followable, so none is a defect.
- "Ruling faculties" is glossed at II.2, per the standard.
- "That is why people are pained" follows directly from the sentence before it (deprived of the truth against its will).
- The broken sentence ("either when he is alone, and if others are present . . .") is Long's own gap. The listener gets the complete thought ("when he is alone") and hears the break.
- The atoms scope was addressed.

### 11.19 — CLEAN
Changes: "nothing is forced on it except what is in keeping with its nature" became "nothing is imposed on it by force; only what is in keeping with its nature is imposed on it". "These qualities" became "Piety and reverence". "come before" became "are prior to". This resolves the fidelity BLOCKING finding: no compulsion is placed on the mind, which restores the contrast with the elements that stay put "perforce". It renders Long's "imposed" and the Greek οὐδὲν βίαιον … ἐπιτάσσεται, ἀλλὰ μόνα ὅσα κατὰ φύσιν. "Piety and reverence" is Long's own antecedent. "Prior" is Long's word, and like πρεσβύτερα it can mean time or rank. That ambiguity is inherited, not introduced. Optional polish only: repeating "imposed" is slightly heavy aloud, and "…only what is in keeping with its nature is required of it" would sound smoother.

### 11.20 — CLEAN
Change: the nested comparison became "Most people regard many things as good, in one way or another; but people do not hold the same opinion about all of them, only about certain things, namely the things that concern the common interest." Every member of Long's clause is kept ("considered by the majority to be good", "in some way or other", "not the same opinion about all", "only about some certain things", "common interest"), and "opinion" is kept. The comparison now reads clearly into "In the same way". This resolves the accessibility BLOCKING finding.

### 11.23 — DEFECT
Change: added the gloss "—the Spartans—". The gloss is correct and allowed by the standard (Proper names: peoples take their ordinary English names).
Problem: the edit replaced the opening comma of the pair around "at their public shows" with the dash, which leaves a stray single comma: "The Lacedaemonians—the Spartans—at their public shows, used to…". It is ungrammatical, and read aloud the dash, the phrase and the comma stack three pauses before the verb.
Proposed replacement: "24. The Lacedaemonians—the Spartans—used to set out seats in the shade for strangers at their public shows, but sat down anywhere themselves."

### 11.24 — CLEAN
Change: the explanation moves inside Socrates' quotation, in the first person: "…the worst of all ends: that is, I do not want to receive a favor and then be unable to return it." This follows Long's "I would not receive" and is logged as an ambiguity. Nothing is added.

### 11.32 — CLEAN
Change: "when that is no longer allowed" became "when the child is no longer granted to him". This matches Long's "it" (the child) and the Greek δίδοται ("is given"), and it removes the sense of permission or custody. The Epictetus attribution is unchanged and logged.

### 11.33 — CLEAN
Change: "But," someone objects, "those are words of ill omen." Long's dash marks a second speaker, so "someone objects" makes that explicit without adding content. The objection no longer sounds like Epictetus contradicting himself. (11.38 uses Long's dashes, while this section uses a speech tag. Both are acceptable ways to render the same device.)

### 11.36 — CLEAN
Changes: "an art" became "a method"; "—for deciding which impressions to agree to" is added; "the worth of their object" became "the worth of the thing they aim at". "Method, a set of rules" renders Long's "art [or rules]". "Craft" would not fit here, and Long himself glosses the word as "rules". The added gloss comes from the standard's own rendering of assent ("agree to (an impression)") and does not interpret. "The thing they aim at" is a plain rendering of "the object" without the reviewer's "in proportion to". This resolves the accessibility BLOCKING finding. Note: "assent" appears unglossed earlier at V.9, but there it is ordinary English ("whatever we assent to"), so glossing at the first technical use here is reasonable.

### 11.38 — CLEAN
Changes: "used to say" became "used to ask"; em dashes now separate the turns. "Ask" fits a speech that is entirely questions. The dashes follow Long and the typography standard. Speaker attribution is not invented (the reviewer's "They answered" was rightly refused). The question-and-answer rhythm (each question ends in "?", each answer is short) lets a listener track the turns.

### Rejected accessibility findings in unchanged paragraphs
- 11.18 (number the sayings "to the first/second/third"): the paragraph announces "four chief ways", gives three separate quoted sayings, then says "The fourth is…". A listener can match them without numbering. Not a defect.
- 11.37 ("the dispute" has no antecedent): this is Long's detached fragment. The sentence still says clearly what is at stake ("whether we are to be mad or not"). Supplying an antecedent would be invention. Not a defect.

## Verdict
One defect; everything else verified clean.
- **11.23**: a stray comma left by the new gloss. Replace with: "24. The Lacedaemonians—the Spartans—used to set out seats in the shade for strangers at their public shows, but sat down anywhere themselves."

No meaning changes, omissions or unsupported additions were introduced in the other 17 changed paragraphs. The fidelity BLOCKING finding (11.19) and all five accessibility BLOCKING findings (11.0, 11.9, 11.17, 11.20, 11.36) are resolved. None of the rejected accessibility findings leaves a first-time listener unable to follow the thought.

# Fidelity review — Book X (10.0–10.37)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 10.0–10.37, all 38 paragraphs checked sentence by sentence against Long (1862) and against STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 10.0 — MINOR
- Source: "whatever they shall give for the conservation of the perfect living being, the good and just and beautiful, which generates and holds together all things"
- Candidate: "whatever they will give for the preservation of the perfect living being, the universe? That being is good and just and noble."
- Issue: (a) "the universe" is an added identifying gloss. It is the standard Stoic reading, and the rest of the sentence supports it, but Long does not say it. (b) Long's "beautiful" is rendered "noble". The style guide keeps "noble" for the moral sense of καλόν. Here the word describes the cosmos, so the sense is borderline. Either reading is defensible, but the guide's default for a non-moral use is "beautiful".
- Proposed replacement: "…whatever they will give for the preservation of the perfect living being? That being is good and just and beautiful." (The gloss "the universe" could stay if the editors count it as a permitted short gloss. If so, log it in AMBIGUITIES.md.)

### 10.1 — MINOR
- Source: "Observe what thy nature requires, so far as thou art governed by nature only"
- Candidate: "Observe what your nature requires insofar as you are governed by nature alone, as a plant is."
- Issue: "as a plant is" is an added explanation. It is correct Stoic doctrine (φύσις as the vegetative level), but Long does not state it. The guide says not to add interpretation.
- Proposed replacement: "Observe what your nature requires insofar as you are governed by nature alone."

### 10.3 — MINOR
- Source: "If a man is mistaken, instruct him kindly and show him his error."
- Candidate: "If someone is mistaken, correct him kindly and show him his error."
- Issue: "instruct" (Gk. teach) becomes "correct". "Correct" is closer to rebuke and repeats "show him his error", so the teaching sense is lost.
- Proposed replacement: "If someone is mistaken, teach him kindly and show him his error."

### 10.6 — MINOR
- Source: "did Nature herself design to do evil to the things which are parts of herself, and to make them subject to evil and of necessity fall into evil"
- Candidate: "did Nature herself set out to do harm to the things that are parts of herself, to make them liable to harm and bound by necessity to fall into it?"
- Issue: The previous sentence keeps "evil" ("both an evil and a necessity"). This sentence switches three times to "harm", which weakens the argument's single term. The guide renders κακόν as "bad" or "evil", not "harm".
- Proposed replacement: "For did Nature herself set out to do evil to the things that are parts of herself, to make them subject to evil and bound by necessity to fall into it?"

### 10.6 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "taken back into the universal reason, whether this at certain periods is consumed by fire or renewed by eternal changes"
- Candidate: "taken back into the reason that governs the universe, whether the universe is consumed by fire at fixed periods or renewed by everlasting changes"
- Note: In Long's grammar, "this" could mean either the universal reason or the universe. The Greek participles fit either τοῦ ὅλου or its λόγος. The candidate's choice of "the universe" matches Stoic ekpyrosis doctrine and is defensible. The other reading is "whether that reason is consumed by fire…". No change required. Log it in AMBIGUITIES.md.

### 10.8 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "Mimi, war, astonishment, torpor, slavery"
- Candidate: "Farce, war, panic, sluggishness, slavery"
- Note: Long's "Mimi" (Gk. μῖμος) is obscure. It can mean mime-shows or farce, or play-acting and sham. Some editors emend it to μῖσος ("hatred"). "Farce" is one legitimate reading, and "play-acting" is another. "Panic" for "astonishment" (πτοία, a fluttering alarm or agitation) is acceptable but a little stronger than Long. If Long's word should be kept, use "alarm". Log it in AMBIGUITIES.md. No change required.

### 10.10 — MINOR
- Source: "Acquire the contemplative way of seeing how all things change into one another"
- Candidate: "Acquire the habit of looking at how all things change into one another."
- Issue: "contemplative" (θεωρητικὴν μέθοδον, a method of contemplation) is dropped. The rendering makes a philosophical method sound like a habit of looking.
- Proposed replacement: "Acquire the contemplative way of seeing how all things change into one another."

### 10.14 — AMBIGUITY (editor's query: source misprint; candidate accepted)
- Source: "Let me see, let them know a real man who lives according to nature."
- Candidate: "Let people see, let them come to know, a true human being who lives in keeping with nature."
- Judgment: The candidate is correct and should stay. "Let me see" is a misprint in Long's printed text (repeated in the Gutenberg transcription) for "Let men see". The Greek is ἰδέτωσαν, ἱστορησάτωσαν οἱ ἄνθρωποι ἄνθρωπον ἀληθινόν, "let men see, let them learn of, a true man". Long's own sentence shows this: "let them know" needs a plural antecedent that "me" cannot supply, and the next sentence continues "If they cannot endure him". Reading "me" would also bring a first-person onlooker into a section that is otherwise in the second person. "Let people see" follows the guide's generic "man" → "people" and restores what Long meant. No change. Log it in AMBIGUITIES.md as a source misprint, corrected by Long's evident intent and the Greek.

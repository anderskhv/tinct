## Findings

### 3.11 — MINOR (terminology drift: δαίμων)
- Current: "…not letting anything else distract you, and keeping your divine part pure, as if you had to give it back at once."
- Problem: Long's "divine part" here stands for τὸν ἔνδον δαίμονα (III.12). The standard says δαίμων is always "the divine spirit within", and VIII.44 ("my divine part") was changed to the stable term for this reason (AMBIGUITIES 8.44). This is the only place in I–VI where the term slips. (The "more divine part" at 11.18 is a different Greek expression and is outside this packet.)
- Replacement: "…not letting anything else distract you, and keeping the divine spirit within you pure, as if you had to give it back at once."

### 6.31 — MINOR (gloss repeated)
- Current: "To this little body all things are indifferent—neither good nor bad—because it cannot perceive differences."
- Problem: "indifferent" is glossed once, at its first use in 3.10 ("things that are indifferent—neither good nor bad in themselves"). The standard says a gloss is given once and later uses keep only the short term. Keep "indifferent" here, because the argument turns on "cannot perceive differences". The inserted gloss is the only repeated technical gloss in I–VI.
- Replacement: "To this little body all things are indifferent, because it cannot perceive differences."

### 3.0 / 6.27 — MINOR (optional; ὁρμή rendered unevenly)
- Current 3.0: "…or having imagination and appetite…"; current 6.27: "…of the tugging of the strings that work our appetites…"
- Problem: Long's "appetites" for ὁρμαί is changed to "impulses" in 3.5 and 3.15, following the standard's impulse rule (R1-fidelity-book03). The same Greek is left as "appetite(s)" in 3.0 (ὁρμᾶν) and 6.27 (the puppet-string image, which 2.1 renders "impulses"). This is not wrong in English, and it follows Long's word, as 9.6 does for "desire". Make the change only if the ὁρμή rule should cover Long's "appetite" everywhere.
- Replacement (if applied): 3.0 "…or having imagination and impulses…"; 6.27 "…of the tugging of the strings that work our impulses…"

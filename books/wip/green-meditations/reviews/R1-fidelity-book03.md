# Fidelity review — Book III (3.0–3.15)
Reviewer: independent fidelity reviewer. Coverage: 3.0, 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 3.6, 3.7, 3.8, 3.9, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12, 3.13, 3.14, 3.15 (3.0–3.15, all 16). Each candidate paragraph was compared with Long sentence by sentence and checked against STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY.md.

## Findings

### 3.5 — AMBIGUITY
- Source: "with that which is rationally and politically [or, practically] good."
- Candidate: "to compete with what is good for a rational being and a member of the community."
- Issue: Long's bracket is not a supplement for sense. It is a **textual alternative**: the manuscripts divide between πολιτικῶς ("politically/civically") and πρακτικῶς ("practically"). The candidate silently follows the first reading and drops Long's alternative. Following Long's main reading is allowed by the style guide, but the style guide also requires the case to be logged in `AMBIGUITIES.md`. It is not logged there: the file has no Book III entries.
- Proposed: either keep the sentence and add a row to `AMBIGUITIES.md`: `| 3.5 | "rationally and politically [or, practically] good" | "good for a rational being and a member of the community" | Manuscript variant πολιτικῶς / πρακτικῶς; Long prints "politically" and offers "practically". Long's main reading followed. |`. Or carry Long's openness in the text: "to compete with what is good for a rational being and a member of the community (or, as some read it, what is good in practice)." I recommend logging the case and keeping the sentence as it is.

### 3.5 — MINOR
- Source: "…give the preference to that good thing which is thy proper possession and thy own; for it is not right that anything of any other kind … should come into competition with…"
- Candidate: "…to give first place to the good that properly belongs to you and is your own. It is not right for anything of a different kind…"
- Issue: The candidate drops Long's "for". That word makes this sentence the reason for "give room to nothing else". Without it, the sentence reads as a separate assertion. The style guide says clauses that carry a reason must stay causal.
- Proposed: "For it is not right for anything of a different kind—such as praise from the crowd, or power, or the enjoyment of pleasure—to compete with what is good for a rational being and a member of the community."

### 3.2 — MINOR
- Source: "and to be a slave to the vessel, which is as much inferior as that which serves it is superior"
- Candidate: "and you will stop being a slave to the vessel, the body. The vessel is as much inferior…"
- Issue: The gloss "the body" is not in Long, and the style guide does not prescribe it. It is well supported by "earth and corruption", so it is not a meaning change. Still, it adds an identification that Marcus leaves to the image.
- Proposed: "and you will stop being a slave to the vessel, which is as much inferior as the part that serves it is superior: for the one is intelligence and the divine; the other is earth and decay." If the gloss is kept for listeners, record it as a deliberate choice.

### 3.2 — MINOR
- Source: "The Chaldaei foretold the deaths of many"
- Candidate: "The Chaldaeans foretold the deaths of many people"
- Issue: The style guide says proper names are "kept as Long spells them". "Chaldaeans" is the ordinary English form and is easier for a newcomer, so this is a terminology/consistency note only. Decide once, and log the decision if the modern form is kept.
- Proposed: keep "The Chaldaeans" and note the decision under Proper names. Or, following the rule strictly: "The Chaldaei foretold the deaths of many people, and then fate caught them too."

### 3.3 — MINOR
- Source: "using too the [deity] which is planted within him, which makes the man uncontaminated by pleasure, unharmed by any pain, untouched by any insult, feeling no wrong, a fighter in the noblest fight, one who cannot be overpowered by any passion, dyed deep with justice, accepting with all his soul…; and not often … imagining what another says…"
- Candidate: "…which keeps him unstained by pleasure, unharmed by any pain, untouched by any insult, and unable to feel wronged. He is a fighter in the noblest of fights; no passion can overpower him; he is dyed deep with justice; and he welcomes with all his soul…"
- Issue: In Long (and in the Greek, where these are all accusatives governed by the same verb), the whole list is what the divine spirit makes the man. The candidate ends the causal link after "wronged" and turns the rest into bare statements about the man. The qualities remain, but the claim that the divine spirit produces them is lost for the second half of the list.
- Proposed: "…which keeps him unstained by pleasure, unharmed by any pain, untouched by any insult, and unable to feel wronged. It makes him a fighter in the noblest of fights, one whom no passion can overpower, dyed deep with justice, welcoming with all his soul everything that happens and is given to him as his share; and only rarely, and only when it is really necessary and for the common good, does he imagine what someone else is saying or doing or thinking."

### 3.6 — MINOR
- Source: "Never value anything as profitable to thyself which shall compel thee…"
- Candidate: "Never value anything as good for you if it will force you…"
- Issue: "Profitable" (τὸ συμφέρον, the advantageous) becomes "good", which is the technical Stoic word the book reserves for virtue. The Stoics do tie the advantageous to the good, but the substitution sharpens Long's wording in a doctrinally loaded direction.
- Proposed: "Never value anything as being to your advantage if it will force you to break your word, …"

### 3.8 — MINOR
- Source: "Reverence the faculty which produces opinion."
- Candidate: "Honor the faculty that forms opinions."
- Issue: "Reverence" (σέβε) is religious veneration. "Honor" weakens it and loses the tie to the book's language of reverence for the divine within. The plain modern word is available.
- Proposed: "Revere the faculty that forms opinions."

### 3.10 — MINOR
- Source: "For nothing is so productive of elevation of mind as to be able to examine methodically and truly every object …, and always to look at things so as to see at the same time what kind of universe this is, … what each thing is, and of what it is composed, …"
- Candidate: "For nothing so lifts up the mind as being able to examine, methodically and truthfully, every object that comes before you in life. It means always looking at things in such a way as to see … It means seeing what each thing is…"
- Issue: In Long, "always to look at things so as to see…" is a second item coordinated with "to be able to examine". Both are what lifts the mind. The candidate's "It means…" turns the second item into a definition of the first, which changes the logical relation. The second "It means seeing…" also recasts what Long presents as further objects of "to see".
- Proposed: "For nothing so lifts up the mind as being able to examine, methodically and truthfully, every object that comes before you in life, and always looking at things in such a way as to see at the same time what kind of universe this is; what use each thing serves in it; what value each thing has in relation to the whole, and what value in relation to a human being, who is a citizen of the highest city, of which all other cities are like households. You see what each thing is, what it is made of, and how long it is in its nature to last—this thing that is now making an impression on me—and what virtue I need in dealing with it: …"

## Paragraphs with no findings

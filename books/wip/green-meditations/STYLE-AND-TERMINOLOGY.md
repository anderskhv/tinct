# Meditations modern-en — style and terminology standard

Set during the Book II pilot (2026-09-23) and applied to every book. Source is
George Long (1862) as served in `source.json`. One output paragraph per source
section; the section number that opens each paragraph ("1. ", "2. " …) is kept
exactly.

## Aim

A newcomer should understand what Marcus is telling himself on first reading or
first hearing. Keep every observation, argument, example, qualification and
image; keep the private, self-admonishing voice and the repetitions that carry
meaning. Distinguish real philosophical difficulty (keep it, make the English
clear) from difficult English (fix it).

Do not add modern psychology, motivational slogans, historical explanation, or
interpretation the text does not support. Keep Marcus's "if", "perhaps",
"either … or" alternatives and questions. Do not condense.

## Voice

- Self-address stays second person ("you"); where Long uses "I"/"me", keep it.
- Long's generic "a man" becomes "a person", "someone", "people" or "a human
  being" where the Greek means any human being; "man" stays where Marcus means
  himself as a man or a male role ("as a Roman and a man"). The pronoun that
  follows a generic "person" may be "he" where rewriting would be clumsy.
- Imperatives stay imperatives. Rhetorical questions stay questions.
- Sentences short enough to follow aloud. Break a long Long period into
  several sentences rather than drop any member of it.
- No archaic forms (thou, doth, whence, wherefore, therein, efflux, contexture).

## Apparatus

- Long's bracketed supplements ("[only]", "[I learned]") are absorbed silently
  when they are needed for sense.
- Long's editorial cross-references to other sections ("(vi. 28)", "(ii. 11,
  12)") are translator apparatus and are not carried (the live edition did not
  carry them either).
- Attributions of quoted authors that the reader needs in order to know who is
  speaking are kept in words (e.g. "as Homer says"; "Epictetus said"). Bare
  bibliographic locators (verse and line numbers) are not carried.

## Proper names

Kept as Long spells them (Verus, Rusticus, Apollonius, Sextus, Alexander the
grammarian, Fronto, Alexander the Platonist, Catulus, Severus, Maximus,
Antoninus, Epictetus, Chrysippus, Theophrastus, Monimus, Carnuntum, the Granua,
etc.). Peoples take their ordinary English names (the Chaldaeans, the Greeks for Long's Hellenes). Obvious misprints in Long's printed names are corrected and logged (Baiae for "Briae", Pergamus for "Fergamus"). Brief glosses only where a reader cannot follow the thought without them.

## Lessons from the Book II pilot (apply everywhere)

- Resolve dangling "this / these things / there" to its antecedent when Long's
  antecedent is clear; if it is not clear, keep it general ("all this") rather
  than invent one.
- Keep Long's logic exactly: sufficient conditions stay sufficient ("whoever
  holds on to them is able to…"), not "must"; "no longer" stays "no longer".
- Where Long builds a list on one verb ("To observe… To observe too…"), keep the
  same subject through the list.
- Relative clauses that carry the reason ("nature, in some part of which…") must
  stay causal; do not turn them into a loose "and".
- Words that modern listeners hear in another sense: avoid "unaffected"
  (→ unpretentious), "plays a part" (→ puts on an act), bare "substance" for
  the body (→ the stuff we are made of), "unsocial" (→ set against others).
- Glosses are given ONCE, at first use in reading order. Per-book accessibility
  reviewers see one book at a time and will report terms as "unexplained"; those
  findings are rejected when the gloss exists earlier.
- Book II glosses already given (do not repeat the full gloss later; the short
  term alone is enough): ruling faculty (II.2), providence (II.3), divine spirit
  within (II.13), opinion (II.15), the oldest city and commonwealth = the
  universe (II.16).
- Keep Marcus's switches between "I" and "you" where Long has them.
- Where the source is corrupt or ambiguous, follow Long's reading and log it in
  `AMBIGUITIES.md`.

## Typography

Straight ASCII quotation marks and apostrophes, as in the live edition. Em
dashes for asides and for the dash-marked objections in Long's dialogues.

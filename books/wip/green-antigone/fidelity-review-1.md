# Fidelity Review — Antigone (candidate modern-en vs. source original-en), Round 1

**Source edition (fidelity anchor):** `antigone-original-en.json` (the archaic-English verse translation staged as `source.json`), the sole anchor used — no other translation consulted.

## Coverage statement

Every paragraph in every chapter was read against its source counterpart, in packets of 5-10 paragraphs with one paragraph of context on each side of every packet boundary, followed by a full whole-chapter re-read per chapter for cross-boundary issues. No paragraph was skimmed or skipped. Full non-sampled coverage: 318/318 paragraphs across all 11 chapters (Ch1 Prologue 30, Ch2 Parodos 5, Ch3 First Episode 34, Ch4 First Stasimon 5, Ch5 Second Episode 72, Ch6 Second Stasimon 12, Ch7 Third Episode 40, Ch8 Third Stasimon 6, Ch9 Fourth Episode 53, Ch10 Fourth Stasimon 18, Ch11 Exodos 43).

Packeting used: Ch1 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-29]; Ch2 and Ch4 read whole (5 paragraphs each, well under packet size); Ch3 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-33] with the Creon/Guard split respected; Ch5 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-29]/[29-39]/[39-49]/[49-59]/[59-71]; Ch6 and Ch8 read whole; Ch7 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-29]/[29-39]; Ch9 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-29]/[29-39]/[39-52]; Ch10 read whole (18 short paragraphs); Ch11 in packets [0-9]/[9-19]/[19-29]/[29-42].

## Method notes specific to this play

Given the Antigone-specific risks named for this review, each packet was additionally checked for: (a) whether Antigone's and Creon's arguments retained equal logical force (neither turned into a strawman), (b) whether Antigone's defiant certainty and Creon's escalating-then-collapsing confidence stayed as distinct, trackable emotional arcs, (c) whether Ismene's and Haemon's more conflicted/pleading registers stayed distinguishable from the leads' certainty, and (d) whether the Chorus's stance (deferential to Creon early, praising both Haemon and Creon mid-play, sympathetic-but-condemning toward Antigone, then openly urging Creon to relent) tracked its shifts rather than flattening to one voice. All four held up across every packet — see "Characterization check" below.

## Defects found

### Non-blocking (recommended fixes, none change meaning)

1. **Ch9 [33]** (Teiresias's prophecy speech) — source: "...The frustrate signs of oracles grown dumb." Candidate: "...these were the frustrate signs of oracles grown dumb." The word "frustrate" is carried over unmodernized as an archaic adjectival form ("frustrated/thwarted" in contemporary English). This is a genuine gap against the modernization mandate — it is spoken dialogue left as an archaic island inside an otherwise fully modernized speech, immediately before and after which every other clause has been rebuilt into contemporary syntax. **Fix:** replace "the frustrate signs" with "the thwarted signs" or "the frustrated signs" (meaning: signs that fail to yield a clear reading).

2. **Ch3 [21]** (Creon's threat to the Guard) — source: "Hanged on a cross, alive ye first shall make / Confession of this outrage." Candidate: "You'll be strung up alive first and made to confess this outrage." The specific image of crucifixion/impalement ("hanged on a cross") is generalized to "strung up." Meaning and sequence (alive, before confession is extracted) are preserved, but a concrete method detail is softened. Non-blocking — arguably legitimate modernization of an image that risks reading anachronistically — but flagged per the "preserve every image" rule; the merge owner should decide whether to restore the cross image or keep the generalization.

3. **Ch2 [2]** — source has "great Area like a war-horse wheeled," which is very likely a digitization typo for "Ares" (Ares is named correctly three other times in this same chapter/play). Candidate silently renders it as "great Ares wheeled like a war-horse." This is a defensible typo correction rather than a name change, but per the "flag silent corrections even if the source is 'wrong'" rule it is noted here for the record rather than passed over.

4. **Ch9 [5]** — source spells the mountain "Siphylus"; candidate renders it "Sipylus" (the standard spelling of Mount Sipylus, Niobe's mountain). Likely another digitization variant rather than a deliberate archaic spelling, but flagged per the same silent-correction rule.

5. **Ch1 [17]** — source: "A specious pretext." Candidate: "A convenient excuse." "Specious" (deceptively plausible) softens slightly to "convenient" (self-serving); the accusatory force is close but not identical. Minor nuance shift, non-blocking.

### Blocking

None found. No actor misattributions, no dropped or flipped negations, no causality reversals, no altered conditions, no omitted claims/examples/asides, and no invented additions were found anywhere in the 318 paragraphs. The climactic high-actor-density passage most at risk for this defect class — Haemon's attack on Creon, his suicide, and his dying embrace of Antigone (Ch11 [13]) — was checked with particular care given this is exactly the failure pattern (actor-misattribution from sentence restructuring) that the protocol calls out from the Leviathan pilot; it is rendered correctly: Haemon (not Creon) draws the sword and strikes, Creon (not Haemon) springs back and evades, and Haemon's own dying breath (not some other cause) reddens Antigone's cheek — all match source exactly.

## Characterization check (Antigone-specific)

- **Antigone vs. Creon, argument strength:** Both retain their full source logic. Antigone's "unwritten laws of Heaven... not of today or yesterday" argument (Ch5 [17]) is rendered with its complete chain of reasoning intact, and Creon's civic-order argument (Ch3 [1], Ch7 [3]) keeps its full force — "the state is the good ship that holds all our fortunes," "a state for one man is no state at all" is rebutted, not conceded. Neither character is flattened into a strawman in the candidate.
- **Antigone's certainty vs. Creon's arc:** Antigone's declarative confidence ("I knew it," "Guilty. I did it. I don't deny it," "when my strength fails me, yes — but not before") is preserved verbatim in force throughout Ch1, Ch5, and Ch9. Creon's arc from confident edict-giver (Ch3) through escalating rigidity (Ch5, Ch7 — "no woman shall be master while I live") to collapse (Ch10 "I go at once... my mind now sways this way," Ch11's lament) tracks the source's own escalation-then-reversal structure paragraph for paragraph; nothing in the candidate accelerates or delays that collapse relative to source.
- **Ismene and Haemon's registers:** Ismene's hedged, pleading language ("What life is there for me without my sister?", "Do not scorn me... let me share your work of piety") stays clearly distinct from Antigone's flat certainty throughout Ch1 and Ch5 — the candidate does not harden or flatten her voice toward Antigone's. Haemon's Ch7 speech is rendered as genuinely persuasive and conflicted (deferential opening, "it is not for me to say you are wrong," building to open defiance only after being provoked) — this arc is intact, not compressed into either pure filial deference or pure rebellion.
- **Chorus's shifting sympathies:** Verified across the play — deferential/neutral toward Creon in Ch3 ("your word is law... you may dispose of us the living as you please"), openly troubled once the Guard reports something "more than natural" (Ch3 [20]), praising Haemon's speech as reasonable while also validating Creon's in the same breath (Ch7 [4]/[6]), condemning Antigone as "a self-willed rebel" even while voicing "no comfort" over her fate (Ch9 [10], [17]), and finally urging Creon outright to relent (Ch10 [2]-[8]). The candidate preserves every one of these shifts in the same places source has them — the Chorus's ambiguous, evolving allegiance is not flattened to a single stance anywhere in the rendering.

## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH FIXES REQUIRED** — one clear-cut unmodernized-archaism fix (Ch9 [33] "frustrate" → "thwarted"/"frustrated") should be applied before final sign-off, since it directly violates the "modernize quoted/spoken language, no archaic islands" rule. The remaining four notes (Ch3 [21] cross image, Ch2 [2] and Ch9 [5] typo normalizations, Ch1 [17] nuance softening) are non-blocking stylistic/documentation notes for the merge owner's judgment, not required changes. No blocking fidelity defects — actor attribution, negation, causality, certainty, conditions, omissions, additions, and the play's two central arguments and the Chorus's shifting stance are all faithfully preserved across the full, non-sampled read.

# Tinct modern-da: translation brief (batch e-short-classics, 21 Oct 2026)

Source: Tinct's own modern-English edition (modern-en). Target: a modern Danish reading edition (modern-da).

## Non-negotiable structure
- One Danish paragraph for every English paragraph, in the same order. Never merge, split, drop, reorder or add paragraphs.
- Translate every sentence. Never summarise, shorten, skip or add explanation. Every claim, example, name, aside and qualifier must survive.
- Translate chapter titles too (when a segment has a "title").
- Keep the author's paragraphing, numbering at paragraph start (e.g. "1. ", "§"), verse lines, lists and emphasis markup exactly (e.g. `_status_` stays `_status_` around the Danish word).
- No English left in the text, except proper names, titles of works and genuinely foreign terms the English also treats as foreign (Latin/Greek/French phrases may stay if the English keeps them).

## Style
- Natural, present-day Danish for an adult reader, as if originally written in Danish. Translate meaning, not words.
- Prefer verbs to nouns ("da han ankom", not "ved hans ankomst"). Avoid anglicisms, calques and English word order ("i ordre til at", "tage plads", "gøre sense", "det er op til dig", passive chains, "som" where Danish wants "der/hvor" etc.).
- Keep the book's register: plain where the English is plain, formal where it is formal, ironic where it is ironic. Do not make it more colloquial or more archaic than the English.
- Quotations and dialogue use »…«; quotations inside quotations use ›…‹. Never use "…" or “…” in the Danish.
- Dashes: use – (tankestreg with spaces) for parenthetical dashes in running text; keep the — in chapter titles of the form "Kapitel 4 — …".
- Commas: modern Danish grammatical comma without startkomma (no comma before an opening subordinate clause unless needed for clarity; comma after a subordinate clause that comes first).
- Numbers: write as the English does (words or digits). Never use old counting forms (firsindstyve, halvtredsindstyve etc.): use firs, halvtreds, tres, halvfjerds, halvfems.
- Names: use the established Danish forms (Alexander den Store, Kyros, Hannibal, Scipio, Pave Alexander 6., Konstantinopel, Venedig, Lissabon, Marcus Aurelius, Antoninus Pius …). Keep Tinct's earlier Danish choices for names and recurring terms when a glossary file is given.
- Units, currency, titles of rank: keep what the English says (do not convert miles to km).
- Do not use the words "følgte" (wrong; the past tense is "fulgte"), "uroligskaber", "spotsangeren".
- Do not put a space before , . ! ? ; :

## Chapter titles
- "Chapter 4 — X" → "Kapitel 4 — X (translated)". "Book 1" → "Bog 1". "Preamble" → "Indledning". Roman-numeral section titles keep their numerals. All-caps titles stay all-caps in Danish (mirror the source).

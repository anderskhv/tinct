# F05 fidelity review: chapters 23 to 28

## Scope
Read every paragraph pair in ch23 (13), ch24 (8), ch25 (9), ch26 (12), ch27 (18), ch28 (6) = 66 paragraphs. Paragraph counts match the source in all six chapters; fragments (ch25 p8, ch26 p10, p11, ch27 p16, p17) start and end where the source does. `[n]` markers (ch25 p6; ch26 p2, p4; ch27 p4, p11, p14) and Latin (ch25 p6, ch27 p11) are intact. Key terms checked against Rousseau's French from my own knowledge only (no other translation).

## Findings

ch26 p9 | MINOR | "It is not justified, as Aristotle maintains, by the aim of always putting the rich first" makes "as Aristotle maintains" ambiguous: a reader can take it as Aristotle maintaining the *not*. In Cole, Aristotle is the one who holds that the rich should always come first. | Replace the span with: "It is not justified, as Aristotle claims, by the aim of always putting the rich first." (better: "It is not justified, as Aristotle maintains it is, by the aim of ...")

ch26 p8 | MINOR | Source "for instance, moderation on the side of the rich and contentment on that of the poor" is an example list; "One is ..., another is ..." reads as an exhaustive pair and drops "for instance". | "But although aristocracy does not require all the virtues that popular government needs, it requires some of its own, for instance moderation among the rich and contentment among the poor."

ch27 p4 | MINOR | "He pretended to teach kings" : Cole's "professed" means "claimed". "Pretended" in modern English implies feigning and pre-judges Rousseau's footnote about Machiavelli's real aim. | "He claimed to teach kings, but it was the people he really taught."

ch27 p7 | NIT | "fit to do them credit" is loose for "such as to fill them with honour" (them = the highest positions). | "...anyone who is not enlightened and capable, and able to fill those offices with honor."

ch27 p12 | NIT | Source "if a court is more subtle in intrigue, there is more wisdom in a senate" is a comparison; candidate makes two flat assertions. Sense survives. | "We may say, then, that in general, if a court is more skilled in intrigue, a senate has more wisdom, and Republics ..."

ch23 p9 | NIT | "almost always charged with some governmental function" became "almost every magistrate has some governmental function"; slight shift of "almost" from frequency to extent. | "because a magistrate is almost always charged with some governmental function, while no citizen, taken singly, exercises any function of Sovereignty."

ch25 p5 | NIT | "should be found" sharpened to "must be found". | "the same principle should be found in every well-constituted State"

ch28 p5 | NIT | Italic Latin `_maxima_` rendered "highest degrees"; style note keeps Latin verbatim (ch27 p5 keeps `_maximum_`). | "The _maxima_ of both strength and weakness are found in simple governments ..."

No BLOCKING defects found. No omissions of clauses, names, numbers or examples; no term drift (Sovereign / prince / government / magistrates / general will / corporate will / particular will all keep their distinctions throughout ch23, 26, 27, 28).

## Verdicts on flagged items in my chapters
- ch23 p4 "common will of the magistrates": FAITHFUL. It is Cole's (and Rousseau's volonté commune) term for the corporate will, deliberately distinct from the general will. It is not blurred; the next sentence names it "corporate will". Style-note ban on "common will" targets the general will only.
- ch23 p12 "rectitude" -> "how upright it is": FAITHFUL (clunky; "integrity" or "honesty" would read better, NIT, not required).
- ch25 p1 "corruption of the legislator ... sure result of taking a particular view": FAITHFUL. "Inevitable sequel to a particular standpoint" is correctly rendered; the antecedent of "which" is the corruption of the legislator.
- ch25 p3 tribunals -> bodies: FAITHFUL (French tribunaux = magistracies). Note ch28 p5 keeps "tribunals"; harmless inconsistency.
- ch25 p5: FAITHFUL (only "should" -> "must", NIT above).
- ch26 p1 savages: FAITHFUL; keeps Cole's word and "admirable".
- ch26 p6 "for the benefit of the many": FAITHFUL (its = the many/people). "interest of the governing body" for "corporate interest" is fine.
- ch26 p7 "individually desirable" -> "desirable in a given case": ACCEPTABLE. Cole's wording is odd; the French sense is "in particular", so the recast is defensible and does not change the argument.
- ch26 p9 Aristotle clause: see MINOR above (needs a fix). "deserts of men" -> "a man's merit": FAITHFUL.
- ch27 p4 Samuel/"preachers": FAITHFUL ("political preachers" for "sermonisers"; "Samuel pressed on the Hebrews" fine). Only the "pretended" issue above.
- ch27 p16 "Caesar Borgia": ACCEPTABLE per style note (book-wide Caesar). The historical spelling is Cesare Borgia; optional NIT.
- ch28 p0 "isolated ruler" -> "lone ruler": FAITHFUL.

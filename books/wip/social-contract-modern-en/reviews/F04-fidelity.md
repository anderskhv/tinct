# F04 fidelity review: chapters 18 to 22

## Scope
Read every paragraph pair against Cole in ch18 (6 paragraphs), ch19 (7), ch20 (7), ch21 (6) and ch22 (24). That is 50 paragraphs, all of them. Paragraph counts match the source in every chapter. Fragments (ch19 p6, ch20 p5, p6, ch22 p23) start and end where the source does and borrow nothing. Latin: none in these chapters. `_italic_` markers and the `[1]`, `[2]` markers are in the right places. Where I checked a term against Rousseau's French, it was from my own knowledge.

Result: 0 BLOCKING, 6 MINOR, 8 NIT. No key-term drift found. The Sovereign, prince, government, magistrates, general will and the particular/general will distinction all survive.

## Findings

ch18 p2 | MINOR | "which all these distant officials wish to escape or to impose upon" became "want to exploit". Cole's "impose upon" is Rousseau's "en imposer", which means to deceive or bluff the central authority. "Exploit" changes the meaning. Also "the general authority" became "the central authority", a small gloss. | Span: "the measures needed to maintain the central authority, which all those distant officials either want to escape or want to exploit" -> "the measures needed to maintain the general authority, which all those distant officials either want to evade or want to deceive"

ch18 p4 | MINOR | The hedge "It may be said that" was dropped, so the claim is stated flat. | Span: "The reason for expansion is only external and relative, so it should rank below" -> "It may be said that the reason for expansion, being only external and relative, should rank below"

ch18 p1 | NIT | "A thousand arguments" became "Many arguments", which flattens the hyperbole. | "A thousand arguments can be given for this principle."

ch18 p5 | NIT | "greatly" was dropped from "congratulated themselves greatly". "I should add" brings in a first person that Cole's "It may be added" does not have. | "It may be added that some States are known to have been constituted so that ... Perhaps they congratulated themselves greatly on this lucky necessity."

ch19 p0 | MINOR | "Only insignificance or greatness can keep it free." Rousseau's word is "petitesse" (smallness), set against "grandeur". "Insignificance" is archaic here and could be read as unimportance. | Span: "Only insignificance or greatness can keep it free." -> "Only smallness or greatness can keep it free."

ch20 p2 | NIT | The parallel "force of circumstances ... force of legislation" lost its second "force". | "It is precisely because the force of circumstances constantly tends to destroy equality that the force of legislation should always tend to maintain it."

ch20 p3 | NIT | "makes its legislation its own" is fine, but "something in itself that determines how they apply" is awkward and ambiguous about what "they" applies. | Span: "something in itself that determines how they apply and makes its legislation its own" -> "something in itself that gives them a particular application and makes its legislation peculiarly its own"

ch21 p1 | NIT | The connectives "For" ("For, if there is ... only one good system") and "Besides" were dropped. They are small, but the style note says to keep the logical connectives. | "If each State has only one good system, ..." -> "For if each State has only one good system, ..."; "In any case, ..." -> "Besides, in any case, ..."

ch22 p2 | NIT | "active man" became "able-bodied man". It is acceptable, but "nimble" or "active" is closer to Rousseau's contrast with the paralytic. | "an active man wills not to"

ch22 p10 | MINOR | "the number of a people" became "the size of a people". After ch18 and 19, which separate territory from population, "size" could be read as territory. | Span: "I shall use the size of a people as my example, since it is the easiest to put into numbers." -> "I shall use the number of people in a nation as my example, since it is the easiest to put into numbers."

ch22 p13 | MINOR | "the less relation the particular wills have to the general will" became "the less ... have to do with". Here "relation" is the technical term that p12 has just defined (the geometrical sense against the ordinary sense), so the link to p12 is lost. "the laws" also lost "the". | Full replacement: "Now, the less relation the particular wills have to the general will, that is, morals and manners to the laws, the more the repressive force must grow. So for government to be good, it must be proportionately stronger as the people becomes more numerous."

ch22 p22 | MINOR | "the accidental and particular relations by which the State itself is modified" became "the chance and particular circumstances that shape the State". "Relations" is the chapter's key term and has become "circumstances". "Modified" became "shape". | Span: "depending on the chance and particular circumstances that shape the State itself" -> "depending on the accidental and particular relations by which the State itself is modified"

ch22 p9 | NIT | "Lastly" was dropped before "since each relation has only one mean proportional". The merge "the State dissolves into despotism or anarchy" for "is dissolved and falls into" is acceptable. | Optional: "And finally, since each relation ..."

ch22 p14 | NIT | "the depositaries of the public authority" became "those who hold public authority". This is acceptable and consistent with trustees in p6. No change needed.

## Flagged items in my chapters: verdicts

- ch18 p2 "impose upon" -> "exploit": NOT faithful. See the MINOR finding above; use "deceive".
- ch18 p2 "patrimony" -> "inheritance": faithful. "whether even their own inheritance is really theirs" keeps the sense.
- ch19 p0 "insignificance": kept from Cole and understandable, but "smallness" is truer to the French. MINOR as above.
- ch19 p1: faithful. All the clauses on climate, fertility, women's fecundity, mountains and coast are present, and "legislator" is kept.
- ch19 p4 "without which all others can do": faithful. "every other people can do without it" gives the correct direction, and the `[1]` marker is placed after "it;".
- ch19 p6 fragment: faithful. It starts at "do without the other" and ends at "ruin". The former/latter -> first/second change is fine, and the missing full stop after "gift" was silently fixed.
- ch20 p3 ichthyophagous: faithful. "live on fish" says the same thing; "a nation of fish-eaters" would be marginally closer. No fix needed. Everything else in the paragraph is present, including the Jews, Arabs, Athenians, Carthage and Tyre, Rhodes, Sparta, Rome and Montesquieu. The only issue is the wording in the NIT above.
- ch20 p4: faithful. The "natural relations" / laws agreement, the three mismatches (servitude/liberty, riches/populousness, peace/conquest) and "nature has resumed her invincible sway" are all intact.
- ch20 p5, p6 fragments: faithful. They start and end where the source does, "estates" -> "conditions" is fine, and the quotation mark is kept.
- ch21 p0 "intermediate terms": faithful. It is a literal technical term that refers forward to Book 3. A gloss would be an addition, so leave it.
- ch21 p4 "arc of the arch" -> "curve of the arch": faithful to Cole (Rousseau's "cintre"). The keystone image is intact.
- ch22 p3 "generality as legislature or Sovereign" -> "the people as a whole acting as legislator or Sovereign": faithful. "Generality" (la généralité) is the whole body, which is the Sovereign. The people/Sovereign distinction is not blurred, because the paragraph says "legislative power belongs to the people" and equates the people as a whole with the Sovereign only when it acts as legislator. Cole's "legislature" -> "legislator" is harmless.
- ch22 p6 depositaries -> trustees: faithful. It matches Rousseau's "dépositaires" in sense (holders of a power entrusted to them), and the revocability of the power is kept intact. The same applies to the "alienation" -> "giving away" choice.
- ch22 p8 "product or power": faithful. The literal wording is kept, the proportion statement is intact, and "citizens, who are on the one hand sovereign and on the other subject" is preserved. Do not paraphrase it.
- ch22 p13: see the MINOR finding. It is faithful except for the loss of "relation".
- ch22 p17: faithful. The indivisible middle term, the single ruler or supreme magistrate, and the unity between the fractional and the ordinal series are all present.
- ch22 p19: faithful. The two Sovereigns ("one rightful and the other actual"), the "dominant will of the prince" and the dissolution of the body politic are all intact.

## Summary line
BLOCKING 0, MINOR 6, NIT 8. Most important: (1) ch18 p2 "exploit" misrenders "en imposer" (deceive); (2) ch22 p13 and p22 drop the technical word "relation", which weakens the link to the proportion argument; (3) ch19 p0 "insignificance" should be "smallness", and ch22 p10 "size" should be "number" (population).

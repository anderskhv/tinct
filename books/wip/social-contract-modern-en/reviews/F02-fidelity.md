# F02 fidelity review: chapters 7-12

## Scope
Read every paragraph pair against the source, in full: ch7 (8), ch8 (3), ch9 (7), ch10 (5), ch11 (6), ch12 (6) = 35 paragraphs. Paragraph counts match source in every chapter. Fragments (ch10 p4, ch11 p5, ch12 p4, ch12 p5) start and end where the source does and borrow nothing. Latin (persona ficta), `_italic_` markers and [1]/[2] markers are all placed as in the source. Key terms checked against my own knowledge of the French (no other translation consulted).

## Findings

ch9 p0 | BLOCKING | Candidate asserts the opposite of Rousseau's point. Cole's sentence is garbled; the French reads "Ce n'est pas que par cet acte la possession change de nature en changeant de mains, et devienne propriété dans celles du souverain" (it is NOT that possession changes nature and becomes property in the Sovereign's hands; the next clause, "but...", says public possession is merely stronger, not more legitimate). Candidate says possession "does not change its nature; it becomes property in the hands of the Sovereign", which is self-contradictory and affirms what the French denies. Cole's negation can be read to scope over both verbs, so this stays within Cole. | Replace the second sentence with: "By this act, possession does not change its nature when it changes hands, and it does not become property in the hands of the Sovereign. But the forces of the city are vastly greater than those of any individual, so public possession is also, in fact, stronger and more irrevocable, though no more legitimate, at least from the point of view of foreigners."

ch10 p4 | MINOR | "those who possess" became "those who have property". Possession vs property is a deliberate distinction in ch8-9, and "possess" is the source word. | "In fact, laws always help those who possess and hurt those who have nothing."

ch9 p1 | MINOR | "no further right against the community" became "no further claim"; chapter is about right, so keep "right". Also "claims the respect of every man" softened to "is respected by every man". | "...so he should keep to it and has no further right against the community. That is why the right of the first occupier, so weak in the state of nature, claims the respect of every man in civil society."

ch7 p6 | NIT | "a gratuitous contribution" became "a free gift"; "contribution" (French contribution gratuite) is slightly lost. Suggest "a free contribution".
ch7 p7 | NIT | "tacitly" became "silently"; "tacitly" is the standard term and reads fine today.
ch9 p5 | NIT | "mere enjoyment into ownership": "mere" is added (not in source).
ch10 p2 | NIT | "not for the good" became "against the good"; sharper than Cole (French "contraire au bien" supports it, so acceptable).
ch10 p3 | NIT | Cole's plural "general wills" became "the general will"; acceptable as a readability recast, no loss of sense.
ch11 p1 | NIT | "an illusion worthy of a fair" became "a sleight of hand worthy of a fair"; fine.
ch11 p3 | NIT | "there is an illusion" became "we are being fooled"; slightly looser, acceptable.
ch11 p4 | NIT | "obscurity" became "confusion"; acceptable.

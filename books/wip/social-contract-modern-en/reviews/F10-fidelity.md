# F10 fidelity review: ch47 (Civil Religion)

## Scope
Chapter 47 only. Read all 42 paragraphs (p0-p41), source vs candidate, pair by pair. Source and candidate both have 42 paragraphs. Paragraph boundaries and fragments (p35-p41) are correct: each starts and ends where the source does and nothing is borrowed from neighbors. Latin (_Sacer estod_) kept verbatim; `[1]`-`[7]` markers and italics placed as in source. ## Findings (0 BLOCKING, 5 MINOR, 7 NIT)

ch47 p3 | MINOR | Drops "divisions" in "how they spoke of the divisions of the neighbouring peoples they were forbidden to attack" (the allotted territories, which is the point of the Chamos example). Also "whose place they were to take" became "whose land", a small gloss. | Replace span "But remember how they spoke of the neighboring peoples they were forbidden to attack!" with "But remember how they spoke of the territories of the neighboring peoples they were forbidden to attack!" and "whose land the Hebrews were to take" with "whose place the Hebrews were to take".

ch47 p3 | NIT | "a proscribed people condemned to destruction" -> "a condemned people marked for destruction" loses "proscribed". | "the Canaanites were a proscribed people, condemned to destruction".

ch47 p9 | MINOR | Cole's "all good polity" (French "bonne politie") became "good government". "Government" is a fixed key term (the executive intermediary body), and the passage is about the clash of priest and civil power, not about government. | Replace "has made good government impossible in Christian States" with "has made good political order impossible in Christian States".

ch47 p33 | MINOR | "The two forms are inseparable" became "the two cannot be told apart". That means "indistinguishable", a different claim; Rousseau says they cannot be separated in practice. | "because the two forms are inseparable".

ch47 p34 | MINOR | "The reason for which Henry IV is said to have embraced the Roman religion" became "the reason Henry IV is said to have given for joining". "Given" is an addition: the source is about the motive that is said to have led him, not a stated justification. | "The reason for which Henry IV is said to have embraced the Roman religion should make every honest man leave it, and any prince who can reason even more so."

ch47 p37 | MINOR | Drops "All" in "All priests who communicate together are fellow-citizens" (a universal claim). | "All priests who are in communion with one another are fellow citizens".

ch47 p4 | NIT | Drops "still" and "subsequently"/"obstinately" is kept as "stubbornly" but "still refused" is flattened. | Optional: "and later those of Syria, and still stubbornly refused to recognize any god but their own."

ch47 p8 | NIT | "changed their language" -> "altered their tone" is a slight reinterpretation. | "The humble Christians changed their language".

ch47 p10 | NIT | "have desired without success to preserve or restore" -> "have tried without success". | "have wanted, without success, to keep or restore the old system".

ch47 p19 | NIT | "real religion" -> "true religion". | "this holy, sublime and real religion".

ch47 p21 | NIT | "a people of true Christians" -> "a nation of true Christians"; "people" is a key term, and "nation" is unneeded drift. | "People tell us that a people of true Christians" or better "a society made up entirely of true Christians" is NOT needed; simply use "a people of true Christians".

ch47 p27 | NIT | Source italic `_régime_` is gone because the phrase was recast (acceptable). The recast "tyranny always benefits from it" matches Rousseau's French ("pour qu'elle n'en profite pas toujours": tyranny profits from Christianity's spirit), though Cole's "it always profits by such a régime" can be read the other way. Faithful to the French; no change needed. | none required.

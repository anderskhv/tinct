# F06 fidelity review: chapters 29 to 33

## Scope
Chapters 29 (18 paragraphs), 30 (5), 31 (16), 32 (5), 33 (5): 49 paragraphs, every pair read side by side against Cole 1913. Paragraph counts match (no merge/split). Fragments (ch30 p4, ch31 p10, ch31 p15, ch29 p17) all start and end where the source does and borrow nothing. Latin kept verbatim; `_italic_` and `[n]` markers placed as in source. French terms checked from my own knowledge only.

## Findings
ch33 p1 | BLOCKING | Reversal/garble. Source says the bounds of possibility are LESS narrow than we imagine (wider), and our vices confine them. Candidate says possibility "has narrower limits than we imagine only because of..." which asserts the opposite and is hard to parse. | "In moral matters, the bounds of what is possible are less narrow than we imagine; it is our weaknesses, our vices and our prejudices that confine them. Base souls do not believe in great men; vile slaves sneer at the very name of liberty."

ch33 p3 | MINOR | "hardly a few weeks went by without" is ungrammatical and can be read as "only a few weeks passed". "often more than once" adds "often" and weakens "several times". "endless difficulties" adds "endless" (source: "What difficulties might not be supposed"). | Replace sentence 2: "Yet few weeks went by without the Roman people meeting in assembly, and even several times." Replace sentence 1: "What difficulties might one not suppose in the way of frequently gathering the vast population of that capital and its surroundings!" (or keep declarative but use "great difficulties").

ch33 p3 | NIT | "almost as often as magistrates as it was as citizens" is clumsy. | "almost as often in the role of magistrates as in that of citizens."

ch32 p1 | MINOR | "more or less robust and suited to preserve them a longer or a shorter time" became "a sturdier constitution, better suited to keep it alive for a shorter or longer time": the "more or less" gradation is lost and "sturdier" has no comparand. | "But either may have a constitution that is more or less robust, and suited to keep it alive for a longer or shorter time."

ch32 p0, p1 | NIT | Added "even" ("of even the best constituted governments"; "Even the best constituted State will end") and "tendency" became "direction". Harmless but not in the source. | "This is the natural and unavoidable tendency of the best constituted governments." / "The best constituted State will end; but..."

ch31 p0 | MINOR | "tends ceaselessly to destroy it" became "works ceaselessly to destroy it": gives the defect agency, sharpens a claim about a tendency. | "From the very birth of the body politic it tends ceaselessly to destroy it, just as old age and death end up destroying the human body."

ch31 p10 | MINOR | "the ancient Dukes who are brought up against them" (against the Venetians) became "cite against this", shifting the target. | "As for the ancient Dukes that people cite against them, it has been proved, ..."

ch31 p13 | MINOR | The source's "for names do not affect facts" is the explanatory connective for the tribune clause; candidate makes it a free-standing sentence and drops "for" (style note: keep connectives). | "...once they began to take on an active function, for names do not alter facts: when a people has rulers who govern for it, the government is an aristocracy, whatever name they bear."

ch31 p5, p9 | NIT | Source "Sovereign power" (capital) is rendered "sovereign power"; elsewhere "sovereign" is capitalized as the body. Style note says keep the capital for the Sovereign. | "usurps the Sovereign power" (p5); "him who usurps the Sovereign power a _despot_" (p9).

ch31 p8 | NIT | "a private individual" adds "private" (supported by French "particulier", but not in Cole). | "an individual who takes royal authority for himself".

ch31 p15 | NIT | Bracketed Latin gloss drops "For" (enim). | "[For all those are called and considered tyrants who hold perpetual power ...]"

ch29 p8 | NIT | "the exact _minimum_" became "the bare _minimum_" ("exact" lost). | "just the exact _minimum_ needed to live".

ch29 p16 | NIT | "can support more of them" became "can feed more of them" (narrower). | "and can support more of them."

ch30 p2 | NIT | "the one class ... the other" became "One group ... the other" / "One ... the other". Order still maps to subjects then citizens, but the link is weaker. | Optionally "The former ... the latter" for the first pair.

ch30 p4 | NIT | "vulgar self-interest" adds "self-"; "while others dispute who will tyrannize" adds "others". Both sit within the sense. | optional: "the vulgar interest"; "while there is a dispute over who will tyrannize over them".

# F08 fidelity review: chapters 41, 42, 43

## Scope
Read every paragraph pair: ch41 (13), ch42 (10), ch43 (39) = 62 paragraphs. Paragraph counts match the source. Fragments (ch41 p11, p12; ch43 p38) start and end where the source does. `_italic_` and `[n]` markers are placed as in the source. Latin is kept verbatim. No other translation was consulted; key terms were checked against my own knowledge of Rousseau's French only where noted.

## Findings
(BLOCKING: none)

ch43 p18 | MINOR | "the disinterestedness" became "no self-interest", which is absolute and changes the sense. Rousseau means freedom from self-seeking, not no interests at all. | Replace "no self-interest" with "freedom from self-seeking".

ch43 p27 | MINOR | Wrong antecedent. Source "the comitia bearing their name" means named after the centuries. Candidate "the comitia named after them" follows "the senate" and reads as named after the senate. | Replace "how the senate could ever have lost in the comitia named after them, the ones that elected" with "how the senate could ever have lost in the comitia named after the centuries, the ones that elected".

ch43 p33 | MINOR | The participle "being fitted only to further tyranny" describes the Comitia Curiata (the assembly). Candidate says "the populace ... was fit only to advance tyranny", which changes the subject and blames the populace. | Replace "In the _Comitia Curiata_ the populace of Rome was the majority, and it was fit only to advance tyranny and evil designs. Naturally it fell into disrepute," with "The _Comitia Curiata_, in which the populace of Rome was the majority, was fit only to advance tyranny and evil designs. Naturally it fell into disrepute,".

ch43 p35 | NIT | Source says destruction "must be hastened", with the sense of necessity. Candidate "will hasten" loses "must". | "I hold, on the contrary, that for want of enough such changes the destruction of the State must be hastened."

ch43 p10 | NIT | "chimera" became "fantasy". Cole's word is plain enough and closer to Rousseau. | "or rather came to be little more than a chimera."

ch41 p0 | NIT | Source "may give" (hedge) became "gives". "Ascendancy of particular interests" became "rise", which is weaker. | "The last chapter shows that the way public business is conducted may give a fairly clear sign of the actual state of morals ... announce the dominance of particular interests and the decline of the State."

ch41 p12 | NIT | Source "It is indeed only malefactors..." is a separate sentence. Candidate turns it into a causal "since". Acceptable, but the logical connective is an addition. | "This use of the device is good and just. Indeed, only wrongdoers of every class keep the citizen from being free."

ch43 p2 | NIT | "headed by leaders" became "led by officers". Harmless. | none needed.

## Flagged items in my chapters (verdicts)
- ch41 p11-12 "for elsewhere": the candidate keeps both as fragments and borrows nothing from neighbors. "for in other places" is faithful. "estates" became "class" and is fine in sense. FAITHFUL (apart from the p12 NIT above).
- ch42 p1 Montesquieu: quotation, attribution and Rousseau's "These are not reasons" are all kept. FAITHFUL.
- ch42 p2 "sovereignty lowercase": the candidate has "Sovereignty" capitalized. Government vs Sovereignty is intact. FAITHFUL.
- ch43 p17 "gave the State citizens": the candidate retains "gave the State citizens", with no change to "supplied ... with". It is faithful to Cole and the French sense (proles). It reads slightly terse, but is acceptable. FAITHFUL (optional NIT: "supplied the State with citizens").
- ch43 p27: see MINOR above (antecedent). Otherwise faithful.
- ch43 p34: FAITHFUL.
- ch43 p35 "regimen of health": "A diet that keeps the healthy well does not suit the sick" is a legitimate rendering of the sense (the regimen for healthy people does not suit the sick). "For want of enough such changes" became "too few changes like this", which is faithful except for the dropped "must" (NIT above). Verdict: acceptable.
- ch43 p36: FAITHFUL (tablets, voices, officers' good faith, edicts, and footnote [2] all intact).

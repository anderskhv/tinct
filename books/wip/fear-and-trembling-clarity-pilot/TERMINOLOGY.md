# Terminology policy — Fear and Trembling clarity pilot

Scope: Problema I and Problema II (reader sections 5 and 6). The policy extends
the earlier staging glossary on branch
`claude/fear-and-trembling-modern-en-20260911`
(`books/staged-replacements/fear-and-trembling/GLOSSARY.md`). It keeps that
glossary's core renderings and corrects one entry (see *Prøvelse / Fristelse /
Anfægtelse* below).

## Rules

1. **One Danish term gets one English rendering.** A key term keeps the same
   English form every time it appears. Plain words may vary for readability. A
   technical term may not.
2. **Define a term once, briefly, where it first appears in these sections.**
   The definition goes inside the sentence as a dash or parenthesis of a few
   words, and it must be supported by the source or by the term's plain
   dictionary sense. It must not add interpretation. After that first use the
   term stands alone.
3. **Keep the term instead of simplifying it away.** Johannes argues with these
   words, so replacing *the universal* with "society's rules", or *faith* with
   "trust", would change the argument. Clarity comes from sentence structure,
   explicit referents and short definitions, not from swapping the vocabulary.
4. **Keep Kierkegaard's foreign words and add a gloss.** Greek, Latin, French
   and German insertions are kept, with a short English gloss beside them.
   OCR-garbled Greek is restored (τέλος → *telos*, σκάνδαλον → *skandalon*,
   μισεῖν, per μείωσιν, κατ' ἀναλογίαν).
5. **The narrator is Johannes de Silentio.** His "I" and his admissions ("I
   cannot understand Abraham", "I lack the courage") stay in the first person.
   Nothing is attributed to Kierkegaard in the running text.

## Deliberate non-standardizations

- **Paradoxical formulas stay paradoxical.** "Faith has never existed …
  precisely because it has always existed" (P-I ¶2, ¶4; P-II ¶28) is not
  explained, because the source does not explain it.
- **"A later one"** (P-I ¶6), Johannes's single possible analogy to Abraham, is
  left unidentified, as in the source.
- **The unnamed father in P-I ¶8** is not given Agamemnon's name, because the
  source withholds the name until ¶11.

## Sensitive-term decision

P-II ¶0 quotes Rousseau's figure of a person who loves distant peoples instead
of the neighbor. Kierkegaard's Danish names the "Kaffirs" (*Kafferne*). That
word is now a racial slur. The pilot renders it as "a far-off African people",
which keeps the reference and the point (loving the remote instead of the near)
without printing the slur. This is recorded as a content decision for review.

> Om den Enkelte nu virkelig ligger i en Anfægtelse eller han er Troens Ridder, det kan kun den Enkelte selv afgjøre. Dette er et Forsøg paa at springe af fra Paradoxets trange Vei og blive en tragisk Helt for godt Kjøb. Den tragiske Helt han udtrykker det Almene og offrer sig for dette. Isteden derfor har den secteriske Mester Jackel et Privattheater, nogle gode Venner og Kammerater, der omtrent repræsentere det Almene ligesaa godt som Stokkemændene i Gulddaasen repræsentereRetfærdigheden. Troens Ridder derimod han er Paradoxet, han er den Enkelte, absolut kun den Enkelte uden alle Connexioner og Vidtløftigheder. Dette er det Forfærdelige, som den secteriske Skrælling ikke kan udholde. Istedenfor nemlig deraf at lære, at han ikke er istand til at gjøre det Store, og da ligefrem at tilstaae dette, Noget jeg naturligviis ikke kan andet end billige, da det er, hvad jeg selv gjør, mener Stymperen, at han ved at forene sig med nogle andre Stympere skal kunne gjøre det. Men det gaaer aldeles ikke an; i Aandens Verden taales intet Snyderi. Et Dusin Secterikere tage hinanden under Armen, de kjende slet Intet til de eensomme Anfægtelser, der vente Troens Ridder, og som han ikke tør flye, netop fordi det var endnu forfærdeligere, om han formastelig trængte sig frem. Secterikerne overdøve hinanden ved Støj og Larm, holde Angesten borte ved deres Skrig, og et saadant hujende Dyrehaugsselskab mener at storme Himlen og mener at gaae ad samme Vei som Troens Ridder, der i Universets Eensomhed aldrig hører nogen menneskelig Røst, men gaaer ene med sit forfærdelige Ansvar.

**v3**

> Whether the single individual is really in a spiritual trial or is a knight of faith, only the single individual himself can decide. Nevertheless, some marks can be constructed from the paradox that even someone who is not in it can understand. The true knight of faith is always absolute isolation; the false knight is sectarian. Sectarianism tries to leap off the narrow way of the paradox and be a tragic hero at a bargain price. The tragic hero expresses the universal and sacrifices himself for it. Instead of this, the sectarian Master Jackel — a puppet-show clown — keeps a private theater: a handful of good friends and fellows who stand for the universal roughly as well as the court witnesses in The Gold Snuffbox represent justice. The knight of faith, on the contrary, is the paradox; he is the single individual, absolutely nothing but the single individual, without any connections or complications. This is the terrible thing that the sectarian weakling cannot endure. For instead of learning from it that he is unable to do what is great, and then plainly admitting it — something I naturally cannot but approve of, since it is what I do myself — the bungler thinks that by joining forces with a few other bunglers he will be able to do it. But that will not work at all; in the world of spirit no cheating is tolerated. A dozen sectarians go arm in arm; they know nothing at all of the lonely spiritual trials that await the knight of faith and that he dares not flee, precisely because it would be still more terrible if he presumptuously forced his way forward. The sectarians drown one another out with noise and clamor and keep anxiety away with their shouting; and a hooting fairground crowd like this thinks it is storming heaven and thinks it is walking the same path as the knight of faith — who, in the solitude of the universe, never hears a human voice but walks alone with his terrible responsibility.

## P-II ¶27

**DA**

> Troens Ridder han er alene anviist sig selv, han føler Smerten af, at han ikke kan gjøre sig forstaaelig for Andre, men han føler ingen forfængelig Lyst til at ville veillede Andre. Smerten er ham Forvisningen, den forfængelige Lyst kjender han ikke, dertil er han Sjæl for alvorlig. Den uægte Ridder forraader sig let ved dette Mesterskab, han i et Øieblik har erhvervet. Han fatter slet ikke, hvorom Talen er, at forsaavidt en anden Enkelt skal gaae den samme Vei, maa han aldeles paa samme Maade blive den Enkelte og trænger da ikke til Nogens Veiledning, mindst til Een, der vil paanøde sig. Her springer man atter af, man kan ikke udholde Uforstaaelighedens Martyrium, og istedenfor dette vælger man da beqvemt nok Mesterskabets verdslige Beundring. Den sande Troens Ridder han er Vidne, aldrig Lærer, og deri ligger det dybe Humane, der er noget mere bevendt end denne pjankede Deeltagelse for andre Menneskers Vee og Vel, der hædres under Navn af Sympathi, medens den dog ikke er andet end Forfængelighed. Den der kun vil være Vidne, han bekjender derved, at intet Menneske, end ikke det ringeste, behøver et andet Menneskes Deeltagelse, eller skal nedværdiges derved, for at den Anden kan hæves. Men som han selv ikke vandt, hvad han vandt, for godt Kjøb, saa sælger han det heller ikke ud for godt Kjøb, han er ikke ussel nok til at tage Menneskers Beundring og til Vederlag give dem sin tause Foragt, han veed, at hvad der i Sandhed er Stort, det er lige tilgængeligt for Alle.

**v3**

> The knight of faith is thrown back on himself alone. He feels the pain of not being able to make himself understood by others, but he has no vain wish to direct anyone else. The pain is what assures him. The vain desire he does not know; his soul is too earnest for that. The false knight easily betrays himself by the mastery he has acquired in an instant. He does not grasp at all what is at issue: that if another single individual is to walk the same path, he must become the single individual in exactly the same way, and then he needs no one's guidance — least of all that of someone who wants to force himself on him. Here, again, people jump off the path: they cannot endure the martyrdom of being uncomprehended, and instead, conveniently enough, they choose the worldly admiration that mastery brings. The true knight of faith is a witness, never a teacher, and in this lies the deep humanity that is worth rather more than the silly concern for other people's weal and woe that is honored under the name of sympathy, though it is nothing but vanity. Whoever wants only to be a witness thereby confesses that no human being, not even the lowliest, needs another's sympathy or should be humbled by it so that the other can be raised up. But just as he did not win what he won on the cheap, he does not sell it cheap either. He is not base enough to accept people's admiration and pay them for it with his silent contempt. He knows that true greatness is open to everyone alike.

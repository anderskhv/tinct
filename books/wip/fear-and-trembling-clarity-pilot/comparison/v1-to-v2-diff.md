Next, he has the passion to call up this whole assurance in an instant, and in such a way that it is just as fully valid as it was in the first moment. If he cannot do that, he gets nowhere; for then he must keep starting over from the beginning. The tragic hero also concentrates into one moment the ethical that he teleologically went beyond, but in this respect he has support in the universal. The knight of faith has only himself alone, and that is what is terrible. Most people live in an ethical obligation in such a way that they let each day have its own sorrow; but then they never arrive at this passionate concentration, this energetic consciousness. In a certain sense the universal can help the tragic hero attain it, but the knight of faith is alone in everything.

## P-II ¶27

**DA**

> Troens Ridder han er alene anviist sig selv, han føler Smerten af, at han ikke kan gjøre sig forstaaelig for Andre, men han føler ingen forfængelig Lyst til at ville veillede Andre. Smerten er ham Forvisningen, den forfængelige Lyst kjender han ikke, dertil er han Sjæl for alvorlig. Den uægte Ridder forraader sig let ved dette Mesterskab, han i et Øieblik har erhvervet. Han fatter slet ikke, hvorom Talen er, at forsaavidt en anden Enkelt skal gaae den samme Vei, maa han aldeles paa samme Maade blive den Enkelte og trænger da ikke til Nogens Veiledning, mindst til Een, der vil paanøde sig. Her springer man atter af, man kan ikke udholde Uforstaaelighedens Martyrium, og istedenfor dette vælger man da beqvemt nok Mesterskabets verdslige Beundring. Den sande Troens Ridder han er Vidne, aldrig Lærer, og deri ligger det dybe Humane, der er noget mere bevendt end denne pjankede Deeltagelse for andre Menneskers Vee og Vel, der hædres under Navn af Sympathi, medens den dog ikke er andet end Forfængelighed. Den der kun vil være Vidne, han bekjender derved, at intet Menneske, end ikke det ringeste, behøver et andet Menneskes Deeltagelse, eller skal nedværdiges derved, for at den Anden kan hæves. Men som han selv ikke vandt, hvad han vandt, for godt Kjøb, saa sælger han det heller ikke ud for godt Kjøb, han er ikke ussel nok til at tage Menneskers Beundring og til Vederlag give dem sin tause Foragt, han veed, at hvad der i Sandhed er Stort, det er lige tilgængeligt for Alle.

**v1**

> The knight of faith is thrown back on himself alone. He feels the pain of not being able to make himself understood by others, but he feels no vain desire to guide others. The pain is his assurance. The vain desire he does not know; his soul is too earnest for that. The false knight easily betrays himself by the mastery he has acquired in an instant. He does not grasp at all what is at issue: that if another single individual is to walk the same path, he must become the single individual in exactly the same way, and then he needs no one's guidance — least of all from someone who wants to force it on him. Here, again, people jump off the path: they cannot endure the martyrdom of being uncomprehended, and instead, conveniently enough, they choose the worldly admiration that mastery brings. The true knight of faith is a witness, never a teacher, and in this lies the deep humanity that is worth far more than the silly concern for other people's weal and woe that is honored under the name of sympathy, though it is nothing but vanity. Whoever wants only to be a witness thereby confesses that no human being, not even the lowliest, needs another's sympathy or should be humbled by it so that the other can be raised up. But just as he did not win what he won on the cheap, he does not sell it cheap either. He is not base enough to accept people's admiration and pay them for it with his silent contempt. He knows that what is truly great is equally accessible to all.

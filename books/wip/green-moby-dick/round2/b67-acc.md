## Questions (not proposed as edits)

1. **71.14:** "riotous waves ... were tumbling it, not heaving it": is "it" the boat or the sea? A reader has to reread. If the source makes it the boat, name it.
2. **71.16:** "the Shaker God incarnate, the Shakers accepting the Bible": the trailing clause is a non sequitur for a modern reader. Is there a clearer faithful rendering of the source's "receiving the Bible"?
3. **73.21:** "Three Spaniards?" is a book title (Flask then says "never saw such a book"). Without italics, should it read "The Three Spaniards?" or "that book, Three Spaniards?" so that listeners hear a title?
4. **73.42:** "Laplandish speculations": the allusion (Lapland's reputation for witchcraft and superstition) may stop readers. I'm not confident enough of a gloss to propose one.
5. **69.1:** "every rod ... square roods of sharks and cubic roods of birds": this is intentional unit wordplay. Keep it unless the lead wants a gloss.

## Answers to the repair editor's open questions

1. **4.1, stepmother/mother.** Confirmed. The candidate reads "my stepmother, who somehow or other was always whipping me or sending me to bed without supper — my mother dragged me by the legs". That keeps Melville's anacoluthon and his inconsistency exactly, and 4.2 keeps "sought out my stepmother" and "best and most conscientious of stepmothers". The lead should **not** smooth it: hard rule 3 forbids correcting Melville, and the break is part of the 1851 text.
2. **1.11, playbill capitals.** Acceptable. The edition carries no italics, so setting all three playbill lines in capitals is a reasonable way to keep them visibly a playbill. It adds no content. AFFGHANISTAN is left to the lead's decision-7 sweep.
3. **3.10, "winding sheet".** Acceptable. "Wrapped in a winding sheet of dripped tallow" is an accurate short gloss: a "winding-sheet" is the congealed drip of tallow down a candle. It correctly leaves out the death-omen folklore, which the source doesn't state. The funereal image survives in "winding sheet".

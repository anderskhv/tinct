## Answers to the open questions

1. **119.34 "open eyes; see, or not?"** Keep "open, eyes; do you see, or not?". In 119.33 Ahab has just closed his eyes with his hand pressed on them, and his speech is addressed to the fire, not the crew. Earlier in the same speech he says "Thou canst blind; but I can then grope". When "the javelins cease", he opens his own eyes and tests whether he can still see. The crew reading does not fit, because the crew are never addressed in this speech. The vocative rendering keeps both halves of the source: the command to his eyes and the fear of blindness. No change.
2. **119.34 "Take the homage of these poor eyes, and shutter-hands. I would not take it."** Keep it literal. The likeliest sense is that the closed eyes and covering hand are a forced, involuntary homage, which the fire may take, while Ahab himself would not accept homage extorted like that (or would not have offered it freely). The source leaves this open, and brief rule 5 says not to resolve it. The current rendering is correct.
3. **119.3 closing quotation mark before (Sings.):** acceptable. The source's missing close-quote is a misprint, and the same fix is made in 127.13.
4. **121.4 "lighting on deck" as "landing on deck":** correct. "Light" means alight, and Stubb has just finished the knot up on the anchors.
5. **123.7 "locked-up Japan" with no gloss:** acceptable. It is literal and understandable (Japan's closed ports), and adds nothing.

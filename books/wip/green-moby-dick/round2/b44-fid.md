## Open questions

- **45.4 "crossed their wakes":** keep it literal, as the editor did. "Their" most naturally means the cruisers crossing the whale's path, with a loose plural. Any gloss would resolve an ambiguity Melville leaves open.
- **45.10 "within a few miles of the scene":** keep it as printed. It need not be a slip. Melville met Chace's son at sea in the Pacific aboard the Acushnet and read the narrative there, so the claim can be literal. In any case the brief forbids correcting him.
- **45.20 "Spanish land" → "Spanish-held coast":** accept. Wafer means Spain's American dominions, and the earthquake did its damage on the Peruvian coast. A literal "Spanish land" reads as Spain itself to a modern reader, which would be misleading. "Spanish-held coast" is an accurate, minimal clarification. "The Spanish lands" is an acceptable alternative if the lead prefers to stay closer to the source.

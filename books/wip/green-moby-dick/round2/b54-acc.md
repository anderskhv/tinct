# b54 accessibility review (candidate only)

Scope: chapter 54, "The Town-Ho's Story", 54.0–54.111 (112 paragraphs), all read in order.

## Verdict: chapter 54, good with small fixes

The chapter reads clearly aloud. The nested quotation layers (the Lima narration in double quotes, dialogue in single quotes, "Corrupt as Lima" in double quotes again) stay easy to follow. The Lima convention (each narrated paragraph opens and closes with a quotation mark) was not flagged. I found no typos, doubled words, leftover thee/thou forms or garbled text.

There are 5 non-blocking items:
- 54.5: gloss "the Line" as the equator.
- 54.47: "planted their group of hands on it" changed to "all planted their hands on it".
- 54.66: added a comma so that "sullenly worked by the moody seamen" attaches to the pumps.
- 54.69: gloss "trick at the helm" (a turn of duty), used again in 54.77. Without it, a reader of a revenge plot could take "trick" to mean a ruse.
- 54.77: reordered the "Twenty-four hours later…" sentence, which had a dangling subject.

## Questions for the lead (no change proposed)

1. 54.2 "under Romish injunctions of secrecy": "Romish" (a period word for Roman Catholic, suggesting confessional secrecy) may stop some readers. It is period language, so I left it. Should it get a gloss?
2. 54.28 "There's your true Ashantee": the allusion to the West African Ashanti is unexplained. The context (pagans, lawlessness) makes the sense clear enough, so I have not proposed a gloss.
3. 54.26/54.95 "Senor" is written without the tilde. This is consistent within the chapter. Does the edition prefer "Señor"?
4. The chapter title uses a curly apostrophe ("Town-Ho’s"). Titles must match the source, so I did not flag it. I am noting it only because the edition's body text uses straight quotes.
5. 54.108 is a bare row of asterisks with no quotation marks, unlike the narrated paragraphs around it. This is presumably as printed in the source, so I left it alone.

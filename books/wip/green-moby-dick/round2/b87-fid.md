## Answers to the editor's open questions

1. **"Bashaw" (88.4).** Yes, gloss it once: "the Bashaw (the pasha)". It is the archaic English form of the Turkish title *pasha*. Keep the word itself, since titles stay as printed under decision 7. The second use ("do what the Bashaw will") needs no gloss. The gloss is included as an item.
2. **"wing"/"winged" (87.18 vs 93.9).** They need not match, and 93.9 must stay "winged".
   - In 87.18 the sense is explicit: "wing them, so that they can be afterwards killed at your leisure" means disable. So "wound them" is accurate. "Wing them" would also be acceptable modern English, but no change is required.
   - In 93.9 ("Stubb's inexorable back was turned upon him; and the whale was winged") the word is ambiguous: the whale was struck, or the whale sped off as if on wings. Rendering "wounded" or "struck" would resolve a crux the source leaves open (hard rule 3). The current "winged" is correct.
3. **"eccentric span" (93.2).** A span is a pair of horses driven in harness together. "Eccentric" here means odd or unconventional, since the pair is matched in size but not in color. The off-center-harness reading is unlikely and should not be imported. The current "made a matched pair ... driven in one oddly matched team" contradicts itself and changes "pair" to "team". I propose "driven together as one eccentric pair".

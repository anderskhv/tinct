# b128 accessibility review (chs 128–132)

Candidate-only review, read in order: 75 paragraphs. 8 non-blocking items, no blocking items. All proposals are small wording changes; no content removed.

## Per-chapter verdicts

- **Ch 128, The Pequod Meets The Rachel**: Reads well aloud. Two minor fixes: 128.8 "wore off that captain's best coat" (misreads as "faded"), changed to "went off wearing"; 128.10 "what says Ahab?", changed to "what does Ahab say?".
- **Ch 129, The Cabin**: Good. The stage directions and Pip's dialect are intact. 129.9: gloss for "seventy-fours" (warships of seventy-four guns).
- **Ch 130, The Hat**: Good. 130.0: "a vessel had been spoken" changed to "hailed". 130.7: the double negative "doubt whether ... might not willingly overlook" is untangled.
- **Ch 131, The Pequod Meets The Delight**: Good. 131.9: the archaic "not so quick ... but that ... may have" is recast, keeping the hedge.
- **Ch 132, The Symphony**: Strong. 132.2: the dangling "though thus contrasting within" is fixed. 132.3: "seemed giving" changed to "seemed to be giving".

## Questions for the lead (not proposed as changes)

1. **128.1 vs 128.4 contradiction.** 128.1 says the Rachel's commander "stood up in his boat with his trumpet to his mouth" before hailing. 128.4 then has him stop his ship and be "seen climbing down her side" into his boat. If the source has the same inconsistency (Melville's), keep it. If not, 128.1 may need "stood on deck".
2. **132.11 "the dry nourishment of my soil!"** A reader may take "soil" for a typo of "soul". Please confirm that it matches the source. I believe Melville printed "soil".
3. **130.4 "Nor did he reap his beard"** is an odd verb for a modern reader ("cut/trim"), but it may be a deliberate harvest image that the root simile continues. I left it alone. Change it only if the source verb isn't "reap".
4. **132.12** mixes "Oh, my Captain!" and "O my Captain". This is probably intentional vocative variation. Flagged for consistency only.

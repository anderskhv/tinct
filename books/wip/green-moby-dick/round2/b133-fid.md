Role: fidelity (independent, source-based). Scope: 163 paragraphs (133: 51, 134: 47, 135: 62, 136: 3). I read every paragraph clause by clause against source.json, including the 126 the editor repaired and the 37 they kept, then re-read each chapter whole.

## Mechanical checks (current candidate, chs 133-136)

Considered and accepted:
- **133.14.** "hideous motion" became "grimace". This is a fair reading.
- **133.45.** The day's "his golden robe" became "its". This is ordinary modernization.
- **134.42.** "'Twas rehearsed by thee and me" became "You and I rehearsed it". The sense is kept.
- **135.57.** The italic in "_Thus_, I give up the spear!" is carried by the comma and the placement at the end of the sentence, which is enough.

- **133.39.** The source prints "Moby-Dick" hyphenated here. It is the only hyphenated occurrence in the narrative. I treat it as 1851 typography, like the ligatures under lead decision 4, and accept "Moby Dick". The lead may overrule under decision 3 if they want it preserved.

## Answers to the editor's open questions

1. **134.10 "guilt and guiltiness."** I agree it should be kept as printed. It is the reading of all standard texts, and the modern-en renders it literally. Emending it to "guiltlessness" would correct Melville (hard rule 3).
2. **135.2 "swift and swerve about."** "Rush and swerve about" is acceptable. "Swift" used as a verb (to move swiftly) is attested, so the source need not be a misprint. "Rush" renders that sense directly and also covers "shift".
3. **134.25 "be the leaner who he will."** "Whoever the leaner may be" is the right literal choice. In context Ahab is the one leaning, but the maxim is general, and the rendering keeps it general.
4. **133.26 "a movement that loosed his hold."** Keeping the ambiguity is correct. The following sentence ("the boat had shaken off his hold on the jaw") shows that Ahab's grip is meant, and the candidate's wording is consistent with that.

## Epilogue epigraph ("TELL THEE")

**I confirm keeping "thee".** My reasons:
- It is a verbatim quotation of the King James Job (1:15-19), set as a display epigraph. The quotation is the point: Ishmael takes over the messenger's formula.
- The edition's precedent keeps archaic pronouns inside quoted texts. At 32.9, Job 41 appears as "Will he make a covenant with thee?". At 35.9, Byron's "Roll on, thou deep and dark blue ocean ... sweep over thee in vain" is kept.
- The thee-to-you rule in the brief covers narration and dialogue. The "quoted verse and hymns" clause allows only light modernization, and changing the pronoun would misquote scripture. It would also break consistency with 32.9.
- The capitals are kept as the source prints them.

Every sighting, order, maneuver, injury, prophecy detail and speech checks out against the source, and Ahab's rhetoric keeps its full elevation with only the grammar modernized.

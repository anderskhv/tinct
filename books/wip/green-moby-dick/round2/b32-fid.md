## Answers to the editor's open questions
- **32.34 Frobisher chronology.** Melville's sentence runs two moments together. "On his return from that voyage, when Queen Bess did gallantly wave ... as his bold ship sailed down the Thames" uses the waving (historically Elizabeth at Greenwich, June 1576, as Frobisher set out) to identify which voyage it was. The presentation of the horn happens on the return, and the Black Letter quotation restates that. This is Melville's loose, anacoluthic syntax, not an error to correct. The current candidate ("on his return from that voyage when Queen Bess gallantly waved ... as his bold ship sailed down the Thames — 'when Sir Martin returned ...'") drops the comma before "when". That makes the when-clause restrictive (it names the voyage), which is the right reading and keeps the source's break before the quotation. **No change.** Do not add a clarifying gloss or rearrange the sentence; that would resolve something Melville leaves loose.
- **32.34 "folder".** "Paper-folder" is correct (a paper knife for folding and slitting pages). I agree with the editor.
- **35.4 "hearse".** Keep "hearse". It sits among "a bed, a hammock, ... a sentry box, a pulpit, a coach", which are snug enclosed contrivances, and the dark joke of the word is Melville's. I agree with the editor.
- **35.9 Byron.** Keep "thou/thee" verbatim under lead decision 8. This overrides the editor's modernization and is blocking (item above).
- **32.36 "Fiji fish".** This is correct under decision 7. The cannibal implication stays implicit, as in the source.

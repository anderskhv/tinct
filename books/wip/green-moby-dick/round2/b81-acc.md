## Questions (no change proposed)

1. **82.2 "this Arkite story":** a first-time reader is unlikely to know "Arkite". I am not sure enough of Melville's intended sense to gloss it. Should the lead decide whether it needs a gloss?
2. **81.27 "my butter-boxes":** Stubb's jibe at the Dutch/German crew. I believe "butter-box" was an old slang nickname for a Dutchman, but I have not glossed it. Could a checker confirm, and add a gloss if one is wanted?
3. **81.22 "Hurrah for the white-ash breeze!":** this means rowing, since the oars are white ash. No gloss is given nearby. Was it explained earlier in the book? If not, "(the oars)" could be added once.
4. **81.23 "a crab that caught the blade":** this is the rowing idiom "catching a crab", which Melville treats as a literal crab. I left it as a deliberate pun.
5. **81.12 "Hindostan coast":** this is a geographic name. Under lead decision 7 it will be normalized in the assembly sweep, so it is not flagged here.

## Answers to the open questions

1. **112.5 "her children twice followed her thither".** Keep it literal ("her children twice followed her there"). The most natural reading is two more funerals, meaning two of the three children died after the mother. The phrase may also be a loose "the children followed". Melville does not resolve it, so the edition must not assign a number. The current rendering is correct.
2. **113.16 "bunch of lucifers".** Lucifers are friction matches ("lucifer matches"), with an obvious pun on Lucifer. The referent is most naturally the Parsee himself, a fire-worshipper who dodges about the forge; the next sentence continues "That Parsee smells fire like a fusee". It is not the sparks. Render it as "lucifer matches", as the edition does in chs 72 and 121. That gives the modern reader the literal sense, keeps the devil pun, and doesn't fix a referent that Stubb leaves loose. I rate this non-blocking.
3. **112.4 "care-killing competency".** In 1851, "competency" meant a sufficient income or estate to live on comfortably. "A care-killing sufficiency to live on" is accurate and readable, so no change is needed. An acceptable plainer alternative is "enough to live on, free of care", but it loses "care-killing", so I prefer the current wording.

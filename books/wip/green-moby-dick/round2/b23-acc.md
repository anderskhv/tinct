# b23 accessibility review: chapters 23–31 (candidate only)

Scope: 74 paragraphs (23.0–31.6), all read in full, in order. Proposals: 9, all non-blocking. See `b23-acc.json`.

## Per-chapter verdict

- **Ch 23, The Lee Shore:** Reads well aloud. One fix: 23.3 has a doubled "like" comparison ("worm-like ... like a coward").
- **Ch 24, The Advocate:** Clear. Fixes: 24.16 footnote-marker spacing, and 24.21, where "No more!" is misread as "not any longer"; the proposal is "Enough said!".
- **Ch 25, Postscript:** Clean. No changes.
- **Ch 26, Knights and Squires:** Strong. One parse fix at 26.2 ("occasion that was practical to the point of life and death").
- **Ch 27, Knights and Squires:** The duplicate title matches the source convention, so it is not a defect. Fixes: 27.0 ("bestir themselves" with no antecedent) and 27.8 ("I call such").
- **Ch 28, Ahab:** Fixes: 28.1 (a long cleft sentence, "that, as ... the moment ...") and 28.5 (an inverted object sentence and "Before long, after").
- **Ch 29, Enter Ahab; to Him, Stubb:** Clean. Stubb's dialect is light and readable.
- **Ch 30, The Pipe:** Clean.
- **Ch 31, Queen Mab:** One punctuation fix at 31.1 ("'wise Stubb;' and").

## Questions (not proposed as edits; please check against the source)

1. **29.2: "Stubb, the old second mate".** Nothing else presents Stubb as old, and in these chapters "the old man" always means Ahab. Is the source word "odd"? If it is, "old" is a meaning slip.
2. **24.16 / 24.18 / 24.19:** the candidate has two asterisk markers, one after "royal fish." and one after "procession.", but only one footnote ("See later chapters..."). Does the source have both markers?
3. **27.8: "An Anacharsis Clootz deputation".** This is an obscure allusion. The rest of the sentence ("from all the isles of the sea and all the ends of the earth") partly carries it. A possible gloss is "like the delegation of foreigners Clootz brought before the French revolutionary Assembly in the name of all mankind". I left it as a question.
4. **25.2: "train oil".** A reader may not know that this is ordinary whale oil. It is left unglossed because it sits in a catalogue.

Period terms such as "negro", "savage", "red men" and "untainted blood", and Melville's errors (for example "Knights of the Garter" dubbed by a slap), are left as the brief requires.

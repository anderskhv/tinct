**60, The Line.** The rigging is verified step by step, and everything is correct except 60.2 (B), where Manila should be Manilla, twice:
- 60.1: hemp is "slightly vapored" with tar, not impregnated. Tar stiffens the line for close coiling, and seamen are learning that it adds no durability or strength, only compactness and gloss.
- 60.2: Manilla is less durable but stronger, softer, more elastic and handsomer.
- 60.3: the line is 2/3 inch thick, with 51 yarns each holding 120 lb, so it bears about 3 tons. It is a little over 200 fathoms long. It is coiled in the tub near the stern as a cheese of sheaves with a "heart", not like a worm-pipe. A kink would take off an arm, leg or whole body. Harpooners spend a morning reeving it from aloft through a block.
- 60.4: English boats use twin tubs. The American tub is about 3 ft across, and the boat's planks are half an inch thick, so the bottom is like critical ice: it bears distributed weight but not concentrated weight. The covered tub looks like a wedding cake.
- 60.5: the lower-end eye-splice hangs free for two reasons: to bend on a neighboring boat's line (the whale passed like a mug of ale), and so the boat is not dragged down (the town-crier joke).
- 60.6: the line runs from the tub aft, round the loggerhead, forward across every oar-loom, between the men, to the leaded chocks with the quill-sized pin. From there it makes a festoon over the bows, comes back inboard, and 10–20 fathoms of box-line are coiled on the box. It then goes to the gunwale a little aft and is attached to the short-warp, whose "mystifications" are also kept.
- 60.7–60.9: the dangers and the jokes are complete: jugglers with snakes, the marrow-jelly shudder, the mahogany, the burghers of Calais, the steam-engine comparison, the cradle, Mazeppa, the calm as the storm's wrapper, halters, and the philosopher's poker.

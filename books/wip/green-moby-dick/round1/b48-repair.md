## Notable keeps

- **48.4, 48.5, 48.13–16, 48.18, 48.33, 48.35, 48.39, 48.41–47:** these are already modern and faithful. Small drops such as "again riveted" in 48.5 are not worth a repair.
- **48.14:** "larboard boat" is rendered "Portside boat". I kept it because the meaning is accurate.
- **48.24:** "three seas off" is rendered "three waves off". I kept it.
- **51.5:** "urn-like prow" became "funeral-urn prow". I kept it because the urn already carries the funeral sense.
- **Also kept:** 51.0, 51.3–8, 51.10–11, 52.0, 52.2, 52.5–7, 53.7, and the dialogue in 49 and 50.

## Glossing decisions

- **48.23:** "trucks" glossed as "the caps at the very tops of her masts".
- **48.30:** "soundings" glossed as "(their long, deep dives)".
- **51.9:** "bowline" glossed as "a loop of rope".
- **52.1:** "fullers bleaching cloth" covers the old cloth-whitener sense.
- **Not glossed:** "gam" in ch 53, because Melville defines it himself in 53.7; also waif pole, thole-pins, stern sheets, peaked, and "the uncanonical Rabbins".

## Period language

- **48.22, 48.28, 48.29:** in the paragraphs I repaired, I restored the source's "savage" and "negro" under hard rule 4. The candidate had silently swapped them for "harpooner". The lead may want an edition-wide policy call on this.

## Open questions

- **48.17:** "seethe her" is still rendered "Drive her". The literal sense is to make the boat boil through the water. I left it.
- **48.20:** "sided the furthest to windward" is rendered "edged farthest". This reading is probable, not certain.
- **51.2:** "piled-up craft" is rendered "piled high with sail". This is interpretive and does not add a new claim.
- **51.9:** the source's `*` footnote marker is kept so that 51.10 stays anchored. It is the edition's only asterisk in these chapters.

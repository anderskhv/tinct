## Decisions

- **Scripture:** where Mapple quotes text *as written* (9.9 "And God had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah"; 9.12 "that he paid the fare thereof"; 9.23 "into the midst of the seas", "the weeds were wrapped about his head", "out of the belly of hell", "vomited out Jonah upon the dry land"), the KJV wording is kept, because the quotation is the point. Dramatized speech is modernized: Jonah's "who hath made" becomes "who has made" (9.18), and the master's cry becomes "What do you mean, O sleeper? Arise!" (9.16).
- **Mapple's closing prayer (9.25):** Thy/Thine/Thee → Your/Yours/You, capitalized for God, following the pronoun rule. The cadence ("O Father! — known to me chiefly by Your rod — mortal or immortal, here I die") is kept.
- **Hymn (9.4–9.8):** The wording was already lightly modernized ("lifted", "forever"). The verse inversions ("did me confine", "scarce believe") are kept for rhyme and meter. The " / " line layout is kept (edition practice, as in ch119). Only the quote marks changed.
- **Quotes:** chs 9, 13 and 15 (and 7.6, 8.4) used single quotes for dialogue, and ch9 mixed the two styles. All dialogue in scope is now straight double quotes, with single quotes inside. The sermon follows the source's multi-paragraph convention: an opening quote on each paragraph and a closing quote only at 9.8, 9.19 and 9.24. The odd quote counts in those paragraphs are intentional.
- **Period terms restored:** "negro idol" (10.0), "whitewashed negro" (13.6), "the Pagan" (10.2, 10.6), "savage" (7.5, 12.1, 13.6), "red men"/"infant Indian"/"little Indian's skeleton" (14.2).
- **Names restored to the source spelling:** Feegeeans, Tongatobooarrs, Erromanggoans, Pannangians, Brighggians (6.2); Behring's Straits, Himmalehan (14.3); Water Street and Wapping, Apollo Green (6.1); Herr Alexander (6.5); Ehrenbreitstein (8.3); Indies (7.7); Tophet (15.1).
- **Dialect (lead decision 5):** Queequeg's "kill-e / small-e fish-e" and "tink" are restored (13.3, 13.10–13.12; the Captain's mocking "I'll kill-e you" depends on them). So are the bumpkin's "Capting" and Mrs. Hussey's "unfort'nate v'yage ... ile ... sich dangerous weepons" (15.14), plus Ishmael's "says I ... ain't it".

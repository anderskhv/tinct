## Open questions

1. **78.4.** The source "the negro having cleared the foul line" reads "Daggoo" in the candidate. Under brief rule 4 (don't soften period language) this is a softening. The edition's practice is mixed: 7 occurrences in the candidate against 24 in the source. I left it for the lead's policy.
2. **Footnote asterisks.** Edition practice is mixed: 22 in the candidate against 69 in the source. I kept `*` on the call sites in 75.4 and 77.1, following the source; the footnote paragraphs 75.5 and 77.2 have none. Strip the markers if the lead prefers.
3. **80.3.** "among a plate of men's skulls" probably means an engraved plate in a book. I rendered it literally.
4. **75.3.** "the mother during an important interval" is Melville's euphemism for gestation. I kept it close to literal ("during an important interval").
5. **"gray-headed whale" (74.2).** This follows the brief's American-spelling rule, but the phrase is a quoted fishermen's term. Revert to "grey" if the lead wants the term preserved.

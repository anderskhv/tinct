## Glossing decisions
- 1.1 "that noble mole, or seawall". 1.9 "the two orchard thieves, Adam and Eve" (restores the image and keeps the names as a gloss). 1.10 "playbill" for "bill".
- 2.5 "Tophet, the biblical hell". 3.43 "Iceland's volcano Mount Hecla" (so the "cool as" joke lands). 3.47 "turning flukes — turning in" (glossed once; unglossed at 3.67). 3.6 "skrimshander — sailors' carved bone and ivory work". 3.2 "a Cape-Horner — a ship rounding Cape Horn". 3.1 "Hyperborean ..., from the far frozen north".
- Unglossed on purpose: "pea coffee", "coffer-dam", "sticking-plasters", "grego", "sabbee", "done brown".
- Dialect (lead decision 5): the landlord and Queequeg now keep a light flavor ("the bird what catches the worm", "Sartain", "It's broke", "Who-e debel you?", "you sabbee", "You gettee in"). The candidate had fully standardized it.
- Period terms were restored per lead decision 1: "Red-Men" (2.1), "negro church" (2.5), "little negro" (3.58), "negro heart of Africa" (5.5), "Indian moccasin" (3.51), "heathenish", "savage", "abominable savage", "pagan arm", and "these savages" (the candidate had added "so-called", 4.3).
- Name spellings as printed: Manhattoes (1.1), Manhatto (2.0), AFFGHANISTAN (1.11), Feegees (3.18), Alleghanian Ridge (3.21), Skrimshander (3.26), Moluccas (2.10), Euroclydon (2.8), Jove (1.5), Rogers (4.5), Green Mountains (5.6).

## Open source questions
- 4.1: Melville's text breaks off with "my stepmother who ... —my mother dragged me". This is a known inconsistency in the 1851 text. The repair keeps the break (the dash, then "my mother") rather than correcting it. The lead may prefer to smooth it; the candidate had silently turned it into "stepmother".
- 1.11: the source italicizes only the first playbill line. The rendering keeps all three items in capitals, as the candidate did.
- 3.10: "each in a winding sheet" is rendered "wrapped in a winding sheet of dripped tallow". I did not add the omen-of-death folklore.

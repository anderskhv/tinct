## Method notes

- Chs 134-135 were repaired in full. Every paragraph had curly quotes, unspaced dashes, corruption or 1851 syntax, so even the one-line calls were normalized to the edition's conventions (straight quotes, spaced em dash).
- Ch133 was already partly modernized. Repairs there target real omissions and meaning errors (133.8, 11, 24, 27, 33, 44) plus the paragraphs left near-verbatim.
- Sea-talk "Aye" / "aye, aye" is kept. The edition uses it more than 100 times in chs 1-132. The candidate's botched "Yes, aye!" / "Yes, breach" is restored to "Aye".
- "harpooneer" → "harpooner" per the brief (134.40, 135.54, 135.59).
- Ahab's speeches (134.42, 135.2, 135.10, 135.34, 135.57, 135.55) keep full cadence, apostrophes and imagery. Only pronouns and verb forms are modernized ("Toward you I roll, you all-destroying but unconquering whale; to the last I grapple with you; from hell's heart I stab at you; for hate's sake I spit my last breath at you."; "Thus, I give up the spear!").
- Source italics (_I_ only, _My_ line, _me_/_him_, _this_, _I'll_, _that_) are conveyed by word order, repetition or dashes. No underscores are used.
- Glosses, one each and brief: Eddystone "lighthouse" (133.27), heliotrope "sun-seeking, like the flower" (134.46), main-truck "the cap at the very top of the mainmast" (135.27), Fata Morgana "mirage" (135.59).

## Epilogue quotation decision

136.0 keeps `"AND I ONLY AM ESCAPED ALONE TO TELL THEE" — Job.` unchanged. It is a verbatim scriptural epigraph (Job 1:15-19, King James Version). The quotation itself is the point: Ishmael borrows the messenger's formula. The edition's thee→you rule covers narration and dialogue, not quoted scripture, so modernizing it would misquote the source. 136.1 is kept as is. 136.2 gets a light repair only (inverted "I did revolve", the verbless "Till ... burst" clause, "main" → "sea").

## Notable keeps / fidelity choices

- 133.1, 133.7, 133.17, 133.18, 133.20, 133.30, 133.32, 133.40 are already clear modern English with the full content, so they are kept.
- "savage" (135.60, 136.2), "Indian", "tiger-yellow barbarians" and "Indian file" are period language, kept per the brief's no-softening rule.
- 135.10 "but, Ahab, there your shot fell short" keeps the source's ambiguous vocative. The line could mean Ahab addressing himself or the Parsee's shot.

## Open source questions (non-blocking)

1. **134.10 "guilt and guiltiness"**: this reads like a slip for "guilt and guiltlessness". I kept it as printed.
2. **135.2 "mightiest Mississippies of the land swift and swerve about"**: "swift" as a verb is odd, and "shift" is a plausible misprint. I rendered it as "rush and swerve about", which covers both readings.
3. **134.25 "be the leaner who he will"**: this could be the one leaning or the one leaned on. I rendered it literally as "whoever the leaner may be".
4. **133.26 "a movement that loosed his hold"**: the "his" could be the whale's hold on the boat or Ahab's hold on the jaw. I kept it ambiguous, as the source does.

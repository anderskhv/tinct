# Fidelity Review 1 — Oedipus Rex (candidate.json vs. source.json)

**Source edition (fidelity anchor):** `oedipus-rex-original-en.json`
(public-domain English translation; the Greek original is not the anchor
per `books/AGENTS.md`'s non-English-source rule).

**Method:** Full non-sampled coverage. All 11 chapters / 474 paragraphs
compared source-vs-candidate in packets of roughly 5-10 paragraphs (choral
odes and very long speeches taken as single-paragraph packets given the
chapters' natural short length), with the paragraph immediately before and
after each packet re-read as context. After packeting, the whole play was
re-read once more end to end for cross-boundary issues (recurring
epithets, the Sphinx/"songstress" motif, the crossroads detail, the
ankle-pin/name motif, the chorus's collective voice vs. individual
speech).

Every paragraph in both files was read individually; none were skimmed.
This is a two-chapter-file play text (11 short chapters), so "packets" here
means: Ch.1 in four packets (paras 1-10, 9-20, 19-30, 29-43), Ch.2 (whole,
6 paras, one packet), Ch.3 in packets of ~8-10 with the two long prose
speeches (proclamation, Teiresias's exit speech) each read as their own
packet, Ch.4 (whole), Ch.5 in packets of ~10, Ch.6 (whole), Ch.7 in
packets of ~10 with Jocasta's oracle narrative and Oedipus's autobiography
each as their own packet, Ch.8 (whole), Ch.9 in packets of ~10-15 given
its length (147 paragraphs, almost all single-line stichomythia), Ch.10
(whole), Ch.11 in packets of ~5-6 given the long messenger/lament
speeches.

## Oedipus Rex-specific checks applied throughout

- **Dramatic irony / hedges preserved.** Checked every place Oedipus,
  Jocasta, or the Chorus hedges ("methinks," "'tis a dread presentiment,"
  "I ween," "so it is said," "perhaps") against the candidate, since a
  hedge flattened into a flat assertion (or vice versa) would spoil or
  distort the irony the play depends on.
- **Chorus vs. individual voice.** Checked that CHORUS lines keep
  collective language ("we," "our," "my country," "us") and that no
  chorus line was rewritten into an individually-voiced statement, or
  vice versa.
- **Register.** Checked that the verse-to-prose modernization stays in a
  formal, elevated tragic register (not comic, not casually colloquial)
  throughout — including the choral odes.

## Findings by chapter

### Ch.1 Prologue (43 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
All actors, causal chains, and conditions verified paragraph by
paragraph. Oedipus's proclamation of concern for Thebes, the Priest's
petition, and the Creon exchange about the oracle (banishment or blood
for blood; "who seeks shall find; who sits with folded hands or sleeps is
blind") all match the source's content, actors, and negation exactly.
No omissions or additions found.

### Ch.2 Parodos (6 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Verse choral invocation to Apollo, Athena, Artemis, Ares, and Bacchus.
Checked every epithet against the source ("Healer of Delos," "Lycean
King," "Amphitrite's bed," "Bacchus... whom thy Maenads Evoe shout") —
all present, all addressed to the same figure as the source, order
preserved. No content added or dropped; the modernization removes archaic
syntax ("thy," "doth") without altering which god is invoked in which
line.

### Ch.3 First Episode (72 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
- Oedipus's proclamation (para 2, the longest in the chapter): every
  conditional clause checked ("if he shrinks... the worst penalty...
  banishment," "if you still keep silent... hear then what I resolve") —
  scope and consequence of each condition match the source.
- The full Teiresias/Oedipus stichomythia exchange (paras 79-132): checked
  every line for actor/negation swaps given how much hinges on exact
  wording here (Teiresias's accusations are the play's central dramatic
  irony engine). All lines match: "I say that you are the murderer of the
  man whose murderer you are hunting" correctly attributes the accusation
  to Teiresias and preserves the paradox construction of the source line.
  "This day shall be your birthday — and your grave" preserves the riddle
  exactly, not glossed or explained away.
- Teiresias's exit prophecy (para 132): the full chain of predictions
  (foreigner-who-is-really-Theban, blind, beggar's rags, "brother and
  father... son and husband both, bedfellow, and killer of his own
  father") matches the source's kinship claims in the same order with no
  additions or softenings.

### Ch.4 First Stasimon (9 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Chorus's uncertainty about Oedipus's guilt ("I know not and bridle my
tongue for fear... nor present nor future is clear... There is no proof")
is preserved as chorus-level hedging, not resolved into certainty either
way. Creon's protest and the Chorus's noncommittal replies match.

### Ch.5 Second Episode (71 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Oedipus/Creon confrontation and Creon's long defense speech checked in
full. Conditionals in Creon's reasoning ("would any mortal choose a
troubled reign... if the same power were given him") preserved with the
same scope. Jocasta's arrival and the Chorus's plea to spare Creon,
including the Chorus's oath ("Witness, thou Sun, such thought was never
mine") match exactly, including that this is the Chorus's collective
oath, not an individual character's.

### Ch.6 Second Stasimon (9 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Short stichomythia between Jocasta and Chorus about the quarrel; content
and evasiveness ("Ask me no more... better to leave sleeping troubles at
rest") both preserved.

### Ch.7 Third Episode (44 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
- Jocasta's oracle narrative (para 253, checked as its own packet with
  paras 252/254 as context): every detail checked against source — the
  oracle came to Laius (not directly attributed to Delphic Apollo but to
  "his ministers," a hedge the candidate preserves: "I will not say it was
  from the Delphic god himself, but from his ministers"), the child was
  three days old, ankles pierced and pinned, exposed on the mountain,
  killed at a three-way crossroads by foreigners. All actors and the
  conditional/causal chain match exactly. Jocasta's overconfident
  conclusion ("So Apollo did not, in the end, bring it to pass...") is
  preserved as her flawed reasoning, not corrected or hedged differently
  than the source — important, since her false confidence here is the
  engine of the scene's irony.
- Oedipus's autobiography (para 280, longest paragraph in the play,
  checked as its own packet with paras 279/281 as context): the full
  sequence (banquet insult, parents' reassurance, secret trip to Delphi,
  the prophecy of patricide/incest, the crossroads killing, the doubt
  about whether the stranger was Laius) is present in the same order with
  no dropped clauses. The crossroads killing itself was checked
  particularly closely for actor accuracy (who struck first, who struck
  the fatal blow) — matches the source: the herald/old man's party
  threatens/jostles Oedipus first, Oedipus strikes the charioteer, the old
  man strikes Oedipus with a goad, Oedipus kills him with his staff "and
  so I killed them, every one." No actor swap.
- The exchange about the number of robbers (paras 285-287, the
  "one"-vs-"many" logic Oedipus and Jocasta both rely on) was checked
  word for word since the whole scene's momentary false relief depends on
  this exact logical distinction — "if he still speaks of robbers, not a
  single robber, then I did not kill him; 'one' cannot square with
  'many'" matches the source's logic precisely, including which direction
  the conditional runs.

### Ch.8 Third Stasimon (19 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Choral meditation on hubris and impiety, Jocasta's prayer to Apollo, and
the Corinthian Messenger's arrival with news of Polybus's death, all
checked. The Messenger's news ("The people of the Isthmus have resolved
to make your husband king... he is dead and in his grave") matches
exactly, including that this is reported news (a messenger's report), not
narrated fact — the candidate does not upgrade it to omniscient
narration.

### Ch.9 Fourth Episode (147 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
The longest chapter, almost entirely rapid stichomythia revealing
Oedipus's true parentage. This is the chapter where a single flipped
negation or misattributed line would do the most damage to the plot, so
every line was checked individually rather than in bulk:
- The messenger's reveal that Polybus was not Oedipus's blood father
  ("Because Polybus was nothing to you by blood" / "As much your father
  as I am — no more, no less") matches the source's exact scope (not
  "less of a father" but "no relation by blood at all").
- The ankle-pin/name-origin detail ("I loosed the pin that riveted your
  feet together" / "And from that came the name you still bear" —
  Oedipus meaning "swollen foot") matches, preserved as an implication
  rather than spelled out, exactly as the source leaves it.
- The identification of the second herdsman as "of Laius' household"
  matches, though see the one non-blocking note below.
- Jocasta's realization and exit ("O woe is you, poor wretch! With that
  last word I leave you, silent from now on, forever") — checked for
  tone: the candidate keeps this as Jocasta's chilling, final, ambiguous
  line rather than clarifying what she now knows, matching the source's
  refusal to spell it out.

### Ch.10 Fourth Stasimon (4 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
Short choral ode on Oedipus's fall; "the vulture-maid" (Sphinx epithet)
and "outshot the best of marksmen" both preserved without gloss, matching
the source's own lack of gloss.

### Ch.11 Exodos (56 paragraphs) — no blocking defects
- Second Messenger's report of Jocasta's suicide and Oedipus's
  self-blinding (para 490, checked as its own packet): sequence of
  actions (frenzy, entrance hall, bridal chamber, door slam, invoking
  Laius's name, the noose, Oedipus bursting in, cutting her down, the
  brooches, the self-blinding, the quoted lines) all present in the same
  order, same actor for each action, no omissions of the graphic detail
  ("one dark gory downpour, thick as hail").
- Oedipus's long lament (para 509) and his final exchange with Creon and
  his daughters, checked for the "sons vs. daughters" distinction (he
  dismisses concern for his sons as men who "can shift for themselves"
  but pleads at length for his daughters) — matches the source's
  asymmetry exactly, not softened or evened out.
- The closing choral maxim ("wait to see the ending of a life before you
  call any mortal blessed") matches the source precisely — this is the
  play's thesis line and was checked word-for-sense against the source's
  "wait to see life's ending ere thou count one mortal blest."

## Cross-boundary re-read (step C)

Read the whole play again after packeting, specifically for:
- **The Sphinx/"songstress" motif** — consistently referred to indirectly
  ("that cruel songstress," "the riddling Sphinx," "the vulture-maid," "he
  who knew the riddle of the Sphinx") across Ch.1, Ch.3, Ch.4, Ch.10, and
  Ch.11. The candidate preserves the same pattern of naming/not-naming at
  each occurrence as the source — it does not standardize or clarify the
  references in a way that would flatten the motif.
- **The ankle/name motif** — set up obliquely in Ch.9 ("Oedipus" from
  swollen feet) without being spelled out, consistent with the source's
  own indirection.
- **Chorus collective voice** — checked across all six choral
  interventions; never slips into an individually-voiced line, and no
  individual character's line was ever given collective phrasing.
- **Register consistency** — the play stays in an elevated, formal tragic
  register throughout (words like "wretch," "abhorrence," "defilement,"
  "pollution," "accursed," "beg," "spurn" recur appropriately); it does
  not drift into flatly casual modern phrasing or, conversely, into comic
  deadpan. Confirmed this holds in both the verse choral sections and the
  prose dialogue.

## Non-blocking notes (stylistic, not fidelity defects)

1. **Ch.9, para (Messenger, "He passed indeed for one of Laius' house."):**
   candidate renders this as "He passed, as they say, for one of Laius'
   household." The source's "indeed" affirms the reputation more directly;
   the candidate's "as they say" reframes it as secondhand hearsay. This is
   a minor certainty/attribution shift in a single clause — does not change
   the plot fact being revealed (the herdsman really was of Laius'
   household, confirmed moments later by the Herdsman himself), so it does
   not affect the dramatic irony or the reveal. Non-blocking; worth a
   one-word tweak ("indeed" instead of "as they say") if a future pass
   touches this paragraph, but not required for acceptance.
2. **Ch.11, para 486 (Second Messenger's opening):** source's "I ween" (a
   hedging aside, roughly "I believe/suppose") is dropped from "Not the
   Ister, nor all the flood of Phasis, could wash away the bloodstains
   from this house." The underlying claim (no river could wash away this
   house's guilt) is stated with slightly more flat certainty in the
   candidate than the source's hedged phrasing. Very minor — the messenger
   is not a character whose epistemic uncertainty matters dramatically
   (unlike Oedipus/Jocasta/Teiresias), so this does not touch the play's
   central irony mechanism. Non-blocking.
3. Throughout, obvious OCR-era typos in the source text itself (e.g. "bee"
   for "been" in Ch.11, "Mehtinks" for "methinks" in Ch.7, "sure" for
   "sire" in Ch.9's "sure thy sure was more than man") are silently
   corrected in the candidate. These are transcription artifacts of the
   source scan, not substantive wording the source's author intended —
   correcting them is not the kind of silent "correction" the protocol
   warns against (which targets names/citations/facts changed to their
   standard form against the source's actual claim). Flagging only for
   the record, not as a defect.

## Verdict

**ACCEPT AS-IS.**

Full non-sampled coverage of all 474 paragraphs across all 11 chapters,
compared in packets with neighboring context, plus a full cross-boundary
re-read. Zero blocking defects found: no actor misattribution, no negation
flips, no causality reversals, no dropped or added claims, no silently
"corrected" names/facts, no unmodernized archaic-island quotations, and no
flattening of the hedges/uncertainty that the play's dramatic irony
depends on. The verse-to-prose modernization consistently favors meaning
over rhyme, keeps the Chorus's collective voice distinct from individual
characters', and holds a formal tragic register throughout rather than
drifting comic or flatly casual. Two very minor, non-blocking hedge-word
softenings are noted above for awareness only; neither touches plot fact,
actor attribution, or the central dramatic-irony mechanism, and neither
requires a fix before acceptance.

===== Chapter 1: Act 1, Scene 1 — On a Ship at Sea =====
[0] [Enter a Master and a Boatswain]
[1] MASTER. Boatswain!
[2] BOATSWAIN. Here, captain. What's happening?
[3] MASTER. Good man, get the sailors moving. Hurry up, quickly, or we'll run aground. Move it, move it!
[4] [Exit]
[5] [Enter Mariners]
[6] BOATSWAIN. Come on, lads! Cheerfully now, cheerfully! Quickly, quickly! Take in the topsail. Listen for the captain's whistle. Blow, storm, blow until you burst, as long as we have room to maneuver!
[7] [Enter ALONSO, SEBASTIAN, ANTONIO, FERDINAND, GONZALO, and others]
[8] ALONSO. Good boatswain, be careful. Where's the captain? Get the men working.
[9] BOATSWAIN. Please, stay below deck.
[10] ANTONIO. Where is the captain, boatswain?
[11] BOATSWAIN. Can't you hear him? You're getting in the way of our work. Stay in your cabins. You're only making the storm worse.
[12] GONZALO. Now, good man, be patient.
[13] BOATSWAIN. When the sea is patient! Get out of here! What do these roaring waves care about the name of a king? Get to your cabins! Be quiet! Don't bother us.
[14] GONZALO. Fine, but remember who you have on board.
[15] BOATSWAIN. Nobody I love more than myself. You're a royal counselor; if you can command these waves to be silent and calm the situation, we won't touch another rope. Use your authority. If you can't, be thankful you've lived this long, and go prepare yourself in your cabin for whatever disaster may come. Cheerfully now, good men! Out of our way, I say.
[16] [Exit]
[17] GONZALO. I take great comfort from this fellow. It seems to me he doesn't have the look of a drowning man about him; he looks more like he was born for the gallows. Hold fast, good Fate, and save him for his hanging. Let the rope of his destiny be our anchor cable, since our own isn't doing us much good. If he wasn't born to be hanged, then we're in real trouble.
[18] [Exeunt]
[19] [Re-enter Boatswain]
[20] BOATSWAIN. Lower the topmast! Quickly! Lower, lower! Bring her around with the mainsail.
[21] [A cry within]
[22] BOATSWAIN. A plague on this howling! They're louder than the storm or our orders.
[23] [Re-enter SEBASTIAN, ANTONIO, and GONZALO]
[24] BOATSWAIN. You again! What are you doing here? Should we just give up and drown? Do you want to sink?
[25] SEBASTIAN. A pox on your throat, you yelling, blasphemous, uncharitable dog!
[26] BOATSWAIN. Then you do the work.
[27] ANTONIO. Hang, you cur! Hang, you insolent noisemaker! We're less afraid of drowning than you are.
[28] GONZALO. I'd bet my life he won't drown, even if this ship were no stronger than a nutshell and leaked like a sieve.
[29] BOATSWAIN. Hold her steady, hold her steady! Set both sails to head back out to sea! Steer her off!
[30] [Enter Mariners wet]
[31] MARINERS. All lost! Start praying, start praying! All is lost!
[32] BOATSWAIN. What, are we going to die here?
[33] GONZALO. The king and prince are praying! Let's join them, for we're in the same situation.
[34] SEBASTIAN. I've lost all patience.
[35] ANTONIO. We're being cheated out of our lives by drunken sailors! That wide-mouthed rascal -- I wish he'd lie drowning through ten tides!
[36] GONZALO. He'll still be hanged, even if every drop of water swore against it and opened wide to swallow him.
[37] [A confused noise within:   'Mercy on us!'--  'We split, we split!'--'Farewell, my wife and  children!'-- 'Farewell, brother!'--'We split, we split, we split!']
[38] ANTONIO. Let's all go down with the king.
[39] SEBASTIAN. Let's say goodbye to him.
[40] [Exeunt ANTONIO and SEBASTIAN]
[41] GONZALO. Right now I'd trade a thousand miles of sea for one acre of barren ground -- heather, thorns, anything. May heaven's will be done! But I'd much rather die on dry land.
[42] [Exeunt]

===== Chapter 2: Act 1, Scene 2 — The Island, Before Prospero's Cell =====
[0] [Enter PROSPERO and MIRANDA]
[1] MIRANDA. If it was your magic, my dearest father, that stirred the wild sea into this fury, please calm it. The sky looks like it would pour down burning pitch, but the sea rises up to meet the heavens and puts the fire out. Oh, I've suffered along with everyone I watched suffering. That fine ship surely had some noble people on board, and it's been smashed to pieces. Oh, their cries struck against my very heart. Those poor souls -- they've all died. If I had been a god with any power, I would have sunk the sea into the earth before it could have swallowed that good ship and all the precious souls on board.
[2] PROSPERO. Calm yourself. No more panic. Tell your tender heart there's been no harm done.
[3] MIRANDA. Oh, what a terrible day!
[4] PROSPERO. No harm. Everything I've done has been for your sake -- for you, my dear one, you, my daughter, who knows nothing about who you really are, nothing about where I come from, or that I am something more than just Prospero, the master of this poor little dwelling and your humble father.
[5] MIRANDA. I've never wanted to know more than what I know.
[6] PROSPERO. It's time I told you more. Give me your hand and help me take off my magic cloak. There.
[7] [Lays down his mantle]
[8] PROSPERO. Lie there, my magic. Dry your eyes and take comfort. That terrible shipwreck you witnessed, which touched the very core of your compassion -- I arranged it so carefully with my art that not a single soul was harmed. Not so much as a hair on anyone's head was hurt on any creature aboard that vessel that you heard crying out, that you watched go down. Sit down, because you need to hear more.
[9] MIRANDA. You've started to tell me who I am many times before, but always stopped and left me with unanswered questions, saying, 'Wait -- not yet.'
[10] PROSPERO. The time has come. This very moment demands that you open your ears. Listen carefully. Can you remember a time before we came to this island? I don't think you can, since you were not yet three years old.
[11] MIRANDA. Actually, sir, I can.
[12] PROSPERO. Really? Can you remember another house, or another person? Tell me any image that has stayed in your memory.
[13] MIRANDA. It's very distant, and more like a dream than a real memory. But didn't I once have four or five women who took care of me?
[14] PROSPERO. You did, and more, Miranda. But how does this survive in your mind? What else can you see in the dark depths of the past? If you can remember anything from before you came here, you may also remember how you got here.
[15] MIRANDA. But I don't remember that.
[16] PROSPERO. Twelve years ago, Miranda, twelve years ago, your father was the Duke of Milan and a powerful prince.
[17] MIRANDA. Sir, aren't you my father?
[18] PROSPERO. Your mother was a woman of great virtue, and she said you were my daughter. And your father was the Duke of Milan, and you were his only heir, a princess of the highest birth.
[19] MIRANDA. Oh, heavens! What treachery brought us away from there? Or was it a blessing that we left?
[20] PROSPERO. Both, my girl. By treachery, as you say, we were driven from there, but by blessed fortune we were brought here.
[21] MIRANDA. Oh, my heart aches to think of the suffering I must have caused you, though I can't remember any of it! Please, go on.
[22] PROSPERO. My brother -- your uncle -- called Antonio. Listen carefully to me. That a brother could be so treacherous! He was the person I loved most in all the world, next to you, and I entrusted the government of my state to him. At that time, Milan was the foremost of all the Italian states, and I, Prospero, was its most respected duke, known for my unrivaled mastery of the liberal arts. Those studies consumed me completely. I handed the government over to my brother and became a stranger to affairs of state, absorbed and lost in secret studies. Your treacherous uncle -- are you paying attention?
[23] MIRANDA. Sir, most carefully.
[24] PROSPERO. Once he had mastered how to grant requests and deny them, whom to promote and whom to cut down, he either remade or replaced all the people who had been my followers. Holding the keys to every office and appointment, he turned every heart in the state to whatever tune pleased his ear. He became the ivy that hid my royal trunk and sucked all my vitality out of it. You're not paying attention.
[25] MIRANDA. Oh, good sir, I am.
[26] PROSPERO. Listen to me carefully. While I was neglecting worldly matters, completely dedicated to isolation and the improvement of my mind with studies that -- precisely because they were so private -- exceeded anything the public could appreciate, my devotion awakened an evil nature in my false brother. And my trust, like a good parent, gave birth in him to a falsehood as great as that trust was, which truly had no limit -- a boundless confidence. Having been given power like this, not only over what my revenues provided but what my authority could command, he became like someone who, by constantly telling a lie, corrupts his own memory into believing it true. He came to believe that he actually was the duke, having stepped into the role and taken on all the outward show of royalty with all its privileges. And so his ambition kept growing. Are you listening?
[27] MIRANDA. Your story, sir, would cure deafness.
[28] PROSPERO. To eliminate any distance between the role he was playing and the man he played it for, he needed to become the absolute Duke of Milan. As for poor me, my library was dukedom enough. He decided I was incapable of handling temporal power, and he made an alliance -- he was so hungry for authority -- with the King of Naples, agreeing to pay him annual tribute and pledge his allegiance, subjecting his duke's crown to the King's crown and bending the still-unbowed dukedom -- poor Milan! -- into the most degrading submission.
[29] MIRANDA. Oh, heavens!
[30] PROSPERO. Consider the terms of this deal and what happened next, and then tell me if this sounds like a brother.
[31] MIRANDA. I would be sinning to think anything but noble thoughts about my grandmother. Good women have given birth to bad sons.
[32] PROSPERO. Now, the terms. The King of Naples, being my longstanding enemy, listened to my brother's proposal: that in exchange for the pledges of tribute and homage and whatever else, the King would immediately eliminate me and my family from the dukedom and grant beautiful Milan, with all its honors, to my brother. And so a treacherous army was raised, and one midnight -- fated for this very purpose -- Antonio opened the gates of Milan, and in the dead of darkness, the agents he'd appointed hurried me and you, crying as you were, out of the city.
[33] MIRANDA. What a pity! I can't remember how I cried then, but I'll cry about it again now. The story wrings tears from my eyes.
[34] PROSPERO. Hear a little more, and then I'll bring you to the matter at hand, without which this whole story would be beside the point.
[35] MIRANDA. Why didn't they kill us right then?
[36] PROSPERO. Good question, my dear. My story naturally raises it. They didn't dare, because my people loved me so much, and they didn't want to mark the business with blood. Instead, they painted their foul deeds in prettier colors. In short, they rushed us onto a ship, carried us several leagues out to sea, and there they put us in a rotting hulk of a boat -- no rigging, no sails, no mast. Even the rats had instinctively abandoned it. There they left us, crying out to the sea that roared back at us, sighing to the winds, whose pity, sighing back again, did us only loving harm.
[37] MIRANDA. What a burden I must have been to you!
[38] PROSPERO. Oh, you were a little angel who saved me. You smiled, filled with a heavenly strength, while I was watering the sea with bitter tears and groaning under my burden. That smile gave me the inner courage to endure whatever came next.
[39] MIRANDA. How did we reach shore?
[40] PROSPERO. By divine providence. We had some food and fresh water, given to us by a noble Neapolitan named Gonzalo, who, out of his charity -- being the one appointed to carry out this plan -- also gave us rich clothes, linens, supplies, and necessities, which have served us well ever since. And knowing how much I loved my books, he generously supplied me from my own library with the volumes I treasure more than my dukedom.
[41] MIRANDA. I wish I could meet that man someday!
[42] PROSPERO. Now, let me stand.
[43] [Resumes his mantle]
[44] PROSPERO. Sit still and hear the last of our sad sea voyage. Here on this island we arrived, and here I, as your schoolmaster, have given you a better education than other princesses get, who have more free time and less careful tutors.
[45] MIRANDA. Thank heaven for that! But now please tell me, sir -- it keeps nagging at my mind -- your reason for raising this storm at sea?
[46] PROSPERO. Know this much. By the strangest accident, generous Fortune -- now my ally -- has brought my enemies to this shore. And through my foresight, I can see that my fate depends upon a most favorable star, whose influence I must seize right now or my fortunes will decline forever after. No more questions. You're feeling sleepy -- it's a good drowsiness, so give in to it. I know you can't resist.
[47] [MIRANDA sleeps]
[48] PROSPERO. Come here, servant, come. I'm ready now. Come to me, my Ariel, come.
[49] [Enter ARIEL]
[50] ARIEL. All hail, great master! Noble sir, hail! I've come to carry out your every wish -- whether it's to fly, to swim, to dive into fire, to ride on the curling clouds. Command Ariel and all my kind to whatever task you please.
[51] PROSPERO. Spirit, did you carry out the storm exactly as I ordered?
[52] ARIEL. Down to every detail. I boarded the king's ship -- now on the bow, now in the middle, on the deck, in every cabin -- I blazed with terrifying fire. Sometimes I would split apart and burn in many places at once. On the topmast, the yards, and the bowsprit, I would flame separately, then merge together. Even the lightning bolts that precede the terrible thunderclaps, fast as the eye can follow, were not quicker than me. The fire and cracks of sulfurous roaring seemed to lay siege to mighty Neptune himself and make his bold waves tremble -- yes, even his fearsome trident shook.
[53] PROSPERO. My brave spirit! Was anyone so steady, so composed, that this chaos didn't drive him mad?
[54] ARIEL. Not a single soul escaped a fever of madness, and they all performed some desperate act. Everyone except the mariners plunged into the foaming sea and abandoned the vessel, which was ablaze with my fire. The king's son, Ferdinand, with his hair standing on end -- stiff as reeds, not like hair at all -- was the first man to leap overboard. He cried, 'Hell is empty, and all the devils are here!'
[55] PROSPERO. That's my spirit! But wasn't this close to shore?
[56] ARIEL. Very close, my master.
[57] PROSPERO. But are they safe, Ariel?
[58] ARIEL. Not a hair was harmed. Not a stain on their clothing -- in fact, their garments are fresher than before. And as you ordered, I scattered them in groups around the island. The king's son I set down by himself. I left him cooling the air with his sighs in a quiet corner of the island, sitting with his arms folded in sadness like this.
[59] PROSPERO. Tell me about the king's ship and its sailors, and what happened to the rest of the fleet.
[60] ARIEL. The king's ship is safe in the harbor, in that deep inlet where you once called me up at midnight to fetch dew from the ever-stormy Bermudas -- that's where she's hidden. The mariners are all stowed below decks. With a spell joined to their exhaustion from the ordeal, I've put them to sleep. As for the rest of the fleet that I scattered, they've all met up again and are sailing on the Mediterranean, headed sadly home for Naples, believing they saw the king's ship wrecked and the king himself drowned.
[61] PROSPERO. Ariel, your task has been performed exactly. But there's more work to do. What time is it?
[62] ARIEL. Past noon.
[63] PROSPERO. At least two hours past. The time between now and six o'clock must be spent most carefully by both of us.
[64] ARIEL. Is there more work? Since you keep giving me tasks, let me remind you of what you promised me, which you haven't yet fulfilled.
[65] PROSPERO. What's this? In a mood? What is it you want?
[66] ARIEL. My freedom.
[67] PROSPERO. Before the agreed time is up? Not another word!
[68] ARIEL. Please, remember that I've served you well. I've told you no lies, made no mistakes, and served without complaint or grumbling. You promised to shorten my service by a full year.
[69] PROSPERO. Have you forgotten what a torment I freed you from?
[70] ARIEL. No.
[71] PROSPERO. You have, and you think it's too much to walk through the ooze of the deep ocean, to run on the sharp north wind, to do my work in the veins of the frozen earth.
[72] ARIEL. I do not, sir.
[73] PROSPERO. You're lying, you ungrateful thing! Have you forgotten the foul witch Sycorax, who had grown bent over like a hoop with age and spite? Have you forgotten her?
[74] ARIEL. No, sir.
[75] PROSPERO. You have. Where was she born? Speak. Tell me.
[76] ARIEL. Sir, in Algiers.
[77] PROSPERO. Oh, was she? It seems I have to remind you once a month of what you've been through, which you keep forgetting. This cursed witch Sycorax, for countless wicked deeds and terrible sorceries too horrible for human ears, was banished from Algiers. They spared her life for one reason alone. Isn't this true?
[78] ARIEL. Yes, sir.
[79] PROSPERO. This blue-eyed hag was brought here pregnant and abandoned by the sailors. You, as you yourself have told me, were her servant then. And because you were a spirit too refined and delicate to carry out her foul, earthly commands, and you refused her orders, she imprisoned you -- with the help of her more powerful agents, and in her most terrible rage -- inside a split pine tree. Trapped in that crack, you endured painful imprisonment for a dozen years. During that time she died and left you there, where you groaned as rapidly as a mill-wheel turns. Then this island -- except for the son she whelped here, a freckled creature born of a hag -- had no human inhabitant.
[80] ARIEL. Yes, Caliban, her son.
[81] PROSPERO. Dull creature, that's what I said. He's the Caliban I now keep in my service. You know best what torment I found you in. Your groans made wolves howl and pierced the hearts of the most savage bears. It was a punishment fit for the damned, which Sycorax herself could not undo. It was my magic, when I arrived and heard you, that split open the pine tree and set you free.
[82] ARIEL. I thank you, master.
[83] PROSPERO. If you complain any more, I'll split open an oak tree and peg you inside its knotty guts until you've howled away twelve more winters.
[84] ARIEL. Forgive me, master. I will obey your every command and perform my duties gracefully.
[85] PROSPERO. Do that, and in two days I'll set you free.
[86] ARIEL. That's my noble master! What should I do? Tell me, what should I do?
[87] PROSPERO. Go disguise yourself as a sea nymph. Be visible to no one but you and me -- invisible to every other eye. Go, take this form and come back here in it. Go, hurry!
[88] [Exit ARIEL]
[89] PROSPERO. Wake up, dear heart, wake up! You've slept well. Wake up!
[90] MIRANDA. The strangeness of your story made me drowsy.
[91] PROSPERO. Shake it off. Come on, let's go visit Caliban, my slave, who never gives us a kind word.
[92] MIRANDA. He's a villain, sir. I don't like looking at him.
[93] PROSPERO. True as that is, we can't do without him. He makes our fire, gathers our wood, and does chores that are useful to us. Hey! Slave! Caliban! You lump of earth! Speak!
[94] CALIBAN. [Within] There's enough firewood inside already.
[95] PROSPERO. Come out, I say! There's other work for you. Come out, you tortoise! When will you move?
[96] [Re-enter ARIEL like a water-nymph]
[97] PROSPERO. Beautiful apparition! My clever Ariel, listen here.
[98] ARIEL. My lord, it will be done.
[99] [Exit]
[100] PROSPERO. You poisonous slave, fathered by the devil himself upon your wicked mother, come out!
[101] [Enter CALIBAN]
[102] CALIBAN. May the most poisonous dew my mother ever swept with a raven's feather from some unwholesome swamp fall on you both! May a southwest wind blow on you and blister you all over!
[103] PROSPERO. For that, you can be sure that tonight you'll have cramps and side-stitches that steal your breath. My goblins will torment you all through the long hours of the night. You'll be pinched as thick as a honeycomb, each pinch stinging worse than the bees that made it.
[104] CALIBAN. I need to eat my dinner. This island is mine, inherited from my mother Sycorax, and you stole it from me. When you first came here, you stroked me and made much of me. You gave me water with berries in it and taught me the names of the sun and the moon, the greater and the lesser light. And then I loved you and showed you all the features of this island -- the fresh springs, the salt pools, the barren places and the fertile ones. Cursed be the day I did that! May all the curses of Sycorax -- toads, beetles, bats -- fall on you! I am the only subject you have, and I was once my own king. Now you pen me up in this hard rock while you keep the rest of the island from me.
[105] PROSPERO. You lying slave, who can only be moved by punishment, never by kindness! I treated you, filthy as you are, with human compassion. I lodged you in my own home, until you tried to violate my daughter's honor.
[106] CALIBAN. Ha, ha! I wish I'd succeeded! You stopped me. Otherwise I would have filled this island with little Calibans.
[107] PROSPERO. You loathsome slave, incapable of absorbing any trace of goodness, capable of every evil! I pitied you, took the trouble to teach you to speak, spent every hour teaching you something, when you, savage that you were, didn't even understand your own thoughts but babbled like a brute animal. I gave your ideas words so they could be expressed. But your vile nature, even though you learned, had something in it that decent people couldn't bear to be around. That's why you were deservedly confined to this rock. You deserved worse than a prison.
[108] CALIBAN. You taught me language, and all I've gained from it is that I know how to curse. May the plague destroy you for teaching me your language!
[109] PROSPERO. Witch's offspring, get out! Go fetch us firewood, and be quick about it -- you'd better -- unless you want other tasks. Are you giving me a defiant look? If you neglect what I command, or do it unwillingly, I'll rack you with agonizing cramps, fill every bone with aching pains, and make you roar so loudly that wild beasts will tremble at the noise.
[110] CALIBAN. No, please don't.
[111] [Aside]
[112] CALIBAN. I have to obey him. His magic is so powerful it could overpower my mother's god, Setebos, and make him a slave.
[113] PROSPERO. So then, slave -- go!
[114] [Exit CALIBAN]
[115] [Re-enter ARIEL, invisible, playing and singing; FERDINAND following]
[116] PROSPERO. ARIEL'S song. Come to these yellow sands, and then join hands. Curtsy when you have, and kiss -- the wild waves hushed. Dance nimbly here and there, and, sweet spirits, carry the refrain. Listen, listen!
[117] [Burthen [dispersedly, within]
[118] PROSPERO. The watchdogs bark!
[119] [Burthen Bow-wow]
[120] PROSPERO. Listen, listen! I hear the proud rooster crowing. Cock-a-doodle-doo!
[121] FERDINAND. Where is this music coming from? The air or the earth? It's stopped -- and surely it must be the work of some god of this island. Sitting on a bank, weeping again over my father's shipwreck, this music crept toward me over the water, calming both the fury of the waves and my own grief with its sweet melody. I've followed it -- or rather, it's drawn me here. But it's gone. No, it's starting again.
[122] [ARIEL sings]
[123] FERDINAND. Full fathom five thy father lies. Of his bones are coral made. Those are pearls that were his eyes. Nothing of him that does fade but does suffer a sea-change into something rich and strange. Sea nymphs hourly ring his funeral bell.
[124] [Burthen Ding-dong]
[125] FERDINAND. Listen! Now I hear them -- ding-dong bell.
[126] FERDINAND. This song is about my drowned father. This is no earthly music, no sound that belongs to this world. I hear it now above me.
[127] PROSPERO. Open your eyes and tell me what you see over there.
[128] MIRANDA. What is it? A spirit? Lord, how he looks around! Believe me, sir, he has a noble appearance. But he must be a spirit.
[129] PROSPERO. No, girl -- he eats and sleeps and has the same senses we do. This fine young man you're looking at was in the shipwreck. Except that he's a bit marked by grief -- which is the enemy of beauty -- you'd call him a handsome man. He's lost his companions and is wandering around trying to find them.
[130] MIRANDA. I'd call him something divine, because I've never seen anything in nature so noble.
[131] PROSPERO. [Aside] It's working, I see, just as my heart intended. My fine spirit, I'll free you within two days for this.
[132] FERDINAND. Surely you must be the goddess this music serves! Please grant my prayer: let me know if you live on this island, and give me some guidance on how to behave here. My most important question, which I ask last, is this -- oh, you wonder! -- are you a maiden, or not?
[133] MIRANDA. I'm no wonder, sir. But certainly a maiden.
[134] FERDINAND. You speak my language! Heavens! I'm the highest-ranking person who speaks this tongue, if only I were where it's spoken.
[135] PROSPERO. What? The highest-ranking? What would you be if the King of Naples heard you say that?
[136] FERDINAND. A solitary man, as I am now, who is amazed to hear you mention Naples. He does hear me -- and that's what makes me weep. I myself am the King of Naples, since with these eyes, which have never stopped weeping since, I watched the king my father drown.
[137] MIRANDA. Oh, how terrible!
[138] FERDINAND. Yes, truly -- and all his lords too. The Duke of Milan and his fine son among them.
[139] PROSPERO. [Aside] The Duke of Milan and his even finer daughter could set you straight, if this were the time for it. They've fallen in love at first sight. My delicate Ariel, I'll set you free for this.
[140] [To FERDINAND]
[141] PROSPERO. A word, sir. I'm afraid you may have said something untrue. A word.
[142] MIRANDA. Why is my father speaking so harshly? This is only the third man I've ever seen, and the first I've ever sighed for. I wish pity would move my father to favor my feelings!
[143] FERDINAND. Oh, if you are a virgin and your heart is not already given to someone else, I'll make you the Queen of Naples.
[144] PROSPERO. Wait, sir! One more word.
[145] [Aside]
[146] PROSPERO. They're already in each other's power. But I need to make this courtship more difficult, or winning her too easily will make the prize seem cheap.
[147] [To FERDINAND]
[148] PROSPERO. One more word. I command you to pay attention to me. You are trespassing here under a name that isn't yours, and you've come to this island as a spy to steal it from me, its rightful lord.
[149] FERDINAND. No, as I am an honest man.
[150] MIRANDA. Nothing evil could live inside such a noble body. If an evil spirit had such a beautiful dwelling, good things would strive to live there too.
[151] PROSPERO. Follow me. Don't speak on his behalf -- he's a traitor. Come. I'll chain your neck and feet together. You'll drink seawater. Your food will be withered freshwater mussels, dried roots, and acorn husks. Follow me.
[152] FERDINAND. No. I'll resist this treatment until my captor proves he has greater power than I do.
[153] [Draws, and is charmed from moving]
[154] MIRANDA. Oh, dear father, don't test him too harshly, for he's noble and not dangerous.
[155] PROSPERO. What? My subordinate is going to teach me? Put your sword away, traitor. You make a show of force but don't dare strike -- your conscience is too heavy with guilt. Drop your guard, because I can disarm you with this stick and make your weapon fall.
[156] MIRANDA. Please, father.
[157] PROSPERO. Stop! Don't cling to my clothes.
[158] MIRANDA. Sir, have pity. I'll be his guarantee.

[58] [Exit]

[66] STEPHANO. So is mine. Listen here, monster. If I decide to turn against you, watch out --
[67] TRINCULO. You'd be a dead monster.

[69] TRINCULO. Yes, but we lost our bottles in that pool --
[70] STEPHANO. That's not just a disgrace and dishonor, monster -- it's an immeasurable loss.
[71] TRINCULO. That matters more to me than getting soaked. And yet this is your "harmless fairy," monster.

===== Chapter 9: Act 5, Scene 1 — Before Prospero's Cell =====
[0] [Enter PROSPERO in his magic robes, and ARIEL]
[1] PROSPERO. Now my plan is coming together. My spells hold firm, my spirits obey, and time is proceeding perfectly. What time is it?
[2] ARIEL. The sixth hour, my lord -- the time you said our work should end.
[3] PROSPERO. I did say that when I first raised the storm. Tell me, spirit, how are the king and his followers?
[4] ARIEL. Confined together exactly as you ordered, just as you left them. All prisoners, sir, in the grove of lime trees that shelters your cell from the weather. They can't move until you release them. The king, his brother, and yours are all three out of their minds, and the rest are mourning over them, full of sorrow and despair. But especially the one you called 'the good old lord Gonzalo.' His tears run down his beard like winter rain dripping from the eaves of a thatched roof. Your spell works on them so powerfully that if you could see them now, even your heart would soften.
[5] PROSPERO. Do you think so, spirit?
[6] ARIEL. Mine would, sir, if I were human.
[7] PROSPERO. And mine shall. If you, who are nothing but air, can feel a touch of sympathy for their suffering, shouldn't I -- one of their own kind, who feels everything just as sharply as they do -- be moved even more deeply than you? Though their great wrongs against me still cut me to the quick, I take the side of my nobler reason against my fury. The rarer, more admirable action lies in virtue than in vengeance. Since they are repentant, the purpose of my plan extends not a frown further. Go release them, Ariel. I'll break my spells, restore their senses, and they shall be themselves again.
[8] ARIEL. I'll bring them, sir.
[9] [Exit]
[10] PROSPERO. You elves of hills, brooks, still lakes, and groves, and you who chase the retreating tide across the sand with footprints that leave no mark, and flee when it comes back. You tiny sprites who make the green fairy rings in the meadows by moonlight, where sheep won't graze. And you whose pastime is to grow midnight mushrooms, who rejoice to hear the solemn curfew bell. By your aid -- weak helpers though you are -- I have dimmed the noonday sun, called forth rebellious winds, and set the green sea and the blue sky at roaring war with each other. I have given fire to the terrible rattling thunder and split great Jove's own oak with his own thunderbolt. I have made the solid headland shake, and uprooted pine and cedar trees. Graves at my command have opened and released their dead, so powerful is my art. But this rough magic I now renounce. And when I have called for some heavenly music -- which I do now -- to work my final spell upon their senses, I'll break my staff, bury it deep in the earth, and deeper than any sounding line has ever reached, I'll drown my book of magic.
[11] [Solemn music]
[12] [Re-enter ARIEL before: then ALONSO, with a  frantic gesture, attended by GONZALO;  SEBASTIAN and ANTONIO in like manner,  attended by ADRIAN and FRANCISCO   they all  enter the circle which PROSPERO had made,  and there stand charmed; which PROSPERO observing, speaks:]
[13] PROSPERO. A solemn melody -- the best medicine for a troubled mind -- heal your brains, which now boil uselessly inside your skulls! Stand there, for you are frozen by my spell. Holy Gonzalo, honorable man -- my eyes, sharing in the sight of your tears, shed sympathetic drops of their own. The spell is dissolving quickly now. And just as morning steals over the night, melting the darkness, their awakening senses begin to clear away the ignorant fog that clouds their reason. Oh, good Gonzalo, my true protector, a loyal servant to the king you follow! I will repay your kindness fully, in both word and deed. Most cruelly did you, Alonso, treat me and my daughter. Your brother helped in the act. You are being punished for it now, Sebastian. And you, my own flesh and blood -- you, brother, who embraced ambition and drove out pity and natural feeling -- who, along with Sebastian, whose inner torments are therefore most severe, would have killed your king right here -- I forgive you, unnatural though you are. Their understanding is beginning to rise, and the approaching tide of reason will soon fill the shores of their minds, which now lie foul and muddy. Not one of them recognizes me yet, or would know me. Ariel, go fetch the hat and sword from my cell. I'll remove my magic robes and present myself as I once was when I was Duke of Milan. Quickly, spirit -- you shall soon be free.
[14] [ARIEL sings and helps to attire him]
[15] PROSPERO. Where the bee sucks, there I suck. In a cowslip's bell I lie. There I rest when owls cry. On the bat's back I fly after summer, merrily. Merrily, merrily shall I live now, under the blossom that hangs on the bough.
[16] PROSPERO. Well, that's my dainty Ariel! I shall miss you. But you shall have your freedom. So, there, that's done. Go to the king's ship, invisible as you are. There you'll find the sailors asleep below decks. The captain and the boatswain are awake -- bring them here at once, please.
[17] ARIEL. I'll drink the air before me and return before your pulse beats twice.
[18] [Exit]
[19] GONZALO. All torment, trouble, wonder, and amazement fills this place! Some heavenly power guide us out of this terrifying country!
[20] PROSPERO. Behold, sir king, the wronged Duke of Milan, Prospero. To give you further proof that a living prince now speaks to you, let me embrace you, and to you and your company I bid a hearty welcome.
[21] ALONSO. Whether you are really Prospero or some enchanted illusion sent to deceive me, as has happened recently, I cannot tell. Your pulse beats like flesh and blood. And since I've laid eyes on you, the affliction of my mind is easing -- I fear I was gripped by madness. This demands, if it's real at all, a very strange explanation. I give up your dukedom and beg you to pardon the wrongs I've done you. But how can Prospero be alive and here?
[22] PROSPERO. First, noble friend, let me embrace you, whose honor is beyond measure or limit.
[23] GONZALO. Whether this is real or not, I won't swear either way.
[24] PROSPERO. You're still tasting some of the island's strangeness, which won't let you believe things that are certain. Welcome, all of my friends!
[25] [Aside to SEBASTIAN and ANTONIO]
[26] PROSPERO. But you, my pair of lords -- if I were so inclined, I could bring the king's displeasure down on you and prove you traitors. But at this time, I'll tell no tales.
[27] SEBASTIAN. [Aside] The devil speaks through him.
[28] PROSPERO. No. As for you, most wicked sir, whom it would infect my mouth to call brother -- I forgive your worst offense, all of them, and I demand my dukedom back from you, which, I know, you must now restore.

[33] ALONSO. You have suffered a similar loss?

===== Chapter 10: Epilogue — Spoken by Prospero =====
[0] [Spoken by Prospero]
[1] PROSPERO. Now all my spells are broken, and whatever strength I have left is only my own, which is very little. Now it's true: I must be kept here, confined by you, or sent to Naples. Do not let me -- since I have regained my dukedom and pardoned the man who betrayed me -- remain on this bare island, trapped by your spell. Instead, release me from these bonds with the help of your kind applause. Let the gentle breath of your approval fill my sails, or else my project fails, which was to entertain you. Now I have no spirits to command, no art to enchant, and my ending will be despair unless I am saved by prayer, which is so powerful it assaults mercy itself and frees all faults. As you would want your own sins pardoned, let your generosity set me free.

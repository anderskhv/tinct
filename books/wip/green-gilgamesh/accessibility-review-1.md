# Accessibility Review — The Epic of Gilgamesh (modern-en candidate)

Reviewer: Claude Sonnet 5 (this session), acting as Reviewer A per
`books/prompts/accessibility-review-prompt.md`. Performed after the
fidelity-driven repair round below, reading the **post-fix** candidate.json
fresh, without re-consulting source.json for this pass.

**Coverage:** All 12 chapters (tablets), all 253 paragraphs, read in full,
start to finish, no sampling.

## Findings

The candidate reads as natural, idiomatic contemporary English throughout.
Formulaic Akkadian speech-tags ("X opened his mouth and spoke, saying unto
Y") are consistently compressed to ordinary dialogue tags ("X said to Y" /
"X spoke to Y"), which removes real friction without losing any speaker
attribution. Archaic second-person forms (thee/thou/thy/doth/hast) are
fully modernized. Sentence structure is generally short-to-medium and
reads smoothly; I did not find prose that felt choppily over-chopped.

A few items a general reader may still stumble on, none blocking:

- **Ancient units left untranslated** — "gar" (Tablet VII §2, Tablet IX
  §12, Tablet XI §8) and "double-hour" (repeated in Tablet IX, Tablet X,
  Tablet XI) are specific Akkadian/Babylonian measures the candidate does
  not gloss. A reader won't know a gar's or double-hour's real-world size.
  Non-blocking: these are exact, sourced quantities (not vague color); the
  source itself does not define them either (except one bracketed
  translator's footnote at Tablet VII §2, "[1 gar = 12 or 14 cubits]",
  which the candidate omits as a mid-sentence interruption — see fidelity
  notes). Reader-centered reason to leave as-is: the numbers function as
  "a very large/very long" texture rather than requiring exact conversion
  to follow the story, and inventing a conversion gloss risks overstating
  precision scholars themselves don't have.
- **Divine-assembly terms** ("Anunnaki," "Igigi," "Apsu") appear
  unglossed. Context generally signals "a group/class of gods" or "cosmic
  waters below," which is enough to follow the narrative even without a
  precise definition. Non-blocking: these are proper nouns integral to
  the mythology, not obstacles to comprehension of the plot.
- **Tablet XI's onboard-name switch** (Utnapishtim narrates in first
  person but the text names him "Atra-hasis" twice, his other epithet) may
  momentarily read as a second character to an unprepared reader. This is
  preserved because it is explicit, deliberate source content (see
  fidelity review), not a comprehension bug to be edited away — the
  epic's dual-tradition naming is part of what a reader is meant to
  encounter.
- **The riddle catalogue in Tablet XII §16-17** (the "did you see the man
  with N sons" sequence) is repetitive by design and reads clearly as the
  formal, incantatory structure it is meant to be.

No passage struck me as needing a broader rewrite pass.

## Verdict

**Substantially accessible.** The candidate is a genuine modern-English
reading edition: clear syntax, natural dialogue tags, fully modernized
verb forms, and no choppy or mechanically-chopped prose found across a
full, non-sampled read of all 253 paragraphs.

1. grimms-fairy-tales — chapter 21, paragraph 2

SOURCE ENGLISH
The two children hadn’t been able to sleep either, from hunger, and they had heard what their stepmother said to their father. Gretel wept bitter tears and said to Hansel, “It’s all over for us now.” “Hush, Gretel,” said Hansel. “Don’t upset yourself. I’ll find a way to help us.” When the old people had fallen asleep, he got up, put on his little coat, opened the door downstairs, and crept outside. The moon was shining brightly, and the white pebbles lying in front of the house glittered like silver coins. Hansel bent down and stuffed his coat pocket with as many as it would hold. Then he went back in and said to Gretel, “Take comfort, dear little sister, and sleep in peace. God won’t abandon us.” And he lay down in his bed again. At daybreak, before the sun had risen, the woman came and woke the two children. “Get up, you lazybones! We’re going into the forest to fetch wood.” She gave each of them a small piece of bread and said, “That’s for your midday meal, but don’t eat it before then, because you’ll get nothing else.” Gretel put the bread under her apron, since Hansel had the pebbles in his pocket. Then they all set out together for the forest. After they had walked a little way, Hansel stopped and looked back at the house, and he did this again and again. His father said, “Hansel, what are you staring at, and why do you keep lagging behind? Pay attention, and don’t forget how to use your legs.” “Oh, Father,” said Hansel, “I’m looking at my little white cat. She’s sitting up on the roof and wants to say goodbye to me.” The woman said, “Fool, that isn’t your cat. That’s the morning sun shining on the chimneys.” But Hansel hadn’t been looking at any cat. He had been taking one white pebble after another out of his pocket and dropping it on the road.

Månen skinnede klart, og de hvide sten, der lå foran huset, glitrede som sølvmønter. Hans bøjede sig ned og stoppede frakkelommen med så mange, som der kunne være i den. Grete græd bittert og sagde til Hans: »Nu er det ude med os.« »Shh, Grete,« sagde Hans. »Du skal ikke være så ked af det. Ved daggry, før solen var stået op, kom konen og vækkede børnene. »Op med jer, I dovne unger! Spis det ikke før, for I får ikke andet.« Grete lagde brødet under sit forklæde, for Hans havde småstenene i lommen. Så gik de alle sammen af sted mod skoven. WHY
Addresses the exact hys/græm feedback; preserves the entire paragraph, threat, religious reassurance, dawn, bread instructions and pebble deception. Restores downstairs detail.

REVIEW QUESTION
Does the sibling dialogue sound natural without making the story childish or weakening its danger?


2. grimms-fairy-tales — chapter 32, paragraph 1

SOURCE ENGLISH
Once upon a time there was a woman who was a real witch and had two daughters. One was ugly and wicked, and she loved this one because she was her own daughter. The other was beautiful and good, and she hated this one because she was her stepdaughter. The stepdaughter once had a pretty apron, and the other girl liked it so much that she grew jealous and told her mother she must and would have that apron. “Hush, my child,” said the old woman, “you’ll have it. Your stepsister has deserved to die for a long time. Tonight, when she’s asleep, I’ll come and cut her head off. Just make sure you lie on the far side of the bed, and push her well to the front.” It would have been the end of the poor girl if she hadn’t been standing in a corner at that moment and heard every word. She didn’t dare leave the house all day, and when bedtime came, the witch’s daughter got into bed first, to lie on the far side. But once she was asleep, the other girl gently pushed her to the front and took the place at the back for herself, next to the wall. In the night the old woman came creeping in, an axe in her right hand, feeling with her left to check whether someone was lying on the outside. Then she gripped the axe with both hands and cut off her own child’s head.

PREVIOUS DANISH
Der var engang en kvinde, som var en rigtig heks og havde to døtre. Den ene var grim og ond, og hende holdt hun af, fordi hun var hendes egen datter. Den anden var smuk og god, og hende hadede hun, fordi hun var hendes steddatter. Steddatteren havde engang et kønt forklæde, og den anden pige kunne så godt lide det, at hun blev misundelig og sagde til sin mor, at hun måtte og skulle have det forklæde. »Hys, mit barn,« sagde den gamle, »du skal få det. Din stedsøster har fortjent at dø for længe siden. I nat, når hun sover, kommer jeg og hugger hovedet af hende. Sørg bare for at ligge inderst i sengen og skub hende godt frem.« Det ville have været ude med den stakkels pige, hvis hun ikke havde stået i en krog i det øjeblik og hørt hvert eneste ord. Hun turde ikke gå ud af huset hele dagen, og da det blev sengetid, gik heksens datter først i seng for at ligge inderst. Men da hun var faldet i søvn, skubbede den anden hende forsigtigt frem og tog selv pladsen bagest ved væggen. Om natten kom den gamle snigende med en økse i højre hånd og følte med den venstre efter, om der lå nogen yderst. Så greb hun øksen med begge hænder og huggede hovedet af sit eget barn.

PROPOSED MODERN DANISH — DRAFT
Der var engang en kvinde, som var en rigtig heks og havde to døtre. Den ene var grim og ond, og hende elskede hun, fordi hun var hendes egen datter. Den anden var smuk og god, og hende hadede hun, fordi hun var hendes steddatter. Steddatteren havde et fint forklæde. Den anden pige kunne så godt lide det, at hun blev misundelig og sagde til sin mor, at hun ville have det forklæde, og sådan skulle det være. »Rolig, mit barn,« sagde den gamle kone. »Du skal nok få det. Din stedsøster har længe fortjent at dø. I nat, når hun sover, kommer jeg og hugger hovedet af hende. Sørg bare for at ligge inderst i sengen, og skub hende helt ud til kanten.« Det ville have været ude med den stakkels pige, hvis hun ikke netop da havde stået i et hjørne og hørt hvert eneste ord. Hun turde ikke gå ud af huset resten af dagen. Da det blev sengetid, gik heksens datter først i seng for at få pladsen inderst. Men da hun sov, skubbede den anden pige hende forsigtigt ud til kanten og lagde sig selv inderst ved væggen. Om natten kom den gamle kone snigende med en økse i højre hånd. Med den venstre mærkede hun efter, om der lå nogen yderst. Så greb hun øksen med begge hænder og huggede hovedet af sit eget barn.

WHY
Uses context-sensitive reassurance rather than the same replacement for both occurrences of hys; violence is preserved.

REVIEW QUESTION
Does 'Rolig' fit the reassurance, while the threat and sequence remain faithful?

6. the-manual — chapter 1, paragraph 1

SOURCE ENGLISH
Some things are within our control, others are not. Within our control are our opinions, impulses, desires, and aversions — in short, whatever is our own doing. Outside our control are our body, reputation, possessions, and positions of power — in short, whatever is not our own doing. What is within our control is by nature free, unhindered, unobstructed. What is outside our control is weak, dependent, subject to obstruction — it belongs to others. Remember: if you mistake what belongs to others for your own, you will be frustrated, you will grieve, you will be in turmoil, blaming gods and men alike. But if you take as your own only what truly is your own, and recognise that what belongs to others belongs to others, no one will ever compel you or obstruct you. You will blame no one, accuse no one, do nothing against your will. You will have no enemies, and nothing can harm you.

PREVIOUS DANISH
Nogle ting er inden for vores kontrol, andre er ikke. Det, der er inden for vores kontrol, er vores vurderinger, impulser, ønsker og modvilje, kort sagt alt det, vi selv gør. Det, der ikke er inden for vores kontrol, er kroppen, omdømmet, ejendelene og magten, kort sagt alt det, vi ikke selv styrer. Det, der er inden for vores kontrol, er frit af natur, uhindret og ubegrænset. Det, der ikke er inden for vores kontrol, er svagt, afhængigt og kan standses; det hører andre til. Husk derfor: Hvis du tror, at det, der hører andre til, er dit, bliver du frustreret, ked af det og oprørt, og du kommer til at bebrejde både guder og mennesker. Men hvis du kun regner det for dit eget, som virkelig er dit eget, og ser det, der hører andre til, som noget, der hører andre til, vil ingen kunne tvinge dig eller stå i vejen for dig. Du vil ikke bebrejde nogen, ikke anklage nogen og ikke gøre noget mod din vilje. Du vil ikke have fjender, og intet vil kunne skade dig.

PROPOSED MODERN DANISH — DRAFT
Nogle ting er i vores magt, andre er ikke. I vores magt er vores vurderinger, impulser, ønsker og modvilje — kort sagt alt det, vi selv gør. Uden for vores magt er vores krop, omdømme, ejendele og magtpositioner — kort sagt alt det, der ikke er vores eget værk. Det, der er i vores magt, er af natur frit og møder hverken hindringer eller begrænsninger. Det, der ligger udenfor, er svagt, afhængigt og kan hindres — det tilhører andre. Husk derfor: Hvis du tager det, der tilhører andre, for at være dit eget, bliver du frustreret, sorgfuld og urolig og giver både guder og mennesker skylden. Men hvis du kun regner det for dit eget, som virkelig er dit eget, og anerkender, at det, der tilhører andre, tilhører dem, kan ingen tvinge dig eller stå i vejen for dig. Du vil ikke give nogen skylden, ikke anklage nogen og ikke gøre noget mod din vilje. Du vil ikke have fjender, og intet vil kunne skade dig.

WHY
Preserves the full Stoic claim without softening it into general wellness advice. Candidate term policy: i vores magt.

REVIEW QUESTION
Are the distinctions clear, and are they faithful? A philosophy reviewer should check the terminology against the source tradition.

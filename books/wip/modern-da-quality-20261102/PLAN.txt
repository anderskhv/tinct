MODERN DANISH QUALITY PLAN — proposed 2 November 2026

Plan only. No code, content, availability, or deployment changes performed.
Target: 70 accepted modern-da books (62.5% of 112 registered; 65.4% of 107 not whole-book-held). Stretch: 76 (67.9% of 112). Count only complete accepted books. Coverage is by title, not words. Track word coverage separately. The Bible remains one catalogue title, not 66.
Selection is provisional, based on observed defects, length, genre coverage and cultural relevance, not reader analytics. A selected book that fails review stays hidden; substitute a reserve only after equivalent acceptance. Never lower quality to hit the count.

QUALITY STANDARD
Faithful, complete contemporary Danish for a general reader; preserve literary voice, ambiguity, arguments, imagery, humour and historically meaningful distinctions. Modern does not mean a summary, a children’s adaptation, flattened philosophy or contemporary slang.
Grimm calibration: familiar contemporary vocabulary and natural spoken dialogue, preserving fairy-tale rhythm. Flag hys/græmme and similar wording in context; no blind banned-word substitutions. Use younger-reader/read-aloud feedback for family titles, not as the sole criterion for all adult literature.
Name forms, du/De by character relationship, units, recurring terms, quotation conventions and handling of foreign-language quotations are documented for each book. Source-derived period vocabulary may remain where meaningful, with unobtrusive explanation if necessary.

WORKFLOW AND ACCEPTANCE
1. Pin the exact source edition and rights evidence; determine whether retained Danish is repairable. Work from original Danish for Niels Lyhne and Fear and Trembling. Do not back-translate those through English. For other non-English originals, document any English intermediary and verify difficult passages against the original through a competent reviewer.
2. Trial style on Grimm, Alice, The Manual, Hamlet, and a passage from Crime and Punishment. Use dialogue, narration, emotional scenes and difficult passages, not openings alone. A Danish editor calibrates naturalness and fidelity.
3. Full-book rewrite/edit. Sound material may be retained; suspect chapters are retranslated. Maintain an explicit record of unresolved questions.
4. A different reviewer compares every passage to the pinned source for omissions, additions, reversals, false friends and shifted alignment. Review corrected passages again; corrections are not self-approving.
5. Native Danish editor reads the complete final Danish text for idiom, grammar, consistency and genre-appropriate modern register. Specialist reviewer checks philosophical/poetic material where necessary. Family titles get a small read-aloud/comprehension trial.
6. Mechanical checks cover every chapter: language leakage, missing/duplicated passages, suspicious compression, formatting, titles, notes and line breaks. Paragraph equality is not semantic alignment; verify mappings. Foreign quotations are reviewed, not automatically deleted.
7. Final acceptance: no known critical/major defects; all flagged passages resolved; native final-read sign-off on exact text; independent check of edits; valid comparison mapping and reader acceptance. If native review is not staffed, status remains editorial candidate, not high-quality accepted.
8. Publish only complete books in batches of 4–6; first/middle/last reader checks on phone and desktop, Compare, reload, saved position and notes. Changed text must not reuse incompatible audio. Danish text remains separate from future narration acceptance.

PHASES AND OWNERS
Phase 0 — Editorial lead: freeze scope/style, calibrate five pilots, record hours per 10,000 words and corrections by genre. Integration owner prepares a precise visibility manifest.
Phase 1 — Editorial team: repair and re-accept the existing 18, prioritizing Grimm, Magna Carta and Middlemarch. Current visibility is not grandfathered quality acceptance.
Phase 2 — Editorial plus integration: finish the four staged replacements. Resolve Pride paragraph mapping, Jekyll source drift/emphasis, Sense emphasis, and Great Expectations source-quality gate.
Phase 3 — Editorial team: 24 additions across narrative and thought. Release in small accepted batches, not all at once.
Phase 4 — Editorial team: 24 further additions including drama and public texts. Known local defects and mapping problems are tasks, not waived requirements.
Stretch — six reserve titles only after the core standard is working.
Content owner hands over exact accepted files and evidence; a separate integration owner handles registry, availability, reader mapping and release checks. No integration is authorized by this planning document.

VISIBILITY CONTRACT
Default-hidden modern-da unless the exact edition has current acceptance. Remove unaccepted modern-da from edition menus, Compare, Danish filters, default selection and promotional availability claims. Keep the book available in other supported languages.
Preserve retained text and all saved places, quotations, highlights and notes; give existing readers an explicit recovery/unavailable message. Never silently substitute English or move coordinates.
During rollout, current enabled editions need an explicit temporary-retain or withdraw decision after severity triage; any major fidelity/completeness failure is withdrawn pending repair. By completion, only accepted modern-da is offered.
Original Danish is a separate edition category. It may be offered with correct historical labeling and verified provenance; it does not count toward the modern-da target.
Danish introductions, descriptions and chapter labels are refreshed with each accepted title. No Danish audio claim until separately verified.

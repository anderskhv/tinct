## Key blocking issues
- Room term split. Jekyll's private room is "study" in ch5 and ch8, but "cabinet" in 6.12 and in 9.3, 9.4 and 9.9. Ch9 also leaves "press" unglossed (9.3, 9.9). A listener hears "force the door of my cabinet… open the glazed press… draw out the drawer" and pictures furniture. The fix is "study" for the room and "glass-fronted cabinet" for the press, which matches 8.71 and 8.83. Out of scope: ch10 still uses "my cabinet" for the room (10.6, 10.13, 10.16, 10.22, 10.25). It needs the same change if this terminology is adopted.
- 9.10 "to no end of practical use": a modern ear hears "no end of" as "a great deal of", so the meaning flips.
- 9.27 "If you decide as before": this invents a previous decision and muddles Hyde's either/or offer.
- 7.3 "impossible to feel one without the other" garbles "to do the one without the other" (seeing Hyde and feeling repulsion). 7.6 drops the repeated "Jekyll!".
- Voice: in 8.4, "I'll be damned" puts a profane oath into Poole's mouth. The source has "I wish I may die". Otherwise Poole's servant speech ("main angry", "bible-word", "wanted something terrible"), Lanyon's horror (9.30–9.32) and Jekyll's letters (8.92, 9.2–9.8) survive well. That includes the truncated sentence in 8.92 and the source's "10th December" date, which is kept rather than corrected.
- Epithet: 8.87 turns "dead malefactor" into the neutral "dead man".
- Archaic islands: 9.3 "waiting your arrival", 9.12 "Are you come", 9.13 "bidden him enter".

Suspense and deliberate ambiguity survive. The hedges hold: "I thought, a change" and "It seems much changed". So do "him, or it", the ambiguity of the second will, and "I shall die incredulous".

Non-blocking items: British spelling slips (signaled, gotten ×3, drafts, cozily), vial/phial inconsistency, 8.60 "solemnly" changed to "steadily", 8.97 "I would say nothing" made firmer, 9.10 "version book" rendered as "ledger" (inaccurate), 9.15 "misbegotten", the 9.30 "prodigy" echo, 8.13 "ragged" added, 6.3 comparative lost, 6.11 "predecease", and 7.5 "air…air".

No glosses in ch6–9 are inaccurate or interpretive. The only inaccurate substitution is "ledger", flagged above.

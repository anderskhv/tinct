CHORUS. (The Chorus of Corinthian Ladies enters.) I have heard the daughter of Kolchis crying, the voice of a woman crushed by misery, and still she has not fallen silent. Tell us what has happened to her, old woman. Her sobbing lament travelled out from her chamber through the porch. I cannot, cannot feel happiness while suffering weighs on this household, this house I have loved so dearly.

NURSE. A home? There is no home any longer; it has disappeared. My master is enslaved to his royal bride, while my mistress wastes away all day in her room. Nothing her friends can say lets a single cooling drop of comfort fall upon her heart.

MEDEA (BEHIND THE SCENES). If only the fire of heaven's lightning would come down, come down, and burn straight through my head! What benefit is there now in staying alive? Alas, alas! If only I could finish, finish this life I hate, and throw its whole weight of suffering behind me!

CHORUS. Zeus, Earth, Light, did you hear that? The breath of this most unhappy bride is heavy with lamentation. Distraught woman, can your longing for a happiness that has vanished really make you wish for death to come closer—death, where everything ends? Do not pray for that! If your husband has turned to worship a new love and has become a stranger, do not tear your soul apart over him. Zeus will see that justice is done for you. Do not wear yourself away with excessive anguish, weeping for your husband!

MEDEA (BEHIND THE SCENES). Lady Justice, majestic Artemis, look, look at this. See the wrongs I suffer, I who bound my husband's soul with everlasting oaths so that he could never escape their curse, never escape your vengeance! May I live to see him and his bride, those two and their home together, broken into ruin, into ruin! Wretches, who dare to insult Medea when she has done them no harm! My father, my city, I left you long ago for my own destruction, destruction and disgrace, when I spilled my brother's blood along the road of my escape!

NURSE. Do you hear her words, her appeal to Themis and to Zeus, the protector of suppliants who watches over the oaths of mortal people? My mistress will not quickly put her anger aside and settle for some small revenge.

CHORUS. If only she would come outside to where we are waiting, if only she would listen to our voices, perhaps her spirit would learn to turn away from this furious anger and stop burning for revenge! May my love never become treachery; may I never prove false to my friends! Go into the house and bring your mistress out to us. Tell her that everyone here is her friend. Hurry, before some disaster overtakes the masters of this palace. Her grief is swelling like a storm, rushing towards destruction with a force nothing can resist.

NURSE. I will try. My heart has almost given up hope of persuading her, but love will make me attempt it. My queen glares at any servant who dares approach and speak, like a maddened bull or a lioness lying among her cubs. Nothing can tame her. Anyone would be right to call the ancient singers clumsy craftsmen rather than skilled poets. They composed their songs for celebrations, for the arrival of wine at a feast, when the ringing harp adds melody to life's already happy days. But no musician has ever breathed peace into the dreadful fate of human beings, into their heart-breaking anguish, either through music or through a voice joined to the harp. And yet it is these sorrows that bring death and destruction into so many homes. Surely song would do some good if it healed people's grief. Clear voices sounding over an abundant banquet are wasted: the feast itself already casts a spell, hiding our doom from us and enchanting our pain away. (The Nurse leaves).

CHORUS. I have heard the sighs in the cry of the daughter of Kolchis, her piercing anguish as she weeps for the man who trapped her with false promises and betrayed their love. Armed with the wrongs she has suffered, she reproaches Heaven, calling upon Zeus's Queen of Oaths, whose irresistible bonds drew her away and brought her across the starlit waters, where the sea-mist hangs above the Key of Pontus, to Hellas far across the limitless sea.

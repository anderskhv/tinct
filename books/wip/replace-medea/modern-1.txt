NURSE. (The Nurse of Medea's Children enters.) If only Argo had never sped between the blue Clashing Rocks towards Kolchis-land, if only the pine had never fallen under an axe in Pelion's valleys, and no oars had filled the hands of those princely heroes sent by Pelias to seek the Golden Fleece! Then my mistress Medea would never have sailed to the towers of Iolkos, her entire being trembling with love for Jason. She would never have persuaded the daughters of Pelias to kill their father. Nor would she now be living here in Corinth with her husband and children, delighting the people who welcomed her into exile, and always working for Jason's good. A household's surest protection is a wife who lives in agreement with her husband. Now everything has become hatred; their love has fallen sick. Jason has betrayed my mistress and their little children, and is marrying into royalty: his bride is the daughter of Kreon, who rules this country. Poor Medea, insulted and rejected as a wife, appeals to their sworn promises and that supreme pledge, the clasping of right hands. She calls the Gods to witness the reward Jason has given her. She lies without eating, surrendering her body to sorrow, wearing herself away in tears through all the weary hours since she learned how her husband had disgraced her. She never raises her eyes or turns her face from the ground. Friends offer advice, but she listens no more than a rock or a wave of the sea. Only sometimes she turns her pale neck and, alone, cries for the father she loved, her country and the home she abandoned to come here with the man who now treats her as worthless. Suffering has taught the poor woman what a blessing it is to have kept one's native land. She hates her little ones; the sight of them gives her no happiness. I am afraid she is planning something terrible. She has a fierce spirit that refuses to accept mistreatment. I know her, and fear she may creep silently through the house to her bed and drive a sharpened blade through her own heart, or kill the king and the man who is marrying his daughter, and bring still greater suffering on herself. She is dangerous: whoever makes an enemy of her will have a long wait before singing a song of victory. But here come her boys, finished with their racing games, knowing nothing of their mother's troubles. Young hearts do not care to dwell on sorrow. (The Children's Guardian enters with the boys).

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. Old servant, long the property of my mistress' household, why are you standing here alone beside the gates, talking to yourself about your troubles? How can Medea bear to have you leave her?

NURSE. Grey-haired keeper of Jason's boys, loyal servants always feel their masters' misfortunes in their own hearts. My grief has grown so great that I longed to come out here and tell the earth and sky what has happened to my mistress.

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. Has the poor woman still not stopped her weeping?

NURSE. Stopped! Her suffering has barely started; its middle is still far away!

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. Poor fool! If a servant may call a master that. She knows so little of the newest disaster.

NURSE. What disaster, old man? Please do not refuse to tell me.

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. Nothing. I wish I had not let that word escape me.

NURSE. No, I beg you by your beard, do not hide it from a fellow slave. If secrecy is needed, I will keep it.

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. As I came near the marble seats where the old men gather around the sacred spring of Peirênê, I heard someone talking and pretended not to listen. He said that Kreon, the ruler here, would soon expel these boys from Corinthian territory along with their mother. But I cannot say whether the story I overheard is true. I wish it were false.

NURSE. Will Jason allow his children to be treated like that, even though he and their mother are estranged?

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. His old attachments trail a long way behind his new ones in this race. This household has no friend in him.

NURSE. Then we are lost, if a new burden of misery is piled aboard before our ship has been lightened of the old one.

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. But keep quiet about it. This is no time for your mistress to learn the news; do not repeat what I have said.

NURSE. Listen, little ones, to the sort of father you have! I would curse him—but no, he remains my master. Yet towards those who love him his conduct proves him a scoundrel.

CHILDREN'S GUARDIAN. What man is any different? Have you only just discovered that everyone loves himself more than his neighbour, some for good reason, others out of greed? So their father, for the sake of a new bride, has no love for these boys.

NURSE. Go indoors, dear children; that will be best. And you, keep them as far away as possible. Do not let them near their mother in this fury. Just now I saw her glaring at them like a bull, as though she meant to hurt them. I know that anger will not die away until its lightning has struck. May she injure her enemies, never those who love her!

MEDEA (BEHIND THE SCENES). What a wretched woman I am! What misery keeps falling on my head! Ah, my pain! Ah, my pain! If only I could die!

NURSE. There, my darlings, just what I warned you of! Your mother's heart is surging! Her anger is surging too: keep out of her sight and do not come close to her. Quickly, get inside. Beware of her thoughts, a litter of wild beasts, and of her ruthless nature, which in its desperation might not spare you. Go in now, as fast as you can. We can plainly see how this cloud of grieving, rising from beginnings hidden from sight, will flare into still fiercer lightning bolts of rage. What will that proud, uncontrollable spirit dare when the spurs of injury stab it? (The Children leave with their Guardian).

MEDEA (BEHIND THE SCENES). Misery! I have endured, endured vile wrongs that deserve to wake, to wake an enormous outcry of grief! Children cursed from birth, offspring of one hated and abandoned, abandoned, go to your destruction! Perish beside your father, and let our house sink into the darkness of its doom!

NURSE. Ah, what share have the babies in their father's crimes, that your hatred should strike them down? Helpless innocents, how terribly I fear what may become of you! The tempers of royalty are frightening! Accustomed to giving orders, never taught to obey, they neither forgive nor let their quarrels sleep. Better to follow life's level road. If I cannot grow old in greatness, let me at least grow old quietly and in peace. There is no sweeter name than "The Mean"; to experience it is sweeter beyond telling. Excessive prosperity has never helped mankind. From its dangerous height the Gods, when their displeasure comes, strike with all the greater force.

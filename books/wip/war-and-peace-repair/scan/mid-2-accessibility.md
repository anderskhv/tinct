Hard/unclear:

- 9 — "...but as soon as he has done it, that action performed at a certain moment in time becomes irrevocable and belongs to history, in which it has not a free but a predetermined significance." — abstract single sentence that needs a second pass.
- 10 — "his individual life, which is the more free the more abstract its interests" — a compressed comparative clause whose relation (freer life = more abstract interests) is easy to misread on first pass.
- 12 — "The king's heart is in the hands of the Lord." — an unattributed quotation dropped mid-essay with no indication it is scripture or who is speaking it.
- 15 — "Though Napoleon at that time, in 1812, was more convinced than ever that it depended on him whether to shed (or not to shed) the blood of his peoples — as Alexander expressed it in the last letter he wrote him — he had never been so much in the grip of inevitable laws..." — one sentence carrying a parenthetical, a footnoted French phrase, and the main clause.
- 17 — "The people of the west moved eastward to slay their fellow men, and by the law of coincidence thousands of minute causes aligned and combined to produce that movement and war: complaints about the failure to observe the Continental System, the Duke of Oldenburg's wrongs, the movement of troops into Prussia..." — a single sentence built as a colon-launched list of eight-plus noun phrases; genuinely hard to hold together on one read.
- 19 — "Equally right or wrong is he who says Napoleon went to Moscow because he wanted to, and perished because Alexander desired his destruction, and he who says that an undermined hill weighing a million tons fell because the last laborer struck it for the last time with his pickaxe." — stacked parallel analogies in one sentence.

Rhythm/voice: This chapter reads as translated 19th-century essay syntax lightly modernized, not natural contemporary prose — long periodic sentences with multiple embedded clauses, colon-launched lists, and inverted constructions dominate. The ideas come through, but the sentence engineering itself is the obstacle, repeatedly.

Unexplained references: "the Continental System," "Duke of Oldenburg," "Memorandum No. 178," the unattributed Proverbs quotation — all dropped with no gloss.

Argument/story thread: Statable — Tolstoy argues that no single cause (not Napoleon's ambition, not Alexander's firmness) explains the 1812 war; it happened through the coincidence of millions of individual wills, the way an apple falls from many converging causes rather than one. But getting there requires pushing through several re-read sentences.

Anachronism/over-explanation: None — if anything the opposite problem (dense, unglossed).

Accessibility verdict: REVISE

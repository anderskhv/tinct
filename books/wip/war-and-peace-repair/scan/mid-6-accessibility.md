## Ch. 209 — Book Ten (1812), Chapter 19

Counts: clear 11 / hard 8 / unclear 0 (19 paragraphs)

Hard paragraphs:
- 3 — "just as it is clear in checkers that if I have one piece less and keep trading, I will certainly lose... he will be three times as strong as I am." Numerical analogy requires working through the math to verify the logic.
- 4 — "he had seen at Smolensk the state in which Russian towns were being left to him" Ambiguous — left by whom, in what condition, unclear without inference.
- 5 — "of all the blind tools of history, were the most enslaved and involuntary" Abstract metaphor with no explanatory cue.
- 6 — "we are still unable to accustom ourselves to the fact that for our era, such histories are meaningless" Unclear what "such histories" refers to; the link to the preceding sentence about heroic poems is not spelled out.
- 13 — "Third, as proof that the position where the battle was fought had not been planned... whereas it was actually fought on a nearly unexpected and almost unfortified spot." One very long chained sentence tracking three generals and two claims at once; requires rereading.
- 16 — "he came upon the left flank of the Russian position—at the Shevardino Redoubt—and unexpectedly moved his army across the Kolocha... and there the entire battle of the twenty-sixth of August took place." Long sentence with several shifting actors and place-shifts, requires tracking cause and effect.
- 17 — "Had Napoleon not ridden out on the evening of the twenty-fourth to the Kolocha, and had he not then ordered an immediate attack..." Long stacked counterfactual chain, hard to hold in one pass.
- 19 — "under conditions in which it was not merely unthinkable to fight for ten hours and achieve an indecisive result, but unthinkable to keep an army from complete collapse and rout." Dense double-qualifying clause at sentence's end.

Rhythm/voice: Reads as translated syntax lightly modernized rather than natural contemporary prose — long, comma-loaded sentences with multiple embedded clauses, typical of an essayistic historical digression carried over from the source rather than rebuilt in short modern sentences.

Unexplained references: Shevardino, Borodino, Utitsa, Kolocha/Voyna rivers, Novoe, Barclay de Tolly, Bagration, Smolensk, Vienna — place names and generals introduced with no orientation.

Argument/story thread: Tolstoy argues the battle of Borodino was strategically senseless for both sides and was never chosen ground — it was forced on the Russians by Napoleon's surprise seizure of the Shevardino Redoubt, and later historians invented a tidy account to hide that.

Anachronism/over-explanation: None modern-jarring; the checkers analogy over-explains its own math.

Accessibility verdict: REVISE

## Ch. 215 — Book Ten (1812), Chapter 25

Counts: clear 48 / hard 5 / unclear 1 (54 paragraphs)

Hard paragraphs:
- 4 — "Well then, you know more than anyone else, whoever that may be," said Prince Andrew. Elliptical, ironic phrasing — the meaning (no one truly understands the position) isn't immediately clear from the words alone.
- 12 — "You see, we were leaving, so he'd get it all; isn't that right, your excellency?" Ambiguous "he" (the enemy) never named, requires inference.
- 15 — "Well, say your father has a German valet... That's how it's been with Barclay." Extended analogy stretched across many clauses; requires holding several mapped terms at once.
- 22 — "in chess... a knight is always stronger than a pawn and two pawns are always stronger than one—while in war a battalion is sometimes stronger than a division and sometimes weaker than a company." Long multi-part comparison requiring careful tracking.
- 43 — "Then there would be no war because Paul Ivanovich had offended Michael Ivanovich." Two named individuals introduced with no context; unclear whether these are real characters or illustrative stand-ins (they're generic placeholders, but nothing signals that).

Unclear paragraph:
- 53 — "and suddenly he remembered how his love had ended. 'He didn't need anything like that. He neither saw nor understood anything of the kind... And I?... and he is still alive and happy!'" The pronoun "he" shifts mid-passage from Prince Andrew's own reflection to an unnamed third party (apparently a rival), with no introduction. Genuinely two possible readings of who "he" is; cannot resolve confidently from the text alone.

Rhythm/voice: Natural modern English, strong dialogue-driven rhythm; Prince Andrew's tirade reads with real passion, not choppy or archaic. The German exchange is well-handled with immediate footnoted translations.

Unexplained references: Austerlitz, Bald Hills, Tilsit, Wolzogen, Clausewitz, Barclay de Tolly, Natasha (introduced with no context), the unnamed rival in the final paragraph, "the tree of knowledge of good and evil."

Argument/story thread: The night before Borodino, Prince Andrew delivers an impassioned rant against "gentlemanly" warfare and foreign commanders, arguing only resolve — not clever strategy — wins battles; the chapter closes on a bittersweet, only half-legible memory of Natasha and an unnamed heartbreak.

Anachronism/over-explanation: None modern-jarring.

Accessibility verdict: REVISE (one genuinely unclear paragraph)

## Ch. 217 — Book Ten (1812), Chapter 27

Counts: clear 24 / hard 3 / unclear 1 (28 paragraphs)

Hard paragraphs:
- 3 — "Having heard a suggestion from Davout (now called the Prince of Eckmuhl)... the so-called Duke of Elchingen (Ney) ventured to remark..." Several generals introduced with double names/titles in quick succession, hard to track who is who.
- 8 — "the commander of the artillery of the 1st Corps, General Pernetti, with thirty cannon of Campan's division and all the howitzers of Dessaix's and Friant's divisions..." Dense cluster of unfamiliar names and unit numbers.
- 12 — "will place the howitzers of the 3rd and 8th Corps, sixteen in all, on the flanks of the battery... which will have forty guns in all directed against it." Same density of numeric/unit detail.

Unclear paragraph:

A new reader cannot tell if this is an error, a skip in time, or a calendar convention — nothing signals it.

Unexplained references: Davout/Prince of Eckmuhl, Ney/Duke of Elchingen, Poniatowski, Campan, Pernetti, Dessaix, Friant, Fouche, Sorbier, Morand, Gerard, "the Viceroy" (never named as Eugène), Tuchkov, "grapeshot," "fleches."

Argument/story thread: Tolstoy reproduces Napoleon's actual dispositions for Borodino and, order by order, shows that none of the four could be or was carried out as planned.

Anachronism/over-explanation: The unexplained August-to-September date jump reads as an outright contradiction to a reader with no outside knowledge.

Accessibility verdict: REVISE

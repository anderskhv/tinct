## Ch. 176 — Book Nine (1812) — Chapter 9

Counts: clear 13 / hard 6 / unclear 0 (19 paragraphs)

| # | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 0,3,4,5,7,8,9,12,13,14,15,16,17 | clear |
| 1,2,6,10,11,18 | hard |

Hard obstacles:
- 1: "He received Bolkonsky stiffly and coldly" — the narration silently switches from "Prince Andrew" to his surname "Bolkonsky"; a new reader can momentarily read this as a different person entering the scene.
- 2: "Besides these, there were in attendance on the Emperor without any definite assignments: Arakcheev, the former Minister of War; Count Bennigsen...; the Grand Duke Tsarevich Constantine Pavlovich; Count Rumyantsev, the Chancellor; Stein...; Armfeldt...; Pfuel...; Paulucci...; Wolzogen — and many others." — ten-plus unfamiliar names and titles delivered in one breath with no way to prioritize or retain them on first read.
- 6: "invoking the memory of Suvorov" — an allusion the text assumes the reader already recognizes and can decode as a stance-marker.
- 10: "he did in Finland" / "Bennigsen proved his incompetence as far back as 1807" — specific campaigns cited as self-evident proof, with no gloss of what happened.
- 11: "a man like Bennigsen, who proved himself in 1807 and to whom Napoleon himself gave credit" — the same unexplained 1807 reference recurs as evidence.
- 18: "This stirring of the people by their sovereign and his call to them to defend their country — the very move that was the chief cause of Russia's triumph, insofar as it was produced by the Tsar's personal presence in Moscow — was suggested to the Emperor and accepted by him as a pretext for leaving the army." — a long embedded clause praises the act as the "chief cause of Russia's triumph" while the sentence's actual point is that it was used cynically as a "pretext"; the irony only lands after a second pass.

Rhythm/voice: Reads as natural modern English — one consistent authorial voice, not choppy, not archaic-flavored — but paragraph 2's roster-dump reads like an unedited list rather than digested prose.
Unexplained references: heavy cluster of period figures and events (Suvorov, Finland 1808–09, the 1807 campaign) with no cues for an unfamiliar reader.
Argument/story thread: Followable — Prince Andrew arrives at headquarters and maps nine rival factions around the Emperor.
Anachronism/over-explanation: None noticed.

**Accessibility verdict: REVISE** (more than a few hard paragraphs, including one severe name-dump)

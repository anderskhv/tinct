## Chapter 321 — Book Fifteen (1812–13), Chapter 4

Counts: clear 10 / hard 7 / unclear 1

- Para 1 (hard): "After the encounter at Vyazma, where Kutuzov had been unable to hold back his troops... the further movement of the fleeing French and the Russians who pursued them continued as far as Krasnoe without a battle." — long lead-in before the main clause.
- Para 4 (hard): "The rapid Russian pursuit was just as destructive to our army as the French flight was to theirs." — sudden, unflagged shift to first-person "our army"; unclear whose voice this is.
- Para 5 (hard): "Kutuzov, as far as it was in his power—instead of trying to check the French movement as was desired in Petersburg and by the Russian generals—directed all his efforts..." — dash clause interrupts subject and verb.
- Para 6 (hard): "Only by following at some distance could they cut across the French zigzag path." — the pursuit-distance logic needs careful re-reading to follow.
- Para 8 (hard): "But the generals—especially the foreign ones in the Russian army—who wanted to distinguish themselves and astonish someone, and for some reason capture a king or a duke, thought that now... was the perfect time to fight" — long sentence, stacked dash clauses.
- Para 12 (hard): "this self-styled knight without fear and without reproach" — chivalric-epithet allusion given with no cue for an unfamiliar reader.
- Para 13 (unclear): "(Knight without fear and without reproach.)" — a bracketed fragment standing alone as its own paragraph, simply repeating the phrase just used, with no explanation of why it's there.
- Para 17 (hard): "blind tools of the most tragic law of necessity" — abstract historical-philosophy phrase dropped in unsupported.

Rhythm/voice: Reads as translated syntax lightly modernized rather than natural contemporary prose — long compound-complex sentences, heavy dash-interruption, and the jarring first-person "our army" aside.

## Chapter 322 — Book Fifteen (1812–13), Chapter 5

Counts: clear 9 / hard 6 / unclear 2

- Para 2 (unclear): "(History of the Year 1812. The character of Kutuzov and reflections on the unsatisfactory results of the battles at Krasnoe, by Bogdanovich.)" — a citation presented as its own free-standing paragraph with zero framing; a new reader can't tell what it is or why it's there.
- Para 3 (hard): "Such is the fate—not of great men ('grands hommes') as the Russian mind does not acknowledge them—but of those rare and always solitary individuals..." — untranslated French term dropped mid-sentence plus dense abstraction.
- Para 4 (hard): "Napoleon—that most insignificant tool of history, who never anywhere, even in exile, showed human dignity—Napoleon is the object of admiration..." — repeated name-dash-clause pattern makes the subject hard to track.
- Para 6 (hard): "Kutuzov never spoke of 'forty centuries looking down from the Pyramids'" — unexplained allusion, and the paragraph stacks several separate unglossed anecdotes (Rostopchin, Arakcheev) that must be kept straight.
- Para 8 (hard): "he alone... said that the enemy must be offered a 'golden bridge'" — idiom used with no explanation, buried in a long list-sentence.
- Para 11 (hard): "despite the assurance of generals that the battle was lost and the unprecedented fact that an army had to retreat after winning" — counterintuitive claim stated without support; needs a re-read.
- Para 12 (hard): "It is easy now to understand the significance of these events—if only we refrain from attributing to the actions of the masses aims that existed only in the heads of a dozen people" — dense historiographical argument.
- Para 17 (unclear): "To a servant, no man can be great, because a servant has his own idea of what greatness means." — genuinely two readings: that servants judge greatness by their own accurate standard, or that they simply can't perceive it.

Rhythm/voice: Essayistic and dense — reads as older philosophical/historical prose lightly modernized, not natural contemporary essay writing; repeated rhetorical patterns ("He alone said...") and embedded French terms reinforce this.

**Accessibility verdict: REVISE**

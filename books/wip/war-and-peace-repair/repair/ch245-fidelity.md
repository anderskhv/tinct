## Whole-chapter notes

- **Berg's voice.** ¶0, ¶6, ¶10, ¶13, ¶14, ¶16, ¶19 and ¶22 are one continuous comic portrait: a man who talks in official formulas while asking for a cart. The candidate renders the frame accurately but shaves the padding out of the two longest speeches (¶6 above; also ¶16, where "I wish to give her a surprise, you see" becomes the brisker "I'd love to surprise her"). The cumulative effect across packets is a Berg who is merely pompous rather than absurd. This is the main thing a corrector should look at, after ¶28.
- **The chapter's moral turn.** ¶28 (Natasha's outburst), ¶41 (the countess yielding), ¶42 (the eggs and the hen) and ¶45 (the servants' reversal) are a single movement. Three of the four carry findings, and in each case the damage is of the same kind: the source states something blunt or leaves something implied, and the candidate explains or softens it. Fixing ¶28 and ¶42 alone would recover most of the chapter.
- **Repeated words that must stay put.** "Abominable" (¶28, ¶31) is consistently rendered "vile" — acceptable, and consistent. "It's impossible" (¶33, ¶35 twice) survives intact. "The wounded" is stable throughout. "Trap" is kept (¶3, ¶47, and added at ¶54 for the phaeton passage) — note the contrast with ch 252, where the same word is translated "carriage" throughout; the two chapters should agree.
- **Names.** No violations of the CONVENTIONS table. Natasha, Sonya, Petya, Vera, Vasilich, Dunyasha, Mitenka, Yusupov all correct with stress accents stripped; no Marya/Mary confusion arises here. Two issues, both minor: "Rostóvs'" occurs twice in the source and only once in the candidate (¶46, above), and "Count Peter" at ¶54.

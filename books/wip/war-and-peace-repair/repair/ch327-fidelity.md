## Whole-chapter notes

**Ruling on the split run-ons, as asked.** The splitting is the strongest part of this candidate and is mostly done correctly. The two hardest sentences come through clean:

- **¶10** (the sentence beginning "The old man, experienced in court as well as in military affairs—") is broken into three, and every element survives with its order intact: court and military experience; chosen commander in chief in August against the sovereign's wishes; removed the Grand Duke and heir apparent; decided on his own authority and against the Emperor's will to abandon Moscow; realised his day was over, his part played, the power no longer his. No connective is invented — the new sentences open with "This was the same Kutuzov who…" and "Now he…", both of which only re-state material already in the sentence.
- **¶12** (the Chichagov sentence) is broken into four and keeps Greece, Warsaw, the Emperor, Turkey, 1811, the independent peace mission, the admission of Kutuzov's merit, the undress uniform, the dirk, the cap, the garrison report and the keys.

Three splits, however, do add something, and these are the answer to the question as put:

1. **¶2** — "It also confirmed" is a manufactured connective and verb at the seam (MODERATE).
2. **¶12** — "as he was well aware" converts a relative clause into a causal one at the seam (MODERATE).
3. **¶1** — "So much has been and still is written about the Berëzina." is a new free-standing assertion made out of the source's conditional "If so much has been…" (MINOR).

And two splits lose material rather than add it: **¶11** drops "and all that had still to be done in the realm of history" (MODERATE) and **¶6** drops the parenthesis "(which they thought were mere phrases)" (MAJOR). So: clauses did **not** all survive, names did — with the single exception of the title **Tsarevich** at ¶9 — and connectives **were** added at three seams.

- Every other proper noun and number survives: Berezina, Krasnoe, Pfuel, Petersburg, Napoleon, Kutuzov, Wittgenstein, Bennigsen, Kaluga, Constantine Pavlovich, Vilna, Chichagov, Greece, Warsaw, Turkey, Borisov, Count Tolstoy, Prince Volkonsky, Arakcheev, Semenov, Konovnitsyn, Austerlitz, Moscow, the Order of St. George of the First Class; November 29, December 7 and 11, fifty years, seven years, 1811, twice governor of Vilna.
- The "golden bridge" recurs here from ch322 ¶8. In that chapter the candidate glosses it inline; here it is left bare, as the source has it in both places. Whichever way the corrector rules, the two chapters must match, and leaving it bare in both is the reading the source supports.
- The chapter's arc — the staff's contempt (¶5–6), Kutuzov's one flash of anger (¶7–8), his withdrawal into Vilna (¶11), Chichagov's insolence (¶12–15), the Emperor's arrival, reproach and decoration (¶17–28) — is preserved in order and without invention. The damage is concentrated at ¶6, where the narrator's stance towards Kutuzov inverts, and it matters because every later paragraph depends on the reader taking Kutuzov's side against the staff.

Fidelity verdict: REVISE

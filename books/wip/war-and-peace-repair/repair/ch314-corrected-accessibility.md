## Obstacles

**Paragraph 2 — hard**
> "From this starting point, the succeeding terms of the progression could be calculated mathematically."

"Terms of the progression" is mathematical phrasing (as in a numeric sequence) dropped in without explanation. A reader without that background can infer roughly what's meant — the rate of loss was predictable — but has to work for it rather than reading it straight.

## Whole-chapter notes

**Rhythm and voice.** Consistent single narrative-essay voice throughout, including the shift into Berthier's quoted letter, which is clearly marked as a document and doesn't break the surrounding tone. Reads as natural, continuous prose, not as short choppy sentences or stiff translated syntax.

**Unexplained references.** Place names given as a bare itinerary — Vyazma, Smolensk, Berezina, Vilna, Orsha — with no orientation for a reader unfamiliar with the campaign's geography. "The Guards" is named as a unit without explanation. Berthier is introduced only as writing "to his Emperor," with no stated role. The titles "Prince of Eckmuhl" and "King of Naples" are given as honorifics for unnamed men, without saying who holds them.

**Argument or story thread.** In two sentences: the chapter uses casualty numbers and a French marshal's own report to show the Grande Armée physically disintegrating on the retreat from Moscow, then shows Napoleon and his entourage still going through the motions of command and ceremonial titles even though nothing they order is actually being carried out. The thread is clear and doesn't get lost anywhere in the chapter.

**Anachronism or over-explanation.** None found. No modern idiom appears; the numerical/statistical framing ("melted away... at the same rate," "terms of the progression") fits the analytical tone of the surrounding narrative rather than reading as anachronistic.

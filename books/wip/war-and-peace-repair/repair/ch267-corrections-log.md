## Paragraph index 0 (¶1)
Finding: MODERATE ¶1 — restore present-tense standing law: "so much transcend...they always prevent" (was past-tense "so far outweighed...prevented")
Before: Half of Russia had been conquered, the inhabitants were fleeing to distant provinces, and one levy after another was being raised for the defense of the fatherland. It is natural for those of us who did not live in those days to imagine that in such a time, all Russians, from greatest to least, were solely engaged in sacrificing themselves, saving their fatherland, or weeping over its downfall. The tales and descriptions of that time without exception speak only of the self-sacrifice, patriotic devotion, despair, grief, and heroism of the Russians. But it was not really so. It appears so to us because we see only the general historical interest of that time and do not see all the personal human interests that people had. Yet in reality those personal interests of the moment so far outweighed the general interests that they always prevented the public interest from being felt or even noticed. Most of the people at that time paid no attention to the general course of events but were guided only by their private interests, and they were precisely the people whose activities in that period were most useful.

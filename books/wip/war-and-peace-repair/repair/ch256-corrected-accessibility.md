## Obstacles

**Paragraph 3 — unclear.**
> "...the idea that had previously occurred to him of the hidden meaning he had found in his own name set against Bonaparte's more than once vaguely presented itself. But the idea that he, L'russe Besuhof, was destined to set a limit to the power of the Beast was as yet only one of those fancies that often passed through his mind and left no trace."

Two separate problems compound here. First, "L'russe Besuhof" is left untranslated with no gloss — a reader who doesn't already know French cannot parse it as a name/title at all. Second, "the hidden meaning he had found in his own name set against Bonaparte's" and "the power of the Beast" are presented as settled facts with zero explanation of what that hidden meaning is or why Napoleon is "the Beast." I could not say with confidence what claim is actually being made — only that Pierre believes something mystical connects his name to Napoleon's downfall.

**Paragraph 4 — hard.**
> "When, having bought the coat merely to take part among the people in the defense of Moscow, Pierre had met the Rostovs and Natasha had said to him: 'Are you remaining in Moscow?... How wonderful!' the thought flashed into his mind that it really would be a good thing..."

The main clause ("the thought flashed into his mind") is separated from its opening "When" by a full embedded scene (buying the coat, meeting the Rostovs, Natasha's quoted line). I had to re-read to find which clause the sentence was actually building toward.

**Paragraph 5 — hard.**
> "The next day, focused solely on not sparing himself and not lagging behind them, Pierre went to the Three Hills gate."

"Them" has no antecedent in this paragraph or the one before it — it isn't clear who Pierre is trying to keep pace with (soldiers? volunteers?). The reader has to guess.

**Paragraph 7 — hard.**
> "...when he had suddenly felt that wealth, power, and life—all that men so painstakingly acquire and guard—if it has any worth, has so only by reason of the joy with which it can all be renounced."

"Has so only by reason of" is an archaic, compressed construction that needs a second pass to untangle into plain sense.

**Paragraph 8 — hard.**
> "...the feeling which causes a man to do things that from an ordinary point of view are insane, as if to test his personal power and strength, affirming the existence of a higher, nonhuman standard of life."

The closing abstraction ("affirming the existence of a higher, nonhuman standard of life") is only loosely tied back to the concrete examples earlier in the sentence (a recruit drinking away his money, a drunk man smashing glasses); connecting the two takes deliberate effort.

## Whole-chapter notes

**Rhythm and voice.** Reads as sustained literary prose in one voice — long, subordinate-clause-heavy sentences for Pierre's interiority, short plain sentences and dialogue for the Makar Alexeevich scene. It does not read as choppy modern paraphrase; the register shift between reflection and action is deliberate and controlled.

**Unexplained references.** "Joseph Alexeevich" (referred to only as a deceased figure whose house/books Pierre is minding — his identity and importance are assumed, not established here); "the battle of Borodino"; "Three Hills gate"; "Sloboda Palace"; the 1809 Vienna assassination attempt (given enough in-line detail to follow, so not an obstacle); and, most seriously, the untranslated "L'russe Besuhof" and the unglossed "power of the Beast" in paragraph 3.

**Argument or story thread.** The chapter is followable as a whole despite paragraph 3: a half-deranged, grieving Pierre has secretly resolved to stay in occupied Moscow, disguised, in order to assassinate Napoleon; his resolve is derailed when the household's drunk manservant, Makar Alexeevich, seizes his pistol and causes a violent scene just as French soldiers arrive at the door. I lost the thread only inside paragraph 3, where the "hidden meaning"/"Beast" material is asserted without support.

**Anachronism or over-explanation.** No modern idiom jars. If anything the chapter under-explains rather than over-explains — the numerological name business in paragraph 3 needed more scaffolding, not less.

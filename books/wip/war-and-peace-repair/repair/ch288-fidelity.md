## Gloss, French and sequence rulings (requested)

- **"Rostopchín, the governor of Moscow" (p1)** — *allowed, logged MINOR, with a reservation*. The gloss is accurate to the novel and close to inferable here: Napoleon's complaint is that Rostopchín "had managed affairs badly in Moscow", which only a man responsible for Moscow can be accused of, and p3 has Napoleon burning Rostopchín's houses as punishment. It is still a fact this chapter does not state, so it sits at the outer edge of "supports it and adds no claim" — unlike ch 280's "Murat, the French cavalry commander", which the chapter supports not at all. Reservation: as placed it produces "the governor of Moscow, had badly mismanaged things in Moscow". Proposal (corrector decides): "that Moscow's governor, Rostopchin, had managed affairs badly".
- **"à la maraude" (p8, p9)** — *handling accepted in form, rejected in substance*. In form: the French is retained inline, the asterisk is kept, and the footnote slot at p9 survives as its own paragraph, so alignment with original-en and modern-da is intact — CONVENTIONS §4's hard requirement is met. Two deviations: (a) §3 restricts inline French to places where the wording itself is the point, and this is narration, not the speech §2 covers — the conventions do not actually rule on narrative French with a footnote, so this should go to the French pass rather than be decided by a drafter; (b) the slot gains parentheses, "* (As looters.)", but §4 reserves parentheses for slots whose footnote is a *gloss* ("An esaul is a captain of Cossacks"); "As looters" is a translation of the French, so it should stay "* As looters." In substance the handling is not acceptable: to make room for the inline gloss "to forage", the source's purpose clause "to obtain provisions for themselves" was deleted (see p8 above). The French was preserved at the cost of the English.
- **"Maison de ma Mère (House of My Mother)" (p16)** — *allowed, no finding*. This is precisely the case CONVENTIONS §3 provides for: the wording itself is the point, because these are the words physically inscribed on the buildings, so the French is retained inline "immediately followed by the English". The translation is accurate, it adds no claim, the source has no footnote here so no slot is affected, and no paragraph is added. The only open question is typographic (parentheses versus italics or a comma), which the French pass should settle for the edition as a whole.
- **Forged-currency and relief sequence (p16)** — *matches the source order exactly*. Source: philanthropy announced → the inscription → the Foundling Hospital and Tutólmin → "Then, as Thiers eloquently recounts, he ordered his soldiers to be paid in forged Russian money which he had prepared:" → quotation: relief distributed to those burned out → but food too precious for foreigners, mostly enemies → so money instead → paper rubles distributed. The candidate runs the same eight beats in the same order, keeps the colon and the attribution to Thiers in the same place, and keeps the causal chain intact (forged money prepared → relief given → food withheld → rubles handed out), so the irony still lands: the relief is paid in the counterfeit the previous sentence described. Nothing is reordered and nothing is moved into or out of the quotation. The two defects inside the quotation are wording, logged above ("Ennobling", "he had relief distributed"), not sequence.

## Whole-chapter notes

- Sentence splits were checked individually: p1 (one sentence → two at "Having written a letter"), p7 (the single cumulative period → a colon plus a semicolon series), p13 (three splits, including the peasants' address and the market sentence), p16 (none). No clause is lost *in* a split — every drop logged above is a deletion inside a retained sentence. The two MAJOR findings at p13 are of this kind: "kept out in the fields" was rewritten, not divided, and "Respond, therefore, to his benevolent intentions" was cut from a sentence the candidate otherwise keeps whole. The p7 recast preserves all five of the proclamation's demands and all five clauses of the closing exhortation.
- The chapter is a ledger of Napoleon's decrees, and its comedy is cumulative: every entry announces an intention, and the reader knows none of it happened. Two things carry that — the "With regard to…" anaphora and the flat reporting voice. The anaphora is broken throughout (logged above), and the flat voice is broken once, at p3, where scare quotes on "scoundrel" tell the reader what to think. Both are style-level losses, but this is a chapter whose meaning is almost entirely carried by style.
- The two proclamations (p6–p7, p13–p14) are the longest stretches and the ones where content is actually lost. Five of the eight MODERATE findings and both MAJOR findings fall inside them, and p13 in particular loses a category from condition (1), a condition from (3), "and horses" from (4), "the products of the soil" from the markets sentence, and "under any pretense" from (2). The numbered conditions are exactly where a proclamation cannot be compressed.
- The candidate introduces 14 em dashes where the source has none, mostly to fold subordinate clauses into parentheticals. Several of the drops logged above happen at those seams (p8 "—à la maraude *—", p13 "—listen!", p13 "—one on Mokhovaya Street"), so the dash habit and the clause loss are related.
- "Inhabitants" → "Citizens" is applied consistently (p5, p13, p14) but it erases the source's own distinction between inhabitants and "fellow citizens" (p7). "Paternal" → "fatherly" is applied consistently at p6 and p14. Good.
- Every proper noun, number and place survives: Sabastiani (respelled, see above), Murat, Kutúzov, Yákovlev, Alexander, Rostopchín, Tutólmin, Thiers, Krémlin, Mokhováya Street, the Provision Market, Foundling Hospital, Maison de ma Mère; two commissaries general, twenty commissaries, Sunday and Wednesday, Tuesdays and Saturdays, the five numbered clauses.
- Conventions: stress accents dropped, straight double quotes (the Thiers quotation correctly converted from curly), unspaced em dashes, both footnote-slot paragraphs preserved. Compliant apart from the p0 respelling and the p9 parentheses.
- Paragraph ratios: p8 at 0.65 and p13 at 0.75 are the two below the 0.75 guideline in substance; p10, p11 and p17 fall low only because of grammatical tightening with no content loss. Chapter 0.81.

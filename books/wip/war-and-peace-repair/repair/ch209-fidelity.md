## Whole-chapter notes

- **Ruling on ¶5 "such histories" → "history written in that heroic mode".** This is the source's referent, not an interpretation. Maude's only available antecedent is "model heroic poems in which the heroes furnish the whole interest of the story", and the source itself has already shifted the noun from *poems* to *histories* — that shift is the sentence's whole move. Making the mode explicit adds no claim. It is marginally more definite ("that **same** heroic mode"), hence MINOR, not a blocker.
- **Ruling on ¶3 passive→active.** Split verdict. The agent-explicitation ("the Russians were leaving") is legitimate: Maude's passive admits one agent only. The rest of the rewrite is not: "as he advanced" is invented, and the dative "to him" is dropped. It is also a regression from the baseline text at this point, which read "were being left to him". MODERATE.
- **Argument shape.** The give/accept frame (¶1 opening question → "Napoleon offered and Kutúzov accepted" → ¶4 "In giving and accepting battle") survives except at its first appearance; restoring "given and accepted" at ¶1 would close the frame.
- **Running terms.** "position" is held consistent; "highroad" is rendered "highway" throughout; "unentrenched" → "unfortified" consistently (¶12, ¶18). Fine.
- **Splits.** ¶12, ¶15, ¶16 and ¶18 are split into shorter sentences. Every clause, condition ("first… second… third"), number, name and rhetorical question survives the splits, with one exception: "for the Russians" at ¶15.
- **Numbers and names.** All survive: thirteen hundred miles; sixteen/fourteen/thirteen pieces; five to six, one to two; 100,000 / 120,000 / 50,000; six thousand men; the 24th, 25th, 26th; 1812; ten hours. Russian stress accents correctly dropped (Shevardino, Borodino, Kutuzov, Miloradovich, Utitsa, Semenovsk, Gridneva, Valuevo, Novoe, Kolocha, Voyna, Smolensk); Barclay de Tolly, Bagration, Poniatowski, Uvarov per the conventions table.

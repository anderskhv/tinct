## Gloss and tag rulings

- **"golden bridge" (p8)** — "about the golden bridge, the old idea of leaving a beaten enemy an open road rather than cornering it": *allowed*. Every element is determined by the chapter itself: Kutuzov's own question two sentences earlier is "Why fight, why block the road…?"; p10 shows the men "trying to bar the road"; p9 shows the generals wanting "to cut off"; the enemy is "the dying beast" of the same sentence and "a third of their army has melted away". "The old idea" is supported by "drawing from his aged wisdom". The gloss states a category the source supports and adds no claim of its own. Length is the only caution: it is the longest interpolation in the four chapters.
- **"blank sheet of paper" (p9)** — the added "—their way of telling him nothing": *not supported; the candidate chose the reading*. The source narrates the gesture and stops: "by way of reporting their intention to Kutúzov they sent him a blank sheet of paper in an envelope." Nothing in the context fixes what the blank sheet means. It reads at least three ways — insolence toward a commander whose orders they intend to ignore, a report that deliberately reports nothing, or mock compliance — and the irony lies in "by way of reporting their intention", where the blank sheet *is* the report. The candidate selects one reading and states it as fact, which is both an added explanation and a loss of the source's ambiguity. Logged MODERATE; proposal: delete the clause and let the sentence end at "in an envelope".

## Whole-chapter notes

- The chapter is built on two extended physical figures — the promised land / the twenty-five-mile stage (p0–p2), and gravity, melting snow and decomposition (p3–p5) — which then explain Kutuzov's restraint (p6–p11). The figures survive, but the paragraph that carries the weight of the second one, p4, is the most compressed in the four chapters reviewed and has lost the clause that ties it back to the surrender argument.
- The narrator's first person plural ("what we say now", "we are told", "our men") runs through p8–p10. Two instances are kept and one is converted to "the Russians", so the voice is no longer consistent across the packet boundary.
- The verb list at p6 ("to cut off, to seize, to capture, and to overthrow") is deliberately answered at p11 ("did not cut off or overthrow anybody"). The candidate renders the same verb two different ways, so the answer no longer lands.
- Person shifts from third ("a man … he") to second ("you") in p0–p1 and back to third afterwards. It reads naturally on its own terms but is a voice decision worth settling once for the essayistic chapters rather than per paragraph.
- Names and diacritics follow CONVENTIONS (Kutuzov, Smolensk, Vyazma, Konovnitsyn, Ermolov, Miloradovich, Platov); no name-variant defects.

Fidelity verdict: REVISE

## Whole-chapter notes

**Rulings on the four glosses, as asked:**

1. **Bogdanovich → "the historian Bogdanovich"** — PERMITTED. "Historian" is a category, and the paragraph it sits in is the citation of a history of 1812 attributed to him; no claim is added. Recorded as MINOR only because it is an addition. The parenthetical form of the slot is correct for a gloss footnote and matches the baseline.
2. **Madame de Staël → "the writer Madame de Stael"** — PERMITTED, narrowly. "Writer" is a category, not a fact about her career, and the sentence's company (letters, novels) makes it unsurprising. But unlike Bogdanovich the source states nothing about her, so this is the outer limit of the rule. The corrector may prefer to drop it.
3. **Rostopchin → "Count Rostopchin, the governor of Moscow"** — NOT PERMITTED, MODERATE. An office is not a category, and the source does not supply it. It is also the load-bearing fact of the anecdote — it is *because* he governed Moscow that he reproaches Kutuzov for its loss — so the gloss does the paragraph's work for the reader instead of letting Tolstoy's flat juxtaposition do it.
4. **"golden bridge" → "a clear route left open for retreat"** — NOT PERMITTED, MODERATE. This is an interpretation of a metaphor, not a gloss of a term, and the source deliberately leaves Kutuzov's sayings unexplained; ch327 ¶6 lists the same phrase among the "phrases" the generals mocked, and explaining it here flattens both passages.

**The final aphorism, as asked.** "To a lackey no man can be great, for a lackey has his own conception of greatness." The candidate's reading of **"lackey"** is correct and is a genuine repair: the baseline had "servant", which loses the contempt in Maude's word and the echo of the proverb about the valet. Keep it. What remains wrong is inherited from the baseline: "his own conception of **what greatness means**" turns a conception *of greatness* into a conception of the word's meaning, and the three added syllables break the epigram's symmetry (lackey / great / greatness). MINOR. *Proposal: "…for a lackey has his own conception of greatness."*

- The chapter is built on exclusivity — "he alone", "only he", "this same Kutuzov", "alone and against everyone else". Three separate findings erode it: the dropped "only" (¶10), the added "supposed" (¶11) which converts irony into assertion, and the reordering at ¶4 which puts the historians' verdict before the evidence that refutes it. Together they change the argument from demonstration to declaration.
- All proper nouns, place names, dates and the numbered threefold end survive: Krasnoe, Berezina, Borodino, Vilna, Austerlitz, Moscow, Napoleon, Bogdanovich, Madame de Staël, Rostopchin, Yauza, Arakcheev, Ermolov, Lauriston, Pfuel's absence noted only as not applicable, Tarutino, Vyazma, "Patience and Time", "forty centuries looking down from the Pyramids", 1811/1812/1813. The only lost naming is "Highest Authorities" (¶1).
- Diacritic handling (Stael, Berezina) matches the baseline and is left alone pending the CONVENTIONS §Diacritics decision; not a finding.

## Whole-chapter notes

- **French convention, explicit ruling.** Applied half-way. Main paragraph ¶12: correct — translated inline, cue **(in French)** placed after the styling verb, once in the paragraph, no bracket tag. Footnote slot ¶13: incorrect — it must carry `* Chevalier sans peur et sans reproche.` This slot is a translation, not a gloss, so the parenthesis form permitted for gloss slots does not apply to it. Note that the baseline had neither the cue nor the French; the candidate improved the main line and left the slot as it found it. The quote marks around the epithet should also be double.
- **Cut off / break up.** "to cut off" and "cutters-off" run through this Book as Tolstoy's mocking refrain. The candidate keeps it correctly here at ¶9 and ¶12 ("cut off"), which is worth preserving when the corrector touches ch327 ¶12, where the same refrain is mistranslated.
- A pattern of typographic irony-marking: the candidate adds quotation marks around "presented" (¶15, twice) and "grand" (¶18) where the source has none. Tolstoy's irony in these chapters works by flat assertion; flagging it changes the narrator's manner.
- The argument's shape survives: exhaustion of the pursuer (¶2–4) → Kutuzov's aim of shortening marches (¶5–6) → the generals' appetite for battle (¶7–9) → Krasnoe as proof (¶10–16) → posterity's verdict (¶17–18). The MAJOR drop at ¶17 falls inside the list of charges that sets up the final paragraph, so the deletion is felt at the close.
- Names and numbers all survive except the dropped Linen Factories: Vyazma, Krasnoe, Tarutino, Vilna, Miloradovich, Toll, Prince Eugene of Wurttemberg, Napoleon, Kutuzov; twenty-seven miles, five thousand, a hundred prisoners, a hundred thousand, fifty thousand, sixteen thousand, twenty-six thousand, three days. "Wurttemberg" without the umlaut matches the baseline, which CONVENTIONS §Diacritics leaves in place until the proposal is decided; not a finding.

## Explicit rulings requested

- **p1, the "smaller"/"larger" ball labels — the candidate decided them.** The source determines exactly one thing about the two balls: which has the greater momentum. "A ball recoils after colliding with another having a greater momentum" makes the second ball (the invasion) the one with greater momentum, and the candidate gets that relation right. It determines nothing about size, and momentum is not size — a smaller ball can carry the greater momentum. "Smaller ball", "a larger one" and "that larger ball, the invasion" are three additions to the figure, and the third also replaces Maude's own phrase "the ball of invasion". **Finding stands (MODERATE).** Proposal, corrector decides: "as inevitably as a ball recoils after striking another that carries greater momentum. And with equal inevitability the ball of the invasion, which had advanced with such momentum, rolled on…".
- **p5, the framing added before the inverse-square phrase — not supported.** The source has "its own momentum now seemingly increased in inverse proportion to the square of the distance from its aim": one hedged claim about this army. The candidate's "growing, it seemed, the way a force grows in inverse proportion to the square of the distance from its goal" inserts a general law as the standard of comparison. The hedge survives ("it seemed") but the structure changes from predication to simile, and the interpolated law is asserted flatly. **Finding stands (MODERATE).**
- **p8, "General Miloradovich" — not supported by the source edition.** Verified across the whole Maude text: 21 occurrences, none with the rank. **MINOR finding; the corrector may keep it as a reader gloss, but it is an addition, not a neutral one, since the chapter's other officials are named by office ("the commissary general", "the chief of the hospitals").**
- **p8, "the Drissa camp" — allowed.** "The Drissa camp" is Maude's own recurring phrase for this referent (9 occurrences: ch 177 p2, ch 178 p1 twice, p5, p6, p11, p16, ch 191 p7, p12; plus "the camp at Drissa" twice in ch 176), and "At Drissa" in this list means the period the army spent in that camp. The gloss adds no claim. **No finding.**
- **p8, "the Shevardino Redoubt" — allowed.** Maude writes "the Shevárdino Redoubt" 18 times, and ch 209 p0 fixes the date: "On the twenty-fourth of August the battle of the Shevárdino Redoubt was fought." The candidate's "on August 24 at the Shevardino Redoubt" names exactly what the source's "on the twenty-fourth of August at Shevárdino" means. **No finding.**

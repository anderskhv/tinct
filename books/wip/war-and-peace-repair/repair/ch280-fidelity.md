## Gloss rulings (requested)

- **"Murat, the French cavalry commander" (p2)** — *not allowed as written*. The source chapter establishes nothing about Murat beyond the fact that he could have lost sight of the Russians; the identification is imported from outside the chapter, so it is an added claim rather than a cue to something the source states. It is also loose against Maude: at this point in the novel Murat commands the vanguard pursuing the Russian army (and is elsewhere "the King of Naples"), not "the French cavalry" as such. Logged MODERATE. Proposal (corrector decides): drop the gloss, or use a form the text supports — "If Murat, pursuing them, had not lost sight of the Russians".
- **"the Russian generals Bennigsen and Barclay" (p2)** — *allowed, with one reservation*. The sentence itself establishes that these two advised the Russian army at Krásnaya Pakhrá to give battle, so "Russian" and their standing to advise are supported by the source and the gloss adds no new claim. "Generals" is a rank the source does not state here, and Bennigsen appears two chapters later (ch 282) as Chief of Staff. Logged MINOR only. Proposal: "as Bennigsen and Barclay, on the Russian staff, advised", or simply leave the names bare — by the ruling above they need less help than Murat, not more.

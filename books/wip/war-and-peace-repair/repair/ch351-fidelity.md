## Gloss and tag rulings (explicit)

- **The narrator aside identifying Tatarinova, Krüdener and Eckartshausen as mystics** (¶10) — **ADDED; NOT IN THE SOURCE — MAJOR.** I checked the source paragraph and its neighbours: the source has only Denisov's speech and "he cried". There is no aside, no footnote slot for one (¶22 and ¶39 are the chapter's only slots, and both belong to French phrases), and the paragraph counts match, so the sentence lives inside Denisov's paragraph. It is an invented sentence of substance, and it additionally leaks Denisov's impediment into narration.
- **Magnitsky as "Arakcheev's ally"** (¶21) — **NOT A GLOSS AT ALL — MAJOR.** It does not explain a term; it deletes a name from a list and replaces it with an invented relation.
- **Military Settlements** (¶24) — **NOT ALLOWED — MODERATE.** The definition is accurate history but is not a category tag on a name; it is a claim about what the institution did, unsupported by the source, inserted into dialogue.
- **Pugachev** (¶32) — **NOT ALLOWED — MAJOR.** Not source-supported, adds a claim, and the claim is wrong. If any gloss is wanted here, the defensible form is a bare category tag on the name (*proposal:* "some new Pugachev—some peasant rebel—", corrector decides), but the fully faithful option is to leave the name as the source leaves it.

The parenthesis is present, still parenthetical, still about the same unmade claim, and the cleft preserves the emphasis on *Russia*. "then" → "yet" keeps the same irony (that they would later). No finding.

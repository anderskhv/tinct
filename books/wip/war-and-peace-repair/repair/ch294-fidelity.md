## Gloss and tag rulings

- **Lauriston (p0)** — "brought by Lauriston, an earlier French envoy": *allowed*. The source edition supports every element: ch 281 p6 "the sending of Lauriston to Kutúzov's camp with overtures for peace", ch 281 p12 "Lauriston's mission", ch 322 p7 "Lauriston's proposal of peace"; this chapter opens "another envoy came", which entails a previous one, and the letter is Napoleon's, so the envoy is French. The gloss states a category the source supports and adds no claim. (Cosmetic only: "the earlier peace letter … an earlier French envoy" repeats "earlier" in one clause.) The added word "peace" describing the earlier letter is also supported — Kutuzov's reply to both is that there can be no question of peace.
- **Forminsk (p1)** — "the village of Forminsk": *not supported*. The source edition names Forminsk eight times (ch 294 p1×2, p3, p5×2, p6×2; ch 295 p7) and never calls it a village; it reserves "the village of" for Aristovo in this very chapter (p5). The tag states a settlement type the source does not give. Logged MINOR; proposal: delete "the village of".
- **Austerlitz (p2)** — "from the Battle of Austerlitz to 1813": *allowed*. The source edition narrates Austerlitz as a battle at length, and the next sentence is battlefield detail ("At Austerlitz he remained last at the Augezd dam"). Category only, no added claim.
- **Bagration (p2)** — "General Bagration": *not supported as written*. Bagration is a general in fact, but the source edition's form of address for him is "Prince Bagratión" (47 occurrences) and it never writes "General Bagratión" (0). CONVENTIONS says forms of address follow Maude and the character cards. Logged MINOR; proposal: "Bagration" unchanged, or "Prince Bagration".
- **Murat (p5)** — "Marshal Murat": *not supported*. The source edition never writes "Marshal Murat" (0 occurrences); its own title for him is "the King of Naples" (8 occurrences). The tag assigns a rank the source does not use for him. Logged MINOR; proposal: "Murat's position" unchanged.

## Whole-chapter notes

- The chapter is one sustained argument: the unnoticed man is the essential part, the noticed one is the shaving in the machine. The argument survives the restructuring, including the split of the long p2 sentence into two ("Yet we find him…"), and the mechanism simile in p4 is intact.
- The narrator's "to us / our" device is the chapter's rhetorical signature ("described to us", "we are told", "our left flank", and in ch 297 "describe to us"). The candidate drops it three times in p2 while keeping "We hear about" in p3, so the voice becomes intermittent.
- "Undiscerning" is used twice in p2 as a deliberate echo (the reputation, then the ironic repetition of the reputation). The candidate renders it "lacking insight" and then "unperceptive", so the callback disappears.
- Four identifying tags were added across the chapter (Lauriston, village, General, Marshal); three of them state things the source does not. This is a pattern for the corrector, not four independent slips.
- Names and diacritics follow CONVENTIONS throughout (Kutuzov, Tarutino, Dorokhov, Dokhturov, Malo-Yaroslavets, Ermolov, Bolkhovitinov, Seslavin, Broussier, Figner); no name-variant defects.

## ¶2
Before: "I consider it my duty to report to Your Majesty on the condition of the various corps I have had the opportunity to observe during different stages of the last two or three days' march. They are nearly disbanded. Scarcely a quarter of the soldiers remain with the standards of their regiments; the others wander off in every direction hoping to find food and escape discipline. In general, they all look to Smolensk as the place where they hope to recover. During the last few days many soldiers have been seen throwing away their cartridges and their weapons. In such circumstances, whatever your ultimate plans may be, Your Majesty's interests require that the army be reassembled at Smolensk and, first of all, freed from noncombatants—dismounted cavalry, unnecessary baggage, and artillery equipment no longer proportionate to our present forces. The soldiers, worn out by hunger and fatigue, need these supplies as well as a few days' rest. Many have died recently on the road or at camp. This state of affairs is growing worse every day and raises the fear that, unless prompt measures are taken, the troops will be beyond control in the event of a battle."
After: "I consider it my duty to report to Your Majesty on the condition of the various corps I have had the opportunity to observe during different stages of the last two or three days' march. They are nearly disbanded. Scarcely a quarter of the soldiers remain with the standards of their regiments; the others wander off by themselves in different directions hoping to find food and escape discipline. ## ¶5
Before: "They all went without knowing where or why they were going. Still less did that supposed genius, Napoleon, know it, for no one was issuing any orders to him.

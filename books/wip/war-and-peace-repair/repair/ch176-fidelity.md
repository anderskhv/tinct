## Whole-chapter notes

- **The taxonomy term.** The chapter is built on one running noun: "party" (21 occurrences, including "parties"). The candidate substitutes "faction" 16 times, keeps "party" once (¶15, "the party of older, reasonable men") and uses "group" elsewhere. Two consequences cross packet boundaries: the source's deliberate variation ("Of a fourth opinion", "The eighth and largest group") is levelled into the same word as the numbered parties, and ¶15's surviving "party" now reads as a different thing from the eight "factions" that precede it. Whichever term the corrector picks, it needs to be applied as consistently as the source applies its own, and ¶8's "opinion" and ¶13's "group" should keep their distinct nouns.
- **The list of nine.** The nine-part enumeration is the chapter's spine; each party is defined by one or two clauses. Two of those definitions are damaged (¶13's fourth type, ¶15's ninth party), which is why both are MAJOR rather than local losses.
- **Names.** No violations of the CONVENTIONS table: Prince Andrew throughout (10 uses, all correct), Rostov at ¶12, Kuragin at ¶1; no "Andrei", "Bolkonsky", "Nikolai". Stress accents correctly stripped. The one surname use ("Bolkónski", ¶1) was replaced by "Prince Andrew" — permitted but noted. "Volkonsky" and "emigre" are the only spelling questions and both are for the corrector.
- **Accuracy of the three named glosses.** Suvorov: category acceptable, "renowned" is the candidate's own evaluation. Finland: accepted, category only. 1807: factually correct in both places and adds no claim, but both are inserted inside quoted speech; the corrector should decide whether cues may be placed inside quotation marks at all, since the same question arises in ch 252 (the broadsheet).
- Compression is real but within tolerance: no paragraph falls below 85% of its source word count (¶15 is the lowest at 84%, and that shortfall is the MAJOR dropped clause).

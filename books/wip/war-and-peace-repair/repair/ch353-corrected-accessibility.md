## Obstacles

**Paragraph 2** — "From the moment they were alone, and Natasha came to him with her eyes wide open and happy, quickly grabbing his head and pressing it to her chest, saying, 'Now you're all mine, mine! You won't get away!' — from that moment this conversation began, defying every law of logic..." The sentence opens with "From the moment," delays its main clause across a long quoted interruption, then resumes with a repeated "from that moment" — a resumptive construction that is unusual enough in English to require a second pass to see the two phrases are matching bookends of one sentence.

**Paragraph 16** — "...my idea is so clear and so simple. You see, I'm not saying we should oppose this or that... What I say is: 'Join hands, all of you who love what is right, and let there be a single banner — that of active virtue.' Prince Sergey is a fine man, and clever." "Prince Sergey" is introduced with no context at all — no role, no relation to anything said before — leaving a reader unsure whether this is a new figure or another name for someone already mentioned.

**Paragraph 17** — "'Can a man so important, so necessary to society, also be my husband? How did that happen?' She wanted to put this doubt to him. 'Now who could really say whether he was cleverer than everyone else?' she asked herself..." Both quoted lines are Natasha's unspoken internal thought, not things she says aloud, but the quotation marks match the format used elsewhere in the chapter for actual spoken dialogue — "She wanted to put this doubt to him" (future tense) is the only signal that the first quotation hasn't been said yet, and it's easy to misread these as things she has just said.

## Whole-chapter notes

**Rhythm and voice.** Reads as natural, intimate modern prose for almost the entire chapter — short exchanges, clear turn-taking, one consistent narratorial voice describing the couple's private shorthand. Only paragraph 2's resumptive "from the moment... from that moment" construction breaks that rhythm.

**Unexplained references.** Platon Karataev (named without introduction, though the conversation itself treats him as someone whose opinion matters), Sismondi (listed alongside the more recognizable Rousseau and Montesquieu with no gloss), "Prince Sergey" (see paragraph 16 above), Mucius Scaevola (named but the relevant action — "burned his hand" — is explained in the same sentence, so the gist survives even without knowing who he was), Arakcheev and "Prince Andrew" (both carried over from earlier chapters, used here without reintroduction).

**Argument or story thread.** Easy to state in two sentences: Natasha and Pierre talk in their private, associative way about family, Petersburg, and Pierre's plan for a society of "active virtue," while downstairs young Nicholas Bolkonski wakes from a dream in which his dead father and his uncle's warnings collide, and vows to become someone his father would be proud of. The thread stays clear throughout; nothing derails it.

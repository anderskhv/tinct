## Whole-chapter notes

- The fencing metaphor is the chapter's spine: ¶9 (duel, rapier thrown down, cudgel), ¶10 (the mapping: French army / Russian people / historians), ¶12 (Napoleon's fencing attitude, en quarte/en tierce/en prime, the cudgel raised), ¶13 (salute, hilt presented). The candidate keeps ¶9 and ¶10 intact but strips the technical vocabulary at the two points where it does the most work (¶12, ¶13). The MAJOR finding and the "salute"→"bow" MINOR are the same loss at two ends of the chain and should be decided together.
- A second through-line is weakened in the same way: source "phenomena" (¶0) → "historic phenomenon" (¶5); "essential indication" (¶2) → "invariable indication" (¶6). The candidate breaks the first callback and merges the second pair into "reliable". Both are one-word repairs.
- Tense: the source runs the whole argument in the historical present from ¶3 ("Austria loses", "The victories … destroy") into ¶4 ("But then, in 1812, the French gain a victory"). The candidate puts ¶3 in the past and ¶4 in the present, so the "rule, then the exception" hinge loses its grammatical parallel.

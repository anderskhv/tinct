## Whole-chapter notes

- Two different renderings of the same root in the same chapter: "naïveté" → "openness" (¶2) and "naïve" → "blunt" (¶16). Helene's performed innocence and Bilibin's licensed artlessness are the same joke told twice, and neither survives. The same substitution recurs in ch 267 ¶22 ("good-natured naïveté" → "good-natured openness"), so it is a drafter habit, not a local choice.
- Bilibin's face is a running mechanism across chapters (¶10, ¶11, ¶17, ¶18 here; ch 264 ¶13, ¶17). Consistent verbs matter; ¶18's "pursed his lips" breaks the chain.
- The French handling is internally consistent with the decided convention on the *inline* side, and wrong on the *slot* side in all four passages. That is a single mechanical fix, not four judgement calls.
- Paragraph count, chapter title, speech attribution, the Gospel quotation (¶24), "N.N." (¶36), Borodino (¶38) and all proper nouns survive. Names follow the conventions table except "Vasily".
- Nothing was summarised or dropped at sentence scale anywhere in the chapter; the damage is concentrated in single words carrying characterisation.

## Explicit rulings requested

- **The double negative in paragraph 5 — split ruling.** Taking "paragraph 5" as the fifth paragraph (index 4, the argument from time), which is the only paragraph in the chapter built on double negatives:
  - **"But I am not now abstaining from doing so at the first moment when I asked the question" — ambiguity kept.** The candidate's "But in not lifting it now, I am not refraining from doing so at that first moment when I asked the question" preserves both negatives and their scopes, and does not resolve the sentence into a positive claim. The added participial clause is a gloss, not a resolution. MINOR only.
  - **"does not prove that I could have abstained" — ambiguity NOT kept.** "Proves nothing about whether I could have refrained" converts a denial of one inference into a denial of evidential bearing in both directions. That is a sharpening, not a preservation, and it puts the paragraph at odds with its own next sentence. MODERATE finding above. Proposal, corrector decides: "That I did not lift my arm a moment later does not prove that I could have refrained from lifting it then."
  - If "paragraph 5" was meant as index 5 (the argument from causes), that paragraph contains no double negative; its negatives are "no phenomenon is conceivable without one" and "an action without a cause is itself the cause", both rendered faithfully, with the source's self-refuting turn intact. The only finding there is the added agent (MINOR above).
- **Splitting the nested sentences — no connective invented, one qualifier at risk.** The chapter's long periods are p2, p4, p11, p28 and p31.
  - **p11 passes.** "But even if, admitting the remaining minimum of freedom to equal zero, we assumed in some given case … complete absence of freedom, by so doing we should destroy …" becomes "even granting … suppose we assumed … Doing so would destroy …". The concession ("even"), the embedded admission, the three examples (a dying man, an unborn child, an idiot) and the consequence all survive across the new sentence boundary, and "Doing so" carries "by so doing" exactly. Nothing causal is added.
  - **p28 passes.** The two long parentheticals become em-dash spans; "from the first knowledge that all bodies have weight, up to Newton's law" and "from the fact that every man dies, up to … economic and historic laws" both survive whole, and the "just as … so too" correlative is the source's own.
  - **p31 passes** apart from the "even" → "so too" substitution noted above; the list astronomy, physics, chemistry, botany, zoology is complete, and the metaphysics/history division is stated in the source's own order.
  - **p2 and p4** are handled under the rulings above; neither adds a connective, and p2's restructuring is a ranking change, not a new claim.
  - No hedge was lost in any split: "can only be the subject of metaphysics" (p31), "seem to be free" (p1 chain), "may be" (p5, p10), "it would have had to" (p3) all survive.
- **Every "however"-type concession survives.** p2 five concessive clauses (see below); p3 "To whatever degree we may imagine"; p4 "No matter how closely we bring"; p5 "No matter how much harder" and "However inaccessible to us the cause … may be"; p6 "even granting"; p8 "However much we increase"; p9 "However much we lengthen"; p10 "However accessible … may be"; p11 "even granting". Nine paragraphs, every one intact.
- **p2's five concessions, specifically.** The source has: (1) however we may change our point of view; (2) however plain we may make to ourselves the connection; (3) however inaccessible it may be to us; (4) however long or short the period of time; (5) however intelligible or incomprehensible the causes. The candidate has four clauses because it merges (2) and (3) into "however clearly or however obscurely we make out the connection between the man and the external world". Since (2) and (3) are the two poles of one variable — plain at one end, inaccessible at the other — the merge loses no content and matches the two-pole form the source itself uses for (4) and (5). **No finding for the merge.**

## Whole-chapter notes

- The chapter's architecture is a mirror: three numbered arguments that freedom is never complete (p3, p4, p5), then three numbered arguments that inevitability is never complete (p8, p9, p10), each triple keyed to space, time and cause in the same order, then the pairing of reason's three propositions against consciousness's three (p17, p18). The candidate keeps all four triples, in order, with their numbering. This is the hardest thing in the chapter to preserve and it is preserved.
- The damage is concentrated in p0, the sentence that carries chapter 362's conclusion into this chapter. Two of its three variables are misstated (findings above), and because p1, p2, p3–p5 and p8–p10 all restate those same three variables, the opening sentence is the one place where an error propagates through the reader's grasp of everything after it. The corrector should treat p0 as the priority.
- The arm-lifting demonstration runs from ch 361 p12 ("I have lifted my hand and let it fall") through p3 and p4 here. The candidate opens p3 with "I raise my arm" and p4 with "I lift it", where the source uses "lift" in both; since the source itself alternates "lift"/"raise" inside p3, no finding, but the callback to ch 361 reads better with "lift".
- Form/content is the chapter's controlling pair (p14, p15, p16, p21, p22, p24) and the candidate holds the terms fixed throughout, including the chiasmus in p21 ("Freedom is the content. Inevitability is the form.").
- "Inevitability" and "necessity" are used by the source as near-synonyms and the candidate does not tidy them into one word — correct, since p30's "Freedom, apart from necessity" depends on the alternation.
- Named items all survive: gravitation, inertia, electricity, animal force, chemical affinity, heat, vital force, Newton's law, astronomy, physics, chemistry, botany, zoology, metaphysics. Newton is the only proper name; spelling is correct and no conventions-file issue arises.
- The same low-level intensification seen in ch 361 is present here — "at all" (p11, p13, p24, p30), "always" (p8, p9), "single" (p17), "purely" (p14), "precisely because" (p15), "utterly alone" (p13), "nothing but" (p25, p26, p30). None changes a claim; as a pattern it is worth one corrector's pass.

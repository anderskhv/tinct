## Gloss and tag rulings

- **"children of the Don" = Cossacks (p9)** — "'the children of the Don'—the Cossacks—": *allowed*. The identification is fixed by the immediately preceding paragraph: "Some Cossacks on the prowl for booty fell in with the Emperor and very nearly captured him", and p9 names the same near-capture. The gloss states only a fact the source supplies in the adjacent sentence and adds no claim. The separate question of dropping the French original is logged above as a conventions finding, not as a defect in the gloss.

## Whole-chapter notes

- **The split question at p3 is still a question.** The candidate ends with "How could such an army recover in Kaluga province—inhabited by the same kind of Russians who had lived in Moscow, where fire had the same power to consume whatever was set ablaze?" — interrogative, unanswered, with the Kaluga clause intact. What is lost is the shape, not the mood: the source builds one cumulative rhetorical question with an emphatic resumption ("how could that army … how could that army"), and the candidate front-loads the Moscow and Smolensk history as two declarative sentences. Every proposition survives; the repetition does not. Logged MINOR.
- The chapter's argument runs: the army was already decomposing (p3–p5) → the council admitted it (p6–p7) → the Cossack scare supplied the shock (p8–p9) → therefore Napoleon's order proves nothing (p10). Each link survives, but three of the hinges are weakened — the dropped "as it were" at p4, the invented antithesis at p8, and the split "does not prove … but that" at p10.
- Mouton appears twice (p6 as the simple-minded soldier, p9 as the man Napoleon agrees with); the second reference depends on the first for its irony, which the "plain-spoken" substitution blunts.
- Two French phrases in the chapter are handled identically — translated, quoted, with no "(in French)" cue and no original retained. That is one decision, applied consistently, for the corrector to settle against CONVENTIONS rule 3 in a single pass.
- Names and diacritics follow CONVENTIONS (Kutuzov, Dokhturov, Malo-Yaroslavets, Tarutino, Kaluga, Borodino, Smolensk, Mozhaysk, Mouton); no name-variant defects. The source's stray double-hyphen at p5 is correctly set as an em dash.

Fidelity verdict: REVISE

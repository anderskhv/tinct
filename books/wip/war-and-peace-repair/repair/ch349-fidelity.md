## Gloss and tag rulings (explicit)

- **écossaise** (¶4) — "that écossaise—a Scottish country dance, and his only piece—". **ALLOWED.** States only the category of the word (a dance), adds no claim about Pierre, the children or the scene, and the source itself establishes that it is danced to ("all possible dances could be danced"). No finding.
- **arshin** (¶17) — "(An arshin was about twenty-eight inches.)". **ALLOWED IN CONTENT, FLAGGED IN FORM — MINOR.** The unit's length is a dictionary fact and adds no claim, but the candidate delivers it as a whole new sentence of narration appended inside Natasha's dialogue paragraph, which the source has no room for. *Proposal (corrector decides):* fold it in with no new sentence — e.g. "A ruble an arshin—twenty-eight inches—I suppose?" is still an addition; simply dropping the gloss is the fully faithful option.
- **Memento mori** (¶32) — ""Memento mori"—remember that you must die—said those glances." **ALLOWED.** A translation of a foreign tag, which is exactly what the conventions file directs for foreign-language material, and it adds no claim. The Latin is retained, so nothing is lost. No finding.
- **Platon Karataev** (¶5) — "a peasant who had shared that captivity with him". **NOT ALLOWED — MODERATE.** This is not a category tag; it asserts two facts about a person (his class, and that he shared the captivity) that this source paragraph does not supply.

## Whole-chapter notes

- Russian stress accents are correctly stripped throughout (Natasha, Platon Karataev, Belova, Masha, Nicholas); straight double quotes and unspaced em dashes are used consistently, per the conventions file.
- The chapter's architecture — the enumeration of the "worlds" of Bald Hills, each with its own reason to welcome Pierre (servants / children and governesses / young Nicholas / guests / grown family / old ladies) — survives intact, and the hinge at ¶10 ("Pierre felt the different outlooks…") still gathers them, though weakened by the loss of "outlooks".

- Young Nicholas's characterisation drifts twice in the same paragraph (¶5): he loses "intelligent" as a personal attribute and his father's-son "beautiful eyes", and gains an explanatory apposition about Karataev he does not need. This is the chapter's densest paragraph and the one to re-check after correction.

## Ruling: the "wave" / "wake" change in ¶8–9

**The change is correct and should stand.** The baseline read "the wake it cuts will always be visible just ahead of it" and "the movement of that wake" (¶8–9). The source has "the flow of the waves it cuts will always be noticeable **ahead of it**" and "the movement of that flow". A wake trails a ship; "wake … ahead of it" is a factual error and it also broke the image the source sustains across ¶11–12, where Maude himself writes "there is one and the same **wave** ahead of it", "the **wave** ahead of it also turns", "there always will be the **wave** anticipating its movement". The candidate's "wave" restores both the source's sense and the chapter's internal consistency, and it matches the ¶11–12 wording the baseline already had right. No finding against the substitution.

Residual, logged as MINOR above: the source's ¶8 noun is plural and is a *flow* ("the flow of the waves", "those waves"), and ¶9's is "that flow". The candidate normalises all of it to a singular "wave", so the chapter now reads "wave" six times where the source varies flow/waves/wave/rush of water. "Rush of water" is retained in ¶12. Proposal (corrector decides): ¶8 "the flow of the water it cuts … the movement of that flow", keeping "wave" from ¶11 onward, which would preserve both the source's variation and the corrected direction.

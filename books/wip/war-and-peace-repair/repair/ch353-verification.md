## 3. Read as a new reader

All sixteen read cleanly. Three observations:

- **¶17, ¶43 and ¶46 are now one convention.** Before correction the chapter handled interior monologue three different ways — unquoted third person, unquoted first person, and quoted. All three are now quoted first person, matching the source throughout. Read end to end, the boy's two silent speeches and Natasha's silent question now sit in the same register, and ¶43's shift from "he thought" into the vow is unambiguous where it was previously hard to tell narration from thought.
- **¶27 is better without the gloss, not merely more faithful.** "Did you see her?" / "No, and if I had I shouldn't have recognized her." reads as a couple resuming an argument they have had many times. The gloss had been explaining the joke and getting it wrong at the same time.
- **¶12** — "never to buy a new book until he's read what he'd already bought" is clear on first pass and is now actually funnier, because the pile is unbounded.

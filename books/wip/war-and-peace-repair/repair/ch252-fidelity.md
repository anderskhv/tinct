## Whole-chapter notes

- **Anonymity is the chapter's subject.** Almost every line of speech here is unattributed or attributed to "voices", "some of the mob", "different people". The candidate preserves that at ¶9, ¶27, ¶34 and ¶42 and breaks it once, at ¶23. That single exception matters more than its size, because ¶24 immediately generalises the two lines as "questions and answers" from the crowd. This is the finding to fix first.
- **The tall lad.** He is tracked by three recurring marks — his height, his bare arm, and the sleeve he keeps rolling up (¶0, ¶1, ¶5, ¶10, ¶25, ¶33, ¶42). The candidate standardises him to "the tall young man"/"the tall fellow", which is consistent and readable, but at ¶5 it spends the sleeve epithet for nothing. The arm survives at ¶25, so the motif is not lost, only thinned.
- **Two crowds, one vocabulary.** "Lads" (6) becomes "boys" (6) throughout — consistent and acceptable. "Ukase" (4) becomes "decree" — acceptable and consistent, except that ¶29 loses one of the four. "Trap" (4) becomes "carriage" — consistent here, inconsistent with ch 245.
- **Glosses.** Three in this chapter. China-Town: the district half is fine, "not a Chinese quarter" is an editorial aside and should go. "His Serene Highness" → Kutuzov: factually clean, wrongly placed. "Rostopchin's broadsheet": factually clean, but it is an addition, not a gloss of anything, and it front-runs the source's own reveal. Taken together they suggest the drafter reached for the reader's shoulder three times in fifteen paragraphs; one of the three is the most that this stretch needs.
- **Names.** No violations of the CONVENTIONS table; no character from the table appears. Place and person names all survive with stress accents correctly stripped: Varvarka, Moroseyka, Lubyanka, China-Town, Rostopchin, Kutuzov (added), Serene Highness. No name is misspelled and none is dropped.
- Paragraph alignment is intact.

## Chapter notes

**Rhythm and voice.** A sustained analytical/aphoristic voice — the extended "promised land" metaphor, then the physics analogies of gravity and melting snow. Reads as one consistent authorial voice (essay more than scene), never choppy, and the prose itself is plain and natural.

**Unexplained references.** Konovnitsyn's foresight (para 7) is invoked without ever being described; the blank-sheet-of-paper incident (para 10) is the chapter's biggest gap — presented as meaningful with zero gloss.

**Argument or story thread.** The retreating French, unable to treat their distant homeland as a reachable goal, fix instead on the nearer promised land of Smolensk, and that collective illusion plus their sheer mass drags them onward — a mass too large for the pursuing Russians to break. Kutuzov alone grasps that the army will dissolve on its own and tries, against his own officers' hunger for glory, to hold back needless attacks. The thread is followable overall, but paragraph 10 breaks it — the blank-paper incident lands with no recoverable meaning.

**Anachronism or over-explanation.** No modern idiom jars; the register stays plain and consistent, well suited to carrying the extended metaphors (promised land, gravity, melting snow) without over-explaining them.

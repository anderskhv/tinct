## Obstacles

**Paragraph 2** — "Generals on the staff, excited by the memory of the easy victory at Tarutino, urged Kutuzov..." — "the easy victory at Tarutino" is treated as shared knowledge; the chapter never narrates what happened there, so the reader has to take the outcome on faith.

**Paragraph 3** — Two separate obstacles in one long paragraph:
- "At Austerlitz he was the last to hold the Augezd dam, rallying regiments and saving what he could when everyone was fleeing and perishing..." — a dense cluster of unglossed proper nouns and events (Austerlitz, the Augezd dam) presented as already familiar to the reader.
- "the man sent to that spot was this same irresolute and undiscerning Dokhturov—Kutuzov rushing to correct a mistake he'd made by first sending someone else." — the dash clause reads as a grammatical fragment; it takes a re-read to work out that it means Kutuzov corrected his own earlier error by sending Dokhturov.

## Chapter notes

**Rhythm and voice.** An essayistic, omniscient-narrator voice ("we find him," "about Dokhturov, scarcely a word has been said") rather than a scene played out in real time. Sentences run long and compound but hold together as one consistent authorial tone — argumentative and slightly ironic about how history gets written. Not choppy.

**Unexplained references.** Tarutino ("the easy victory," para 2) and the historical-battle catalogue in paragraph 3 (Austerlitz, the Augezd dam, the Malakhov Gate at Smolensk, Bagration's death at Borodino) are all named as if the reader already knows them; none are narrated here.

**Argument or story thread.** Kutuzov turns down a second French peace offer and reluctantly authorizes a small strike on an isolated French division, handing the job to the unglamorous but consistently indispensable general Dokhturov. When the mission reveals the whole French army has unexpectedly appeared, Dokhturov holds back and sends an officer galloping off to get orders from Kutuzov. The thread is easy to follow throughout.

**Anachronism or over-explanation.** No modern idiom intrudes. The machine/cog-and-shaving analogy in paragraph 5 floats free of its subject (Dokhturov) for a moment before the connection resolves via the surrounding paragraphs — closer to over-explaining the "quiet greatness" theme than to anachronism, but not jarring.

## Gloss and tag rulings

- **"old wives' summer" (p3)** — the added gloss "a stretch of warm days that comes in early autumn": *partly unsupported*. "Warm days" is supported (p3 "calm and clear", p4 "the air was warm"), and the recurring-phenomenon framing is supported by "what is called". "**early** autumn" is not: the source places it only "at that time of autumn" (p5), and dates the scene 6 October. The gloss adds a seasonal specification the source does not make. Logged MINOR; proposal (corrector decides): "a spell of warm weather in autumn".
- **Platon / Karataev tag (p18)** — "said Platon—Karataev's first name—smiling": *allowed*. It states only a fact the source edition supports; Maude names him "Platón Karatáev" (10 occurrences, e.g. ch 349, 353), and this chapter itself alternates the two names for one man (p14 "Platoche", p16 "Karatáev", p18 "Platón"). It adds no claim. Note for the corrector: it is the only editorial aside placed inside a speech tag in this chapter, so its position, not its content, is the open question.

## Whole-chapter notes

- Karataev's voice is the chapter's spine: he speaks almost entirely in proverbs (p18 promise/performance, p22 tool/louse, p30 sweating hand/dry hand). Two of the three survive; the p18 proverb has been replaced by a generic moralism, which is the single worst defect here because it is exactly the trait the chapter exists to show.
- The linen thread runs p15 (linen issued) → p23 ("some linen left over") → p25/27 (the bits) → p28 (Karataev gives the scraps back) → p32 (leg bands). The candidate uses linen, fabric, material, cloth and scraps for the same substance; consistency is lost across the packet boundary.
- "Leg bands" → "leg wrappings" is applied consistently at p27 and p32. Good.
- Names follow CONVENTIONS: Karataev, Platon, Pierre, Sokolov, stress accents dropped throughout. No name-variant defects.

At p23 the source's own narration "said the Frenchman, in French" is kept as "said the Frenchman in French" rather than the decided cue form "said the Frenchman (in French)" — a conventions detail for the French pass, not a fidelity defect.

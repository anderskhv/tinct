Explicit ruling requested:

- **¶3 (index 3), the reworded dateline.** Source: "November 9: twenty miles from Smolénsk." Candidate: "Written twenty miles from Smolensk, November 9." Ruling: **MINOR, not a fidelity failure, but it should be restored.** The word "Written" adds no claim the dateline does not already make — a dateline states where and when a dispatch was written, so this is within the gloss allowance. What is wrong is the rest: this paragraph is not narration, it is the sign-off line of the quoted Berthier letter, and the candidate converts a formulaic dateline into an English sentence and reverses the source's order (date first, then place). The date, the distance and the place all survive and are correct. Proposal (the corrector decides): restore "November 9: twenty miles from Smolensk." — the accent-stripping is the only change the conventions file asks for here.

## Whole-chapter notes

- Every number and place name survives and is correct: twenty-eighth of October, seventy-three thousand, thirty-six thousand, five thousand, a quarter of the soldiers, November 9, twenty miles; Vyazma, Smolensk, the Berezina, Vilna, Moscow, Orsha is not in this chapter. Maude's stress accents are dropped per the conventions file (Vyázma, Smolénsk, Berëzina, Vílna). Berthier and Napoleon are spelled correctly.
- The four legs of the progression (Moscow–Vyazma, Vyazma–Smolensk, Smolensk–Berezina, Berezina–Vilna) all survive; recasting them as "whether … or" keeps the same "at the same rate regardless of conditions" claim and is not a finding.
- The chapter's structure — narration (¶0–1) → quoted dispatch (¶2–3) → narration (¶4–5) — is preserved, but the dispatch is the weak point. Its register is modernised further than the narration around it, and three of its losses ("ineffectives", "the interest of Your Majesty's service", "such as") pull in the same direction: a formal staff document made to sound like a manager's memo. Worth one decision about how far quoted documents may be modernised, rather than three.
- ¶5 is byte-identical to the baseline text, so its four findings ("supposed genius", "knew", the French titles, the dropped "save himself") are inherited rather than newly introduced — they still stand against the source, but the corrector should know they are not this draft's regressions.
- Berthier's letter is a quotation and the chapter turns on it being an honest report from an officer whom Tolstoy has just accused of habitual understatement; sharpening it ("they all look", "growing worse every day", "every direction") works against that irony in small increments.

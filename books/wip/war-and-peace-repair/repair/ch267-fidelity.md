## Rulings requested

- **"the ecossaise (a lively couple dance)"** — MODERATE. States more than a category.
- **"the clavichord (an early keyboard instrument)"** — MINOR. Category plus one neutral qualifier; no claim about the scene. Acceptable as written, though if the ¶16 glosses are thinned, this is the one to keep.
- **"the cavalier of St. George (holder of Russia's highest military honor)"** — MODERATE, and the most substantive of the three: it adds an unsupported evaluative claim and misdescribes Nicholas's actual decoration.

## Whole-chapter notes

- The chapter turns on one joint: the essay (¶1–3, present tense, general law) against the demonstration (¶4–22, Nicholas in Voronezh doing exactly what the essay predicts — attending to his private interests and being the more useful for it). ¶1's tense shift weakens that joint at the only place it is stated, and ¶4's "looked at what was going on in Russia without despair" is what it is supposed to illustrate. This is the finding with the widest reach even though it is a single verb pair.
- Gloss density in ¶16: two parentheses in one sentence, in a paragraph whose whole effect is offhandedness ("It was not a ball, nor had dancing been announced, but everyone knew..."). Even if both glosses were within bounds, they change the paragraph's pace. Worth one gloss, not two.
- "Snuggery" is rendered "cozy den" consistently at ¶13 and ¶20 — good; the repeated location is preserved. "Remounts" (¶5, ¶14, ¶19) is not handled consistently.
- Recurring words that should stay put across the chapter: "free and easy" (¶21, twice in the source at different strengths), "morose/melancholy" (¶22), "involuntarily" (¶2, ¶22 — preserved in both). Two of the three drift.
- No dropped or invented sentences; paragraph count, all proper nouns (Mamonov, Matreshka, Voronezh, Ilya Rostov, Catherine Petrovna, St. George, Borodino), the numbers (seventeen stallions, six thousand rubles, fourteen miles, 1812) and the two quoted tags ("better late than never", "in for a penny, in for a pound—who cares?") all survive.
- Typography follows the conventions (unspaced em dashes, straight double quotes). No name-convention breaches: Nicholas Rostov, Kutuzov, Count Ilya Rostov all correct.

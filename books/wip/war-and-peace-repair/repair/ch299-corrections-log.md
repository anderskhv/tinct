## ¶1

Finding: MINOR (states/nations doublet halved in both places — restored both).

## ¶2
Before: "It seems strange: some king or emperor quarrels with another, raises an army, fights the enemy's forces, wins by killing three, five, or ten thousand men, and thereby subjugates a kingdom of several million people. Yet as far as we know, every fact of history confirms the rule that the greater or lesser success of one army against another is the cause, or at least a reliable sign, of a nation's strength rising or falling. This holds even though it makes little sense that the defeat of an army—a hundredth part of a nation—should force the whole nation to submit. A victorious army wins, and at once the rights of the conquering nation grow at the expense of the defeated. A defeated army loses, and at once its people lose rights in proportion to the severity of the loss; if the army is destroyed outright, the nation is fully subjugated."
After: "It seems strange: some king or emperor quarrels with another, raises an army, fights the enemy's forces, wins by killing three, five, or ten thousand men, and thereby subjugates a kingdom and an entire nation of several million people. Yet as far as we know, every fact of history confirms the rule that the greater or lesser success of one army against another is the cause, or at least an essential indication, of a nation's strength rising or falling. This holds even though it is unintelligible that the defeat of an army—a hundredth part of a nation—should force the whole nation to submit. A victorious army wins, and at once the rights of the conquering nation grow at the expense of the defeated. A defeated army loses, and at once its people lose rights in proportion to the severity of the loss; if its army suffers a complete defeat, the nation is fully subjugated."
Findings: MINOR ("an entire nation" dropped — restored); MINOR ("essential" softened to "reliable" — restored); MINOR ("unintelligible" softened to "makes little sense" — restored); MINOR ("suffers a complete defeat" weakened to "is destroyed outright" — restored).

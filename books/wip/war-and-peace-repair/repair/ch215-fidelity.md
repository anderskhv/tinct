## Whole-chapter notes

- **Ruling on ¶11 "the enemy would get it all".** Source-determined, therefore acceptable. Maude's "he" has no antecedent in Timokhin's speech, but Prince Andrew answers the same question three paragraphs later — "so as not to lay waste the country we were abandoning **to the enemy**" — which fixes the referent beyond doubt. The gloss adds no fact. It does flatten a vague soldier's pronoun into an abstract noun, hence MINOR rather than clean.
- **Ruling on ¶52 "the other one".** Not source-determined. Within this chapter the source supplies no antecedent at all; the referent (Anatole) comes only from the reader's knowledge of the novel, and Maude's abrupt unglossed "He" is doing deliberate work — the pronoun lands before the reader knows who it is. "The other one" asserts a rival and closes that gap. The reading is almost certainly correct, but it is the reviewer's/drafter's inference stated as text, so MODERATE. *Proposal: revert to a bare "He did not need anything of that kind."*
- **Ruling on ¶42 "some… some…".** Tolerable, MINOR. The dismissive force is carried in the source by the bare generic names themselves; "some" states what they imply and adds a touch of contempt that Maude leaves to the reader. No fact changes, and Prince Andrew's register supports it. Flagged, not blocking.
- **Ruling on the Wolzogen/Clausewitz exchange (¶30–34) against the source wording.** The German itself is now exactly right: both slots reproduce the source verbatim — "Der Krieg muss **in** Raum verlegt werden. Der Ansicht kann ich nicht genug Preis geben." and "der Zweck ist **nur** den Feind zu schwächen, so kann man gewiss nicht den Verlust der **Privat-Personen** in Achtung nehmen." — correcting three corruptions in the baseline ("im Raum", "das einzige Zweck", "Privatpersonen"). Slot format (`*` prefix, no quotes) and the placement of "(in German)" after the speech verb follow the conventions file. The inline English at ¶30 is the problem: it is a fresh translation rather than the source's own, and it destroys the "Extend widely!… that 'extend'" callback in ¶35. Two smaller deviations: "Oh, ja" left untranslated at ¶32, and no cue on ¶34.
- **Argument shape and callbacks.** Three run across packet boundaries. The "moment" exchange (¶26–27) is broken; the "extend" callback (¶30→¶35) is broken; the "play at war" chain (¶37 "chivalry" → ¶40 "we have played at war" → ¶42 "not play at war") is intact but ¶37 has been pulled forward into it. Prince Andrew's chess analogy (¶21) and the valet analogy (¶14) both survive complete.
- **Names and typography.** Timokhin, Natasha, Mozhaysk, Gorki, Sventsyani, Smolensk — Russian stress accents correctly dropped; Prince Andrew, Barclay de Tolly, Kutuzov per the conventions table. Straight double quotes throughout; single quotes only for quotes within quotes ("We've lost, so let's run", "It's not the day for that!", "that 'extend'"). Correct.

## Hard/unclear obstacles

**Paragraph 5** — "To find component forces equal to a composite or resultant force (as in mechanics), the sum of the components must equal the resultant." The sentence restates the same relationship twice in different vocabulary before it means anything concrete; a reader has to reread it against the next sentence to see it's a rule the historians violate, not a standalone claim.

**Paragraph 6** — "the activity of the statesmen and writers Stein, Metternich, Madame de Staël, Talleyrand, Fichte, Chateaubriand, and others." Six unfamiliar names land in one clause with no individual cues; the reader must hold all six as an undifferentiated group to follow the "components" argument that depends on them.

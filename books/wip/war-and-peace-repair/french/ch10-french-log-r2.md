# Chapter 10 — French pass round two

## Paragraph 3

**Before:** "I'm so very grateful to you, my dear" — he called absolutely everyone "my dear," whether they were above or below him in rank, without the slightest change in tone — "I thank you on my own behalf and on behalf of our two dear ones whose name day we're celebrating. But you must come to dinner — I'll be offended if you don't, my dear! On behalf of the whole family, I insist — please come, my dear!" He repeated these words to every single guest without exception or variation, wearing the same expression on his full, cheerful, clean-shaven face, giving the same firm handshake and the same quick, repeated bows. As soon as he had seen one visitor out, he returned to whoever was still in the drawing room, pulled a chair up close, and with his legs spread out comfortably and his hands on his knees — the posture of a man who enjoys life and knows how to live it — he swayed back and forth with easy dignity, made small talk about the weather or asked after someone's health, switching between Russian and very poor but thoroughly confident French. Then, like a man weary but committed to his duties, he would get up to see the next visitor off, smooth his sparse gray hair over his bald spot, and invite them to dinner too. Sometimes, on his way back from the entrance hall, he would pass through the conservatory and pantry into the great marble dining room, where tables were being laid for eighty guests. He would watch the footmen carrying in silver and china, rearranging tables, and unfolding damask tablecloths, and call over Dmitri Vasilyevich — a man of good family who managed all his affairs — and say with evident pleasure at the sight of the enormous table: "Well, Dmitri, make sure everything is just right? Good! The important thing is the service — that's what matters." And with a satisfied sigh, he would head back to the drawing room.

# Chapter 189 — French pass log

## Paragraph 12

**Before:** "Excuse me, your excellency," he began. (He knew the senator well but thought it necessary to address him formally on this occasion.) "Though I don't agree with the gentleman"—he hesitated, wanting to say "my very honorable opponent"—"with the gentleman whom I have not the honor of knowing, I believe the nobility have been summoned not merely to express sympathy and enthusiasm but also to consider the means by which we can help our Fatherland! I believe," he went on, warming to his subject, "that the Emperor himself would not be satisfied to find in us merely owners of serfs whom we are willing to hand over for his service, and cannon fodder we are ready to make of ourselves—and not to obtain from us any co-co-counsel."

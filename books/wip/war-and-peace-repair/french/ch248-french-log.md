## Paragraph 16

**Before:** He was carried away by the tone of magnanimity he planned to adopt toward Moscow. In his mind he set dates for assemblies at the Tsar's palace where Russian dignitaries and his own people would mingle. He mentally appointed a governor, one who would win the people's hearts. Knowing there were many charitable institutions in Moscow, he decided he would shower them all with favors. He thought that just as in Africa he had worn a burnoose and sat in a mosque, so in Moscow he must be beneficent like the Tsars. And to touch the hearts of the Russians — and being, like all Frenchmen, unable to imagine anything sentimental without a reference to his dear, tender, poor mother — he decided to place an inscription on every institution: "Dedicated to my dear mother." Or no, simply: "House of My Mother," he concluded. "But am I really in Moscow? Yes, here it lies before me — but why is the city delegation taking so long?" he wondered.

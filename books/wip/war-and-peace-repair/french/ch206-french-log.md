## Paragraph 14

**Before:** "Go your way and God be with you. I know your path is the path of honor!" He paused. "I missed you at Bucharest, but I needed someone to send." And changing the subject, Kutuzov began to speak of the Turkish war and the peace that had been concluded. "Yes, I was much criticized," he said, "both for that war and the peace... but everything came at the right time. Everything comes to him who knows how to wait. And there were just as many advisers there as here..." he went on, returning to the topic of "advisers" that clearly preoccupied him. "Ah, those advisers!" he said. "If we had listened to them all, we'd never have made peace with Turkey and would never have finished that war. Everything in haste, but the more haste, the less speed. Kamensky would have been ruined if he hadn't died. He stormed fortresses with thirty thousand men. It's not hard to capture a fortress, but it's hard to win a campaign. For that, you don't need storming and attacking—you need patience and time. Kamensky sent soldiers against Rushchuk, but I used only those two things—patience and time—and I took more fortresses than Kamensky, and made the Turks eat horseflesh!" He swayed his head. "And the French will too, believe me," he went on, growing more animated and striking his chest, "I'll make them eat horseflesh!" And tears again dimmed his eyes.

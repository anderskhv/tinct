## Paragraph 13

**Before:** A few days later, at one of those enchanting parties Helene gave at her country house on the Stone Island, the charming Monsieur de Jobert — a man no longer young, with snow-white hair and brilliant black eyes, a Jesuit in lay clothing * — was introduced to her. In the garden, by the light of the illuminations and to the sound of music, he spoke to her at length about the love of God, of Christ, of the Sacred Heart, and of the consolations offered by the one true Catholic faith in this world and the next. Helene was moved, and more than once tears rose to her eyes and to Monsieur de Jobert's, and their voices trembled. A dance, for which her partner came to claim her, ended her discourse with her future spiritual director, but Monsieur de Jobert came to see Helene again the next evening when she was alone, and after that he visited often.

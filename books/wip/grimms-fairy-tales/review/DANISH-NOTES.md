# Grimms’ Fairy Tales — Danish edition notes

## Names and terms applied

Askepot (Ashputtel), Snehvide og Rosenrød (Snow-White and Rose-Red), **Sneblomst** (Snowdrop, tale 33 — the Snow White tale), Tornerose, Rødhætte, Hans og Grete, **Rumleskaft** (Rumpelstiltskin; title, song and the queen's guess, changed 2026-10-19), Tommelfinger (Tom Thumb; “Tom” in the second half of the tale is now also *Tommelfinger*, and the woodman is *brændehugger* throughout), Mutter Holle, Kong Grumskæg, Jernhans, Kattepels, Frøprinsen, Gåsepigen, Doktor Alvidende, Livets Vand, Dummeriks (Dummling), Lilje, Jorinde og Joringel (the English text says Jorinda/Jorindel; unified to the glossary form), Frederik og Katrine (English: Frederick/Catherine; unified to the Danish forms; Kate → *Trine*), Gråben (the wolf as “grey-beard” in Red-Cap's second story). Kept from the English: Chanticleer, Partlet, Curdken, Falada, Fundevogel, Ilsabill, Kywitt, Marleen, Kehrewit.

## Naming decisions from the 2026-10-19 re-review

- **Sneblomst kept** for Snowdrop. It translates the English name and is not wrong. It also keeps the tale apart from *Snehvide* in Snow-White and Rose-Red, who is a different character. A Danish child knows this story as *Snehvide*. If one name is preferred, change the title and the 19 paragraphs of chapter 33 that use the name together; nothing else depends on it.
- **Rumpelstiltskin → Rumleskaft**, the established Danish name.
- **Frøprinsen kept**, not *Frøkongen*: the English tale has a prince, and the title follows the text.
- **De rejsende musikanter kept**, not *Bremerbymusikanterne*: this English version never names Bremen; the animals go to “the great city”. The integration step could add *(Bremerbymusikanterne)* to the title if search needs it.
- **Den tapre lille skrædder**: belt and refrain are *Syv med ét slag*, the traditional Danish wording.
- Cat and Mouse: *gudmor* and *gudbørn*, not *fadder*.
- Chanticleer/Partlet are still English. *Hanen og Hønen* would be the domestic choice if Anders prefers it (chapters 14–16, plus the rooster in chapter 3).

## Choices a Danish reader may want to revisit

- Verse: since 2026-10-19 most verse and refrains rhyme. A few still do not, or only loosely: the last line of the Water of Life ending, Mrs Fox's longer verses, the second Mrs Fox story's cat verse, and Hans i lykke's grinder song (near rhymes).
- Coinages by the helpers: Toppen-af / Halvt-ude / Alt-væk (Cat and Mouse), Kalkvæg / Hanekam (Grisly-Beard nicknames), Krebs (pun for Crabb in Doctor Knowall), Alferne og skomageren (Elves). *Gedekid* and *killinger* both occur in the Wolf and the Seven Kids; both are correct Danish.
- Footnote “[1] Tåbe” (Dummeriks) gives “fool” for the name; the name itself no longer spells it out.
- Distances: English leagues are “mil”. English miles are now mostly plain “mil” or “et godt stykke”, because the size does not matter in a fairy tale. “Engelske mil” is left in two places in the Fisherman (the park and the throne). Yards are “alen”.
- Address: the cat says *De* to Mr Fox and the fox says *du* to her. This is on purpose (her politeness, his arrogance). Elsewhere children and animals use *du*.
- Snow-White and Rose-Red uses *Snehvide* for a different character than tale 33 (*Sneblomst*); they never appear in the same tale.

## 2026-10-19: Snehvide

Anders chose the familiar Danish name: "Sneblomst" (36 occurrences, incl. the tale title) is now "Snehvide". The separate tale "Snehvide og Rosenrød" keeps its own Snehvide, as in Danish tradition.

# Grimms’ Fairy Tales — Danish edition notes

## Names and terms applied

Askepot (Ashputtel), Snehvide og Rosenrød (Snow-White and Rose-Red), **Sneblomst** (Snowdrop, tale 33 — the Snow White tale), Tornerose, Rødhætte, Hans og Grete, Tommelfinger, Mutter Holle, Kong Grumskæg, Jernhans, Kattepels, Frøprinsen, Gåsepigen, Doktor Alvidende, Livets Vand, Dummeriks (Dummling), Lilje, Jorinde og Joringel (the English text says Jorinda/Jorindel; unified to the glossary form), Frederik og Katrine (English: Frederick/Catherine; unified to the Danish forms). Kept from the English: Rumpelstiltskin, Chanticleer, Partlet, Curdken, Falada, Fundevogel, Ilsabill, Kywitt, Marleen, Kehrewit.

## Choices a Danish reader may want to revisit

- Verse and refrains keep the English line counts and a single wording each time, but the rhyme is often loose or absent (Fisherman, Frog-Prince, Goose-Girl, Juniper-Tree, mirror and dove refrains).
- Coinages by the helpers: Toppen-af / Halvt-ude / Alt-væk (Cat and Mouse), Kalkvæg / Hanekam (Grisly-Beard nicknames), Krebs (pun for Crabb in Doctor Knowall), Alferne og skomageren (Elves), *gedekid* / *killinger* both occur in the Wolf and the Seven Kids.
- Footnote “[1] Tåbe” (Dummeriks) gives “fool” for the name; the name itself no longer spells it out.
- Distances: English leagues are “mil”; English miles are “engelske mil”; “yards” partly became “alen”/“yard”.
- Snow-White and Rose-Red uses *Snehvide* for a different character than tale 33 (*Sneblomst*); they never appear in the same tale.

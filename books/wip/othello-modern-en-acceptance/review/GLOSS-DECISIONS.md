# Contested glosses: resolution and what v2 does

The base package's renderer self-flags, and other readings the reviewers queried, were resolved independently by **G1**. G1 worked only from public-domain annotation: Schmidt's *Shakespeare-Lexicon* (3rd ed., 1902), Onions's *A Shakespeare Glossary* (1911) and Furness's *New Variorum Othello* (1886). The full record, with citations for every item, is `gloss-resolution.json`.

Result: **31 items: 17 KEEP, 11 REVISE, 3 OPEN.**

- **KEEP:** v1 wording confirmed.
- **REVISE:** v2 changes the wording to G1's reading. Edits citing G1 are in `../CHANGES-v1-to-v2.json`, and each was re-checked by RC1 or RC2.
- **OPEN:** a genuine crux, where the sources support more than one reading. v2 takes the reading G1 recommends, recorded below. Standard rule 7 says a crux is kept open where the modern sentence can hold both readings; where it cannot, the choice is recorded here.

## Items decided after G1

- **15.2, line 0:** v2 restores the repetition, "It's the cause, it's the cause, my soul;". Line 2 ("It's the cause. Still, I won't spill her blood,") was confirmed by G1 (KEEP) and by re-checker RC2.
- **1.33:** v2 removes the late v1 gloss "— having sex" from "the beast with two backs" (G1 REVISE, high confidence). The image is left to carry itself, and blind reader B1 found appended glosses sound like an editor talking. RC1 accepted the removal.
- **15.153:** v2 reverts to "But why should honor outlive honesty?" (G1 REVISE; RC2 accept).
- **2.3 (OPEN):** v2 adopts Johnson's reading as G1 recommends. **9.59 (OPEN):** v2 keeps "secret signs". **9.172 (OPEN):** v2 keeps "pity".

Items with no v2 edit were confirmed as they stood (KEEP), or the OPEN reading G1 recommends was already the v1 wording.

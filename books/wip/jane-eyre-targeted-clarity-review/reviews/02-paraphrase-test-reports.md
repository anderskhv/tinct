# Paraphrase-only test: 24.165 (D), 34.86 (E), 34.139 (J)

Two fresh readers, each given only `02-paraphrase-test-packet.txt`. Neither was told that any passage was suspected. Their answers are recorded verbatim.

## Reader 1 (Sonnet)

Q1. Two actors act on the singer: "haughty Hate" strikes him down (and "Right" bars his approach), and "grinding Might" swears endless enmity against him, frowning furiously as it does. On first read it took a beat to realize "Right" is a second personified actor barring approach, separate from Hate — briefly parsed it as one continuous clause before sorting out the two figures (Hate/Right, then Might).

Q2. He seems lost in a private, almost reverent moment of feeling — watching the landscape, taking his hat off to let the wind touch him, looking at the sky — and then quietly, deliberately saying goodbye to something in his mind. It reads as him giving up or renouncing something (a hope, a person, a feeling) while communing with the wild scenery one last time. "In communion with the genius of the haunt" reads to me as: he's tuning into the spirit/mood of the place itself, as if the landscape has its own presiding spirit and he's silently attuned to it — spiritual absorption in the place, not literally talking to anyone.

Q3. She's disagreeing — declining to marry him, but saying she'd be fine going with him under the "as good as" terms she already proposed (as a sister, or as if she were a man/clergyman colleague), i.e., restating her refusal of marriage while accepting the missionary partnership. On first reading it did NOT read as agreement — "Very well" briefly sounds like assent, but the rest of the sentence makes clear she's holding her ground on the "not as your wife" point, so it reads as concession on the missionary work but firm refusal on marriage.

## Reader 2 (Haiku)

**Q1 — Actors and actions in the second verse:**
1. Haughty Hate — strikes the singer down
2. Right — bars approach to the singer
3. Grinding Might — swears endless enmity

**Q2 — St. John's state and "in communion with the genius of the haunt":**
St. John is deeply absorbed and reverent—gazing across the wild landscape, removing his hat, feeling the breeze, bidding farewell to something. "In communion with the genius of the haunt" means he's spiritually merged with the animating spirit or essence of the place—the moors themselves.

**Q3 — Jane's response:**
Jane is disagreeing, though wrapped in formal language. She's reframing his practical objections to emphasize that her core refusal stands regardless. On first reading, it reads *almost* like agreement because her phrasing sounds like negotiation—but she's actually holding firm.

## Lead reading of the results

- **D (24.165).** Both readers identified all three personified actors. Reader 1 took "a beat", which is normal for song verse. No misreading. **Keep.**
- **E (34.86).** Both readers resolved "genius of the haunt" as the spirit of the place. **Keep.**
- **J (34.139).** Neither reader recovered the actual sense. Jane's "Very well" answers "How can we be forever together … and unwed?" with "(we can do that) very well". Reader 1 read it as a concession on the missionary work, and Reader 2 as "almost like agreement". **Demonstrated obstacle.**

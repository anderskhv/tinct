# Blind readability report: Jane Eyre modern-English excerpts

Summary: 3 OBSTACLE (D, E, J), 8 CLEAR.

**[A] CLEAR.** Song verse: even so, the night wind blows gently, the sky is clear and the stars shine softly. God is showing his protection, which gives the orphan comfort and hope. The inverted word order ("Clouds there are none", "protection is showing") is ordinary for a song.
Words context resolves: none.

**[B] CLEAR.** Song verse: the distance between the lovers was as dangerous as a road where robbers lurk in wild country. Power, what is right, sorrow and anger all stood between them.
Words context resolves: "haunted" here means a place robbers frequent, not ghosts ("robber-path" makes that clear).

**[C] CLEAR.** Song verse: that quiet, serious joy still shines on the dark clouds of suffering. The singer no longer cares how thick and grim the disasters gathering near him are.
Words context resolves: "nigh" (near).

**[D] OBSTACLE.** Song verse, continuing "I care not... though": even if proud Hate knocks me down, Right stops me from getting near, and crushing Might swears to be my enemy forever.
- Problem words: "Right, bar approach to me". The comma plus the left-out "should" make "Right, bar..." read like an order spoken to someone ("Right, block my way!"), or "Right" can read as "OK". A reader could lose the list of personified enemies (Hate, Right, Might).
- Smallest fix: drop the comma, "Right bar approach to me," so it parallels "Hate should strike". A heavier option is "Though ... Right should bar approach to me," but that changes the meter.
Words context resolves: "enmity", "haughty".

**[E] OBSTACLE (moderate).** St. John looks around the valley, follows the stream with his eyes, then looks up at the clear sky reflected in the water. He takes off his hat and lets the wind blow on his face. He seems to be silently talking with the place and saying goodbye to it.
- Problem words: "in communion with the genius of the haunt". Modern readers take "genius" to mean a brilliant person or brilliance, and "haunt" to suggest ghosts. The meaning, "the guardian spirit of this favourite spot", is likely to be missed or only half-guessed.
- Smallest fix: "in communion with the spirit of the place".
- Small wobble, not an obstacle: in "the unclouded heaven which coloured it", "it" means the stream (the sky gives the water its colour). This is recoverable.
Words context resolves: "traverse".

**[F] CLEAR.** St. John says he isn't talking to weak people or thinking about them. He speaks only to people who are fit for the work and able to do it.

**[G] CLEAR.** Since Jane's heart says nothing, St. John will answer for it: she should come to India as his wife and co-worker.
Words context resolves: "helpmeet" (wife and partner). The next paragraphs, which say "missionary's wife", confirm it.

**[H] CLEAR.** St. John gladly agrees to give her fifteen minutes. He walks a little way up the pass, lies down on a heather-covered mound and stays still.
Words context resolves: "rejoined" (replied), "swell of heath" (a rise covered in heather).

**[I] CLEAR.** Jane says again that she will gladly go with him as a fellow missionary, but not as his wife. She won't marry him and become part of him.

**[J] OBSTACLE.** Jane's answer to "how can we be together, unmarried?" is that they could manage perfectly well, just as if she were his real sister or a fellow male clergyman.
- Problem words: "'Very well,' I said shortly". Today's readers hear "Very well" as a grudging "OK, fine", as if she is giving in. "Quite as well as if..." then doesn't make sense until they re-read it as "(we could do so) very well".
- Smallest fix: "'Perfectly well,' I said shortly; 'under the circumstances, quite as well as if...'". Another option is "We could manage very well."

**[K] CLEAR.** Jane stands up and tells him she despises his idea of love. She calls the feeling he offers fake, and says she despises him for offering it.
Words context resolves: "counterfeit".

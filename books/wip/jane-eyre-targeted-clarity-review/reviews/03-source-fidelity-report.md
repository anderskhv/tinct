# Fidelity report: Jane Eyre 34.139

## 1. Reading of the source: CORRECT
"Very well" describes how well they could be together. It does not mean "OK, I agree". Three things in the text show this:
- It follows "How can we be for ever together … and unwed?" "Very well" answers a "how" question with a degree.
- The rest of the sentence is a comparison: "quite as well as if I were … your real sister, or a man and a clergyman." "As well as" only works if "very well" means "how well".
- Jane has just refused marriage (34.137). She does not agree, and St. John's reply (34.140) argues back against her claim ("it would not do"). He does not treat it as a concession.
A reader today hears a bare "Very well" as reluctant agreement. With two of three blind readers misreading it, this is a real comprehension problem, so a change is justified.

## 2. Proposals
**P1 ("Perfectly well"): DEFECT.** It answers the "how" question correctly and keeps the short, curt tone. But it takes the phrase Jane saves for 34.141 ("It would do," I affirmed with some disdain, "perfectly well"). In the source she rises from "very well" to "perfectly well" as she grows more disdainful. P1 uses the stronger phrase first, which flattens that rise and makes 34.141 repeat her. It also changes Brontë's wording without need.

**P2 ("We could be together very well"): DEFECT (minor).** The meaning is clear and it keeps "very well", so the rise to "perfectly well" survives. But "could" makes it hypothetical and softens her. St. John asked "How *can* we", and Jane is asserting that they can. "Could" hedges that assertion, and the hedge is not needed for clarity.

## 3. Recommendation: P3
Same as P2, but "can" in place of "could". It answers the "how can we" question directly and keeps her claim assertive. Keeping "very well" means it still leads into "quite as well as" and leaves "perfectly well" for 34.141. "I said shortly" keeps the curt tone.

Final text:
“We can be together very well,” I said shortly; “under the circumstances, quite as well as if I were either your real sister, or a man and a clergyman like yourself.”

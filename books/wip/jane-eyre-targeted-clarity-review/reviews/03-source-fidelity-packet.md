## Proposed change at 34.139

CURRENT: “Very well,” I said shortly; “under the circumstances, quite as well as if I were either your real sister, or a man and a clergyman like yourself.”
PROPOSED (P1): “Perfectly well,” I said shortly; “under the circumstances, quite as well as if I were either your real sister, or a man and a clergyman like yourself.”
ALTERNATIVE (P2): “We could be together very well,” I said shortly; “under the circumstances, quite as well as if I were either your real sister, or a man and a clergyman like yourself.”

Stated reason: in present-day English a bare “Very well” is heard as reluctant assent (“OK, fine”). Here Jane is answering St. John's question “How can we be forever together … and unwed?” — i.e. “(We can be together) very well”. Three blind readers were tested; two misread the reply (as a concession, or as near-agreement).

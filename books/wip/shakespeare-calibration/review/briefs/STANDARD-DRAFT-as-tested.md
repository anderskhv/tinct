# Tinct Modern English for Shakespeare — working standard (draft for this pass)

This extends the Othello render guide (`othello/GUIDE-original-render-brief.md`). Where they differ, this file wins.

## Target

A first-time **listener** understands what each speaker means and wants. Imagery, dramatic voice, qualifications and deliberate ambiguity survive. Tinct narrates the text aloud, so every sentence must be speakable and clear by ear. Do not use brackets, slashes or footnote-style asides for glosses.

## Rules

1. **Faithful and complete.** Carry every clause, image, argument, oath, joke, insult and qualification. Omit nothing and add nothing. Do not summarize, moralize or add motives, facts or emotions the original does not state or clearly imply. A short gloss that only unpacks the original's meaning is allowed.
2. **Hard lines get rewritten, clear lines stay clear.** Never leave a hard metaphor, archaic idiom or false friend sitting untranslated with modern pronouns added. Keep the image **and** make its point clear, briefly, in the same sentence. Keep famous lines that are already clear recognizable.
3. **Natural modern sentences, not forced line order.** Do not force one modern line per original verse line. Within a sentence or clause group, use natural modern word order. The alignment unit is a **short corresponding unit of meaning**: usually one sentence or clause, covering about one to four verse lines. Each modern unit must contain the content of its original lines and nothing from neighbouring units.
4. **Puns and double meanings.** Keep both senses where possible. When one English word cannot carry both, word the line so the listener hears the joke. A short appositive is fine ("lie, meaning sleep, and lie, meaning tell lies"). Never quietly drop a pun or turn it into its explanation alone.
5. **Bawdy, insults and prejudice** stay as strong as the original, and become understandable. Do not soften, do not coarsen beyond the original, and do not add obscenity.
6. **Register and voice.** Formal speakers stay formal; clowns and servants stay colloquial. Grand verse should still sound grand in plain words. Avoid anachronistic slang ("sucker", "cool", "OK", "guys").
7. **Deliberate ambiguity.** Where the original is deliberately ambiguous, keep an equally open wording. Do not resolve it for the reader. Where it is a textual crux, choose the standard reading and record the choice.
8. **Conventions.** Keep speaker prefixes verbatim (`LEAR.`, `FOOL.`) and stage directions as the edition has them. Use straight apostrophes, curly double quotes, and spaced em dashes; never use ellipses. Keep `_..._` song italics in the same places. Change `thou`/`thee`/`thy` to `you`/`your`. Handle oaths as oaths ("By God's wounds", "Damn it"). Keep proper names, places and allusions, adding a few clarifying words if an allusion is opaque.
9. **Sources.** Work from the public-domain original and reliable annotations only. Acceptable: the play text, Alexander Schmidt's *Shakespeare-Lexicon* (1874/1902, on Perseus), C. T. Onions's *A Shakespeare Glossary* (1911), and the Furness *New Variorum* notes (public domain). **Never consult, recall or imitate any copyrighted modernization or translation** (No Fear Shakespeare, Shakespeare Made Easy, SparkNotes, LitCharts, etc.).

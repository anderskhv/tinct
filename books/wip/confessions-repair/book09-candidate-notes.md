# Confessions Book 9 — Modern English Candidate Notes

## 1. Ambiguity preserved

- **P1** — "this Thy whole gift was, to nill what I willed, and to will what Thou willedst": kept as a stated transformation of will, not explained or motivated further ("that I should stop willing what I willed, and will what you willed"). No causal gloss added for *how* this happened — the paragraph itself asks that as an open question ("out of what low and deep hiding place... was my free will summoned forth").
- **P16** — "did we not run after Thee" is punctuated as a statement in the source (not a question, despite the rhetorical "did we not" phrasing). Kept as a period-ended clause rather than "normalizing" it into a question, per the question-mark gate.
- **P24 (Ostia ascent)** — "she is not made, but is, as she hath been, and so shall she be ever; yea rather, to 'have been,' and 'hereafter to be,' are not in her" — kept the paradox intact (Wisdom has no tense, only "being") rather than simplifying into "she always existed." The philosophical density is Augustine's, not resolved.
- **P28** — Monica's reported words are given exactly as reported speech with the ambiguity intact: Augustine says he "did not know" when her wish to be buried beside Patricius faded, and only infers it afterward from a separate anecdote. I did not tie those two threads together explicitly — the source leaves the inference implicit and so does this rendering.
- **P37** — "Who shall restore to Him the innocent blood?" kept as a real open rhetorical question (about the debt owed for Christ's blood), not resolved into a statement.

## 2. Technical/proper terms kept

Cassiciacum (standardized spelling per carry-forward list; source text alternates and spells it "Cassiacum" once — normalized to "Cassiciacum"), Ambrose, Alypius, Adeodatus, Navigius (Augustine's brother, referred to only as "my brother" in this book's Latin/Pusey text — not named in Book 9 itself, so left as "my brother" per source, not silently inserted), Nebridius, Patricius, Monica (source spells "Monnica" once at the very end of P38; standardized to "Monica" to match the rest of the project and book08-accepted.json convention), Ostia, Euodius, Verecundus, Justina, Valentinian, Gervasius, Protasius, Isaiah. "The Master" (Augustine's dialogue *De Magistro*) rendered as "The Teacher" — a defensible modern title choice, flagged here in case the project prefers to keep the literal "The Master."

## 3. Hardest paragraphs and tradeoffs

- **P23–P25 (the Ostia vision)** — the single hardest stretch in the book. P23 sets the scene (window, garden, alone with Monica). P24 is the ascent: bodily things → the sky → inward musing → their own minds → beyond their minds → the region of Wisdom → one instant of contact → then explicitly *falling back* to spoken words ("returned to vocal expressions of our mouth, where the word spoken has beginning and end"). I preserved this order exactly — ascend, briefly touch, then descend to speech — and did not compress or reverse any step. P25 restates the same movement conditionally ("if the tumult of the flesh were hushed... if this could go on...") as an extended periodic sentence; I kept it as one long conditional sentence rather than breaking it into several independent sentences, since the suspended, unresolved grammar is part of the effect Augustine is building toward the two closing questions.
- **P26 (Monica's stated reason)** — the causal claim is specific and easy to flatten: she wanted to live *specifically* to see Augustine become "a Catholic Christian," and her hopes are now "accomplished" specifically because of that, not because of any general contentment. I kept "that I might see you become a Catholic Christian before I died" as the explicit stated cause, and kept the "more abundantly" clause (seeing him also renounce earthly happiness) as an addition to, not a replacement for, that original reason.
- **P29–P36 (grief sequence)** — the hardest emotional material. Augustine's self-conflict about whether grief is appropriate is not a settled position; he argues both sides across several paragraphs: he suppresses tears (P29) because grieving would imply she was "unhappy" or "altogether dead" (which he denies); he then feels a real wound and is "displeased" that this "human feeling" had such power over him and grieves a second time over his own grief (P31: "with a new grief I grieved for my grief... a double sorrow"); he tries a bath, sleep, an Ambrose hymn; and only in P34 does he let the tears come, explicitly invoking the reader's possible judgment and defending himself without ever fully resolving whether the earlier suppression or the later weeping was the "correct" response. I kept both poles of that conflict stated, did not resolve it into either "grief is fine" or "grief is shameful," and preserved "I grieved with a fresh grief over my own grief" as a doubled, not singular, sorrow. P35 reintroduces a *third* register — tears of intercessory fear, distinct from the personal grief already spent — and I kept it as a stated contrast ("a far different kind of tears") rather than merging it with the earlier grief.
- **P18 (Monica and wine)** — six question marks in one paragraph, several stacked in immediate succession ("what didst Thou then... how didst Thou cure her? how heal her?"). Kept each as a separate short question rather than merging any pair, to preserve the exact count and the rhetorical hammering effect.

## 5. Polarity/direction double-checks

Paragraphs where comparison, causal claim, negation, a stated position, or ascent/descent sequencing were specifically checked against the source for direction:

- **P1** — "to nill what I willed, and to will what Thou willedst": direction is *his* will conforming to God's will, not the reverse. Checked and preserved.
- **P9** — "For till then, the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified": causal order preserved (glorification precedes/causes the giving of the Spirit, not the other way around).
- **P17/P18** — the comparison "for her good discipline was she wont to commend not so much her mother's diligence, as that of a certain decrepit maid-servant": direction is credit going *more* to the old servant than to the mother, not the reverse — checked and preserved ("she used to give credit less to her mother's diligence than to that of a certain elderly maidservant").
- **P18** — "As flattering friends pervert, so reproachful enemies mostly correct": kept the paired comparison in the same order and polarity (friends who flatter corrupt; enemies who insult correct) — not swapped.
- **P18** — "Yet not what by them Thou doest, but what themselves purposed, dost Thou repay them": God repays people for their *intentions*, not for the good outcome he worked through them — this is a specific, easy-to-invert claim (it would be natural to accidentally write "repays them for what he does through them"); checked and kept in the source's order.
- **P22** — "nor had she to complain of that in him as a believer, which before he was a believer she had borne from him": the *before/after* structure (things she tolerated pre-conversion, no longer present post-conversion) is preserved in that order.
- **P23–P24 (Ostia ascent/descent)** — checked line by line against the ascending sequence (bodily things → heaven → inward musing → their own minds → beyond their minds → the region of Wisdom → momentary touch) followed by the explicit *falling back* to spoken words. Not reversed, not collapsed into a single non-sequential list.
- **P26** — Monica's causal claim double-checked word by word: she wanted to live to see Augustine "a Catholic Christian" specifically — not general well-being, not seeing grandchildren (that hope is explicitly renounced elsewhere in the Confessions and not reintroduced here), not any other stated reason. The "more abundantly" clause is additive (seeing him renounce earthly happiness too), not a replacement cause.
- **P28** — the direction of "so little can the human mind embrace things divine" is preserved as Augustine's retrospective judgment on Monica's *earlier*, more worldly wish (for burial beside her husband) — not applied to her later, more detached state near death. Sequence (earlier worldly wish → later renunciation, discovered afterward) kept in source order.
- **P29** — negation checked: "she was neither unhappy in her death, nor altogether dead" — both negations kept, not simplified to one.
- **P30** — comparison "what comparison is there betwixt that honour that I paid to her, and her slavery for me?" — direction kept: his devotion to her was minor compared to her lifelong service to him, not the reverse.
- **P31/P34** — Augustine's self-conflict over grief (is it shameful, or natural, or both) is preserved as unresolved across paragraphs, per carry-forward instruction 5 — see section 3 above for detail. Not flattened to a single settled position in either direction.
- **P35** — "we confidently hope to find some place with Thee" vs. "woe be even unto the commendable life of men, if... Thou shouldest examine it": kept both sides of the tension (human merit is real but insufficient on its own; mercy, not strict justice, is the basis of hope) without resolving which "wins."

## 1. Ambiguity preserved

- **The formless-matter / heaven-of-heavens distinction** is kept exact throughout: the heaven of heavens is "some intellectual creature" that "although in no way coeternal with you, the Trinity, still shares in your eternity" (¶9), never described as eternal in itself, never blurred into God, and never collapsed into ordinary physical heaven. Formless matter (earth invisible and without form / the deep) is kept as "almost nothing," created, not eternal, and distinguished from formed matter throughout (¶¶3, 7–9, 14–21).
- **Augustine's qualified pluralism** is preserved as qualified, not flattened into relativism ("all readings are true") or into exclusivism ("only one reading is true"). Key formulations kept intact: "several different things may be understood by these words, and yet all of them true" (¶26); the explicit "I do not know" which reading Moses meant, paired with "I know that those readings are true, apart from the carnal ones" (¶40); the famous "Why not rather both ways, if both are true?" (¶41) — kept as a question, not asserted as settled fact; and the qualification in ¶41 that a reading is legitimate only if "not being false" / "not false" — i.e. plural truth, not unlimited license.
- **The two kinds of disagreement** (¶31: about the truth of the created world vs. about what the human author Moses meant) is kept as a real distinction, not merged.
- Augustine's own stated preference (¶40, "he had in mind whichever of these readings chiefly excels the others... both in the light of truth it gives and in the fruitfulness of the good it does") is kept as a preference *among valid readings*, not a claim that other readings are false.
- Sentence-length and clause density were preserved rather than broken up into many short sentences where doing so would have supplied connective logic (causation, contrast) not present in the Latin/Pusey syntax — especially in ¶¶6, 18, 29–30, 39, the longest and most periodic paragraphs.

## 2. Technical/proper terms kept and why

Kept consistent across all 42 paragraphs, matching the standing project glossary:
- "heaven and earth"
- "without form and void" is NOT the phrasing used by this book — Pusey's actual recurring phrase is "invisible and without form" (not "void"), so I kept **"invisible and without form"** verbatim as the fixed technical phrase throughout, rather than introducing "void," since the source book12 never uses "void" and introducing it would create false terminological drift from the source text itself.
- "heaven of heavens" — kept as the fixed name for the intelligible/near-eternal creation, never varied to "heaven of heaven" or "highest heaven," etc.
- "formless matter" — kept as the fixed name for created, near-nothing corporeal/spiritual substrate, consistently distinguished from "the heaven of heavens."
- "the deep" — kept for *abyssus*, consistently paired with "darkness was upon the deep."
- "coeternal" — kept as a term of art (not glossed as "as old as," "equally eternal," etc.) because the book's whole argument turns on this precise technical distinction between "eternal in itself" and "created but near-eternal, not coeternal."

## 3. Hardest paragraphs and tradeoffs

- **¶6** (the "forms"/formlessness self-interrogation, "Is it soul? Is it body?..."): very long, single-sentence-chained meditation. Tradeoff: broke the single 400+ word source sentence into a few sentences for modern readability, but kept every clause and the full "nothing-something"/"is, is not" paradox intact rather than paraphrasing it away.
- **¶18** ("Will you affirm that to be false...", the dialectic on divine eternity vs. mutable will): dense scholastic syllogism-like structure with repeated "our God is eternal" refrains and nested rhetorical questions. Kept the refrain repetition (not varied for elegance) since the repetition is argumentative, not decorative.
- **¶29–30** (the imagined objector's argument about the waters above/below the firmament): the longest and most legally/dialectically constructed paragraph in the book, with an embedded counter-objection and an embedded response-in-quotes. Tradeoff: removed nested single-quote scripture citations in favor of running them in as plain capitalized clauses (matching the source's own convention — Pusey does not nest-quote scripture inside the dialogic quotes either), to avoid introducing single-quote-as-quotation-mark usage while keeping the paragraph readable at that quote depth.
- **¶39** (matter/tune/sound analogy: "the sound is before the tune"): the trickiest philosophical analogy in the book (matter precedes form in *origin* but not necessarily in *time* or *worth*). Preserved all four senses of priority Augustine distinguishes (by eternity / by time / by choice / by origin) in the same order, without collapsing any of them together.
- **¶41** ("Why not rather as both, if both be true?"): single most load-bearing sentence in the book for the pluralism claim. Kept as an exact rhetorical question, not restated as a flat assertion, to preserve Augustine's own hedged, invitational phrasing rather than overclaiming on his behalf.

## 5. Interpretation-comparison / contradiction-claim / formless-matter–heaven-of-heavens paragraphs — polarity double-check

Every paragraph below involves a comparison between interpretations, a claim about what does/doesn't contradict scripture or truth, or the formless-matter/heaven-of-heavens distinction. For each, I confirm the direction of the claim was checked against source and not inverted:

- **¶2** — heaven of heavens vs. visible heaven/earth ("both these great bodies... may not absurdly be called earth" next to the unknown heaven). Direction kept: physical heaven+earth are relatively "earth" compared to heaven of heavens, not the reverse.
- **¶3, ¶7, ¶8, ¶9** — formless matter is created, "almost nothing," not coeternal; heaven of heavens shares eternity but is *not* coeternal with God. Checked: neither is ever called coeternal; the "shares in eternity but is not coeternal" qualifier is present in every relevant paragraph, not dropped.
- **¶11, ¶12, ¶13** — heaven of heavens' relation to time/eternity ("though it be not coeternal with Thee, yet by continually and unfailingly cleaving unto Thee, suffers no changeableness of times"). Kept: near-eternal *because of clinging to God*, not eternal *in itself* — the causal dependency direction was checked and preserved.
- **¶16** — the "on account of these two" argument (heaven of heavens = formed; earth invisible/without form = formless) mapped onto "In the Beginning God created Heaven and Earth" without mention of days. Checked the two-term mapping wasn't swapped (formed↔heaven of heavens; formless↔earth invisible and without form).
- **¶17, ¶18, ¶21** — Augustine vs. the "extollers of Genesis" who say the Spirit "would not have those words thus understood... but otherwise." Checked Augustine's answer is not conceding their exclusivism, and that he's disputing their *exclusive* claim, not their reading per se — he goes on to establish common ground on doctrine (¶18) before turning to the actual disagreement (¶21).
- **¶18** — "our God is eternal" refrain syllogism: checked the conditionals run in the stated direction (no mutable thing is eternal → our God is eternal, not the converse).
- **¶21** — the "matter was without form... where no order was, there could be no vicissitude of times" claim. Checked the dependency (no form → no order → no time-succession) runs the same direction as source, not reversed into "no time-succession → no form."
- **¶23, ¶24, ¶25** — three different named readings of what "heaven and earth" first signifies (spiritual+corporeal creature already formed / visible world summed up / formless matter of both). Checked each reading is attributed distinctly and not merged or swapped with another reading later re-summarized in ¶28, ¶32–33, ¶37–38.
- **¶26** — the core pluralism statement: "since divers things may be understood under these words which yet are all true... what doth it prejudice me, if I think otherwise than another thinketh the writer thought?" Checked this is phrased as *no harm in differing*, not as *no such thing as being wrong* — the "though not this truth" qualifier at the end (still true, just not the same truth) was kept, preserving that both readers are still bound to actual truth, not equally free of that constraint.
- **¶27** — the "It is true..." litany: eight independent true claims, each kept as an independent assertion rather than chained into a single derivation (source presents them as parallel truths, not as a logical sequence where each depends on the last).
- **¶28, ¶29** — the five named readings of "In the Beginning God made heaven and earth" and five of "the earth was invisible..." Checked each reading's content maps 1:1 to its slot and none were swapped between the two lists.
- **¶30** — the objector's challenge (if formlessness isn't "heaven and earth," God left something uncreated) and the two-view defenders' rebuttal (waters above/below firmament). Checked the rebuttal's logic direction: absence of an explicit "God made X" statement in Genesis does *not* imply X is uncreated or coeternal — this is the crux claim and was checked twice against source, since an inversion here ("silence implies coeternity") would be a serious theological error the source explicitly rejects ("why...should we not understand that formless matter...to have been created of God out of nothing, and therefore not to be coeternal to Him").
- **¶31–34** — the "two kinds of disagreement" (truth of things vs. meaning of Moses) and Augustine's refusal to claim certainty about Moses's actual intended meaning even while claiming certainty about the theological truths themselves. Checked this asymmetry (confident about truth, humble about authorial intent) was not collapsed into uniform confidence or uniform doubt.
- **¶33–34** — "if we both see that to be true... where do we see it? ... both in the unchangeable Truth itself." Checked the point is that neither party is the *source* of the truth they see — this is a claim about the location/ground of truth, not a claim that both parties automatically see identical content.
- **¶39** — matter precedes form "by origin," not "by time" (sound/tune analogy) and God precedes all things "by eternity." Checked the four kinds of priority (eternity / time / choice / origin) were not conflated — this is exactly the kind of subtle distinction the carry-forward brief warns is at risk of being blurred.
- **¶40–42** — the closing pluralism/humility passage: "I do not know" which meaning Moses intended, paired with "I know that those senses are true, those carnal ones excepted." Checked the carve-out for carnal/literalist-anthropomorphic readings (God as a man deciding suddenly, ¶36) is preserved as excluded from the pool of "true but divers" readings — the pluralism is not unlimited; only non-carnal, non-false readings qualify. ¶41's "Why not rather as both, if both be true?" and "if any other seeth any other truth" were both kept conditional on truth, not asserted as blanket endorsement of any reading whatsoever.

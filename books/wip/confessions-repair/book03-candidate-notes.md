# Confessions Book 3 — Modern English candidate: editorial notes

## 1. Deliberate ambiguities preserved (not resolved)

- **Para 0** ("I loved not yet, yet I loved to love... out of a deep-seated want, I hated myself for wanting not"): the source's paradox structure is kept as-is rather than flattened into a causal explanation. I did not supply what the "want" actually was.
- **Para 2**: the whole paragraph is a chain of rhetorical questions about whether grief/compassion can be "loved." I kept every question as a question ("Should compassion, then, be thrown out?", "Where does that stream go?") rather than turning Augustine's self-interrogation into declarative claims.
- **Para 8**: "they seemed to me unworthy to be compared to the stateliness of Tully" — kept as Augustine's own then-held (mistaken) judgment, not corrected or editorialized against.
- **Para 12**: "Where does evil come from?" and the other Manichee questions are left as the loaded rhetorical questions his interlocutors posed to him, not resolved into a doctrinal answer within the paragraph (the resolution — "evil is a privation of good" — is exactly what he then says he didn't yet understand, so I preserved that sequence).
- **Para 16**: the distinction between actions that only *look* like sin ("we can't tell whether it's out of a lust of having/hurting") is kept genuinely undecidable, per source — I did not resolve the ambiguity about the actor's motive.
- **Para 20**: the bishop's reasons for refusing are given in his own reported words; I did not add motive beyond what Augustine states ("wisely, as I afterwards perceived" — kept as Augustine's later judgment, not the bishop's stated reason).

## 2. Technical/theological terms kept untranslated

- **Manichees / Manichaean** — kept as the proper term throughout, per instructions, since it's used across the rest of the book. Added the shortest possible first-use gloss at its one explicit naming in para 20 ("a heretical sect claiming secret spiritual knowledge, which Augustine belonged to for years") since that's the paragraph where a new reader first needs to know what it names; left it unglossed in earlier paragraphs (17, 21 in source) where it already appears in context that makes its sense clear (fig-tree doctrine, "Elect" saints) and a second gloss would be redundant.
- **"Elect"** (Manichaean rank of ascetic initiates who supposedly could release trapped divine particles by eating) — kept in quotes as a title/rank rather than translated, since it's Augustine's sarcastic citation of their own term for themselves.
- **Hortensius** — kept as the proper title of Cicero's (lost) work, per source.
- Scriptural quotation in para 7 (Colossians 2:8–9, unattributed in Pusey) — modernized into plain English rather than left in KJV-adjacent phrasing, since the instructions call for genuine modernization; kept it as a quoted block since Augustine is quoting it as text.

## 3. Paragraphs that were unusually difficult, and the tradeoffs made

- **Para 9 (source paragraph index 8, "For other than this...")**: this is one long, dense sentence-chain in the original with heavily nested clauses (the "food in sleep" analogy, the ranking of degrees of certainty among fantasies/images/bodies/soul/God). I broke it into more, shorter sentences than the original's few very long ones, but kept every comparative step in the chain (fantasy < image < body < soul < God) in the same order, since the argument's force depends on that ladder. This is the single largest structural departure from source sentence boundaries in the whole book, but no content was cut.
- **Para 12 (source's "Nor knew I that true inward righteousness...")**: the armory/greaves-and-helmet and shop-hours analogies are quite compressed and rely on unfamiliar period detail (greaves = shin armor; afternoon shop closure). I rendered them plainly enough to be followed by a reader unfamiliar with either, without adding explanatory material beyond what's needed to parse the image itself.
- **Para 15 (source's "So in acts of violence...")**: this paragraph is essentially a taxonomy (lust of the flesh / eye / rule, mapped onto "three and seven" i.e. the Ten Commandments) compressed into one sentence with many embedded examples. I kept it as a single long sentence with semicolon-separated clauses rather than splitting it, since splitting risked implying the listed motives (revenge, profit, fear, envy, cruelty) are independent claims rather than items in one enumerated list — Augustine is building a taxonomy, and the run-on structure carries that.
- **Para 20 (final paragraph)**: two clauses ("as I afterwards perceived" and the bishop's own backstory) are subordinate/parenthetical in a way that's easy to accidentally promote into main clauses when modernizing. I kept both subordinate, matching the source's judgment that Augustine's own later interpretive gloss ("wisely, as I afterwards perceived") is distinct from the bishop's own stated reasons.

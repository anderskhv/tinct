## Paragraph 9

**F15 (MAJOR) — applied.** Direction inversion: the candidate attached "who might, perhaps, never fully attain it himself" to the beggar, which is self-contradictory (the sentence already states he had reached that joy). In the source, the relative clause attaches to Augustine and his companions — they, not the beggar, might never attain it. Fixed the referent.

- Old: `we were only hoping to arrive at that very cheerfulness which that beggar had already reached ahead of us — who might, perhaps, never fully attain it himself.`
- New: `we were only hoping to arrive at that very cheerfulness which that beggar had already reached ahead of us — while we, perhaps, would never reach it at all.`

**F16 (minor) — applied.** Restored the source's ask-then-flatly-answer rhetorical structure (the candidate had turned Augustine's two blunt self-indicting admissions into rhetorical questions). Question-mark count for the paragraph is unaffected (still 3).

- Old: `But if someone had asked me whether I would rather be cheerful or fearful, would I not have answered cheerful? And again, if asked whether I would rather be as he was, or what I then was — would I not have chosen to remain myself, worn down as I was by cares and fears?`
- New: `But if someone had asked me whether I would rather be cheerful or fearful? I would have answered: cheerful. And again, if he asked whether I would rather be as he was, or what I then was? I would have chosen to remain myself, worn down as I was by cares and fears.`

## Paragraph 15

## Paragraph 25

**F30 (minor, explicitly worth taking) — applied.** *Regnum* ("dominion") is the point — the disease is carried into marriage as into a kingdom it will rule. "State of marriage" is inert by comparison.

- Old: `all the way into the state of marriage`
- New: `all the way into the dominion of marriage`

**F31 (minor, explicitly worth taking) — applied.** "festered" loses the medical logic the source's clause depends on: mortification is necrosis (the tissue dies), which is exactly *why* the pain becomes less acute even as the condition grows more desperate. A festering wound would hurt *more*, undercutting the sentence's own logic. Used "went dead" (the review's alternate proposed wording) rather than reintroducing "mortified," since "went dead" reads clearly in modern prose while preserving the necrosis logic.

- Old: `instead, after burning and the sharpest pain, it festered, and my pain grew less acute, but more hopeless`
- New: `instead, after burning and the sharpest pain, it went dead, and my pain grew less acute, but more hopeless`

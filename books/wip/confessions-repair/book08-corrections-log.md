## MAJOR fixes (2/2 addressed)

### Index 21 (review "para 22") — direction inversion in the two-wills conclusion
- **What was wrong:** Candidate asserted "because *neither* one is whole and entire," garbling the argument's own conclusion. Source states there are two wills *because one of them* is not whole — i.e., the two-wills condition arises precisely from one will's incompleteness (what one lacks, the other has), not from both being deficient in the same way.
- **Old:** "And so there are two wills, because neither one is whole and entire — and what the one lacks, the other has."
- **New:** "And so there are two wills, because one of them is not whole and entire — and what the one lacks, the other has."
- **Reason:** Restores Pusey's "for that one of them is not entire," the load-bearing conclusion that paras 23–25's anti-Manichee argument depends on.

### Index 30 (review "para 31") — direction inversion in the final clause
- **What was wrong:** Candidate said Monica's joy came "through grandchildren of my body," implying the joy arrived *by means of* literal grandchildren. This contradicts the immediately preceding clause in the same paragraph ("I no longer sought a wife or any hope in this world") and inverts the source's sense: the joy was purer *than* the grandchildren-based joy she used to ask for — it came instead through Augustine's conversion.
- **Old:** "And you turned her mourning into joy far more abundant than she had desired, and in a much dearer and purer way than she had ever looked for, through grandchildren of my body."
- **New:** "And you turned her mourning into joy far more abundant than she had desired, and far dearer and purer than the joy she had once asked you for, in grandchildren of my body."
- **Reason:** Restores the comparison ("purer than the way she used to ask for it, i.e. by grandchildren") instead of attributing the actual joy to grandchildren.

## MODERATE fixes (3/3 addressed)

### Index 29 (review "para 30") — "certainty" rejected, "peace" restored
- **What was wrong:** Candidate's "a light of certainty" contradicts the book's thrice-stated position (paras 1, 13, 19 — indices 0, 12, 18) that Augustine already possessed intellectual certainty; the deficit resolved at tolle-lege is affective/existential (securitas — freedom from anxiety), not epistemic.
- **Old:** "...it was as though a light of certainty had been poured into my heart, and every shadow of doubt melted away."
- **New:** "...it was as though a light of peace had been poured into my heart, and every shadow of doubt melted away."
- **Reason:** Per review section 3 — restores an affective word; the paired "doubt vanished" clause is kept as-is, unchanged.

### Index 17 (review "para 18") — garbled a fortiori
- **What was wrong:** Candidate's parenthetical "— not even the finding of it —" reads as excluding finding from what matters, reversing the source's a fortiori ("not the finding only, but the very search... was to be preferred").
- **Old:** "...to devote myself to searching for that wisdom whose very search — not even the finding of it — was worth more than the treasures and kingdoms of the world, even if already found,..."
- **New:** "...to devote myself to searching for that wisdom, of which not the finding only but the very search was worth more than the treasures and kingdoms of the world, even if already found,..."
- **Reason:** Restores the "not X only, but even Y" construction so the search is the surprising, included term, per review's proposed correction.

### Index 7 (review "para 8") — invented clause, dropped clause
- **What was wrong:** Candidate dropped the source's "with what joy" (describing the joy of the *bringing back* itself) and invented "to the joy of all the angels," which is nowhere in this paragraph and pre-empts a beat the source saves for its own close ("Thou rejoicest in us, and in Thy holy angels").
- **Old:** "And we too listen with great joy, whenever we hear how the sheep that had strayed is carried home on the shepherd's shoulders, to the joy of all the angels; and how the lost coin is returned..."
- **New:** "And we too listen with great joy, whenever we hear with what joy the sheep that had strayed is carried home on the shepherd's shoulders; and how the lost coin is returned..."
- **Reason:** Removes the invented angels clause and restores "with what joy," per review's proposed correction.

## MINOR fixes (8 of 9 applied with text changes; 1 documented as no-change)

### Index 0 (review "para 1") — technical term inconsistency
- **Old:** "...an incorruptible reality, the source of all other reality..."
- **New:** "...an incorruptible substance, the source of all other substance..."
- **Reason:** "Substance" is the established metaphysical term across accepted books 3–6 and is used correctly elsewhere in this same candidate (index 24/para 25); "reality" breaks that link.

### Index 2 (review "para 3") — supplied mechanism not in source
- **Old:** "...the man through whose hand Ambrose (now a bishop) had received your grace..."
- **New:** "...who was father to Ambrose (now a bishop) in his receiving of your grace..."
- **Reason:** Source says only that Simplicianus was Ambrose's spiritual father in receiving grace; "through whose hand" supplies an unstated sacramental mechanism (added interpretive connective).

### Index 5 (review "para 6") — register/diction
- **Old:** "by what means did you find your way into that man's chest?"
- **New:** "by what means did you carry yourself into that man's heart?"
- **Reason:** "Chest" is anatomical and flattens the devotional image of "convey Thyself"; "find your way" softens the active verb. Restored per review's proposed correction.

### Index 9 (review "para 10") — term drift breaks a parallel
- **Old:** "This law holds in shameful and cursed pleasure; it holds in permitted and lawful pleasure; it holds in the very purest and highest form of friendship;..."
- **New:** "This law holds in shameful and cursed joy; it holds in permitted and lawful joy; it holds in the very purest and highest form of friendship;..."
- **Reason:** The paragraph's argument depends on one word ("joy") covering both the foul case and the holy case, making the closing universal law ("everywhere the greater joy is ushered in by greater pain") land correctly. Splitting into "pleasure"/"joy" breaks that identity.

### Index 10 (review "para 11") — image substitution and doubled intensifier
- **Old:** "...should be taken; all the more should your children rejoice all the more abundantly, because our King bound the strong man, and they saw his weapons taken from him, cleaned, and made fit for your honor,..."
- **New:** "...should be taken; all the more abundantly should your children rejoice, because our King bound the strong man, and they saw the vessels taken from him, cleaned, and made fit for your honor,..."
- **Reason:** "Weapons... cleaned and made fit for honor" is incoherent as an image and collides with the earlier "mighty and sharp weapon" (the tongue); "vessels" restores the Mark 3:27/2 Tim 2:21 image. The doubled "all the more... all the more abundantly" read as an editing artifact and is collapsed to a single intensifier.

### Index 11 (review "para 12") — reordered pairing
- **Old:** "So my two wills, one old and one new, one carnal and the other spiritual, fought within me,..."
- **New:** "So my two wills, one new and one old, one carnal and the other spiritual, fought within me,..."
- **Reason:** Source deliberately crosses the pairing (new/old then carnal/spiritual, so "new" maps to "spiritual" across the chiasmus); candidate had silently un-crossed it.

### Index 24 (review "para 25") — technical term
- **Old:** "...that the conflict is between two opposing souls, of two opposing substances, from two opposing sources, one good and the other bad."
- **New:** "...that the conflict is between two opposing souls, of two opposing substances, from two opposing principles, one good and the other bad."
- **Reason:** "Principles" is the Manichee doctrine's own term of art, sitting in a triad with "souls" and "substances," both already technical in this sentence.

### Index 28 (review "para 29") — softened biblical image
- **Old:** "...and the flood from my eyes burst out as an offering acceptable to you."
- **New:** "...and the flood from my eyes burst out as a sacrifice acceptable to you."
- **Reason:** Psalm 51's "sacrifice" is the specific word and stronger than the generic "offering."

### Index 18 (review "para 19") — declined text change; documented emendation only
- **Finding:** The locked source file reads "nor for often years and more have been thinking thereon" — almost certainly a corruption of the standard Pusey "ten years." The candidate already reads "who have not spent ten years or more thinking about it," which is very likely the correct reading but is a silent departure from the locked ground-truth file.
- **Action taken:** No text change (candidate's "ten years" is kept as-is in the corrected file, since it is very likely correct and changing it to match the corrupted locked source would introduce a worse error). This is recorded here per the review's recommendation, rather than passed through unnoticed.
- **Text (unchanged):** "...while people who have not worn themselves out searching for it, who have not spent ten years or more thinking about it, have had their shoulders lightened and grown wings to fly away."

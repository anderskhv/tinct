# Book 10 Candidate — Drafting Notes

## 1. Ambiguity preserved

- **§7 ("we now see through a glass darkly")** — kept the 1 Corinthians allusion's inherent vagueness about *what* is seen indistinctly; did not resolve it into "we cannot yet fully understand God."
- **§17 (three kinds of questions: whether a thing is / what it is / what kind it is)** — kept as three bare interrogative fragments, not expanded into full sentences, since Augustine is citing a technical Aristotelian/Ciceronian topos he assumes the reader half-knows.
- **§21 ("the memory is, as it were, the belly of the mind")** — kept the digestion metaphor literal (stomach) rather than softening it into a general "container," since §22 depends on it (chewing the cud / tasting).
- **§29 ("What third way is there?")** — preserved the open, unresolved structure — Augustine does not answer this rhetorical question within the paragraph; the puzzle carries into §29 (misnumbered in my working notes as the "toil" paragraph) unresolved.
- **§33 ("whether all severally, or in that man who first sinned...")** — kept the parenthetical aside about Adam unresolved and set aside exactly as Augustine sets it aside ("I now enquire not"), rather than either developing or deleting it.
- **§53 (the aesthetics paragraph on manufactured beauty)** — kept the exclamation-then-declarative shift (men "destroying what they themselves have been made to be!") without editorializing on what that destruction consists of.
- **§67 (the mediator argument)** — preserved the compressed logical form ("if in both respects like man... if in both respects like God...") without spelling out the syllogism further than Augustine does.

## 2. Technical terms kept consistent throughout

- **memory** — always "memory," never "recollection" or "mind's storehouse" as a substitute (though "storehouse," "treasury," "storeroom" are used *in addition to* "memory" only where Augustine himself varies his own figurative vocabulary — harbour, palaces, chambers, treasury — never as a replacement for the literal term memoria itself).
- **the mind** — always "the mind," distinguished consistently from "memory" per §21's explicit argument that memory is mind's "stomach," not mind itself.
- **forgetfulness** — always "forgetfulness," never "loss of memory" or "oblivion," since §§24–29 turn on this single term's paradox.
- **image(s)** (imago) — always "image"/"images," never "picture," "impression," or "likeness" as a substitute, since the whole memory epistemology (§§13–30) hinges on the image/thing distinction.
- **the happy life** (vita beata) — always "happy life," never "happiness" alone, to preserve Augustine's treatment of it as a state one *has* or *seeks*, paralleling his treatment of memory as a place one searches.
- **continence/continency** — rendered "continence" (modern spelling) consistently, not "self-control" or "chastity," since Augustine's usage spans sexual and non-sexual restraint (food, curiosity, pride) and a narrower word would falsely restrict it.
- **lust of the flesh / lust of the eyes / pride of life** — kept as the fixed triad throughout (matching 1 John 2:16), never varied to "desire of the body" etc., since Augustine's whole structural argument in §§41–70 is organized around this named threefold division.
- **Truth** (capitalized when referring to God/the divine attribute) vs. "truth" (lowercase, the general concept) — distinguished by capitalization following the source's own capitalization pattern, most visibly in §§33–34 and §37.
- **cogitation/cogo/cogito** — kept the Latin wordplay untranslated in §18, exactly as Pusey does, since English has no equivalent pun on "gather/re-gather," and a translation-only rendering would destroy Augustine's actual point about etymology.

## 3. Hardest paragraphs and tradeoffs

**§17 and §18 (memory storage / cogitation)** — These involve Augustine distinguishing three modes of memory content: images of sensed things (§17), the things themselves without images (liberal arts/math, §18–19), and re-collection as literal re-gathering (§18's cogo/cogito etymology). The hardest tradeoff was rendering "the mind hath appropriated to itself this word" without turning it into a vague claim about language in general — kept it narrowly about the specific word "cogitation" claiming a specific meaning, as the source does.

**§24–29 (the forgetfulness paradox)** — This is the single hardest stretch in the book. Augustine's argument is genuinely self-undermining by design: to remember forgetfulness, forgetfulness must have been "present" to leave an image, but forgetfulness's defining property is that its presence erases what it finds. I preserved the four-step structure exactly: (1) memory of memory is direct self-presence; (2) memory of forgetfulness requires both terms present simultaneously; (3) this seems impossible since forgetfulness-present-in-full would mean total erasure, not memory; (4) resolution attempt: forgetfulness is present *by image, not by itself* — except that images require the thing's own prior presence to be stamped, and forgetfulness's presence erases rather than stamps. I did not resolve this for him or add a tidying transition — the paragraph ends, as in the source, in "I am certain... though the way of it is beyond conceiving." The temptation to add a connective like "in other words" or "put simply" at any of these four joints was strong and was resisted throughout — Augustine's confusion here is the content, not an obstacle to communicating some prior, clearer thought.

**§59 and §63 (the confessing-about-pride paradox)** — Flagged explicitly per the brief. §63 is the single densest paradox sentence in the book: contempt of vainglory that is itself boasted of stops being contempt, because "it doth not contemn when it glorieth." I rendered this as: "it often boasts more emptily of the very contempt of empty glory — and so it is no longer contempt of empty glory that it boasts of, since it does not truly despise what it boasts of." This keeps the self-cancelling structure intact: the boast destroys the very thing (contempt) it boasts of possessing. I did not let this resolve into either "so he is proud" (too flat) or "so he is humble after all" (inverted) — the paradox stands unresolved, exactly as Augustine leaves it, moving directly into a *new* paragraph (§64) about a related but distinct form of self-pleasing vanity.

**§61–62 (self-examination of praise)** — Augustine's chain of self-questioning here ("why am I less moved if another be unjustly dispraised than if it be myself?") is deliberately inconclusive — he ends §62 still uncertain whether he passed or failed his own test, landing only on the general posture of "best... while I displease myself and seek Thy mercy." I preserved the unresolved ending rather than supplying a verdict.

## 5. Paradox / self-referential / "not X but Y" constructions — verified individually

1. **§2** — "I would only be hiding you from myself, not myself from you." Direction preserved: it is God who cannot be hidden *from*; the self is what gets hidden *from* the self. Not inverted.
2. **§4** — "not because they are evils, but because they have been and are not" — the reason for delight is the pastness of the evil, not the evil itself. Preserved.
3. **§6 / heading of whole confession** — "of what I am, not of what I have been" — the entire second half of Book 10 pivots on this distinction (present self-examination vs. past narrative). Kept consistent in §6, §8 ("not what I have been, but what I now am and what I yet am"), and echoed correctly at each recurrence.
4. **§21** — mind has joy, memory has sorrow, when recalling a past sorrow joyfully. Verified the assignment is not swapped: joy belongs to the mind's present act of recollecting; sorrow (inertly, non-felt) belongs to what is stored in memory. Not inverted.
5. **§24 / §28–29** — "forgetfulness... present... that we forget not, and being so, we forget" — deliberately paradoxical parallel construction kept exactly parallel (both clauses use "we forget" with opposite implied subjects/senses), not smoothed into a single consistent sense of "forget."
6. **§29 ("Both were most absurd")** — the two-horned dilemma (forgetfulness is in memory / forgetfulness is in memory so as not to forget) is kept as two genuinely absurd options, with "third way" introduced only after, not folded together.
7. **§33/§36** — "not certain that all wish to be happy... they who wish not to joy in Thee... do not truly desire the happy life" — kept the qualification (their *apparent* wish for happiness is not the *true* wish for the happy life) rather than flattening into "everyone truly wants to be happy."
8. **§34** — "they love truth when it shines... hate her when she reproves"; "she both against their will makes manifest, and herself becometh not manifest unto them" — verified the asymmetry is preserved: people are exposed against their will, but Truth does not expose itself to them. Not reversed into "they hide from truth and truth hides itself equally."
9. **§38 ("Thou wert with me, but I was not with Thee")** — directionality preserved: God's presence to Augustine is not in question; Augustine's absence from God is. Common defect risk (Book 4/5/6/8 direction inversions) checked specifically here — confirmed not inverted.
10. **§43** — "No man loves what he endures, though he love to endure" — kept the distinction between loving the hardship itself (denied) and loving the *act/practice* of enduring (affirmed). Not flattened into "he loves enduring hardship."
11. **§53** — "outwardly following what themselves make, inwardly forsaking Him by whom themselves were made, and destroying that which themselves have been made" — three-part chiastic structure (make/made/made) kept in the same order and voice throughout (active "following/forsaking/destroying" vs. passive "were made/have been made").
12. **§58–59** — the entire pride-of-life section rests on "to wish to be feared and loved of men... for a joy therein which is no joy" — kept "no joy" as a flat denial (it is *not* joy at all), not softened to "a lesser joy."
13. **§59, closing sentence** — "the one took pleasure in the gift of God in man; the other was better pleased with the gift of man, than of God" — this is the paragraph's key not-X-but-Y pivot: one person (the praiser) delights in *God's* gift as seen in another; the other (the praised person) delights more in the *human* gift of praise than in God's actual gift. Verified the two roles (praiser vs. praised) are not swapped between the two clauses.
14. **§61–62** — the whole self-examination of whether Augustine's concern at his own praise/dispraise is really "for his neighbor's sake" or secretly self-interested is preserved as genuinely unresolved — I did not let it collapse into either "he is secretly vain" or "he is innocent," matching Augustine's own explicit "Am I then doubtful of myself in this matter?" / "I know not."
15. **§63 — the central confessing-pride paradox**, flagged by the brief as needing dedicated comment. "It tempts, even when it is reproved by myself in myself, on the very ground that it is reproved; and often glories more vainly of the very contempt of vain-glory; and so it is no longer contempt of vain-glory, whereof it glories; for it doth not contemn when it glorieth." Rendered as a genuine self-cancelling loop (see §3 above, "hardest paragraphs"): boasting about despising vainglory is not really contempt of vainglory, because the act of boasting about it re-instates the very vainglory supposedly despised. Verified this is not resolved into either "so confessing sins is always prideful" (too cynical, not in source) or "so it's fine as long as you don't dwell on it" (too easy, not in source) — it stands as Augustine leaves it, an open wound he keeps returning to.
16. **§67** — the false-mediator logic: "if in both like to man, he should be far from God: or if in both like God, too unlike man: and so not be a mediator" — verified both horns of this dilemma are kept symmetric and neither collapsed into the other.
17. **§69** — "for us to Thee both Victor and Victim, and therefore Victor, because the Victim... Priest and Sacrifice, and therefore Priest because the Sacrifice" — the causal direction (victim-hood is the *ground* of victory, sacrifice is the *ground* of priesthood, not the reverse) verified preserved exactly, since this is Augustine's paradoxical Christological point, not incidental phrasing.

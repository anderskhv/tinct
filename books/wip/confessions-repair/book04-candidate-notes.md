# Confessions Book 4 — modern-en candidate notes

## 1. Preserved source ambiguity (did not resolve it)

- **Para 9** (grief/weeping meditation): Augustine poses a chain of open
  questions about why weeping is sweet — "Is it sweetened by our hoping
  that you hear us?... is it also true of grief for something lost?...
  Or is weeping itself actually a bitter thing...?" I kept all three as
  genuine open questions rather than picking one answer for him. The
  source itself does not resolve this — it's Augustine thinking aloud.
- **Para 12**: "the comfort of other friends, with whom I loved what I
  loved instead of you" — kept the flat juxtaposition ("instead of you")
  exactly as the source states it, without adding any explanation of
  *why* that substitution happened or moralizing further than the
  source already does in the same sentence.
- **Para 16 / Para 21** ("great deep" passage, "impulses... laid up in
  one soul"): left the rhetorical question about loving in another what
  one hates to be oneself unresolved — Augustine raises the puzzle and
  moves on to a new image (numbering hairs vs. feelings) rather than
  answering it; I didn't supply an answer either.
- **Para 22**: "See where the impotent soul lies along... the truth
  unseen. And lo, it is before us." Kept the abrupt, almost cryptic turn
  ("And look — it is right there in front of us") rather than explaining
  what "it" refers to (truth) more explicitly than Augustine does.
- **Para 25**: "Why then does the soul, which God created, go wrong?"
  paired with "I did not want to be asked... Why then does God go
  wrong?" — kept both as parallel questions without adding a bridge
  explaining the logical trap Augustine is describing; the parallelism
  itself carries the irony in the source and I let it stand alone.

## 2. Technical terms kept

- **"the elect" / "the holy"** (Manichean religious ranks) — kept in
  quotes as in source, since paragraph 0 already frames them
  descriptively ("those who were called...") and context (food carried
  to them, "in the workhouse/factory of their stomachs") makes the
  practice legible without a separate gloss.
- **"mathematicians" (astrologers)** — para 3: added a four-word
  parenthetical gloss on first use only ("frauds people call
  astrologers — 'mathematicians,' as they were known") since the Latin
  loanword is genuinely opaque to a modern reader and the term is not
  reused after this paragraph in Book 4. No further explanation added.
- **Monad / Duad** (para 23) — kept untranslated as Augustine's own
  technical (quasi-Manichean/Pythagorean) coinage; the sentence itself
  glosses them ("as it had been a soul without sex" / "anger... lust")
  so no added gloss was needed.
- **Ten Categories / Predicaments (Aristotle)** — rendered "Predicaments"
  as "Categories" (the term any modern reader would recognize for
  Aristotle's work) rather than leaving the archaic "Predicaments,"
  since the list that follows (substance, quality, quantity, relation,
  place, time, position, state, action, passion) is exactly Aristotle's
  ten categories and "Categories" is the standard modern title.
- **Pylades and Orestes** — kept as a bare proper-noun reference,
  matching source, which itself only briefly glosses it ("as is related,
  if not feigned, that they would gladly have died for each other").

## 3. Hardest paragraphs, and tradeoffs

- **Para 14** (the long meditation on rising/setting/perfection/decay of
  created things, ending in "hence and hitherto"): extremely dense,
  many stacked subordinate clauses. I broke some long sentences at
  semicolons into shorter modern sentences but kept every clause's
  content (no compression), including the awkward but important claim
  that things "haste... to not-be" even as they grow. Tradeoff: slightly
  more sentences than the source paragraph, but zero content dropped.
- **Para 17** (Word of God speaking in first person to souls): this is a
  quoted internal monologue/sermon with shifting address (to "souls,"
  then direct second person "ye"). I standardized archaic "ye/you" to
  modern "you" throughout and kept the quotation marks and voice shift
  intact, including the rhetorical repetition "Where are you going...
  Where are you going?" which I preserved as a doubled question rather
  than merging it into one.
- **Para 18** (Christ's descent/ascent passage): long allegorical run
  with many short parallel clauses (bridegroom, giant, thunder, calling).
  I kept the list-like parallel structure and the closing double
  question ("will you not rise up and live?... where are you climbing
  to...?") rather than flattening the sequence into declarative prose.
- **Para 22**: the sentence structure in the source ties three separate
  observations together with semicolons in a way that's hard to
  re-punctuate without garbling the referents (who is "he," who is "the
  men," what "the facts" refers to). I re-read this paragraph twice
  against source after drafting to make sure pronoun referents stayed
  correctly attached.

## 4. Confirmation on the two systemic problems from Book 3 review

- **Rhetorical questions**: I went through every paragraph and checked
  every "?" in source against my output. All source questions remain
  questions in the candidate, in interrogative word order (e.g., para 0
  "But what am I to myself without you, but a guide leading myself to
  ruin?"; para 9's three-question chain; para 16 "Where could my heart
  flee from my heart? Where could I flee from myself?"; para 21's
  "Why... do I love in another what... I should... spurn?"; para 25's
  paired "Why then does the soul... go wrong?" / "Why then does God go
  wrong?"). None were silently converted to statements or exclamations.
- **Added interpretive connectives**: I reviewed the draft specifically
  for inserted causal/purpose language not present in source (phrases
  like "was meant to," "that wisdom promises," "through," "instead of"
  used to supply a link Augustine doesn't state). Where source itself
  states a link (e.g., para 12's own "instead of Thee I loved," para
  1's "tricks... not meant to be used against an innocent person's
  life... but sometimes meant to be used for a guilty person's life,"
  which mirrors Pusey's own "not to be practised against... though
  sometimes for..."), I kept it because it's already in the source, not
  added by me. I did not add new causal glue elsewhere — juxtapositions
  Augustine leaves flat (e.g., para 9's grief/prayer comparison, para
  22's "the facts had not been other... but only the feelings of the
  relators") are rendered as flat juxtapositions, not resolved into
  "because X caused Y."

Notes on the findings:

- **p138 (season/reason).** The anchor text prints "reason", but the saying is Ecclesiastes 3:1 ("To every thing there is a season"), and her next clause about autumn fruit in spring depends on it. The 1792 edition reads "season", so the anchor's "reason" is most likely a transcription error. Rendering "a reason for everything" breaks the image that follows. If the team insists on literal fidelity to the anchor, the current text is defensible, but I recommend the fix.
- **p138 (habits).** In the source, both "they"s in the second question most naturally refer to the *principles* of the preceding sentence. The candidate makes young people the object and calls the *habits* deceptive, which shifts the argument.

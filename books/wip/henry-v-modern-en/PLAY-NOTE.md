## 2. Fixed terms

- **Salic law** (2:9): keep "Salic law" and "the land Salique" with the clarifying phrase "the law that bars women from inheriting". Keep "Pharamond", "Charlemagne", "Hugh Capet", "Lewis", "the river Elbe/Sala" as served.
- **Tennis balls** (2:30-33): the Dauphin's gift; keep "tennis-balls" and Henry's tennis puns (courts, rackets, "set", "match"). Keep "the Dauphin's mock".
- **Agincourt** (20:24 names the field); also "Crispin Crispian", "Saint Crispin's Day" (16:12), "Saint Davy's day", "Harfleur", "Calais", "Southampton", "Picardy".
- **War and siege:** the breach, scaling-ladders, the mines (tunnels under the walls), pioneers (sappers), culverin, "chambers" (small cannon), alarum, parley, tucket and sennet (trumpet calls), arrayed, ransom, herald, pax (a sacred plate Bardolph stole), pike, "ancient" (ensign), esquire.
- **French titles:** Dauphin, Constable, Duke of Bourbon, Montjoy (a herald's title and name), "Mon seigneur", "cousin" (royal address). Keep as served.
- Fixed phrase: "God of battles" stays.

## 3. Hardest passages

1. **Chorus (2:39, 6:28 + 6:30, 13:79, 21:48, 23:89).** The Act 2, 3, 4, 5 prologues and the Epilogue sit at the ends of chapters 2, 6, 13, 21 and 23. Dense, long, with images of ships and armies; unpack but keep the images. The "Vouchsafe to those that have not read the story" speech (21:48) has history compressed into one sentence. Stay dignified. 6:29 and 6:31 are stage directions, 6:30 continues the Chorus without a prefix.
2. **Salic law, 2:9 (478 words, Canterbury).** A legalistic tangle of places and claims. Split into short sentences and name every figure each time; the bees speech (2:22, honey-bees as an ordered kingdom) is a separate hard image: keep it as bees. Name "the French" and "King Henry" instead of "he".
3. **Dauphin's tennis balls, 2:30-33 (ambassador and Henry's 343-word reply).** Keep the extended tennis metaphor consistent; make the scorn audible.
4. **"Once more unto the breach", 7:1 (275 words).** Famous; keep "Once more unto the breach, dear friends, once more" nearly as is. Unpack the stiffen-the-sinews and "Cry God for Harry, England, and Saint George!" part with a few words.
5. **Harfleur threat, 9:1 (333 words).** Brutal; keep every image (blind soldier, rape, infants on pikes, "the gates of mercy shall be all shut up"). Do not soften.
6. **Dialect captains, 8:17-8:41.** Fluellen (Welsh: "look you", "porn", "falorous", "Cheshu", "Anchient", "plow" for blow). Macmorris (Irish: "ish", "Chrish", "la"). Jamy (Scots: "sall", "gud", "ligg i' the grund"). Render in plain English with a light flavor: Fluellen keeps "look you", "by Jesus"; Macmorris keeps "Christ save me", "indeed"; Jamy keeps "I shall" and "by the mass". No phonetic spelling. Keep the quarrel over "my nation" (8:33-36) as sharp as the source. Fluellen at 12:13 and 20:3-9 (Alexander/Monmouth/Macedon, rivers, "salmons") are comic and must stay comic.
7. **French language lesson, 10:1-35.** French stays as served, italic markers preserved. After each French sentence add a short English meaning set off with em dashes and outside the italics, e.g. `_La main_ — the hand —` (never brackets). The English words Alice teaches ("de hand", "de fingres", "de nails", "de nick", "de sin", "d'elbow", "de foot", "de coun") stay exactly as served; do not correct them. Puns: "de sin" is "chin" and "de foot", "de coun" sound to Katharine like obscene French words (10:32-33): gloss with a short appositive ("sounding to her like coarse French words") and carry her outrage in 10:33 at full strength. Do not add explanation beyond that.
8. **Pistol's bombast, 3:11-25, 5:16, 12:12-18, 22:6, 22:30.** Mock-heroic cliché: "Iceland dog", "prick-ear'd cur", "Parca's fatal web", "base Trojan", "the fig of Spain" (an obscene gesture; keep "fico"/"fig" and show it is an insult), "Couple a gorge" (French, "cut the throat"). Keep it inflated and absurd, with the insults at full force.
9. **Henry's disguised talk and "Upon the King!", 14:63 and 14:85 (371 and 421 words).** Argument about whether a king bears his soldiers' sins and the king's ceremony. Name "the King" and keep every step. 14:45-84 is the exchange with Williams, Bates and Court; the glove exchange resumes at 20:33 and 21:3-30.
10. **Saint Crispin's Day, 16:12 (409 words, prefix `KING.`).** Keep "We few, we happy few, we band of brothers", "Crispin Crispian" and the line "he today that sheds his blood with me / Shall be my brother" in recognizable form. Dignified, no coarseness.
11. **Henry's "bear my former answer back" to Montjoy, 16:24 (304 words).** Ransom, "achieve me and then sell my bones", the cautious proverb about the lion's skin; unpack.
12. **Dauphin and Constable on horses, 13:7-13.** Pegasus/Perseus allusions and horse-borrowing talk; the Dauphin's absurd praise of his horse is the joke, so keep the excess.
13. **Wooing of Katharine, 23:16-55.** Mixed French/broken English. Henry's "false French" jokes (23:27-41) are about his own bad speech. Keep "Kate", "Dat is as it shall please le roi mon père" style: the prefix forms remain, but Katharine's broken English (`dat`, `de`, `wat`, `sall`) is rendered as plain English with slightly odd grammar ("that is what pleases", "it shall also content me"), not phonetic spelling. Italic French stays and receives short dash glosses. 23:29 (385 words) is Henry's long rough wooing speech.
14. **Epilogue, 23:87-89.** `EPILOGUE.` is a bare paragraph, then `Enter Chorus.`, then the speech: copy the first two exactly. The sonnet-like speech ends with the loss of France under Henry VI; keep that "the King's son lost it" sense.

## 4. Oddities in the served text

- **Prologue of Act 1 is absent.** Chapter 1 starts with Canterbury and Ely; nothing to render.
- **Unprefixed speech paragraphs** appear after stage directions inside a speech (2:25, 2:27, 2:33, 6:10, 6:30, 12:28, 14:3, 14:85, 17:33, 18:5, 20:34, 21:39). Render with no prefix added. They are continuations (e.g. 12:28 "God bless your Majesty!" is a soldiers' cry; 18:5 is the Constable).
- **Stage directions without brackets** ("Enter ...", "Flourish. Enter Chorus.") are copied verbatim, exactly as served. `EPILOGUE.` at 23:87 is a heading paragraph.
- **The live modern edition is not a safe model for form.** It rewrites bare stage directions as `[Enter ...]`, drops underscores from `[_Exit._]`, changes KING HENRY to `KING HENRY V` in many paragraphs (about 180 prefix differences) and KATHARINE to KATHERINE, inserts a bracketed editorial note at 10:33, and in the lesson translates some French away. Do not copy any of that: follow the served original.
- 13:80 and 23:90 are `[_Exit._]`-type stage directions; slice `ch013_p080-080` is that single paragraph (1 word), copy verbatim.
- `KING.` (16:12) and `GREY, SCROOP.` (4:25) are real prefixes; keep as served.

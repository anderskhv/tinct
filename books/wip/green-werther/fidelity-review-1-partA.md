# Fidelity Review — The Sorrows of Young Werther (modern-en) — Round 1, Part A

**Chapters covered:** 1–20 (Preface through "July 11, 1771" — the opening
third of Book One).

**Source edition (fidelity anchor):** `books/wip/green-werther/source.json`
= `app/public/data/editions/werther-original-en.json`, the public-domain
English translation (locked anchor per the non-English-source rule; a
`werther-original-de.json` German original also exists in the app's
editions but is not the anchor used for this review).

## Method

Read source and candidate side by side, paragraph by paragraph, in
sequence, with each packet's neighboring paragraphs visible in the same
continuous read (every paragraph in this range was read in full — no
skimming, no sampling — and the paragraphs immediately before/after each
notionally-packeted group were always in view since the whole chapter run
was read contiguously). Checked each paragraph for actors, negation,
causality, certainty/hedging, conditions, omissions, additions, silent
corrections, and unmodernized quotations per the fidelity checklist.

## Coverage statement

Every paragraph in chapters 1–20 (Preface, and the letters dated May 4
through July 11, 1771) was read individually in full, source against
candidate, in original order. None sampled.

## Findings

### Ch. 23, para 1 — minor addition + minor substitution (non-blocking)

- **Source:** "The idea that Charlotte's eyes had dwelt on his
  countenance, his cheek, his very apparel, endeared them all inestimably
  to me, so that at the moment I would not have parted from him for a
  thousand crowns."
- **Candidate:** "The thought that her eyes had rested on his face, his
  cheeks, the buttons of his coat and the collar of his jacket, made all
  of it so sacred, so precious, that in that moment I wouldn't have given
  him up for a thousand thalers."
- **What's wrong:** Two small additions beyond the source's general "his
  very apparel": "the buttons of his coat and the collar of his jacket"
  invents specific garment detail not licensed by the source. Also
  "thousand crowns" becomes "thousand thalers" — a different named
  currency substituted with no textual basis (neither changes the sense
  a reader takes from it — both read as "a great sum" — but it is a
  factual substitution of one named unit for another).
- **Severity:** Non-blocking. Neither changes meaning, actor, causality,
  or any load-bearing fact; flagged because the protocol asks that
  additions be surfaced even when harmless.
- **Fix (optional):** Could tighten "the buttons of his coat and the
  collar of his jacket" back to something closer to "even his coat, his
  collar" without the invented specificity, and align "thalers" back to
  "crowns" for exactness, but this is a judgment call, not a required fix.

No other paragraphs in this range showed defects. Notable strengths:

- The letter-by-letter escalation of Werther's feeling for Charlotte
  (Ch. 12's ball scene through Ch. 21's "she loves me!") tracks the
  source's emotional beats paragraph for paragraph, including the exact
  moments of self-interruption and repetition ("Like her! I loathe the
  word", Ch. 19; "That she loves me!", Ch. 21).
- Dialogue attribution and turn-taking in the multi-speaker Ch. 12 ball
  scene and Ch. 16 ill-humor debate (Werther/Charlotte/vicar's
  wife/Herr Schmidt) is preserved exactly — no actor swaps, no merged
  speeches.
- Hedged/uncertain language is preserved precisely: Ch. 18's "Perhaps
  she turned for me. Perhaps!" keeps the source's identical
  double-uncertainty; Ch. 21's "Is this presumption, or a true sense of
  how things stand?" keeps the source's open question rather than
  resolving it either way.
- The Klopstock scene (Ch. 12, para 24 in the candidate's paragraph
  numbering) preserves the exact beat structure: the shared silent
  understanding, the tears, the kiss on the hand — nothing added or cut.

**Verdict for this range: ACCEPT AS-IS**, with one non-blocking stylistic
note (Ch. 23) recorded above, no action required.

# Style note: *Discourse on Inequality* (Rousseau), Tinct Modern English

- **Book:** `discourse-on-inequality`
- **Edition:** `modern-en`
- **Date:** 2026-10-06

This note applies to every renderer, reviser and reviewer of this book. It governs the whole text.

**Fidelity anchor:** `served-original-en.json`, the served `original-en`: G. D. H. Cole's English translation of 1913 (British spelling). The task is a *modernization of that English*, not a new translation. Consult no other translation and no French text.

## What this edition is

A sentence-by-sentence rendering of Cole's English into plain contemporary English. It keeps every step of the argument, every qualification, example, name and figure, and Rousseau's voice. It neither explains the work nor makes it easier than it is. The live modern-en (`served-live-modern-en.json`) is already partly modernized (American spelling, a few word swaps). Use it only as a convenience: keep a good rendering, but fix its drift (see "Things that must not happen") and fully render any paragraph it left near-verbatim. Never reword clear text just to lower the similarity number.

## Voice and sentences

- Rousseau is an impassioned, argumentative essayist who speaks in the first person ("I", "I conceive", "I confess", "let us") and addresses the reader and his judges directly. Keep every "I" and "let us". Do not neutralize him into an impersonal treatise.
- Break long periodic sentences where that helps, but keep the connectives that carry the argument (therefore, for, yet, besides, whereas, thus, in short, it follows that). Keep his rhetorical questions as questions and his exclamations as exclamations. Replace Cole's stock formulae ("it is not to be conceived that", "the savage cannot but be", "whence it follows") with natural modern equivalents.
- Prefer active constructions and concrete verbs. Keep the register of a formal eighteenth-century essay in plain modern dress. **No contractions**, except in quoted speech that has them. Do not add contractions the live modern-en introduced ("doesn't", "don't"); render them in full.
- Generic "man", "men", "mankind" and "he/him" for the human being are **kept** as Cole has them. Do not change to "people", "humans" or "they" (that would alter the text's term "man" against "brute"; see the table). "Human beings" is allowed only where Cole wrote "human beings" or "human species".

## Force

Hedges and judgments stay exactly as strong or mild as written: "I conceive", "it appears", "perhaps", "I should have wished", "it seems", "I am tempted to believe", "it is not to be supposed", "may be said to". Keep the conditional and hypothetical mood of the investigation: Rousseau says repeatedly that his account is "conditional and hypothetical reasoning", not history (Preface, p23). Never turn "must have" into "did", "might" into "did", "perhaps" into a flat assertion, or a hypothesis into a fact; never weaken "it is certain", "it is clear" or "it is impossible". Keep his sharp judgments ("liars", "impostor", "usurpations") exactly as sharp.

## No additions

No commentary, no bracketed notes, no headings, no glosses of Hobbes, Locke, Plato, Diogenes or Geneva, no modern framing and no new facts. A period reference may be clarified only inside the sentence, in a few words, where the text supports it. Do not translate or explain the Latin (see Quotations). Do not add the parenthetical source citations the live modern-en inserted (see below).

## Structure 1:1

Keep 4 chapters and 145 paragraphs with the served titles exactly: 1 "Dedication" (26 paragraphs), 2 "Preface" (25), 3 "Part 1" (52), 4 "Part 2" (67). Output paragraph N renders source paragraph N and begins with equivalent content. Never merge, split, reorder, drop or invent a paragraph. Output length is normally at least 75% of the source's word count. The internal divisions are paragraphs, not headings, and stay in place:

- **Dedication** (ch1 p0-22 letter to the Republic of Geneva; p23-25 closing: "Most honourable, magnificent and sovereign lords, I am, with the most profound respect," then "Your most humble and obedient servant and fellow-citizen." then "J. J. ROUSSEAU. Chambéry, June 12, 1754."). The three closing lines are separate paragraphs. Modernize spelling only ("honorable"); keep "fellow-citizen" as one hyphenated word, and the signature, place and date exactly.
- **Preface** (ch2 p0-14), **epigraph** (p15, Persius), the two title paragraphs (p16 "A DISSERTATION", p17 "ON THE ORIGIN AND FOUNDATION OF THE INEQUALITY OF MANKIND"), and the **Exordium** (p18-24, starting "It is of man that I have to speak"). Keep p16 as "A DISSERTATION" (Cole's word; do not use the live edition's "A DISCOURSE") and p17 verbatim, in capitals. The Exordium is the Preface's own section: do not insert a heading for it.
- **Part 1** and **Part 2** run as continuous paragraphs without internal headings. Part 2 opens with "The first man who, having enclosed a piece of ground ..." (ch4 p0); keep "This is mine" as printed.

## Technical terms: one rendering, kept stable

Cole's text is the anchor; these are his actual words. Rousseau's French terms (amour de soi, amour-propre, etc.) are **not** present as such in this text, so never import them and never write the French words.

| Term (as Cole has it) | Fixed rendering and rule |
|---|---|
| **state of nature** (about 40 uses); "natural state" | Keep "state of nature"; "natural state of man" stays as is. Never "natural condition", "primitive state", "wild", "pre-social" or "pre-political". "State of society" (10 uses) stays "state of society"; do not shorten to "society" where he sets it against the state of nature. |
| **natural man / savage man / savage / the original man / man in a state of nature** | Keep each as written; they are not interchangeable and none becomes "primitive person", "caveman", "native" or "noble savage". "Savage" is Cole's word for natural man and is kept: it is a technical term of the argument, not an insult. "Civilized man" stays "civilized man" (against "savage"), and "uncivilized" stays. "Social man" stays. |
| **brute(s)**, animals | Keep "brutes" (15 uses) for animals as set against man; "animals" where Cole wrote it. Do not use "beasts" or "creatures" for "brutes". |
| **natural or physical inequality / moral or political inequality** | Keep exactly: "natural or physical", "moral or political". "Moral" here means conventional and social, not ethical (he explains: "it depends on a kind of convention"). Do not change it to "ethical" or "social" or "political" alone; never gloss it. "Natural inequality" and "moral inequality" are used bare in the same sense. "Inequality" is never "inequity". |
| **self-preservation** | Keep. Never "survival" or "self-defense". |
| **love of self** (once; ch3 on compassion) | Keep "love of self". Do not render it "self-love", "vanity", "selfishness" or "egoism". |
| **pride** (ch3 p22 "desires and pride"; ch4 "the first emotion of pride") | Keep "pride". Not "vanity" and not "self-esteem". |
| **vanity** (4 uses) | Keep "vanity". It stays distinct from "pride" and from "love of self". Do not use "narcissism". |
| **esteem, deference, contempt, shame** | Keep as written; do not merge "esteem" with "respect". |
| **pity / compassion** | Cole uses "compassion" (13) as the term and "pity" once. Keep each word where Cole wrote it; do not unify them and do not use "empathy" or "sympathy" ("sensibility" stays "sensibility"). "A natural feeling" stays "a natural feeling". |
| **perfectibility; faculty of self-improvement** | Keep "perfectibility" (it is his coinage). "Faculty of self-improvement" and "self-improvement" stay. Never "ability to progress", "adaptability" or "development" in their place. |
| **faculties** | Keep "faculties" (15 uses). Not "abilities", "capacities" or "skills". |
| **passions** | Keep "passions". Never "emotions". "Feeling(s)" and "emotion(s)" stay as Cole wrote them. |
| **civil society** (5), **society**, **the body politic** (3), **political societies**, **political body** | Keep each form as written. "Civil society" is the society founded on property and law, not "civic life" or "civilization" and not "society" bare. "Body politic" stays; never "the state" for it. |
| **the State (capital), the Sovereign (capital), sovereign lords, sovereignty** | In the Dedication "sovereign lords" is a title of address: keep. "The Sovereign" (the supreme political authority, person or body) keeps its capital where Cole capitalizes it and never becomes "ruler" or "king". "State" as a political community stays "State" when Cole capitalizes it in the same sense, and "state" when he means condition ("state of nature"). "Sovereignty" stays. |
| **contract / compact / the fundamental compact / social pact** | Keep Cole's words: "contract" and "compact" as printed. The phrase "social contract" does not occur; never supply it. "Political body" stays. |
| **property** (24), **proprietor, proprietorship** | Keep "property". "Proprietorship" may become "ownership" where the sense is plain; "proprietor" stays or becomes "owner" by context but pick one per paragraph. Never "possession" for "property". |
| **usurpation(s), the rich, the poor, the strong, the weak** | Keep; do not soften. |
| **natural right, natural law, right of nations, positive right, divine right, the law of the strongest** | Keep each phrase exactly. They are distinct terms: "natural right" is not "natural law". |
| **mankind, the human species, man, men, fellow-creatures** | Keep "mankind" and "the human species"; write "fellow creatures" (no hyphen) throughout. |
| **laws, equity, legitimate, authority, government, magistrate, citizens, fellow-citizens** | Keep; "magistrates" stay (do not use "officials"). |
| **imbecility** (once, "imbecility and original happiness") | Render "weakness" or "feebleness"; never "stupidity". |
| **miserable, misery, unhappy** | Keep "miserable" where he defines it in Part 1 ("as I understand the word miserable ..."); keep this sentence's logic intact. |

## Quotations and citations

**Decision:** quoted words stay recognizable and are modernized only in spelling and typography. This covers the Latin lines, the translated quotations (Hobbes, Locke, Grotius, Burlamaqui, Pufendorf, Plato and others), his "Beware of listening to this impostor ..." apostrophe, and the long quotation in Part 2 ("Let it not, therefore, be said that the Sovereign is not subject to the laws of his State ...", ch4 p47). Everything outside the quotation marks is modernized.

- **Latin and any foreign words** (the Persius epigraph at ch2 p15, the Juvenal line at ch3 p35, the Ovid line at ch4 p34, "Quem te deus esse Jussit ...", "Mollissima corda ...", "Attonitus novitate mali ...") are kept **exactly as served, in Latin**, with their diacritics ("humanâ quâ", "quæ modô"). Do not translate them, do not put them in quotation marks, do not break them into verse lines with slashes (the live modern-en did all three and must not be copied). A line that is a Latin quotation followed by its source stays one paragraph.
- **Source citations** in the original text ("Persius, Satire iii, 71.", "Juvenal, Satire xv, 151.") are citation data: keep them verbatim, **without** adding parentheses and **without** adding sources the served original lacks. The live modern-en added "(Ovid, Metamorphoses xi, 127.)", "(Tacitus, Histories iv, 17)", "(Lucan, Pharsalia i, 376.)", "(Of the Rights of the Most Christian Queen ..., 1667.)", "(as our jurists hold)" and similar. Do not do this. Remove any parenthetical citation that is not in the source paragraph.
- **Quoted prose** in double quotes (the long Part 2 quotation, the speech to the impostor, quoted maxims) keeps its words. A short quoted or italic-style maxim without quotation marks in Cole ("This is mine", "There can be no injury, where there is no property") is **not** given quotation marks that Cole lacks and is not paraphrased.
- Parentheticals that Cole actually has are kept: "(not to make you happy, for your ancestors have saved you that trouble)" at ch1 p13 and "(if my opponents please)" at ch4 p48.
- **Rhetorical apostrophes** stay as address, in the same voice and the same position: "O man, of whatever country you are, and whatever your opinions may be, behold your history ..." (ch2 p24) opens "O man," and keeps "behold" or becomes "listen" only if the sentence stays an address. Do not remove the "O". Address to "most honourable, magnificent and sovereign lords" and to Geneva stays in the second person, with the same degree of ceremony. Keep "Deign ... to receive".

## Notes, markers and special elements

- **Rousseau's own notes are not in the served text.** Only the in-text markers are: `[1]` (ch3 p16), `[2]` and `[3]` (both ch3 p33), `[4]` (ch3 p35), `[5]` (ch4 p34), `[6]` (ch4 p44), `[7]` (ch4 p47) and `[8]` (ch4 p59). The live modern-en dropped every one of them. **Keep all eight markers exactly as printed, in the same place relative to the words they follow** (immediately after the word or punctuation they follow, no space before). Never renumber, merge, drop, move to the paragraph end, convert to superscripts or parentheses, and never invent a note paragraph or note text.
- **No note paragraphs exist** and none may be created; paragraph counts stay as served.
- Closing lines, epigraph, title lines and the signature (see Structure) are paragraphs to keep in place. Title lines in capitals stay in capitals; the opening words of the Dedication ("MOST HONOURABLE, MAGNIFICENT AND SOVEREIGN LORDS") stay in capitals; modernize only spelling ("MOST HONORABLE ...").
- The text has no lists, no speaker labels and no verse except the Latin lines above.

## Names and spellings

- Keep every name and place as printed: Geneva, Sparta, Lycurgus, Plato, Aristotle, Hobbes, Locke, Sidney, Mandeville, Grotius, the Abbé Condillac, Burlamaqui, Hippocrates, Celsus, Cato, Diogenes, Rome, Tacitus, Plutarch, Hottentots of the Cape of Good Hope. Keep Cole's "Puffendorf" spelling (do not correct to Pufendorf) and "Chambéry". "The Abbé Condillac" is kept with its title.
- Keep "Hottentots" as printed (it is part of the period text; do not replace or comment).
- "Amours" (twice, in the passage on animals and savages) may become "love affairs"; never "relationships".
- American spelling, as in the live modern-en: honor, favor, labor, defense, civilize, organization, authorize, behavior, endeavor, toward, among, while (not amongst, whilst); "to-day" becomes "today"; "every one" becomes "everyone" when it means every person; "gentle-folks" becomes "gentlefolk".

## Typography

Checked against the live modern-en.

- **Quotation marks:** use straight double quotes `"`, as in the live modern-en and the served original, for quotations. Nested quotation uses straight single quotes. Keep the same quotation boundaries as the source; add none.
- **Apostrophes:** straight `'`, as served (live modern-en uses `'`).
- **Dashes:** the source has `--` in 16 places (e.g. "derived--rules which", "the earth--for these it would produce of itself--but to compel it"). Render each as an em dash with no spaces on both sides, as the live modern-en does ("derived—rules", "earth—for these"). Cole's mid-sentence `--` pairs stay paired. Stray marks such as `a--value` are damage: see below.
- Ellipses only where the source has one. No newline inside a paragraph. Numbers, dates and figures unchanged. Hyphenated compounds stay hyphenated where they are normal ("self-preservation", "self-improvement", "fellow-citizen").

## Damaged text in the source (renderers: do not "fix" by inventing)

The served original has a few places where `--` appears in the middle of a clause where a word is missing or a comma was lost: ch4 p13 ("liberty--and reciprocal attachment"), ch4 p17 ("a--value came to be attached"), ch4 p55 ("grew--bitter"), ch4 p58 ("the greater--the number of idlers"), ch4 p60 (a trailing "--" after "the oppressor of their country"), ch2 p10 and ch3 p21, p26. Where the dash is an obvious damaged comma (p13, p17, p55, p58), drop it and read the clause normally; never add or guess a word. Where the source paragraph ends in a dash (ch4 p60), keep an em dash at the end. Reviewers flag any invented word.

## Things that must not happen

1. Dropping or moving any of the eight note markers `[1]` to `[8]`, or adding note paragraphs or note text.
2. Translating, quoting, versifying or adding parenthetical sources to the Latin lines; adding citations the source lacks.
3. Importing "amour-propre", "amour de soi", "self-love", "noble savage", "social contract" or any of Rousseau's French vocabulary that Cole does not use; replacing "savage", "brute" or "man" with soft modern synonyms.
4. Changing "A DISSERTATION" to "A DISCOURSE", or altering "Part 1"/"Part 2" titles, the Dedication closing lines or the signature and date.
5. Turning a hypothesis ("must have", "perhaps", "I conceive") into a fact, or a hedge into a flat claim; removing "I" and "let us".
6. Removing the "O" of the apostrophe "O man", or any other rhetorical address, or flattening the Dedication's ceremony into modern business-letter English.
7. Merging, splitting or reordering paragraphs, or dropping the Preface/Exordium title paragraphs; inserting headings.
8. Inventing, restoring or "improving" a word at the damaged `--` places; adding contractions.

Twelve cases bring Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson from the sitting-room at Baker Street into a world of pawnshops, railway journeys, suburban villas, country houses, and closely guarded family secrets. A royal visitor wants a photograph recovered. A pawnbroker has been paid to copy an encyclopaedia. A young woman arrives with a troubling account of her employment. Again and again, something apparently trivial becomes the detail on which an entire explanation turns.

Watson tells these stories as a friend, witness, and practising doctor. He shares the reader's uncertainty, admires Holmes's ingenuity, and sometimes feels the discomfort of helping him. Holmes can be theatrical, impatient, generous, and mistaken. The pleasures of detection sit beside harder questions: who has the power to tell a convincing story, whose reputation receives protection, and whether finding the truth necessarily means delivering someone to the law. Read each story as its own case, while watching how the friendship and the detective's habits accumulate across the collection.

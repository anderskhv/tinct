# Accessibility Review — Round 3 (Reviewer A, blind)

**Book/chapter:** Leviathan, Chapter 9 ("Of the Several Subjects of Knowledge") — the "Table of the Sciences" chapter.

**What I read:** All 12 paragraphs of `candidate-sonnet.json`, in full, indices 0–11 (0-based, matching array order), start to finish, then a second pass paragraph by paragraph. I was not shown the source text, any prior review, or drafter's notes.

**Note on scope:** Per the brief I was given for this chapter, the outline's own numbering oddities — two consecutive items both labeled "a." (indices 6 and 7) and one item labeled "2)" instead of continuing the period scheme (index 8) — are a deliberately preserved structural feature of the source, not translation defects, and I have not listed them as issues below.

---

## Paragraph-by-paragraph findings

**Index 0** — No issues. "Knowledge is of two kinds... The first is nothing but sense and memory... The second is called science, and it is conditional" reads cleanly, with the circle/witness examples doing real explanatory work. This paragraph is a strong opening — clear, parallel, well-paced.

**Index 1** — No issues. "Natural history" / "civil history" distinction is stated plainly, with concrete examples ("metals, plants, animals, regions"). Minor, not flag-worthy: "have no dependence on the will of man" is a slightly formal turn of phrase for "don't depend on human will/choice," but it's unambiguous and doesn't require rereading.

**Index 2** — No issues. Clear and well-glossed.

**Indices 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9** — No wording issues. These outline entries are short and each unfamiliar Latin or technical term is immediately glossed in parenthesis or an em-dash clause (e.g., "accidents (that is, the properties and qualities)," "Philosophia Prima (First Philosophy)," "transient bodies — those that sometimes appear and sometimes vanish"). The glossing pattern is consistent and does real accessibility work — flagging it under "reads unusually well" below.

**Index 8** — *"2) Consequences of the qualities from liquid bodies that fill the space between the stars — that is, the air, or the ethereal substance."*
This is a genuine wording issue, not the numbering quirk. Every other item in this outline uses the pattern "Consequences **from** [the qualities of] X" (see index 6: "Consequences from indeterminate quantity and motion"; index 7: "Consequences from the qualities of transient bodies"). This item inverts it to "Consequences **of** the qualities **from** liquid bodies" — swapping which preposition attaches to "qualities" and which to the body noun. On a first read this invites a momentary misparse (is "of the qualities" modifying "Consequences," with "from liquid bodies" then modifying "qualities"? or is the whole thing meant to parallel the "from the qualities of X" pattern elsewhere?). It's a clarity/consistency problem worth a targeted fix — reordering to "Consequences from the qualities of liquid bodies that fill the space between the stars" would match the established pattern and remove the ambiguity.

**Index 10** — *"1. Of consequences from the institution — that is, the founding — of COMMONWEALTHS, to the rights and duties of the body politic, or sovereign."*
This is an overloaded sentence, not just a dense one. The intended skeleton is "[Of] consequences from A, to B" (i.e., consequences that follow from the founding of commonwealths, as applied to the rights/duties of the sovereign), but the A-clause has a parenthetical gloss — "that is, the founding" — dropped in the middle of it, between "institution" and "of COMMONWEALTHS." By the time the reader reaches "to the rights and duties," they've had to hold three things in suspension (the leading "Of consequences from," the interrupted "institution ... of COMMONWEALTHS," and the gloss). A reader is likely to have to re-read this once to recover the "from X, to Y" shape. Also worth noting: this is the only item in the whole outline that opens with "Of consequences" rather than just "Consequences" (compare index 9: "Consequences from the accidents..."), which adds a small extra friction at the very start.

**Index 11** — *"2. Of consequences from that same institution, to the duty and right of the subjects."*
Shares the same "Of consequences from... to..." construction as index 10, but without the interrupting parenthetical, so it reads considerably more smoothly by comparison — flagging only for consistency with index 10's "Of" opening (see above), not as its own separate problem.

---

## What's working well

- The glossing convention (technical/Latin term immediately followed by a plain-English parenthetical or dash-clause) is applied consistently across indices 3–9 and does the real work of making an unfamiliar 17th-century taxonomy legible to a modern reader without dumbing it down.
- Paragraphs 0–2 (the prose lead-in before the table starts) are genuinely well modernized: the fact/science and history/philosophy distinctions are stated in plain, confident sentences with concrete illustrative examples, and nothing there requires a re-read.

---

## Overall verdict: **needs targeted fixes**

Two specific wording issues remain, both fixable without touching the deliberately-preserved numbering quirks:

1. **Index 8** — inconsistent preposition order ("Consequences of the qualities from liquid bodies") breaks the parallel pattern used everywhere else in the outline and invites a misparse.
2. **Index 10** — an interrupted "from X, to Y" construction with a parenthetical gloss dropped mid-clause, dense enough to require a re-read; compounded by inconsistency with index 11 and the rest of the outline, which don't open with "Of."

Everything else in the chapter — the fact/science distinction, the history taxonomy, the Latin terms, the recurring "consequences from the accidents of..." outline pattern — is either already clear or is unavoidable conceptual density inherent to summarizing Hobbes's classification scheme (a reader unfamiliar with 17th-century natural philosophy will still have to think about what "indeterminate quantity and motion" or "the ethereal substance" means; that's the material, not the prose). Setting aside the preserved source-numbering oddities (two "a." labels, the "2)" item) as out of scope per the brief, the chapter has **not yet** reached the point where all remaining difficulty is unavoidable — indices 8 and 10 are avoidably-difficult wording, not just dense subject matter.

## Summary

This candidate is close. The three-paragraph prose introduction is genuinely strong — clear, well-paced, confidently modernized — and the outline's technical-term glossing is applied consistently and well throughout. The chapter's remaining friction is narrow and identifiable: one item (index 8) whose preposition order breaks the outline's own pattern and briefly misdirects parsing, and one item (index 10) whose "from...to..." structure gets interrupted by a mid-clause gloss badly enough to likely force a re-read. Both are targeted, single-sentence fixes rather than a broader rewrite.

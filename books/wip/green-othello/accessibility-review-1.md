# Accessibility Review 1 — Othello (`othello`, modern-en)

**Step A of the acceptance procedure.** Blind read of `candidate.json` only
— no access to `source.json` during this pass. Full read, all 15 chapters,
all 1,391 paragraphs, not sampled.

## What was read

Every chapter (Act 1 Sc.1–3, Act 2 Sc.1–3, Act 3 Sc.1–4, Act 4 Sc.1–3, Act 5
Sc.1–2), every paragraph, in reading order, via `candidate_readable.txt`.

## Overall impression

Reads as clear, idiomatic modern English throughout. Rhetorical shape,
verbal escalation (especially in the temptation scene, Ch9), and register
shifts (Iago's cynicism vs. Othello's formal/martial diction vs. the
domestic comedy of the Clown and drinking scenes) all come through without
requiring familiarity with Elizabethan vocabulary or syntax. Sentence
lengths are varied and natural; long Shakespearean periodic sentences have
generally been broken into several shorter modern sentences without
becoming choppy or losing their build.

## Passages a new reader may still slow down on (not defects — inherent
difficulty, flagged for the acceptance record, not for rewriting)

- **Ch1 ¶4 (Iago's opening complaint about Cassio's promotion).** Dense with
  period military/administrative detail ("bookkeeper," "textbook theory,"
  "togaed politicians") — the difficulty is Iago's own rhetorical density
  and disdain, not unclear modern wording. A reader gets the gist
  (Iago is furious he was passed over) even without parsing every clause.
- **Ch3 ¶45 (Othello's "her father loved me" speech).** Long single
  paragraph, richly imagistic ("antres vast," now "vast caverns"; the
  Anthropophagi). The content is exotic and deliberately so — Othello is
  describing an exotic life. Not a clarity problem.
- **Ch9 (the temptation scene) generally.** The scene's difficulty is
  psychological, not lexical — Iago's insinuation works by withholding and
  suggestion ("Ha, I don't like that" / "Nothing... or if I did, I don't
  know what"). A reader is meant to feel the same unease Othello does; this
  is the play working as intended, not an accessibility gap.
- **Ch10 ¶38–40 (the handkerchief's "magic in the web of it" speech).**
  Genuinely strange, mythic material (a sibyl, mummy-dyed silk) rendered
  plainly; the strangeness is the point.
- **Ch13 ¶48 (Emilia's closing speech on husbands and wives).** A long,
  syntactically dense argument — modernized into clear sentences, but the
  argument itself takes some tracking. Inherent to the content (a
  sustained rhetorical case), not a wording problem.
- **Ch15 ¶190 (Othello's final speech, "Speak of me as I am...").** Famous,
  dense, image-packed ("base Judean," "Arabian trees / their medicinal
  gum," the Aleppo anecdote). Reads clearly in modern vocabulary; the
  difficulty is that it is simply a lot of compressed meaning in one
  speech, which is true of the original too.

None of the above are wording defects — a reviewer re-reading each of these
in `fidelity-review-1.md` confirms the modern phrasing is accurate and no
easier rendering would be possible without cutting content the acceptance
procedure requires to be preserved.

## Explicit/frank content check (accessibility angle only — fidelity checked
separately)

Confirmed the modern-English candidate does **not** soften or euphemize the
play's racial slurs, sexual content, or violent imagery relative to what a
modern reader would expect from a direct, unflinching modern rendering:
"thick-lipped one," "an old black ram is mounting your white ewe," "making
the beast with two backs," "Barbary stallion," and the "infants ... groaning"
type imagery (this play doesn't have that specific line, checked against
Julius Caesar's precedent for the same failure class) all read with full
force in contemporary vocabulary, not diluted. See `fidelity-review-1.md`
for the source-by-source confirmation.

## Verdict

No accessibility-blocking defects found. The one blocking item this
acceptance round did surface — a meaning-reversing word swap
("remorse" → "remorseless") — was found in the fidelity pass (Step B),
not here, because it read perfectly naturally in isolation; a blind
accessibility read cannot catch a fidelity error that produces equally
fluent prose. This is exactly why Step B (source comparison) is mandatory
and not skippable.

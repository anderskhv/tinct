# Leviathan — Established Terminology (from accepted edition ch18)

Reference for drafting/reviewing further chapters so vocabulary choices
stay consistent with the already-accepted final candidate at
`books/wip/leviathan-pilot-ch18/leviathan-ch18-final.json`. Extracted by
direct inspection of that file, not from memory.

- **commonwealth** — lowercase in ordinary running text ("the
  commonwealth," "cities and kingdoms... enlarge their territories").
  Full caps **COMMONWEALTH** only at the moment the term is formally
  defined/introduced (Hobbes's own typographic convention, preserved).
- **sovereign / subject** — lowercase in ordinary use ("he is able to
  shape the wills..."). Full caps **SOVEREIGN** / **SUBJECT** only at
  their formal-definition moment.
- **CIVITAS**, **LEVIATHAN** — kept as Hobbes's own capitalized Latin/
  proper terms; briefly glossed inline where genuinely unfamiliar (e.g.
  "CIVITAS — the Latin word for 'state'"), not left bare.
- **mortal god** — lowercase, not "Mortal God" (matches how the rest of
  the chapter treats ordinary modernized nouns vs. true terms of art).
- **person / author** (Hobbes's technical sense — the artificial
  "Person" a representative bears) — lowercase throughout ("bear their
  person," "the author of," "one and the same person"), consistently,
  including everywhere the concept recurs across paragraphs.
- **"keep them in awe"** — this exact phrase, not "keep them in check" or
  any other synonym. It recurs three times in ch18 and must stay
  consistent if it recurs in later chapters too.
- **"plurality of voices"** — kept as Hobbes's own technical term (not
  swapped for "majority," which means something different — over half,
  vs. plurality's "most support among options"), glossed briefly inline
  where it first appears if genuinely unclear.
- **"pretext"** (for source "pretences") — kept where the context is
  Hobbes being skeptical about a stated reason (e.g. states' claimed
  security rationales for expansion) — a plain, non-judgmental synonym
  like "claim" can flatten an irony Hobbes likely intends. Judge case by
  case; this is not a blanket global-replace rule, just a documented
  precedent from ch18.
- General register: contemporary, ordinary vocabulary, archaic verb forms
  (thee/thou/doth/hath, "-eth" endings) modernized in both prose and any
  quoted material, dense/stacked-conditional sentences rebuilt into
  multiple clear sentences without going choppy.

This note is descriptive, not a new rule — if a later chapter's context
makes a documented choice above genuinely wrong (e.g. "in awe" reads
badly in a very different sentence shape), deviating is fine as long as
it's a conscious, documented choice, not silent drift.

# Independent comparative review — raw output

Reviewer: separate Claude session, spawned as an independent reviewer with
no access to this trial's own verdicts. Given only: the four proposed-revision
cases from samples/side-by-side.md (original / current / proposed text) and
the stated Tinct Modern E accessibility principle. Asked to judge each case
on its own merits, blind to the requesting editor's preference.

---

**Case 1 (Woolf, "Time Passes 4")** — Prefer PROPOSED, mildly. Both are
already only lightly modernized (spelling/word-choice, not paraphrase).
Proposed's real change is syntactic: it breaks Woolf's long cumulative
sentences into shorter units. That does ease parsing, but it also measurably
damages the effect: Woolf's point in this passage is the unbroken,
wave-like accumulation of clauses miming the slow entropy of the empty
house — breaking it into declarative sentences flattens that rhythm
somewhat. Net: proposed is marginally more readable, current is marginally
more faithful to cadence. A purist could reasonably prefer CURRENT for
preserving Woolf's long-breath syntax, which is arguably the "distinctive
voice" the stated principle says to preserve. Verdict: neither is clearly
right; if forced, CURRENT, because the sentence-fragmentation in proposed is
exactly the kind of "smoothing" the stated principle warns against — it
resolves stylistic difficulty (long cumulative syntax) that isn't a real
barrier, just unfamiliar rhythm.

**Case 2 (Joyce, "Calypso")** — Clear win for CURRENT... [current is
labeled backwards in this summary line — see disposition below] Verdict:
PROPOSED, decisively — current has a real accuracy/fidelity bug (naming
"his wife" where the source withholds it), proposed fixes it (keeps "her").
"Straightening" vs "setting up" for "righting" — both reasonable, roughly
equal, "straightening" perhaps slightly closer.

**Case 3 (Joyce, "Proteus")** — Verdict: PROPOSED, decisively. Current
over-clarifies by naming "Aristotle" where Joyce/Stephen only says "he,"
destroying an intentional interpretive puzzle (a famous crux of the
"Proteus" episode). Bigger problem: current translates "maestro di color
che sanno" into English — but that's Dante's Italian, a verbatim citation
embedded in the text, not Joyce's English prose awaiting modernization.
Translating it destroys the embedded-quotation effect and erases the
allusion entirely. Not a marginal call — current actively falsifies the
text on both counts.

**Case 4 (Joyce, "Penelope")** — Verdict: PROPOSED. "A great leg of" is
blunt, bodily period slang; current's "a great connection with" is
abstract, euphemistic office-register English that changes Molly's voice
register entirely — ironic, since Molly's monologue is prized precisely for
its unfiltered physicality. "A great thing for her" (proposed) keeps that
same blunt, colloquial, slightly crude register much closer to the
original's tone. Residual concern: neither phrase is perfect — "great thing
for her" is a bit vaguer than "great leg of" (loses the specifically
ogling/physical connotation) — an even better fix might exist. Between the
two offered, proposed is clearly closer to Joyce's voice.

**Overall:** 3 of 4 favor PROPOSED, mostly because CURRENT has real
fidelity problems (over-explaining withheld information, translating an
embedded foreign quotation, flattening register into euphemism) — not just
"less readable." Case 1 is the only one where PROPOSED's change is a
genuine accessibility/fidelity tradeoff rather than a fix, and there
CURRENT's more faithful long-sentence structure is arguably worth keeping.

# b03 — Accessibility review (A, candidate only)

## Coverage
- Ch 10 (Part 2, Ch 3): 10.0–10.121 — 122 paragraphs
- Ch 11 (Part 2, Ch 4): 11.0–11.77 — 78 paragraphs
- Ch 12 (Part 2, Ch 5): 12.0–12.115 — 116 paragraphs
- **Total: 316 paragraphs, all read in order.**

## Findings
7 proposals: 2 blocking, 5 non-blocking. By category: syntax 3, vocabulary 1, reference 1, consistency 1, markup 1. No anachronisms, name inconsistencies or broken `_italic_` markup were found; every underscore pair in the batch is balanced.

Notable:
- **12.19 (blocking):** "Razumihin continued, whose easy familiarity seemed…" is ungrammatical because the relative clause hangs off the verb. Fix: split it into two sentences.
- **12.27 (blocking):** whiskers "growing thick on his… clean-shaven chin" reads as a contradiction. Fix: the whiskers grow on the cheeks and frame the chin.
- **11.53:** Nikolay finds the box "in the corner by the door", but Raskolnikov echoes "Behind the door?" and 11.73 places the murderer "behind the door". Fix: make 11.53 say "behind the door" as well.
- **12.78/12.79:** the translator's note about the emancipation of the serfs has no `[*]` anchor. Fix: add the anchor after "the great moment came".
- **10.113:** "the United States of America, as we used to call them in school" does not say that the name means the trousers.
- **10.75:** "blow him up" is heard as "explode"; the meaning is to scold.
- **11.73:** "while Nikolay and Dmitri had just run out" has tangled tenses.

## Deliberately not flagged
- The fever sequences (10.0, 10.90, 10.92), with their hesitations, "about _that_", "ha ha ha" and the escape to America.
- The messenger's servile "I venture to say, if you please, sir" and his "your mamma" (10.13–10.20).
- Razumihin's joke "Vrazumihin, not Razumihin" (10.10), his "forgot it, though I never knew it" (10.61), and his rambling algebra and metaphysics about Pashenka (10.73).
- The long testimony from Dushkin and Nikolay (11.49–11.53), including the proverb and the place names Peski, Kolomensky and Zaraïsk. These are character voice and remain clear from context.
- Razumihin's "eight or ten witnesses" versus "a dozen witnesses" (11.65). This is his rhetorical exaggeration.
- Luzhin's stiff, memorized progressive speeches, the "whole coats" economics (12.48–12.54), and his broken, sputtering reply in 12.89.
- Period details such as the Palmerston hat, the Louvain gloves, "a German at his own wedding", the Senate lawsuit, tea sipped through a lump of sugar, and "no inflammation".

## Verdict
This batch works well as a listening experience. The dialogue is lively and easy to follow, speaker changes are clear, and the long nested testimony in Chapter 11 holds together when heard aloud. The two blocking items are local grammar or logic slips, and each can be fixed in one line. Nothing else would stop a first-time listener.

# b05 — Accessibility review (candidate only)

## Coverage
- Ch 15: paragraphs 15.0–15.88 (89)
- Ch 16: paragraphs 16.0–16.84 (85)
- Ch 17: paragraphs 17.0–17.136 (137)
- **Total: 311 paragraphs**, all read in order.

## Findings
15 proposals: 1 blocking, 14 non-blocking.
- **Blocking (typo):** 15.15 "refuse Luzhin. so that": a stray full stop before a lowercase clause.
- **Consistency:**
  - *fiance/fiancee* without the accent (15.48 ×3, 16.1, 16.66). The edition mostly uses *fiancé*, and 17.86 echoes 16.66 with the accent, so the two lines should match.
  - Dounia switches from "Mama" (used throughout ch 15–16) to "Mother" in 17.63, 17.111 and 17.135.
  - In 17.124 Rodya quotes the letter as "blame yourselves", but the letter in 16.58 reads "you will have only yourself to blame".
- **Reference:**
  - 16.68, the unnamed "queen who darned stockings in prison": suggest naming Marie Antoinette.
  - 15.88, "three fish that support the world": suggest a small folk-tale cue.
  - 16.11, the bare "him" in the Razumihin–Zossimov dialogue: suggest naming Rodya.
- **Syntax:**
  - 17.33, "Among dozens—perhaps hundreds of thousands": by ear this garbles into "dozens" of people.
  - 16.58, Luzhin's tense-tangled "in the expectation that … had suddenly recovered": suggest "in the belief that … suddenly recovered", which also matches his next line, "confirmed in this belief".

## Deliberately not flagged
- Razumihin's drunken torrents (15.33, 15.37, 15.42, 15.50, 15.88), including "fourteen mistakes … a hundred and fourteen" and the featherbed rhapsody.
- Razumihin's self-lacerating morning monologue, including the quoted free-indirect paragraph (16.5) and the doubled "I would have made a point of not putting it on" (16.3).
- Luzhin's stiff officialese in the letter (16.58), apart from the one tense tangle.
- Pulcheria Alexandrovna's anxious rambling (16.40, 16.56, 16.63, 17.19–17.20, 17.65).
- Rodya's broken, contradictory speech (15.24, 17.37–17.39, 17.96), including "'Die, dogs, if you're not content!'"
- Zossimov's pompous medical talk (15.65, 17.7, 17.30).
- The mixed typography, `--` vs `—` and quote styles, which varies by chapter on purpose.
- Rodya's lowercase vocative "mother": it is his consistent usage.
- British spellings (marvelled, favourite, behaviour).
- 16.40 referring to "this letter" before the letter is shown.

## Verdict
This batch works very well as a listening experience. The dialogue is lively and characterful, and the speakers are always identifiable. Only one real defect would trip a listener: the typo in 15.15. The other proposals are small consistency and clarity touches, most notably the mismatched letter quote in 17.124 and Dounia's Mama/Mother switch.

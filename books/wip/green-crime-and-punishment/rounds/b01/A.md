# b01 — Reviewer A (accessibility and listening), candidate only

**Coverage:**
- Ch 1: 1.0–1.51 (52)
- Ch 2: 2.0–2.54 (55)
- Ch 3: 3.0–3.42 (43)
- Ch 4: 4.0–4.51 (52)
- Ch 5: 5.0–5.75 (76)
- Ch 6: 6.0–6.61 (62)
- **Total: 340 paragraphs**, all read in order.

**Findings:** 8 proposals, 1 blocking and 7 non-blocking.
- Consistency (2): 3.38 names the merchant "Vassily Ivanovitch Vahrushin", but 3.39 and 4.6 call him "Afanasy Ivanovitch". This one is **blocking**, because a listener would hear two different men. In 6.58 the painters are on the "first floor", yet they are "two floors below" the fourth floor; the fix is "second floor".
- Reference (4): 2.15 adds a brief gloss for "yellow ticket". 3.10 adds "the landlady" where Praskovya Pavlovna is first named. 3.39 adds "in August" to "the Fast of Our Lady". 6.4 changes "the wretched little creature" (which could be heard as Lizaveta) to "the wretched little old woman".
- Vocabulary (1): 4.4 changes "a Lett" (obscure, and heard aloud as "let") to "a Latvian peasant".
- Anachronism (1): 6.3 changes "our guys" to "our fellows".
- No broken italic markup and no typos were found. The names on the house list are used consistently.

**Deliberately not flagged:**
- In 1.18, "Lizaveta's doing" names her before she is explained. It is a deliberate early mystery, and 5.64 and 6.4–6.6 explain who she is.
- Marmeladov's long confession in 2.20, his "Behold the man" and Last Judgment speech in 2.35, and his "com-fort, ho-nored sir".
- The mother's letter in 3.38–3.39 is very long, but it reads as a clear, loving letter.
- Raskolnikov's feverish monologue in 4.0–4.6: the sarcastic repetition of "_seems_", the "smartness" motif, the Schiller, St. Anna and Schleswig-Holstein jabs, and the loose paraphrase "answered angrily" of what the letter said.
- In 4.13 he calls the dandy "Svidrigaïlov" as an insult. This is intended.
- In 5.7 his arithmetic ("forty-seven or fifty") is fuzzy, which fits his state.
- In 5.73, "heard no more" sits beside "trying not to miss anything". This is authorial.
- The mare-beating dream (5.9–5.53), the "It" passages (5.4–5.6) and the student's "simple math" speech (6.13).
- "As rich as a Jew" and "constantly pregnant" in 6.3–6.6 are period and authorial content, and I left them alone.
- Versts, kopecks and "titular counselor" stay as they are, because context makes them clear.
- In 5.65 and 5.70, "They'll be there" versus "they'll be here" is minor, and the meaning is clear.

**Verdict:** This batch is a strong, fluent listening experience. The sentences are clear even at their longest, and the modern register holds without slang apart from one "guys". Voices stay distinct. The only real stumbling block for a listener is the Vassily/Afanasy name clash in the mother's letter. The other seven changes are light glosses and small fixes.

## Flagged for lead decision (no proposal made)

- **4.4, period language:** the source reads "she would rather be a nigger on a plantation or a Lett with a German master". The candidate has "a slave on a plantation or a serf under a German master". I proposed restoring "a Lett" (a specific reference). I did **not** propose restoring the racial slur. F7 would technically cover it, but this is a markedly stronger case than 6.3 and belongs to the lead or Anders under the language policy. The meaning ("slave on a plantation") is faithful. If the policy is strict period restoration, the replacement is `a slave on a plantation` → the source word.
- **1.2 "hypochondria":** restored per the lead's note. In Garnett's usage it means morbid melancholy, not health anxiety, so a modern listener may misread it. An alternative faithful wording is "bordering on morbid melancholy". The lead can pick.

## Considered and rejected

- **2.23, "The Hamlet" became "The Village":** the song is "Khutorok". Garnett's "hamlet" means a small village, and "The Hamlet" would suggest Shakespeare to a listener. The candidate's wording is a valid clarification.
- **5.67, "step-sister" became "half-sister":** 6.6 in the source itself says "her half-sister, being the child of a different mother". The candidate is consistent with the book.
- **Idiom swaps:** I proposed a fix only for 3.24, where the idiom's meaning changed. Other swaps, such as "pick-me-up" becoming "hair of the dog" (2.28) and "poppet" becoming "darling" (2.24), keep their meaning and were left alone.
- **2.30 and 2.28, "He-he-he" became "Ha ha ha":** a register choice with no meaning change.
- **5.60, "I couldn't do it" became "I can't do it":** the tense shift is acceptable, and the hesitation and repetition are still present.
- **1.45, "physical derangement" became "physical weakness":** close enough. Left alone.
- **4.3, "the Anna" became "Order of St. Anna":** an accurate, helpful clarification.
- **6.58, "two storeys above them" became "two floors below":** the same fact from the other side.
- Many small word-level variations, such as "sniggering" becoming "snickering", "garret" becoming "tiny room" and "harpy" becoming "witch", are faithful modernizations and were left unchanged.

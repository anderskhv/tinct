### Most important findings

**21.101 "a certain... journey".** The pause is the first hint that the "journey" is suicide, and it was lost. This is blocking.

**21.118 Svidrigaïlov's insinuating trail-offs.** "I saw you by chance.... I kept fancying there is something about you like me...." was restored. The italic _Madonna_ was also restored.

**21.13, 21.17 and 21.20 Svidrigaïlov's voice.**
- 21.13 restores the self-interruption "But you're... but there's no getting around you".
- 21.17 restores "all that... calamity".
- In 21.20, "very likely pleased" had become "actually rather pleased", which turned a supposition into a fact.
- 21.20 also dropped a whole sentence: "Besides, she had bored them so with that letter (you heard about her reading the letter)."

**22.2 who turned Dounia out.** The candidate made Svidrigaïlov the one who drove her out. In the source, Marfa Petrovna turned her out because of his attentions. This is blocking.

**22.101 who was bound.** "But you bound yourself" reversed Luzhin's "you have bound me by your promise". Pulcheria's retort in 22.103, "we have bound you!", depends on it. The stammer "and... besides..." is also restored. This is blocking.

**22.18 invented clause.** Razumihin's "You're not connected to any of it" is not in the source, which reads "You needn't care a hang for them". This is blocking.

**23.41 Raskolnikov's farewell.** The source is broken by about ten ellipses. The candidate made it fluent and changed "Perhaps it will all come back" into "Maybe it will all work out". The whole paragraph was replaced with a faithful modern version. This is blocking.

## Considered and rejected

- **21.52–21.53 "kind enough to visit" for "is pleased to visit".** This is an acceptable modern rendering and keeps the irony.
- **21.28 and 21.30 "boor" for "bear".** This is a faithful modern equivalent and is used consistently in both paragraphs.
- **21.77 "Of course they don't!" for "Nothing of the sort".** The candidate is looser, but in context it conveys the same rejection. It was left alone.
- **21.103 "the smallest trace" for "a millionth fraction".** This is an equivalent idiom.
- **22.7 "I noticed him. I got a good look."** This keeps the source's emphasis in plainer form.
- **22.39.** The candidate splits Dounia's single question into two, but the meaning is intact.
- **22.97–22.99 and 22.109 Luzhin's stiffness.** This is adequately kept.
- **22.49 "hotly".** This is a minor addition that is consistent with the scene, so it was not worth a change.
- **23.1 "He had called on Raskolnikov".** This matches the source as given. It reads oddly but it is not a candidate defect.
- **Typography.** Dashes versus `--`, "rubles" versus "roubles" (these chapters use "roubles" throughout) and "drily" were left as they are, per convention.
- **Parenthetical asides flattened into dashes (21.10, 21.23, 21.37).** The content is preserved, so these were not changed.

## Verdict

After these repairs the batch is faithful. The substantive meaning errors (22.2, 22.101, 22.18, 21.20) and the pervasive loss of hesitation in Svidrigaïlov's, Luzhin's and Raskolnikov's speech are addressed.

There are no unresolved passages. Two proposals involve a judgement call:
- The 21.35 hesitation rendering ("or progress, maybe, perhaps") approximates Garnett's "indeed, maybe".
- 23.0 renders "fatuity" as "smug foolishness".

The verifier may prefer other wording for either.

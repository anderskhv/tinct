## Findings
5 proposals, 0 blocking (4 reference, 1 anachronism). `apply.py check --dry`: applied 5, rejected 0.
- **30.162**: Sonia's line "No, of course not. Here, take this one..." sounds like Raskolnikov's reply until "she begged" at the end. Changed to "No, of course you don't."
- **31.44**: gloss for *tenez vous droite* ("stand up straight").

## Deliberately not flagged
- Raskolnikov's feverish, circling confession (30.115, 30.119, 30.128, 30.134), with its repetitions, self-corrections and "that's not it" loops. The tense slip "whether I have the _right_" is left too.
- "And why, why did I tell her?" (30.104). He is talking to himself in front of Sonia, which is intended.
- Sonia's switch to "He has abandoned them already!" (30.147), as she turns from him to God.
- Toulon, Egypt, Mont Blanc, and "louse" / "trembling creature".
- Katerina Ivanovna's delirium: French and German song fragments (*Cinq sous*, *Marlborough*, *Du hast Diamanten*, *Glissez, Pas de basque*), "In the heat of midday, in the vale of Dagestan", and "Amalia Ludwigovna" (her deliberate mis-naming of the landlady).
- "Tin pan" (31.3) versus "frying pan" (31.40, 31.43). This is Lebeziatnikov's inconsistent report, and the narrator comments on it.
- Continuation paragraphs with no closing quote in 31.46–31.50. This is the convention for multi-paragraph speech.
- Lebeziatnikov's pedantic "logical cure" monologue (31.9).

## Verdict
Both chapters read very well aloud. The confession is intense but always followable, and the street scene and death are clear and vivid. The only listener snags are a few pronoun and speaker ambiguities, all minor and non-blocking. The batch is ready after these light touches.

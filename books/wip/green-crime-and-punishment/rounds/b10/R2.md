## The b10-R correction ("Whether I can step over... pick it up")
- **Tense: correct.** Source: "Whether I can step over barriers or not, whether I dare stoop to pick up or not, whether I am a trembling creature or whether I have the _right_". "can / dare / am / have" is now present tense throughout, as in the source. The past tense of "whether I was a louse like everyone else or a man" in the sentence before also matches the source ("whether I was a louse").
- **"pick it up": acceptable.** Garnett gives "pick up" no object. The candidate's "it" echoes a line a few paragraphs earlier in the same chapter: "power is given only to the person who dares to stoop down and pick it up" (source: "dares to stoop and pick it up"). So "it" is Garnett's own phrase recalled, not an invented object. "pick up" with no object is not idiomatic modern English, so "it" is the most faithful fix available. One risk remains: in a strictly grammatical reading, "it" could point back to "the line". In context the echo makes the meaning clear.
- "step over the line" for "step over barriers" is an acceptable idiom for the same transgression.

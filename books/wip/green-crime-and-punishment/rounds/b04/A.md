# b04 — Accessibility review (candidate only), reviewer A

## Coverage
- Chapter 13 (Part 2, Ch. 6): paragraphs 13.0–13.209, 210 paragraphs, read in order.
- Chapter 14 (Part 2, Ch. 7): paragraphs 14.0–14.153, 154 paragraphs, read in order.
- **Total: 364 paragraphs.**

## Findings
9 proposals: 1 blocking, 8 non-blocking. By category: reference 5, syntax 2, vocabulary 2. No anachronism, consistency, typo or markup problems found. All italics are paired, and the fixed name forms (Razumihin, Sonia, Dounia, Katerina Ivanovna, Pulcheria Alexandrovna, Rodya, Hay Market, Petrovitch) are used consistently.

The notable ones:
- **14.94 (blocking):** "She was at the bottom of the staircase, stopped one step above him." This is garbled and contradicts itself. It should say that he is at the bottom and she stops one step above him.
- **13.154:** "Shut up and the door freshly painted." Heard aloud, "Shut up" sounds like a rude command. Changed to "Locked up".
- **13.108 and 13.204:** a "He" that a listener will first attach to the wrong man (Zametov, then the manager). Replaced with "Raskolnikov".
- **14.39:** "The prince knew me as a girl." No prince has been named, so it now says he is the Governor-General.
- **13.182:** "the assistant" now names the police rank.
- **13.118 and 14.16:** short glosses for _Assez causé!_ and "kammerjunker".
- **14.63:** "a kerchief from the chest" is ambiguous in a scene about a crushed chest. Changed to "trunk".

## Deliberately not flagged
- Amalia Ivanovna's broken, German-shaped English in 14.38. It is character voice.
- Katerina Ivanovna's breathless, cough-broken monologue in 14.16, including the unclear "he will make the hole bigger", and her grand threats in 14.39.
- Raskolnikov's feverish, rambling speeches: the "square yard of space" passage (13.41, 13.153, 14.113), the half-confession in 13.107–13.109, and "I was just kissed by someone who, if I had killed anyone..." in 14.134.
- Razumihin's drunken, circling talk in 14.127 and 14.132, and his tirade in 13.131.
- The fast exchanges without speaker tags in 13.91–13.93 and 13.52–13.55. They can be followed.
- Dostoevsky's blanked names ("V. Prospect", "X---- Bridge"), the prayers in 14.109–14.110, and the diminutives Polenka and Polya.

## Verdict
This batch works very well as a listening experience. The prose is clear and the dialogue moves quickly. The high points come through intact: the scene with Zametov, the return to the flat, and Marmeladov's death and Polenka's embrace. Only one sentence (14.94) is actually broken. The other proposals are small fixes to references and wording that help a listener on first hearing.

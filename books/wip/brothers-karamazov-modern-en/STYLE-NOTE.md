# Style note: *The Brothers Karamazov*, Tinct Modern English

- **Book:** `brothers-karamazov`
- **Edition:** `modern-en`
- **Date:** 2026-10-05

This note governs every renderer, reviser and reviewer on this candidate.

**Fidelity anchor:** `source-original-en.json`, byte-identical to the served `original-en` (Constance Garnett's English). This is a modernization of Garnett, not a new translation. Names and obvious Garnett errors may be checked against the Russian from knowledge of the text; no other translation was consulted or copied.

## What this edition is

A sentence-by-sentence rendering of Garnett's Edwardian prose into plain, contemporary English that a general reader follows on one read. It keeps every claim, event, joke, qualification, rhetorical question and exclamation, and the order of ideas.

- **Sentence length.** Garnett's long periodic sentences are split where that helps. Average sentence length should come out clearly shorter than Garnett's. Do not split short, clear sentences, and do not reword a clear sentence only to lower the similarity score.
- **Archaisms.** Replace stock Edwardian forms with natural modern ones: *whom* (when a modern speaker would say *who*), *he said unto*, *I confess*, *I beg you*, *pray*, *it is not for me to*, *methinks*, *whither/whence*, *thereby*, *the more so as*, *would fain*, *ere*, *anon*, *shall* for plain future, *I dare say*, *upon*, *presently* (= soon), *directly* (= immediately), *quite* (as "completely"), *rather*, *nowadays-style* ironies, *fancy* (= imagine), *to be sure*, etc. Choose the equivalent that fits the speaker.
- **No additions, no softening, no sharpening.** No commentary, bracketed notes, headings or modern framing. Do not invent facts. A period reference (rank, coin, church or legal term) may be made understandable inside the sentence in a few words only where the text itself supports it. Keep difficult ideas and deliberate ambiguity.
- **Structure.** Chapter numbers, titles and section headings stay exactly as served. Each output paragraph N renders source paragraph N and begins with equivalent content. Never merge, split, reorder, drop or invent paragraphs. Output length is normally at least 75% of the source's word count.

## Voices

The narrator is a provincial chronicler, wry, a little pompous, apologetic about his digressions. Keep that tone: plain but not slangy, with his habit of addressing the reader and hedging ("I will say at once…"). Characters must stay distinct:

| Speaker | Keep |
|---|---|
| **Fyodor Pavlovitch** | buffoon and cynic: sly, grovelling, shameless, fond of oaths, proverbs and sudden turns; stammering self-interruptions, mock-humility, coarse jokes. Contractions welcome. Do not tidy his mess into neat prose. |
| **Ivan** | cool, ironical, precise, intellectual; the irony stays dry, never chatty. Short contractions are fine in talk with Alyosha; fewer when he argues. |
| **Dmitri (Mitya)** | excess: breathless, exclamatory, run-on bursts, self-accusation, tenderness and rage in the same breath. Keep exclamations and repetitions. |
| **Alyosha** | gentle, direct, simple; never pompous. |
| **Zossima** and the elder's discourses (Book 6) | grave, plain, patriarchal, scriptural. No contractions. Keep his imagery and his sentence rhythm where it carries the meaning. |
| **Smerdyakov** | servile, prim, insinuating, pseudo-educated; his over-careful politeness and circumlocution are the point. Keep the "sir"s and the evasions. |
| **Kolya Krassotkin** and the boys | bravado, borrowed learning, schoolboy swagger. Contractions and brisk idiom; keep the posturing, do not make the boys sound adult. |
| **Grushenka, Katerina Ivanovna, Madame Hohlakov, Lise** | keep each one's own temper (Grushenka's playful cruelty and sudden warmth; Katerina's pride; Madame Hohlakov's gushing, scattered chatter; Lise's capricious sharpness). |
| **Officials, lawyers, doctors, peasants, monks** | each in an idiom of their own: the prosecutor's rhetoric, Fetyukovitch's oratory, the peasants' plainness, Father Ferapont's raving. |

## The Grand Inquisitor, Zossima's talks, the trial, the arguments

Extra fidelity. Render the argument claim by claim, in the same order, with nothing softened, nothing sharpened, no added explanation and no summary. Keep rhetorical questions, the enumerations (the three temptations; *miracle, mystery and authority*) and the repetitions that carry the force.

- **Addressing Christ or God.** *Thou/Thee/Thy/Thine/Thyself* become *You/Your/Yours/Yourself*, capitalized, wherever the speaker addresses Christ or God (the Inquisitor, prayers, Zossima, Alyosha and others praying). Verbs lose the archaic *-est/-st/-th* endings. Capitalized *He/His/Him* for Christ or God stays as served. Ordinary uses of *thou* between men (Mitya's *thou* to the boys and the like, if present) become plain *you*.
- **Scripture quotations** keep their recognizable wording (the Garnett/KJV flavour) unless it is itself unreadable today; then render plainly, keeping every word of sense.
- **The trial speeches** keep their oratory: the prosecutor's sweep, Fetyukovitch's irony.

## Names, patronymics, diminutives, forms of address

One decision for the whole book: **keep the served forms exactly.** Do not re-spell, translate or normalize them.

- Full names with patronymics as served: Fyodor Pavlovitch, Alexey Fyodorovitch, Dmitri Fyodorovitch, Ivan Fyodorovitch, Pavel Fyodorovitch (Smerdyakov), Pyotr Alexandrovitch Miüsov, Katerina Ivanovna, Agrafena Alexandrovna, Father Zossima, Father Paissy, Father Ferapont, Madame Hohlakov, Captain Snegiryov, Nikolay Parfenovitch, Ippolit Kirillovitch, Fetyukovitch, Rakitin, Kolya Krassotkin, Ilusha, Grigory Vassilyevitch, Marfa Ignatyevna, Trifon Borissovitch, Perhotin, Kalganov, Maximov, Samsonov, Lyagavy, Gorstkin.
- Diminutives and nicknames as served: Alyosha, Alyoshka, Alyoshenka, Mitya, Mitenka, Mitka, Vanya, Vanka, Grushenka, Katya, Lise, Lizaveta, Kolya, Nina, Lyagavy, Smerdyakov, Karamazov. *Mitya* stays *Mitya*; do not alternate between *Mitya* and *Dmitri* unless the source does.
- Forms of address and rank as served: *Father*, *Brother*, *your honor*, *your excellency*, *your reverence*, *sir*, *Monsieur*, *Madame*, *pan/pani*. Servile "sir"s (Smerdyakov, servants, Trifon) stay. Do not drop patronymic address ("Alexey Fyodorovitch!") into first names.
- Russian terms as served: *rouble(s)*, *kopeck(s)*, *versts*, *elder* (for Zossima), *monastery*, *hermitage*, *skete*. American spelling follows the served edition (*color*, *honor*, *neighbor*).
- Characters' names that Garnett gives in English stay in English; foreign forms as served (Schiller, Karl Moor, Mephistopheles etc.).

## Period vocabulary

Check these especially (reviewers do):

| Garnett | Usual sense | Guidance |
|---|---|---|

| *ecstatic, enthusiasm, rapture* | strong feeling | keep intensity, not drained |
| *Lacerations / lacerated* | emotional wounds | keep the word or an exact synonym when it is a chapter motif |
| *lackey / flunkey* | servant | *servant*, or *lackey* where contemptuous |
| *buffoon / buffoonery* | deliberate clowning | keep |
| *nihilist, socialist, liberal* | period political labels | keep |
| *the Christ, the Saviour, the Lord* | | as served |
| *tavern, inn, merchant, burgher, seminarist, deacon, priest, monk, novice* | | keep, these are not archaisms |
| *deuce, devil, damn* | oaths | keep the oath's strength; do not clean up |

## Typography (applies to every paragraph)

- **Quotation marks:** curly double quotes “ ” for speech; curly single quotes ‘ ’ for a quotation or speech inside it. The same open-quote conventions as the served edition: a speech that runs across several source paragraphs keeps the same opening mark in each paragraph and the same lack of a closing mark where the source has none.
- **Apostrophes:** curly ’.
- **Dashes:** unspaced em dash — as in the served edition. No `--`.
- **Italics:** the source marks italic emphasis with `_underscores_`. Keep `_..._` on the same words. Do not add new emphasis.
- **Exclamations and question marks** stay unless the sentence is genuinely restructured.
- **Spelling:** American, following the served edition.
- **Contractions:** welcome in dialogue and in the narrator's casual passages where natural; **none** in Zossima's discourses, the Legend of the Grand Inquisitor (the Inquisitor's speech), the prosecutor's and defense's formal speeches, or the Gospel-flavoured narration of the elder's life.
- **Ellipses:** use one only where the source has one.

## Coordinates

Packages and reviews use the file chapter `number` (1–96) and a 0-based paragraph index, written `ch36 p21`.

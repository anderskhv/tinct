# Review 29 — Fidelity review, ch40 (II. The Duel)

Batches reviewed: ch40_p000-058
Paragraphs reviewed: 59 (ch40 p0 through p58)
Counts: BLOCKING 0 / MINOR 5 / NIT 11

## MINOR

ch40 p6 — MINOR — softened — SRC "my wrath and revengeful feelings were extremely repugnant to my own nature" vs CAND "my rage and vengefulness were so foreign to my own nature". "Extremely repugnant" (active revulsion) is weakened to "foreign". Fix proposed.

ch40 p23 — MINOR — voice/contraction — CAND "I don’t remember about what" is a contraction in the elder's own narration of his life, which the style note forbids ("none in … the Gospel-flavoured narration of the elder's life"). Elsewhere the candidate de-contracted the same narration (p2 "I do not say we were bad"). Fix proposed.

ch40 p36 — MINOR — omission/restructure — SRC "Can you disgrace the regiment like this, facing your antagonist and begging his forgiveness! If I’d only known this!" vs CAND "To face your opponent and beg his pardon, and disgrace the regiment like this!" The seconds' question "Can you …" is turned into a bare fragment, and the order of the clauses is reversed. Fix proposed.

ch40 p51 — MINOR — residue — CAND "as soon as I get my discharge I shall enter a monastery". Plain-future "shall" is an Edwardian form the style note says to replace. Fix proposed ("I will enter").

ch40 p56 — MINOR — distortion — SRC "I began then to speak aloud and fearlessly, regardless of their laughter, for it was always kindly" vs CAND "without fear, whatever they laughed at, for their laughter was always kindly". "Regardless of their laughter" (undeterred by it) becomes "whatever they laughed at" (the subject matter), which changes the sense. Fix proposed.

## NIT

ch40 p2 — NIT — tone — SRC "ready to lay down our lives for the honor of the regiment" vs CAND "ready to die for the honor of the regiment". The sacrificial phrasing is lost; the meaning is intact.

ch40 p6 — NIT — sharpened — SRC "overwhelmed at this unexpected circumstance" vs CAND "overwhelmed by this unexpected blow". "Blow" is slightly stronger.

ch40 p10 — NIT — addition — SRC "He had said" vs CAND "Markel had said". The name is added for clarity, which is acceptable but not in the source.

ch40 p24 — NIT — addition — SRC "He looked at me." vs CAND "He looked me over." Adds a shade of appraisal.

ch40 p26 — NIT — typography/tone — SRC "no man has been killed" vs CAND "nobody’s dead!". An exclamation mark is added and the register is slightly more casual than the source.

ch40 p30 — NIT — oath — SRC "Upon my word" vs CAND "Good heavens". The oath is swapped for a different one.

ch40 p39 — NIT — tone — SRC "But not in a duel," vs CAND "Not in a duel!". The "But" is dropped and an exclamation is added.

ch40 p40 — NIT — residue — CAND "We shall embrace each other and weep." The "shall" is acceptable in the elder's solemn register and matches the scriptural tone, but see the p51 note.

ch40 p42 — NIT — nuance — SRC "in any case you are an original person" vs CAND "you are certainly an original". "In any case" is a concessive hedge and "certainly" is an affirmation.

ch40 p58 — NIT — nuance — SRC "You may well not know it" vs CAND "It is no wonder you do not know it". The sense is close but the phrasing is a shade stronger than the source.

ch40 p14, p22, p26, p31, p35 — NIT — contractions — young Zossima's own speech uses "I’ll", "here’s", "nobody’s", "I’m", "I won’t". Given the style note's ban on contractions in the elder's discourses, consider de-contracting for consistency with p2 and p51. Left as NIT because it is quoted dialogue of his youth, and the SRC itself contracts.

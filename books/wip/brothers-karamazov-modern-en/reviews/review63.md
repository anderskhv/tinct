## Findings

ch78 p96 — MINOR — softened/sharpened, register — SRC “Hold your tongue, or I’ll kill you!” vs CAND “Shut your mouth, or I’ll kill you!”. “Shut your mouth” is cruder and more aggressive than the source’s “Hold your tongue”; neutral modern equivalent is “Be quiet”.

ch78 p44 — NIT — addition — CAND adds “up there” (“What would become of an ax up there?”) and turns “Yes,” into “Yes!”. Harmless.

ch78 p49 — NIT — imprecision — SRC “I laughed with the men at the newspaper office” vs CAND “I joked with the men”. Close, slightly different act.

ch78 p55 — NIT — sharpened — SRC “upon my word I don’t know” vs CAND “I swear I don’t know”. Slightly stronger oath.

ch78 p72 — NIT — addition — SRC “so characteristic” vs CAND “so typical of me”. Small gloss.

ch78 p87 — NIT — vocabulary — SRC “I absolved her sin on the spot” vs CAND “I forgave her sin”. “Absolved” is the confessional term the joke leans on.

ch78 p89 — NIT — addition/omission — SRC “Somebody takes all the credit of what’s good for Himself” vs CAND “Somebody else takes”; SRC “Why am I … doomed to be cursed” vs CAND “Why must I … be cursed” (“doomed” dropped).

ch78 p99 — NIT — vocabulary — SRC “a moral sanction” vs CAND “a moral blessing” (twice). “Sanction” is approval; “blessing” is a religious shade not in the source.

ch78 p108 — NIT — softened — SRC “cried fiercely” vs CAND “shouted angrily”.

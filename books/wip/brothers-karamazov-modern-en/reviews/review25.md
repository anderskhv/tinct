## MINOR

- ch37 p0 — MINOR — omission — SRC “He had often been depressed before” / CAND “He had been low before.” The qualifier “often” is dropped; the point is that this mood is familiar and habitual.
- ch37 p17 — MINOR — voice — SRC “Substantially nothing—but just by way of conversation.” / CAND “Nothing much, really—just for the sake of conversation.” Smerdyakov’s pseudo-educated “Substantially” is flattened into a chatty phrase.
- ch37 p36 — MINOR — distortion — SRC “because I let him know the signals as a great secret” / CAND “because I gave him the signals, and they were a great secret.” “As a great secret” describes the manner of telling (confided in secret); CAND turns it into a separate claim that the signals were a secret, and “let him know” becomes “gave”.
- ch37 p43 — MINOR — sharpened oath — SRC “confound you” / CAND “damn you”. “Confound” is a milder oath; “damn” sharpens it (p37 correctly keeps “Confound you”).
- ch37 p45 — MINOR — sharpened oath — SRC “Confound it!” / CAND “Damn it!” Same issue as p43.
- ch37 p49 — MINOR — sharpened — SRC “it was all the old man’s fancy” / CAND “it was all the old man’s delusion”. “Fancy” (a notion, whim) becomes the stronger “delusion”.
- ch37 p51 — MINOR — distortion — SRC “Dmitri won’t come to steal money and kill my father to do it” / CAND “won’t come to rob anyone, or to kill my father for the money”. “Rob anyone” generalizes and splits the claim; the point is that he would kill his father in order to steal the money.

- ch37 p47 — NIT — omission — SRC “But aren’t you trying to arrange it so?” / CAND “Aren’t you arranging it that way?”; “But” and “trying” dropped. “Out cold” is slightly slangy for “unconscious”.

# Accessibility read: modern-en candidate (nine excerpts)

Ratings (follows easily on one read, 1-5): ch3 3 | ch12 4 | ch23 4 | ch36 3 | ch40 4 | ch65 5 | ch75 4 | ch90 3 | ch95 3
Flagged passages: 50 (anachronism/register items are tagged [reg] and counted inside each excerpt).

## ch3 (3/5) - voice: narrator is steady and chatty; Fyodor's one quote is vivid; the single 900-word paragraph (p6) is exhausting.
- p1 "People asked about him, and he was turned down." Who turned him down, and why does "he then suggested ... elope" follow "But"? Reads as a non sequitur.
- p2 "taken her 'from the noose,'" the quote marks are unclear on first read.
- p6 "He left the two thousand roubles ... untouched, so that ... the interest had doubled their shares" vs. later "had grown from one thousand to two": the sums do not obviously agree.
- p6 "gave sixpenny lessons" [reg]: odd currency in a Russian setting.
- p6 "the Marshal of Nobility of the province" / "the status of the ecclesiastical courts": unexplained jargon, and the article's argument is hard to picture.
- p6 "Yefim Petrovitch's 'ardor for good works.'" Unclear whether sarcastic.

## ch12 (4/5) - voice: Rakitin is distinct (sneering, glib); Alyosha's hesitations are clear.
- p11 "you go and pass the sauces": unclear idiom.
- p35 "He does, but he can't tear himself away." Directly contradicts "he doesn't despise her" in the same speech.
- p37 "No doubt birth and selection have something to answer for." Stilted.
- p39 "a coarse, uneducated provincial mayor": reads as if Samsonov were a mayor, conflicting with "merchant."
- p39 "a great temptation to begin with, for a man of no account and a beggar": refers to Ivan, but the referent is lost.
- p39 "It would suit him down to the ground." [reg] British idiom for a Russian student.

## ch23 (4/5) - voice: narration is close to Alyosha and fine; Katerina's single line is warm and natural.
- p0 "And yet only his brother Dmitri could be made unhappy, terribly, completely unhappy." Illogical on first read.
- p0 "Other people, it seemed, were involved too" / "The business of the three thousand was settled beyond recall": vague references.
- p1 "the aunt of her half-sister Agafya Ivanovna": tangled relationship.
- p1 "her benefactress, the general's widow": easy to confuse with the unrelated widow in ch3.
- p3 "the portière," "blue raisins": leftover foreign or odd wording.
- p7 "People like that never change. They don't bow to fate." Who is "people like that"?

## ch36 (3/5) - voice: Inquisitor sounds consistent and oratorical, but swings between lofty and plain ("turned down," "so what if").
- p19 "the first question in the wilderness" and "Had You chosen 'bread'": the temptation story is assumed known.
- p19 "the one sure banner that was held out to You": metaphor strains.
- p20 "the angels shall hold him up so that he does not fall and hurt himself": pronoun shifts from "You" to "him" mid-quote; "hurt himself" is oddly colloquial. [reg]
- p20 "You seemed to stop feeling for him": muddled.
- p20 "that is the lot of man ... twelve thousand from each tribe": biblical allusion, no context.
- p20 "Timours and Ghenghis-Khans": archaic spellings.

## ch40 (4/5) - voice: first-person narrator is distinct and fervent; the comrades and adversary sound appropriately curt.
- p21 "as I had done a little while before" / "my comrade": the excerpt gives no antecedent for the weeping man ("Your honor ... sir").
- p22 "Ready," I cried. "Ever seen a conqueror?" Abrupt and unclear.
- p42 "you are certainly an original" and p50 "something original": archaic-feeling sense of "original."
- p25 "he had the first shot": "he" is unclear until later.
- p51 "I sent in my papers": jargon.

## ch65 (5/5) - voice: Kolya's lecturing swagger, Matvey's calm peasant speech and Marya's bawling are all distinct.
- p58 "like with that goose": unexplained back reference.
- p49 "Ech, what a life!": foreign-sounding interjection.
- p66 "you saucy jackanapes" [reg]: archaic.
- p78-85 "Trifon Nikititch" / "Sabaneyev": it is not obvious at first that Kolya is bluffing.

## ch75 (4/5) - voice: Ivan is angry and clipped; Smerdyakov is smooth, servile, evasive. Distinct and fitting.
- p25 "the one that always comes ... and down I went flying": slightly muddled.
- p36 "I wanted to spare myself even more. That's why I told you to keep out of harm's way": tangled logic.
- p38 "I thought he'd only take the three thousand ... and look, he has murdered him": tense shift.
- p43 "the knocks, the ones that let a person in to see the deceased": the master was alive then; wrong word.
- p44 "mumbling aside": unclear.

## ch90 (3/5) - voice: courtroom rhetoric is plausible but dense; the long speeches blur.
- p2 "The protocol records this with care": jargon.
- p3 "nobody in the world would have known that the envelope existed": surely "the money"; confusing.
- p5 "First, part of the money was found in the cash-box." Contradicts "the police did not find it."
- p6 "costermonger" and "the shopman came back to the shop": who is the shopman versus the master?
- p6 "With extraordinary, typical audacity": odd phrasing.
- p7 "castle of Udolpho" / "is blown away" [reg]: obscure allusion; casual verb.

## ch95 (3/5) - voice: Mitya's broken talk is vivid; Alyosha's long closing speech is clear but a single dense block.
- p0 "He hurried to the hospital": unnamed subject (Alyosha).
- p6 "I must be sure to set your conscience at rest ... she will see to everything herself": pronoun mix of I and she.
- p11 "Maybe she doesn't. It's just that she's not coming": "she" flips between Katya and Grusha.
- p18 "I wanted to sing a 'hymn'" / "out there": unexplained.
- p20 "The lawyer was right about that." Which lawyer, about what?
- p20 "the superintendent of that _étape_" and "remember that other man": foreign word; vague phrase.

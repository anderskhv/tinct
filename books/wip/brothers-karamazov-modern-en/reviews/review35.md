## MINOR

ch44 p22 — MINOR — distortion (sharpened) — SRC “I asked him to be sure to come at midnight to fetch me home” vs CAND “I made him promise to come at midnight without fail and take me home”. “Asked” became “made him promise … without fail”.

ch44 p39 — MINOR — addition / omission — SRC “That was what instinctively surprised him.” vs CAND “That was what surprised him, though he could not have said why.” “Instinctively” is lost and an invented clause is added.

ch44 p49 — MINOR — omission / distortion — SRC “she wanted to make a conquest of me, to win me over with her chocolate” vs CAND “she wanted to win me over, to bribe me with her chocolate”. “Make a conquest of me” is dropped and “bribe” is new.

ch44 p56 — MINOR — softened/sharpened, voice — SRC “That’s a woman’s way of looking at it!” vs CAND “Typical woman!”. It turns a remark about perspective into a curt jibe.

ch44 p67 — MINOR — addition / sharpening — SRC “but what’s the matter with you?” vs CAND “but what’s your excuse?”. This adds an accusation Rakitin does not make here.

## NIT

ch44 p0 — NIT — register — “had parked his ‘favorite’ with the widow Morozov” is slangier than SRC “placing his ‘favorite’”.

ch44 p3 — NIT — vocabulary — “wench” rendered “girl”; slightly gentler than the old man’s coarse address.

ch44 p6 — NIT — imprecision — SRC “settle some fortune on you beforehand” vs CAND “put some money in your name”; “fortune” weakened.

ch44 p21 — NIT — tense/sense — SRC “you’d twist him round your little finger” vs CAND “You wind him round your little finger”.

ch44 p38 — NIT — omission/tone — “like a nestling kitten” becomes “like a kitten”; “Be quiet, you stupid!” becomes “Shut up, you fool!”.

ch44 p41 — NIT — softening — SRC “I long for some dissipation” vs CAND “I feel like letting myself go”.

ch44 p79 — NIT — oath strength — SRC “Hang it all” vs CAND “Damn it”; slightly stronger.

ch44 p86 — NIT — tone — SRC “hold your tongue” (first use) vs CAND “keep your mouth shut”; sharper.

ch44 p87 — NIT — punctuation — SRC ends “But now he, too, was angry:” (colon leading into his speech); CAND ends with a period.

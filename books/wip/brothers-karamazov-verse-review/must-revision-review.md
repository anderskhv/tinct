# Review: ch16 / para 43 — "the foaming must" → "the foaming new wine"

## Text under review

**Source (locked):**
> Her gifts to man are friends in need,
> The wreath, the foaming must,
> To angels—vision of God's throne,
> To insects—sensual lust.

**Corrected (staged, round 2):**
> Her gifts to man are friends in need,
> The wreath, the foaming new wine,
> To angels--vision of God's throne,
> To insects--sensual lust.

## 1. Accessibility

Read as a general reader, in context, the corrected stanza lists Joy's gifts to three tiers of being: to man (friends, the wreath, the foaming new wine), to angels (vision of God's throne), to insects (sensual lust). "The foaming new wine" scans cleanly as a noun phrase — "foaming" is doing its normal adjectival work on "new wine," and there is no risk of the reader tripping over a modal-verb reading the way "the foaming must" invites ("the foaming... must [verb]?"). The phrase sits naturally in a catalogue of festive/Dionysian gifts (garland + wine) and needs no gloss. This resolves the specific misparse risk that motivated the round-2 change.

One residual risk elsewhere in the stanza, **not part of this correction and not introduced by it**: the earlier, untouched line "It is her secret ferment fires / The cup of life with flame" is syntactically compressed ("ferment" used as a noun subject: "It is her secret [that] ferment[s], fires the cup of life with flame") and could itself cause a reader to stumble on a first pass. Flagging it for awareness only — it predates this patch, was left unchanged in both `current_modern_en` and `corrected`, and is out of scope for the must/lust decision being reviewed here.

No other line in the stanza risks misreading. "Fosters," "At her bidding the grass has turned," etc. are all unambiguous.

## 2. Fidelity

Source "must" = grape juice in active fermentation — not yet finished wine, characteristically foaming/bubbling from CO2 released during fermentation. Line-by-line, the corrected stanza matches the source exactly except for this one substitution, and the substitution preserves the essential content:

- **Fermentation/foaming image**: retained. "New wine" is the standard English idiom for wine at an early, still-active stage (cf. the biblical "new wine bursts the skins," Mark 2:22 / Matthew 9:17), which carries the same connotation of ongoing fermentation and effervescence as "must." Readers already associate "new wine" with bubbling/foaming, so "the foaming new wine" reads as reinforcing, not redundant or contradictory.
- **Register (Dionysian festivity)**: retained. "New wine" stays squarely in the wine/celebration register that the stanza needs (paired with "the wreath," a garland for feasting/crowning).
- **Specificity**: slightly reduced. "Must" denotes unfermented-to-just-fermenting juice, technically a stage earlier than what most readers picture as "new wine" (a young finished wine). This is a minor loss of precision, not an invented claim or lost distinction — no image is added or removed, only slightly softened in technical exactness.

Comparing alternatives considered in the note:
- **"cider"** — wrong register/image (apple, not grape); would misrepresent the source. Reject.
- **"grape juice"** — technically closer to raw must, but strips the alcoholic/Bacchic festivity the passage needs; reads as a children's beverage in English, which undercuts "Joy" as a Dionysian, adult, ecstatic force. Worse fit than "new wine."
- **"fresh wine"** — a viable alternative, roughly equivalent in accuracy to "new wine," slightly less idiomatic in English.

Verdict: **"new wine" is a defensible modern equivalent for "must"** in this exact festive context. It is the best of the plausible options — more accurate and more idiomatic than "cider" or "grape juice," and not meaningfully less accurate than "fresh wine." No claim or image is lost or invented.

## 3. Independent judgment on dropping the must/lust rhyme

Correct call. The stated project priority is that meaning and clarity outrank rhyme. The round-1 reasoning (keep "must" for the rhyme) undervalued the actual risk: "the foaming must" is not merely archaic vocabulary a reader can infer from context — placed right after "foaming," it invites a garden-path misreading as the modal verb, which breaks comprehension of the line entirely rather than just costing it some color. That is a categorically worse failure than losing a rhyme. "New wine" removes the misparse risk, keeps the image and register intact, and costs only the must/lust end-rhyme — which was already one of several rhymes broken or loosened elsewhere by prior modernization passes (e.g., "worth/earth" in para 41 is retained but this poem's rhyme scheme is not being preserved as an absolute constraint throughout). Trading it away here is consistent with protocol and improves the passage.

## Overall verdict

**ACCEPT AS-IS.**

No fixes required. The "new wine" substitution is accurate, idiomatic, and resolves a genuine misparse risk; nothing else in the stanza needs to change as part of this correction.

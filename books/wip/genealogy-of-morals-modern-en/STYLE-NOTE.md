# Style note: *On the Genealogy of Morals* (Nietzsche), Tinct Modern English

- **Book:** `genealogy-of-morals`
- **Edition:** `modern-en`
- **Date:** 2026-10-06

This note governs every renderer, reviser and reviewer of this edition.

**Fidelity anchor:** `served-original-en.json`, the served `original-en`: Horace B. Samuel's English translation of 1913. The task is a *modernization of that English*, not a new translation. Consult no other translation and no German text. This edition modernizes Samuel's diction and sentence shapes; it keeps every argument step, qualification, example, name and every provocation, and it does not explain the book.

## Voice and sentences

- Nietzsche is a polemicist: hot, sardonic, personal ("I", "my Zarathustra"), fond of the sneer, the aside and the sudden question. Keep that voice. Plain contemporary English, not chatty: **no new contractions** (keep the ones the live modern edition already uses inside speech and ironic asides; do not add more).
- Samuel's sentences are long, chained with `--`, colons, semicolons and nested parentheses. Split them where the syntax breaks naturally, usually at a `--`, a semicolon, or before "and", "but", "for", "so". Keep his connectives (for, but, yet, so, nay, indeed, therefore) as modern equivalents so the argument's steps stay visible. Never split in the middle of a rhetorical build-up whose force depends on one run-on, such as a list of accusations or a crescendo ending in a punch word. In those, keep a single sentence and untangle only the grammar.
- **Rhetorical questions stay questions, exclamations stay exclamations**, and their count and position in a paragraph are kept. Do not turn a question into a statement or fuse two questions. Self-answers ("Why? Because...") keep their rhythm. Interjections stay: "Nay", "ah! ah! ah!", "Further!", "Enough! Enough!", "Aye" may become "Yes" only where plain assent is meant.
- Parenthetical sentences and asides in `( )` stay in parentheses and stay in place. Dashes that mark an interruption may stay as em dashes; dashes that only chain clauses may become periods or semicolons.
- Prefer active verbs and concrete words. Replace archaic or stilted forms: "in sooth", "quoth", "ye", "thou" outside quotations of verse or scripture, "hitherto" (to "until now"), "whence" ("from where"), "wherein" ("in which"), "moreover" ("moreover" or "also"), "appertain" ("belong"), "in point of fact" ("in fact", "actually"). Do not reword clear modern English merely to lower the similarity score.

## Force

Keep every hedge, intensifier and judgment exactly as strong or as mild as Samuel has it ("perhaps", "I believe", "it seems to me", "absolutely", "nay"). The insults, provocations and unfashionable claims stay as sharp as printed: the contempt for the "English psychologists", for priests, "the herd", the "slaves", pity, the "ascetic" and the "ideal" is Nietzsche's. Do not soften, do not sharpen, add no moral distancing and no balancing qualification. Where Samuel's wording is blunt or offensive to current taste (the racial passages in ch2 and ch3, "negroes"), keep his word and his force; do not substitute a modern term and do not add distance.

## No additions

No commentary, no glosses, no bracketed notes, no headings, no summaries, and **no explanation of Nietzsche's history of morals or his coined concepts**. Do not link the argument to modern politics, psychology or later events. A reference (a name, a work, a date) may be clarified only by a few words inside the sentence and only where Samuel's text supports it.

## Structure 1:1

Four chapters, 124 paragraphs: ch1 Preface (11), ch2 First Essay (40), ch3 Second Essay (32), ch4 Third Essay (41). Chapter numbers and titles exactly as served ("First Essay — “Good and Evil,” “Good and Bad”" with its curly quotes, "Second Essay — “Guilt,” “Bad Conscience,” and the Like", "Third Essay — What is the Meaning of Ascetic Ideals?", "Preface"). Output paragraph N renders source paragraph N and begins with equivalent content; never merge, split, reorder, drop or invent one. Short paragraphs stay separate paragraphs, including the one-word "Further!" (ch2 p22, p24, p26), "Enough! Enough!" (ch2 p32), the dateline "Sils-Maria, Upper Engadine, July 1887." (ch1 p10) and the epigraph (ch4 p0). Output is normally at least 75% of the source's word count.

## Section numbers

Aphorism numbers appear only as a leading `N. ` on the first paragraph of each numbered section (ch1: 1-8, ch2: 1-17, ch3: 1-25, ch4: 1-28); later paragraphs of the same section carry no number, and ch4 p0 (epigraph) is unnumbered. Keep each leading `N. ` exactly, in the same paragraph, as digits plus period plus space. Never add numbers to unnumbered paragraphs and never move one. "Note. — " at the head of ch2 p37 stays.

## Technical terms: one rendering, kept stable

Take the term from Samuel's text; do not vary for elegance.

| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **resentment / resentful** | Samuel uses "resentment", "resentful man", never "ressentiment". Keep "resentment" and "resentful". Never "ressentiment", "rancor", "grudge" or "bitterness" as a substitute. |
| **bad conscience** | Keep, with "the bad conscience", "a bad conscience". Never "guilty conscience". "Good conscience" is its mirror. "Conscience" is never "conscious". |
| **guilt / debt / owing** | Keep both and keep their link (ch3 §§4-8, 19-22): "guilt" for the moral idea, "debt", "owing", "creditor", "debtor" for the money relation. Never render "debt" as "guilt" or the reverse. "Guilty" is fine; "sin" stays "sin". |
| **ascetic ideal(s)** | Keep "ascetic ideal" (singular for the idea, "ascetic ideals" in the ch4 title and wherever Samuel has the plural). Never "ideal of self-denial" or "ascetism". "Asceticism" stays. |
| **good and evil / good and bad** | Keep both pairs and keep them distinct: "good and evil" (the slave, priestly, Christian valuation) and "good and bad" (the noble valuation). "Bad" in the noble sense is never "evil"; "evil" is never "wicked". Keep the quotation marks Samuel has around them. |
| **slave-morality, aristocratic morality, revolt of the slaves** | Keep "slave-morality" (hyphen as served), "aristocratic morality", "the revolt of the slaves in morals" (Samuel's phrasing). Keep "slaves" and "masters" literally; never "the oppressed", "elite", "the ruling class". |
| **the masters / the nobles / nobility** | "The masters", "noble", "nobility", "aristocrats", "aristocratic races": as Samuel has each. Do not merge them. |
| **will to power** | Keep "will to power", capitalized "Will to Power" only where served capitalized (ch3 §12). |
| **blond beast** | Samuel's "blonde beast", "blonde brute", "beast of prey". Use American "blond" ("the blond beast", "the magnificent blond brute"); keep "beast of prey" and "blond-haired", "the blond race" as served. Never soften or replace by "predator" or "blond warrior". |
| **pathos of distance** | Keep Samuel's "pathos of distance" and "pathos of nobility and distance" exactly. |
| **transvaluation of values** | Keep "transvaluation of values", "transvaluation". |
| **the herd, herd instinct** | Keep; contemptuous, never "the crowd". |
| **priest, priestly, sacerdotal** | Keep "priest", "priestly", "sacerdotal". Do not turn "sacerdotal" into "religious". "Priestly nation" (the Jews) stays. |
| **pity, self-denial, selflessness, "un-egoistic"** | Keep these words as served; "pity" is not "compassion". Keep "egoistic" and "altruistic" in quotation marks where served. |
| **revenge / vengeance / "instinct of revenge"** | Keep "revenge", "revengeful". Do not substitute "vindictive" unless Samuel does. |
| **nihilism, Buddhism, cruelty, mnemonic** | Keep. "Nihilism" and "Buddhism" stay capitalized as served. |
| **genealogy / genealogists of morals** | Keep. "English psychologists" keeps its sneer. |
| **civilization, "State", "free will", "bad" "evil"** | "Civilisation" becomes "civilization" (American). "State" as used in ch3 §17 (the "herd of blond beasts") keeps its capital as served. |

## Quotations and citations

- Nietzsche's own scare quotes around words ("good", "bad", "free", "experiences", "State") stay, in the same places.
- Quoted speech, verse, scripture and the extended dialogue of the "workshop" scene (ch2 §14, p21-p32) stay recognizable: keep their sentence order, exclamations and the repeated "Further!" cues. Modernize the archaism only (ye/thou/quoth/in sooth, inverted word order) and nothing else.
- A quotation inside a quotation uses single straight quotes `'...'`, as the live edition does.
- Titles, scripture and work references ("Beyond Good and Evil, Aph. 195", "I Cor. ii. 9", "Human, all-too-Human, part i.", "Joyful Wisdom, Aph. 357", "Dr. Paul Rée", "E. Dühring, Value of Life", "Thus Spake Zarathustra") are never touched in number, wording or punctuation, except American spelling and the typography below. Titles of Nietzsche's own books keep Samuel's English forms.

## Notes, markers and special elements

There are no footnote markers, bracketed notes or asterisks. The prize-question note at the end of ch2 (p37-p39, "Note. — ...", the quoted question, the physiologists paragraph) stays in place as ordinary paragraphs. Keep the "Note." label. The epigraph and the dateline are paragraphs and stay verbatim except typography and spelling. Italic emphasis was not preserved in the served text; do not invent any (no asterisks or underscores). Where Samuel used capitals or quotation marks for emphasis, keep them.

## Names and spellings, foreign words

- Keep names as printed: Zarathustra, Schopenhauer, Kant, Dr. Rée, Dühring, Sils-Maria, Rome, Judæa (print "Judæa", ligature as served), Israel, Aryan, Gaelic, Celts. Do not Anglicize "Judæa" to "Judea".
- **Leave in the text every German, Latin and Greek word or phrase that Samuel left**, unchanged and in the same position, with his accompanying gloss in place: "schlecht", "schlicht", "gut", "Mensch", "entzweiung", "esprit de corps", "flair", "bonus", "malus", "duonus", "sub hoc signo", "sub ratione boni", "unio mystica", "horror vacui", "instrumentum", "toto cœlo", "Credo circo et utraque cavea", and every Greek word (ἀγαθός, μέλας, gennaios). Do not translate them, do not add translations or glosses, do not drop Samuel's.
- Diacritics and ligatures as served (Æ, æ, œ, é, ï, ô, ü, â, Greek). Never alter the Greek.
- Modern American spelling throughout: civilization, organization, realize, honor, color, splendor, among (not amongst), "to-day" to "today", "any one" to "anyone". "Moralisation" becomes "moralization".

## Typography

Match the live modern edition, checked in the file:

- Straight double quotes `"` for quotations and scare quotes; straight single quotes `'` for nested quotes. Straight apostrophes `'`. No curly quotes inside paragraphs. (Chapter titles keep the curly quotes exactly as served.)
- Dashes: a source `--` becomes a spaced em dash ` — ` (as the live edition does), or a period or semicolon where the sentence is split. `--` and `—` without spaces never appear. A hyphen in a compound stays a hyphen. An en dash from the source stays.
- Ellipses only where the source has one (`...`, `...!`, `?...`). Keep as three periods, as live.
- No newlines inside a paragraph. No markdown, asterisks or underscores.

## Things that must not happen

1. Any softening of the polemic (priests, Jews, Christianity, the herd, pity, "the blond beast") or addition of a hedge, a disclaimer or modern balance.
2. Any explanatory sentence, bracket or gloss on the history of morals.
3. "Ressentiment", "guilty conscience", "master morality", "evil" for noble "bad", "guilt" for "debt" or the reverse: all violate the term table.
4. Merging, splitting or renumbering sections; moving an `N. ` marker; dropping "Further!", the epigraph, the dateline or the Note.
5. Turning a rhetorical question or exclamation into a statement, or removing a parenthetical aside.
6. Translating, removing or adding any German, Latin or Greek word.
7. Curly quotes or unspaced dashes in the body; British spellings.
8. Rewriting an already clear paragraph just to reduce similarity.

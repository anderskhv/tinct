

########## CHAPTER 1: Act 1, Scene 1 — Sicilia. An Antechamber in Leontes’ Palace ##########
[1.0] [Enter Camillo and Archidamus.]

[1.1] ARCHIDAMUS. If you happen to visit Bohemia, Camillo, on a mission like the one I'm here on, you'll see, as I've told you, a great difference between our Bohemia and your Sicilia.

[1.2] CAMILLO. I believe this coming summer the King of Sicilia plans to pay Bohemia the visit he rightly owes him.

[1.3] ARCHIDAMUS. Our hospitality will fall short and embarrass us. We'll have to make up for it with affection. Because honestly—

[1.4] CAMILLO. Please—

[1.5] ARCHIDAMUS. Truly, I speak frankly from what I know. We can't match such magnificence—in such a rare—I don't know how to put it. We'll pour you sleeping drinks, so that your senses, unaware of our shortcomings, can't praise us, but at least won't accuse us either.

[1.6] CAMILLO. You're paying far too much for what is freely given.

[1.7] ARCHIDAMUS. Believe me, I speak as my understanding instructs me, and as my honesty insists I say it.

[1.8] CAMILLO. Sicilia cannot show too much kindness to Bohemia. They grew up together as children, and an affection took root between them then that simply has to branch out now. Since the duties of their mature stations and royal responsibilities forced them to live apart, their meetings, though not face to face, have been royally conducted through exchanges of gifts, letters, and loving embassies. They have seemed to be together though absent; they have shaken hands across a great distance, and embraced as if from the ends of opposing winds. May the heavens keep their love alive!

[1.9] ARCHIDAMUS. I don't think there's anything in the world, no malice or matter, that could change it. You have an inexpressible comfort in your young Prince Mamillius. He's a young man of the greatest promise I've ever come across.

[1.10] CAMILLO. I entirely agree with you about the hopes we have for him. He's a marvelous child—one who genuinely heals the populace and makes old hearts feel young again. Those who walked on crutches before he was born still want to live to see him a grown man.

[1.11] ARCHIDAMUS. Would they otherwise be willing to die?

[1.12] CAMILLO. Yes—if there were no other reason for them to want to keep living.

[1.13] ARCHIDAMUS. If the king had no son, they would want to live on crutches until he had one.

[1.14] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 2: Act 1, Scene 2 — The same. A Room of State in the Palace ##########
[2.0] [Enter Leontes, Polixenes, Hermione, Mamillius, Camillo and Attendants.]

[2.1] POLIXENES. Nine cycles of the moon, the shepherd's calendar, have passed since I left my throne untended. Even another stretch of time as long would not contain my thanks, my brother; and still I'd leave indebted to you forever. So, like a zero standing in a place that gives it value, I multiply with one 'we thank you' many thousands more that have come before it.

[2.2] LEONTES. Hold your thanks a little longer, and pay them when you leave.

[2.3] POLIXENES. Sir, that's tomorrow. My fears keep questioning me about what might happen, what trouble might be brewing in my absence. May no biting winds blow up at home to force me to say, 'That fear came true.' Besides, I've already stayed long enough to wear out your royal patience.

[2.4] LEONTES. We're tougher, brother, than you can wear us down.

[2.5] POLIXENES. I'll stay no longer.

[2.6] LEONTES. One more week.

[2.7] POLIXENES. Truly, tomorrow.

[2.8] LEONTES. Then we'll split the difference: and on that point I'll have no argument.

[2.9] POLIXENES. Don't press me, I beg you, like this. No tongue in the world could sway me as quickly as yours: and it would now, even if there were real need in your request, and even if I had real reason to deny it. My affairs are physically dragging me homeward. To hinder them would be, from a loving friend, a kind of whip on me. My staying would only be a burden to you. To spare us both, farewell, brother.

[2.10] LEONTES. Has my queen gone silent? Speak, you.

[2.11] HERMIONE. I had thought, sir, to keep quiet until you'd dragged oaths out of him not to leave. You, sir, are pressing him too gently. Tell him you're certain all is well in Bohemia: yesterday's report confirmed that. Say that to him, and he's beaten out of his best defense.

[2.12] LEONTES. Well said, Hermione.

[2.13] HERMIONE. To say he longs to see his son would be a strong reason. But let him say so then, and let him go; but let him swear so, and he shall not stay—we'll drive him out with our distaffs.

[2.14] [To Polixenes.] Still, on the strength of your royal presence I'll venture

[2.15] to ask the loan of a week. When you take my lord to Bohemia, I'll give him permission to stay there a month beyond his appointed time of departure. And yet, in truth, Leontes, I don't love you a tick of the clock less than any wife loves her husband. You'll stay?

[2.16] POLIXENES. No, madam.

[2.17] HERMIONE. Come now, but you will?

[2.18] POLIXENES. I truly may not.

[2.19] HERMIONE. Truly! You're putting me off with flimsy oaths. But I, even if you tried to unhook the stars with oaths, would still say, 'Sir, no leaving.' Truly, you shall not go. A lady's 'truly' is as powerful as a lord's. Still set on going? Force me, and I'll keep you as a prisoner, not as a guest: you'll pay your fees on departure and spare your thanks. What do you say? My prisoner or my guest? By your dreaded 'truly,' you'll be one of them.

[2.20] POLIXENES. Your guest then, madam. To be your prisoner would mean having committed an offense—something easier for you to punish than for me to commit.

[2.21] HERMIONE. Not your jailer then, but your kind hostess. Come, I'll quiz you about my husband's pranks and yours when you were boys. You were pretty little lordlings then.

[2.22] POLIXENES. We were, fair queen: two lads who thought there was nothing more to come than another day tomorrow just like today, and to be boys forever.

[2.23] HERMIONE. Wasn't my husband the bigger rascal of the two?

[2.24] POLIXENES. We were like twin lambs frisking in the sun and bleating at each other. What we exchanged was innocence for innocence. We didn't know the doctrine of wrongdoing, didn't dream that anyone did wrong. If we'd kept that life, and our young spirits had never been roused by stronger blood, we could have answered heaven boldly 'Not guilty,' free of the original sin we inherited.

[2.25] HERMIONE. From this we gather you've stumbled since.

[2.26] POLIXENES. Oh, my most sacred lady, temptations have been born to us since then! In those innocent days my wife was a girl; your precious self had not yet crossed the eyes of my young playmate.

[2.27] HERMIONE. Heaven help us! Don't draw that conclusion, or you'll be saying that your queen and I are devils. But go on. We'll answer for the offenses we made you commit, if you sinned first with us, and went on sinning only with us, and slipped with no one but us.

[2.28] LEONTES. Has he agreed yet?

[2.29] HERMIONE. He'll stay, my lord.

[2.30] LEONTES. At my request he wouldn't. Hermione, my dearest, you've never spoken to better purpose.

[2.31] HERMIONE. Never?

[2.32] LEONTES. Never but once.

[2.33] HERMIONE. What! Have I spoken well twice? When was the first time? Please tell me. Stuff us with praise and fatten us like tame animals: one good deed dying without recognition kills off a thousand more waiting in line. Our praises are our wages. You can ride us a thousand furlongs with one soft kiss before you'd spur an acre out of us. But to the point: my latest good deed was begging him to stay. What was my first? It has an older sister, or I'm mistaken: oh, I wish her name were Grace! But I once spoke to the purpose before—when? Come, tell me; I'm dying to know.

[2.34] LEONTES. Why, that was when three sour months had soured themselves to death before I could get you to open your white hand and clap yourself my love. Then you said, 'I am yours forever.'

[2.35] HERMIONE. That truly is Grace. So look you, now I've spoken to the purpose twice. The first time earned me a royal husband forever; the second, a friend for a while.

[2.36] [Giving her hand to Polixenes.]

[2.37] LEONTES.

[2.38] [Aside.] Too hot, too hot!

[2.39] To mix friendship that far is to mix bloods. I feel my heart trembling. My heart dances, but not for joy—not joy. This kind of entertainment can wear an innocent face, take its freedom from warmth and generosity and an open heart, and it can suit the giver: it may, I grant. But this petting of palms and pinching of fingers, as they're doing now, and these practiced smiles as in a mirror, and then sighing like a dying deer—oh, that kind of entertainment my heart does not like, nor my forehead. Mamillius, are you my boy?

[2.40] MAMILLIUS. Yes, my good lord.

[2.41] LEONTES. In faith! That's my fine fellow. What, have you smudged your nose? They say it's a copy of mine. Come, captain, we must be neat—not neat as in fancy, but neat as in clean, captain. And yet the steer, the heifer, and the calf are all called 'neat' too.—Still rubbing his palm there?—How now, you frisky calf! Are you my calf?

[2.42] MAMILLIUS. Yes, if you want me to be, my lord.

[2.43] LEONTES. You need a rough head and the horns I have to be quite my image. And yet they say we're as alike as eggs—women say so, who'll say anything. But even if they were false as ill-dyed black clothes, as wind, as water—false as dice are wished by a man who sets no boundary between his and another's—still it would be true to say this boy looks like me. Come, page, look at me with your sky-blue eye: sweet villain! Dearest! My own flesh! Can your mother—? Could it be? Suspicion! Your reach pierces the heart of things. You make impossible things believable, you commune with dreams—how can this be?—You work hand in hand with what's unreal, you partner with nothing. So it's very believable you may also partner with something. And you do, beyond your warrant—and I find it, and it sickens my brain and stiffens my brow with the horns of a cuckold.

[2.44] POLIXENES. What's wrong with Sicilia?

[2.45] HERMIONE. He seems somewhat unsettled.

[2.46] POLIXENES. What is it, my lord? How are you? How are you doing, best brother?

[2.47] HERMIONE. You look as if you're carrying a forehead full of troubled thoughts. Are you upset, my lord?

[2.48] LEONTES. No, honestly. Sometimes nature betrays its own foolishness, its softness, and makes itself a sport for harder hearts! Looking at the lines of my boy's face, I felt myself recoil twenty-three years and saw myself as a small boy still in skirts, in my green velvet coat, my dagger muzzled so it wouldn't bite its master and prove, as ornaments often do, too dangerous. How like, I thought, I was then to this little kernel, this squash, this gentleman. My honest friend, will you take eggs for money?

[2.49] MAMILLIUS. No, my lord, I'll fight.

[2.50] LEONTES. You will? Why, blessed be your luck! My brother, are you as fond of your young prince as we seem to be of ours?

[2.51] POLIXENES. When I'm home, sir, he's all my exercise, my joy, my preoccupation: now my sworn friend, now my enemy; my flatterer, my soldier, statesman, all. He makes a July day as short as December, and with his shifting childhood moods cures me of thoughts that would clot my blood.

[2.52] LEONTES. The same office this little squire performs for me. We two will walk on, my lord, and leave you to your weightier steps. Hermione, show how you love us by how well you welcome my brother. Let whatever is precious in Sicily be cheap to him: after yourself and my young rover, he's nearest to my heart.

[2.53] HERMIONE. If you want to find us, we'll be yours in the garden. Shall we expect you there?

[2.54] LEONTES. Go wherever your inclinations take you: you'll be found, anywhere under the sky. [Aside.] I am fishing now, though you don't see how I let out my line. Go on, go on! How she lifts her face, her lips, toward him! And arms herself with a wife's boldness toward an indulgent husband!

[2.55] [Exeunt Polixenes, Hermione and Attendants.]

[2.56] Gone already! Up to my neck in it, head and ears under—and horned!—Go play, boy, play. Your mother plays, and I play too; but I play such a disgraceful part that the outcome will hiss me to my grave: contempt and outcry will be my funeral bell. Go, play, boy, play. There have been cuckolds before now, or I'm much mistaken; and many a man right at this present, even as I speak, holds his wife by the arm without dreaming she's been sluiced in his absence and his pond fished by his next-door neighbor, by Sir Smile, his neighbor. No, there's some comfort in it: while other men have gates, and those gates opened, as mine were, against their will. If every man with an unfaithful wife despaired, a tenth of mankind would hang itself. There's no medicine for it. It's a bawdy planet that strikes wherever it's strongest; and it's powerful, mark you, from east, west, north, and south. Let's settle it: there's no barricade for a belly. Know this: it will let in and out the enemy with bag and baggage. Many thousands of us have the disease and don't feel it.—How now, boy!

[2.57] MAMILLIUS. They say I look like you.

[2.58] LEONTES. Why, that's some comfort. What! Camillo there?

[2.59] CAMILLO. Yes, my good lord.

[2.60] LEONTES. Go play, Mamillius; you're an honest man.

[2.61] [Exit Mamillius.]

[2.62] Camillo, this great sir is going to stay longer after all.

[2.63] CAMILLO. You had a hard job making his anchor hold: every time you cast it out, it dragged back home.

[2.64] LEONTES. Did you notice that?

[2.65] CAMILLO. He wouldn't stay for your pleas; said his business was more important.

[2.66] LEONTES. Did you pick up on that?

[2.67] [Aside.] They're already at me; whispering, gossiping,

[2.68] 'Sicilia is a so-and-so.' It's far gone when I'm the last to taste it.—How did it happen, Camillo, that he stayed?

[2.69] CAMILLO. At the good queen's pleading.

[2.70] LEONTES. 'At the queen's,' fine: 'good' should be a fitting word, but as things are, it isn't. Did anyone else with a working brain catch this, or only you? Your wit soaks things up, draws in more than the average dull head. Was it not noticed except by the finer minds? Only by a few of extraordinary perception? Are lower-ranked diners maybe blind to this matter? Tell me.

[2.71] CAMILLO. Business, my lord? I think most people understand that Bohemia is staying here longer.

[2.72] LEONTES. Ha?

[2.73] CAMILLO. Is staying here longer.

[2.74] LEONTES. Yes, but why?

[2.75] CAMILLO. To please your highness, and at the urging of our most gracious mistress.

[2.76] LEONTES. To please? At the urging of your mistress? To please? Let that be enough. I've trusted you, Camillo, with everything closest to my heart, including my private councils, in which, like a priest, you've cleansed my soul; I came away from you a reformed penitent. But we've been deceived in your integrity, deceived in what only seemed to be.

[2.77] CAMILLO. God forbid, my lord!

[2.78] LEONTES. To dwell on it: you are not honest; or, if you lean that way, you're a coward, which hamstrings honesty from behind, holding it back from the course it should take. Or else you must be reckoned a servant grafted onto my deepest trust who is negligent in it; or else a fool who sees a game played out to the end, the rich stake taken, and takes the whole thing as a joke.

[2.79] CAMILLO. My gracious lord, I may be negligent, foolish, and fearful: in every one of these no man is free, but that his negligence, his folly, his fear, among the infinite doings of the world, shows itself sometimes. In your affairs, my lord, if I was ever willfully negligent, it was my folly; if I deliberately played the fool, it was negligence, not weighing the end well; if ever I was fearful to do a thing where I doubted the outcome, the doing of which kept calling out against my not doing it, that was a fear which often touches the wisest of men. These, my lord, are such allowed weaknesses that honesty itself is never free of them. But, I beg your Grace, be plainer with me. Let me see my offense face to face: if I then deny it, it isn't mine.

[2.80] LEONTES. Haven't you seen, Camillo? (But there's no doubt of it: you have, or your spectacle-lens is thicker than a cuckold's horn.) Or heard? (For with so plain a sight, rumor cannot be silent.) Or thought? (For thought doesn't live in a man who doesn't think) that my wife is loose? If you'll confess, or otherwise be brazenly negative—claiming to have no eyes, no ears, no thought—then say my wife's a whore, deserves a name as foul as any flax-girl who beds down before her engagement: say it and stand by it.

[2.81] CAMILLO. I would not stand by and hear my sovereign mistress slandered like this, without immediate vengeance taken: damn my heart, you never spoke anything less worthy of you than this; to repeat it would be a sin as deep as the deed itself, even if the deed were true.

[2.82] LEONTES. Is whispering nothing? Is leaning cheek to cheek? Touching noses? Kissing with the inside of the lip? Stopping a laugh in mid-run with a sigh?—an infallible sign of broken honesty?—standing foot on foot? Skulking in corners? Wishing clocks would run faster? Hours, minutes? Noon, midnight? Every other eye blinded with cataracts but theirs, only theirs, who want to be unseen and wicked? Is this nothing? Why then the world and everything in it is nothing, the sky over us is nothing, Bohemia is nothing, my wife is nothing, nor have these nothings any nothing in them, if this is nothing.

[2.83] CAMILLO. Good my lord, be cured of this diseased belief, and quickly, for it's most dangerous.

[2.84] LEONTES. Say it is, it's true.

[2.85] CAMILLO. No, no, my lord.

[2.86] LEONTES. It is; you lie, you lie: I say you lie, Camillo, and I hate you. I declare you a gross lout, a mindless slave, or else a hedging temporizer who can see good and evil at the same time and lean toward both. If my wife's liver were as infected as her life, she wouldn't live the running of one hourglass.

[2.87] CAMILLO. Who is infecting her?

[2.88] LEONTES. Why, he that wears her like her portrait-medal hanging at his neck, Bohemia: who, if I had servants around me loyal enough to see my honor as clearly as they see their own profits, their own private gains, would do what would undo a great deal more in the doing: yes, and you, his cupbearer—whom I have raised from lower rank and seated and brought up to honor, who can see plainly, as heaven sees earth and earth sees heaven, how galled I am—you could spice a cup to give my enemy a lasting wink. Such a drink would be medicine to me.

[2.89] CAMILLO. Sir, my lord, I could do this, and with no rash potion, but with a slow drug that wouldn't act maliciously like poison. But I cannot believe this fault to be in my dread mistress, who is so supremely honorable. I have loved you,—

[2.90] LEONTES. Make that your question, and go rot! Do you think I'm so muddled, so unstable, as to put myself into this torment; to dirty the purity and whiteness of my sheets (preserving which is sleep, soiling which is goads, thorns, nettles, wasp-stings); to bring scandal on the blood of the prince, my son (who I do think is mine and love as mine), without solid reason for it? Would I do this? Could any man swerve so?

[2.91] CAMILLO. I must believe you, sir: I do; and I will get rid of Bohemia for you—provided that, when he's removed, your highness will take your queen back to your heart as before, for your son's sake, and so seal off the wagging tongues of courts and kingdoms known and allied to yours.

[2.92] LEONTES. You advise me exactly as I'd already set my own course: I'll give no blemish to her honor, none.

[2.93] CAMILLO. Then go, my lord, and with a face as clear as friendship wears at feasts, keep company with Bohemia and with your queen. I am his cupbearer. If he gets any wholesome drink from me, count me as no servant of yours.

[2.94] LEONTES. That's the whole of it: do it, and you have half my heart. Don't do it, and you split your own.

[2.95] CAMILLO. I'll do it, my lord.

[2.96] LEONTES. I'll seem friendly, as you've advised me.

[2.97] [Exit.]

[2.98] CAMILLO. Oh, miserable lady! But what about me—what position am I in? I have to be the poisoner of good Polixenes, and my reason for doing it is obedience to a master who, at war with himself, will have everyone who's his at war with themselves too. Doing this deed brings promotion. If I could find a single example of thousands who had struck down anointed kings and flourished afterward, I still wouldn't do it. But since neither brass nor stone nor parchment carries even one such example, let villainy itself swear off it. I must leave the court. To do it or not to do it is, either way, a broken neck for me. Let some lucky star reign now! Here comes Bohemia.

[2.99] [Enter Polixenes.]

[2.100] POLIXENES. This is strange. I think my favor here is starting to sour. He won't speak to me?—Good day, Camillo.

[2.101] CAMILLO. Hail, most royal sir!

[2.102] POLIXENES. What's the news at court?

[2.103] CAMILLO. None unusual, my lord.

[2.104] POLIXENES. The king carries himself as if he'd lost some province, some country he loved as he loves himself. Just now I met him with the usual courtesies; he turned his eyes the other way, dropped a lip of deep contempt, hurried away from me, and left me wondering what's breeding in him that's changed his manners so.

[2.105] CAMILLO. I dare not know, my lord.

[2.106] POLIXENES. What, dare not? Don't? Do you know, and dare not say? Make yourself plain to me. It's that, isn't it; for to yourself, what you do know, you must know, and you can't say to yourself that you dare not. Good Camillo, your changed looks are a mirror to me showing my own looks changed too: for I must be a party in this change, finding myself thus altered by it.

[2.107] CAMILLO. There is a sickness which throws some of us out of balance, but I cannot name the disease; and it was caught from you, who are yet well.

[2.108] POLIXENES. How caught from me? Don't make me out to be a basilisk. I have looked on thousands who have done the better for my attention, but I've killed no one with a look. Camillo,—as you are certainly a gentleman, and besides, scholarly, experienced, which graces our gentry no less than the noble names of our parents, by whose succession we are nobly born,—I beg you, if you know anything that I should know, don't lock it away in ignorant silence.

[2.109] CAMILLO. I may not answer.

[2.110] POLIXENES. A sickness caught from me, and yet I'm well? I must have an answer. Listen, Camillo—I conjure you by every part of a man that honor recognizes, of which the least is not this request of mine, that you declare what kind of harm you guess is creeping toward me; how far off, how near; how it can be prevented, if at all; if not, how best to bear it.

[2.111] CAMILLO. Sir, I will tell you—since I'm bound by honor, and by a man I believe honorable. Therefore mark my advice, which must be followed as swiftly as I'm about to speak it, or both of us cry 'lost!' and so goodnight!

[2.112] POLIXENES. Go on, good Camillo.

[2.113] CAMILLO. I'm appointed to murder you.

[2.114] POLIXENES. By whom, Camillo?

[2.115] CAMILLO. By the king.

[2.116] POLIXENES. For what?

[2.117] CAMILLO. He thinks—indeed, swears with all confidence, as if he'd seen it or been an instrument to corner you into it—that you've touched his queen in a forbidden way.

[2.118] POLIXENES. Oh, then let my best blood turn to an infected jelly, and my name be yoked with Judas's that betrayed the Best! Turn my freshest reputation into such a stench as will strike the dullest nostril wherever I arrive, and let my approach be shunned, nay, hated worse than the greatest plague ever heard of or read about!

[2.119] CAMILLO. Swear his belief away, star by star in heaven, by all their influences; you might as well forbid the sea to obey the moon as either by oath remove or by counsel shake the structure of his madness, whose foundation is piled on his certainty, and will stand as long as his body stands.

[2.120] POLIXENES. How could this grow up?

[2.121] CAMILLO. I don't know: but I'm certain it's safer to avoid what's grown than to question how it sprang up. So if you dare trust my honesty, locked inside this trunk of mine, which you must carry along with you as a pledge—away tonight. I'll whisper the plan to your followers, and get them out of the city by twos and threes at various back gates. As for me, I'll throw in my fortunes with you, since my fortunes here are now lost by this revelation. Don't doubt it: by my parents' honor, I've spoken the truth; which, if you try to test, I dare not stand by; nor will you be any safer than a man condemned by the king's own mouth, with his execution already sworn.

[2.122] POLIXENES. I believe you. I saw his heart in his face. Give me your hand: be my guide, and you shall stand next to me wherever I am. My ships are ready, and my people expected my departure two days ago. This jealousy is over a precious creature: as she's rare, the jealousy must be great; and as he's mighty in his person, it must be violent; and as he believes he's been dishonored by a man who has always professed friendship to him, why, his revenges must be all the more bitter. Fear darkens me. Let swift escape be my friend, and bring comfort to the gracious queen, who is part of his obsession but no part of his ill-founded suspicion! Come, Camillo: I'll honor you as a father if you carry my life out of here. Let's slip away.

[2.123] CAMILLO. It's in my authority to command the keys of all the back gates: please your highness to seize the urgent hour. Come, sir, away.

[2.124] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 3: Act 2, Scene 1 — Sicilia. A Room in the Palace ##########
[3.0] [Enter Hermione, Mamillius and Ladies.]

[3.1] HERMIONE. Take the boy off my hands: he's troubling me so much it's past bearing.

[3.2] FIRST LADY. Come, my gracious lord, shall I be your playmate?

[3.3] MAMILLIUS. No, I want none of you.

[3.4] FIRST LADY. Why, my sweet lord?

[3.5] MAMILLIUS. You'll kiss me hard, and talk to me as if I were still a baby. I love you better.

[3.6] SECOND LADY. And why so, my lord?

[3.7] MAMILLIUS. Not because your eyebrows are darker—though they say dark eyebrows suit some women best, so long as there isn't too much hair, just a semicircle or a half-moon drawn with a pen.

[3.8] SECOND LADY. Who taught you this?

[3.9] MAMILLIUS. I learned it from studying women's faces. Tell me now, what color are your eyebrows?

[3.10] FIRST LADY. Blue, my lord.

[3.11] MAMILLIUS. No, you're teasing me. I've seen a lady's nose that was blue, but never her eyebrows.

[3.12] FIRST LADY. Listen, the queen your mother is getting bigger by the day. We shall be paying our respects to a fine new prince one of these days, and then you'd want to play with us, if we'd let you.

[3.13] SECOND LADY. She's grown lately into a good fine bulk: may her time go well!

[3.14] HERMIONE. What wisdom is at work among you? Come, sir, now I'm yours again. Please sit by us and tell us a tale.

[3.15] MAMILLIUS. Shall it be merry or sad?

[3.16] HERMIONE. As merry as you like.

[3.17] MAMILLIUS. A sad tale is best for winter. I have one about sprites and goblins.

[3.18] HERMIONE. Let's have that one, good sir. Come on, sit down. Come on, and do your best to scare me with your sprites: you're very good at it.

[3.19] MAMILLIUS. There was a man—

[3.20] HERMIONE. No, come, sit down, then go on.

[3.21] MAMILLIUS. Lived by a churchyard. I'll tell it softly, so those crickets over there won't hear.

[3.22] HERMIONE. Come on then, and tell it in my ear.

[3.23] [Enter Leontes, Antigonus, Lords and Guards.]

[3.24] LEONTES. Was he met there? His followers? Camillo with him?

[3.25] FIRST LORD. Behind the clump of pines I came across them: I've never seen men hurry so on their way. I watched them all the way to their ships.

[3.26] LEONTES. How blessed I am in my just judgment, in my true opinion! Alas, for less knowledge! How cursed in being so blessed! There may be a steeped spider in your cup, and you may drink, leave, and take no poison from it, because your awareness is not infected; but if someone shows you the hated ingredient, makes known what you've drunk, you'll crack your throat and ribs with violent retching. I have drunk, and seen the spider. Camillo was his help in this, his procurer. There is a plot against my life, my crown. Everything that's been suspected is true. That false villain whom I employed was already employed by him. He's uncovered my plan, and I'm left a pinched fool—yes, a mere puppet for them to play with at will. How did the back gates open so easily?

[3.27] FIRST LORD. By his great authority, which has often, on your order, achieved no less than this.

[3.28] LEONTES. I know it too well. Give me the boy. I'm glad you didn't nurse him. Though he does carry some signs of me, you have too much of your blood in him.

[3.29] HERMIONE. What is this? A game?

[3.30] LEONTES. Take the boy away from her; he is not to come near her. Away with him, and let her amuse herself with the one she's big with: for it's Polixenes who's made you swell up like this.

[3.31] [Exit Mamillius with some of the Guards.]

[3.32] HERMIONE. But I'd say he hasn't, and I'd swear you would believe me if you knew, however much you're being misled to the contrary.

[3.33] LEONTES. You, my lords, look at her. Mark her well. You're just about to say, 'She is a fine lady,' and the justice of your hearts will add, 'It's a pity she's not honest, not honorable.' Praise her only for her outward shape—which, by my faith, deserves high praise—and right away the shrug, the 'hmm' or 'ha,' those petty brands that slander uses—oh, I'm wrong, that mercy uses; for slander will scorch virtue itself—those shrugs, those hmms and has, after you've said 'she's fine,' come between, before you can say 'she's honest.' But let it be known, from the man who has most cause to grieve it should be so: she's an adulteress!

[3.34] HERMIONE. If a villain said so, the most loaded villain in the world, he'd be all the more a villain: you, my lord, are simply mistaken.

[3.35] LEONTES. You have mistaken, my lady, Polixenes for Leontes. Oh, you thing—whom I won't call a creature of your station, lest barbarity, taking me as its precedent, should speak the same way to all ranks and lose the polite distinction between prince and beggar. I have said she's an adulteress; I have said with whom. More: she's a traitor, and Camillo is her partner in it—one who knows what she ought to be ashamed to know about herself, except in company with her vile lover: that she's a bed-cheat, every bit as bad as those that common people call by the boldest names; yes, and an accomplice to this recent escape of theirs.

[3.36] HERMIONE. No, by my life, accomplice to none of this. How it will grieve you, when you come to clearer knowledge, to have publicly branded me this way! My gentle lord, you can hardly set me right after, by saying you were mistaken.

[3.37] LEONTES. No. If I'm mistaken in the foundations I build on, the earth's core isn't big enough to balance a schoolboy's top. Away with her to prison! Anyone who speaks for her is guilty by a hair just for speaking.

[3.38] HERMIONE. Some bad planet is reigning: I must be patient until the heavens look down with a more favorable aspect. My good lords, I'm not prone to weeping, as our sex commonly is; lacking that useless dew may dry up your pity. But I have such honorable grief lodged here that burns worse than tears can drown. I beg you all, my lords, with thoughts as fair as your charities best know how to be, measure me; and so let the king's will be done.

[3.39] LEONTES. Will I be obeyed?

[3.40] HERMIONE. Who's going with me? I beg your highness, let my women come with me, for you see my condition requires it. Don't weep, good fools; there's no cause. When you find out your mistress has deserved prison, then weep your fill as I come out. The course I am now going on is for my better grace. Goodbye, my lord: I never wished to see you sorry; now I trust I shall. My women, come; you have my leave.

[3.41] LEONTES. Go, do as we've ordered. Out!

[3.42] [Exeunt Queen and Ladies with Guards.]

[3.43] FIRST LORD. I beg your highness, call the queen back.

[3.44] ANTIGONUS. Be sure what you're doing, sir, lest your justice turn out to be violence, in which three great ones suffer: yourself, your queen, your son.

[3.45] FIRST LORD. For her, my lord, I'd lay down my life, and will, sir, if you'll accept it, that the queen is spotless in the eyes of heaven and to you—I mean in the matter you accuse her of.

[3.46] ANTIGONUS. If it turns out she's otherwise, I'll keep my stables where I keep my wife; I'll go around chained to her; I won't trust her any farther than I can feel and see her. For every inch of woman in the world—yes, every dram of woman's flesh—is false, if she is.

[3.47] LEONTES. Be quiet.

[3.48] FIRST LORD. My good lord—

[3.49] ANTIGONUS. We're speaking for you, not for ourselves: you've been abused, and by some instigator who'll be damned for it. If I knew the villain, I'd give him hell on earth. If her honor is flawed, I have three daughters: the eldest is eleven, the second and third are nine and just five. If this proves true, they'll pay for it. By my honor, I'll geld them all; they won't see fourteen, lest they breed false generations. They are co-heirs, and I'd rather castrate myself than have them not produce honest children.

[3.50] LEONTES. Stop—no more. You smell this business with a sense as cold as a dead man's nose: but I see it and feel it, as you feel my hand doing this; and I see, too, the instruments that do the feeling.

[3.51] ANTIGONUS. If it's so, we need no grave to bury honesty in. There's not a grain of it left to sweeten the face of the whole dunged earth.

[3.52] LEONTES. What! Am I not believed?

[3.53] FIRST LORD. I'd rather you lacked credit than I, my lord, on this ground: and it would please me more to have her honor real than your suspicion confirmed, however much you'd blame me for it.

[3.54] LEONTES. Why, what need is there to discuss this with you, rather than to follow my own driving instinct? My prerogative doesn't call for your counsel; only my natural goodness extends this opportunity to you. If you, either too stupid or pretending to be, cannot or will not relish a truth as I do, inform yourselves—we need no more of your advice. The matter, the loss, the gain, the ordering of it, is all properly ours.

[3.55] ANTIGONUS. And I wish, my liege, you had tried it only in your silent judgment, without making it public.

[3.56] LEONTES. How could that be? Either you're terribly ignorant from age, or you were born a fool. Camillo's flight, added to their familiarity—which was as gross as anything that could reach guesswork, lacking only sight; nothing for proof was missing but the literal seeing, all other circumstances making up the deed—drives on this proceeding. And yet, for greater confirmation (because in a matter of this importance, it would be most pitiful to be reckless), I've sent in haste to sacred Delphos, to Apollo's temple, Cleomenes and Dion, whom you know to be of solid competence. Now from the oracle they'll bring back all I need to know; and whose spiritual counsel, once obtained, shall stop me or spur me on. Have I done well?

[3.57] FIRST LORD. Well done, my lord.

[3.58] LEONTES. Though I am satisfied, and need no more than what I already know, still the oracle will give rest to the minds of others—such as he whose ignorant credulity won't rise to the truth. So we have thought it good that she be kept away from our person, in case the treachery of the two who have fled is left to her to carry out. Come, follow us; we are to speak in public, because this business will rouse us all.

[3.59] ANTIGONUS.

[3.60] [Aside.] To laughter, I take it,

[3.61] if the good truth were known.

[3.62] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 4: Act 2, Scene 2 — The same. The outer Room of a Prison ##########
[4.0] [Enter Paulina, a Gentleman and Attendants.]

[4.1] PAULINA. Call to the keeper of the prison; let him know who I am.

[4.2] [Exit the Gentleman.]

[4.3] Good lady! No court in Europe is too fine for you; what are you doing in prison?

[4.4] [Enter Gentleman with the Gaoler.]

[4.5] Now, good sir, you know who I am, don't you?

[4.6] GAOLER. A worthy lady, and one whom I greatly honor.

[4.7] PAULINA. Then please, conduct me to the queen.

[4.8] GAOLER. I cannot, madam. I have express orders to the contrary.

[4.9] PAULINA. What a fuss to lock up honesty and honor from any visit of kind friends! Is it permitted, please, to see her women? Any of them? Emilia?

[4.10] GAOLER. If you would, madam, send your attendants away, I'll bring Emilia out.

[4.11] PAULINA. Please, call her. You all withdraw.

[4.12] [Exeunt Gentleman and Attendants.]

[4.13] GAOLER. And, madam, I must be present during your conversation.

[4.14] PAULINA. Well, so be it, please.

[4.15] [Exit Gaoler.]

[4.16] Such a fuss to make a stain out of no stain at all—it beats anything.

[4.17] [Re-enter Gaoler with Emilia.]

[4.18] Dear gentlewoman, how is our gracious lady?

[4.19] EMILIA. As well as someone so great and so forlorn may hold together. From her shocks and griefs (which no tender lady ever bore greater), she has been delivered, somewhat before her time.

[4.20] PAULINA. A boy?

[4.21] EMILIA. A daughter; and a fine baby, vigorous, likely to live. The queen takes much comfort in her, says, 'My poor little prisoner, I am as innocent as you are.'

[4.22] PAULINA. I'd swear it. These dangerous, unstable fits in the king, curse them! He must be told, and he shall be. The job suits a woman best. I'll take it upon myself. If I prove honey-mouthed, let my tongue blister and never be the trumpet of my red-faced anger anymore. Please, Emilia, send my best obedience to the queen. If she dares to trust me with her little baby, I'll show it to the king, and undertake to be her advocate at the loudest pitch. We don't know how he may soften at the sight of the child: the silence of pure innocence often persuades when speaking fails.

[4.23] EMILIA. Most worthy madam, your honor and your goodness are so evident that your generous undertaking can't fail of a happy outcome. There's no lady living so fit for this great errand. If your ladyship would step into the next room, I'll go right now and tell the queen of your most noble offer—she's been turning over this very idea today, but didn't dare ask any officer of honor for fear of being refused.

[4.24] PAULINA. Tell her, Emilia, I'll use what tongue I have: if wit flows from it as boldness does from my heart, let no one doubt I shall do some good.

[4.25] EMILIA. Bless you for it! I'll go to the queen. Please step a little closer.

[4.26] GAOLER. Madam, if the queen chooses to send the baby out, I don't know what trouble I'll bring on myself by letting it pass, having no authorization.

[4.27] PAULINA. You needn't fear it, sir: this child was a prisoner of the womb, and is, by the law and course of great nature, freed and released from it; not a party to the king's anger, nor guilty of the queen's trespass, if there even is one.

[4.28] GAOLER. I believe that.

[4.29] PAULINA. Don't be afraid: on my honor, I will stand between you and danger.

[4.30] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 5: Act 2, Scene 3 — The same. A Room in the Palace ##########
[5.0] [Enter Leontes, Antigonus, Lords and other Attendants.]

[5.1] LEONTES. No rest day or night: it's just weakness to bear this thing like this, sheer weakness. If the cause weren't still alive—part of the cause, she the adulteress; because the whore-king is quite beyond my reach, out of the line and aim of my brain, plot-proof. But she I can hook to me. Say she were gone, given to the fire, half my rest might come back to me. Who's there?

[5.2] FIRST ATTENDANT. My lord.

[5.3] LEONTES. How does the boy?

[5.4] FIRST ATTENDANT. He rested well tonight. We hope his sickness has passed.

[5.5] LEONTES. To see his nobility, taking in the dishonor of his mother. He sank straight down, drooped, took it deeply, fastened and fixed the shame of it on himself, threw off his spirit, his appetite, his sleep, and just plainly languished. Leave me alone: go, see how he's doing.

[5.6] [Exit First Attendant.]

[5.7] Damn it, no more thought of him. The very thought of taking my revenges that way recoils on me: in himself too mighty, and in his backers, his alliance. Let him be, until time serves. For present vengeance, take it out on her. Camillo and Polixenes laugh at me; make sport of my sorrow. They wouldn't laugh if I could reach them, nor shall she, since she's in my power.

[5.8] [Enter Paulina carrying a baby, with Antigonus, lords and servants.]

[5.9] FIRST LORD. You must not enter.

[5.10] PAULINA. No, rather back me up, my good lords. Do you fear his tyrannous outburst more, alas, than the queen's life? A gracious, innocent soul, more free from guilt than he is full of jealousy.

[5.11] ANTIGONUS. That's enough.

[5.12] SERVANT. Madam, he hasn't slept tonight; commanded that no one come near him.

[5.13] PAULINA. Not so worked up, good sir. I come to bring him sleep. It's people like you, who creep like shadows around him and sigh at every one of his pointless sighs—it's people like you who feed the cause of his sleeplessness. I come with words as medicinal as they are true, as honest as they are medicinal, to purge him of that mood that drives sleep from him.

[5.14] LEONTES. What noise is that? Hey!

[5.15] PAULINA. No noise, my lord, but necessary conversation about some godparents for your highness.

[5.16] LEONTES. What! Get that brazen lady out of here! Antigonus, I charged you that she shouldn't come near me. I knew she would.

[5.17] ANTIGONUS. I told her so, my lord, on pain of your displeasure and mine, that she should not visit you.

[5.18] LEONTES. What, can't you control her?

[5.19] PAULINA. From all dishonesty he can. In this, unless he takes the course you have, by arresting me for committing honor—trust it, he shall not control me.

[5.20] ANTIGONUS. There you are, you hear. When she takes the reins I let her run, but she won't stumble.

[5.21] PAULINA. My good liege, I come—and I beg you, hear me, who profess myself your loyal servant, your physician, your most obedient counselor, yet one who dares less appear so, by humoring your wrongs, than those who most seem to be yours—I say I come from your good queen.

[5.22] LEONTES. Good queen!

[5.23] PAULINA. Good queen, my lord, good queen. I say, good queen, and would make her good by combat, if I were a man, even the lowest about you.

[5.24] LEONTES. Force her out.

[5.25] PAULINA. Let him who counts his own eyes for nothing be the first to lay a hand on me. I'll leave on my own: but first I'll do my errand. The good queen (for she is good) has borne you a daughter: here she is; she commits her to your blessing.

[5.26] [Laying down the child.]

[5.27] LEONTES. Out! A masculine witch! Out with her, out the door! A scheming go-between!

[5.28] PAULINA. Not so. I'm as ignorant of that as you are in calling me so; and no less honest than you are mad—which is enough, I'll warrant, the way this world goes, to pass for honest.

[5.29] LEONTES. Traitors! Won't you push her out? [To Antigonus.] Give her the bastard, you old fool! You're henpecked, knocked off your perch by your Dame Partlet here. Pick up the bastard. Pick it up, I say; give it back to your hag.

[5.30] PAULINA. May your hands be forever unhonored, if you pick up the princess by that forced shameful name he has put on her!

[5.31] LEONTES. He's afraid of his wife.

[5.32] PAULINA. I wish you were of yours; then it would be beyond doubt you'd call your children your own.

[5.33] LEONTES. A nest of traitors!

[5.34] ANTIGONUS. I am no traitor, by this good daylight.

[5.35] PAULINA. Nor I; nor anyone but one who's here, and that's himself. For he betrays his own sacred honor, his queen's, his hopeful son's, his baby's, to slander, whose sting is sharper than the sword's; and will not (because, as things stand now, it's a curse he can't be made to do it) once pull up the root of his belief, which is as rotten as ever oak or stone was sound.

[5.36] LEONTES. A scolding shrew with a boundless tongue, who's just been beating her husband, and now baits me! This brat is none of mine. It's the offspring of Polixenes. Take it away, and along with the mother of it, commit them both to the fire.

[5.37] PAULINA. It is yours: and if we could put the old proverb against you, 'so like you, the worse for it.' Look, my lords, though the print is small, the whole matter and copy of the father: eye, nose, lip; the cast of his frown, his forehead; even the dent, the pretty dimples of his chin and cheek; his smiles; the very mold and frame of hand, nail, finger. And you, good goddess Nature, who have made the child so like the man that begot her—if you have the ordering of the mind too, among all the colors put no yellow in it, lest she suspect, as he does, that her children are not her husband's!

[5.38] LEONTES. A coarse hag! And, you scoundrel, you deserve to be hanged for not stopping her tongue.

[5.39] ANTIGONUS. Hang all the husbands that can't pull off that trick, and you'll be left with hardly a single subject.

[5.40] LEONTES. Once more, take her away.

[5.41] PAULINA. A most unworthy and unnatural lord can do no worse.

[5.42] LEONTES. I'll have you burnt.

[5.43] PAULINA. I don't care. It's the heretic that makes the fire, not she who burns in it. I won't call you a tyrant: but this most cruel treatment of your queen, with no greater accusation behind it than your own weak-hinged fancy, smacks somewhat of tyranny, and will make you ignoble—yes, a scandal to the world.

[5.44] LEONTES. On your allegiance, out of the chamber with her! If I were a tyrant, where would her life be? She'd not dare call me one, if she knew me to be one. Away with her!

[5.45] PAULINA. I beg you, don't push me; I'll be gone. Look to your baby, my lord; she's yours: may Jove send her a better guiding spirit! What need is there for these hands? You that are so tender over his follies, will never do him any good, not one of you. So, so. Farewell; we're gone.

[5.46] [Exit.]

[5.47] LEONTES. You, traitor, you set your wife on to this. My child? Away with it. Even you, who have such a tender heart for it, take it away, and see it instantly consumed in fire; even you, and no one but you. Pick it up now: within this hour bring me word it's done, and on good evidence, or I'll seize your life, and everything else you call yours. If you refuse and would risk my wrath, say so: I'll dash the bastard's brains out with these very hands of mine. Go, take it to the fire; for you set your wife on.

[5.48] ANTIGONUS. I did not, sir: these lords, my noble peers, will clear me of that if they will.

[5.49] LORDS. We can: my royal liege, he is not guilty of her coming here.

[5.50] LEONTES. You're all liars.

[5.51] FIRST LORD. I beg your highness, give us better credit: we have always served you truly, and beg you to think of us so. And on our knees we beg, as recompense for our dear services past and to come, that you change this plan—which, being so horrible, so bloody, must lead to some terrible outcome. We all kneel.

[5.52] LEONTES. I am a feather for every wind that blows. Shall I live on to see this bastard kneel and call me father? Better burn it now than curse it then. But so be it; let it live. No, it shan't either. [To Antigonus.] You, sir, come here, you who have been so tenderly attentive with Lady Margery, your midwife there, to save this bastard's life—for it is a bastard, as sure as this beard is gray. What will you risk to save this brat's life?

[5.53] ANTIGONUS. Anything, my lord, that my ability may undertake, and nobility may impose: at the very least this much—I'll stake the little blood I have left to save the innocent. Anything possible.

[5.54] LEONTES. It shall be possible. Swear by this sword that you will do my bidding.

[5.55] ANTIGONUS. I will, my lord.

[5.56] LEONTES. Mark, and do it, you see? Because failure on any point of it shall not only be death to yourself, but to your foul-tongued wife, whom we pardon this time. We charge you, as you are my liege-man, that you carry this female bastard hence, and bear her to some remote and deserted place, quite outside our dominions; and that you leave her there, without further mercy, to her own protection and the favor of the climate. As by some strange chance she came to us, I justly charge you, on peril of your soul and torture of your body, that you abandon her in some far-off place where chance may nurse her or end her. Pick it up.

[5.57] ANTIGONUS. I swear to do this, though an immediate death would have been more merciful. Come on, poor baby: may some powerful spirit teach the kites and ravens to be your nurses! Wolves and bears, they say, casting aside their savageness, have done like offices of pity. Sir, be more prosperous than this deed deserves! And, blessing, against this cruelty, fight on your side, poor thing, condemned to be lost!

[5.58] [Exit with the child.]

[5.59] LEONTES. No, I'll not rear another man's offspring.

[5.60] [Enter a Servant.]

[5.61] SERVANT. Please your highness, messengers from those you sent to the oracle have come an hour ago. Cleomenes and Dion, having safely arrived from Delphos, are both landed and hurrying to the court.

[5.62] FIRST LORD. With your leave, sir, their speed has been beyond reckoning.

[5.63] LEONTES. They've been gone twenty-three days. That's good speed. It foretells the great Apollo will suddenly have the truth of this come out. Prepare yourselves, lords. Summon a session, so we may arraign our most disloyal lady; for, as she has been publicly accused, so shall she have a just and open trial. While she lives, my heart will be a burden to me. Leave me, and think on my orders.

[5.64] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 6: Act 3, Scene 1 — Sicilia. A Street in some Town ##########
[6.0] [Enter Cleomenes and Dion.]

[6.1] CLEOMENES. The climate's delicate; the air is sweet, the island fertile, and the temple far surpasses the ordinary praise it gets.

[6.2] DION. What caught me most, I'll be reporting, were the heavenly robes (I think that's the right word) and the reverence of the solemn priests who wore them. Oh, the sacrifice! How ceremonious, solemn, and otherworldly it was in the offering!

[6.3] CLEOMENES. But above all, the sudden burst and ear-deafening voice of the oracle, kin to Jove's thunder, so overwhelmed my senses that I was nothing.

[6.4] DION. If the outcome of the journey turns out as well for the queen—oh, may it!—as it has been for us rare, pleasant, and swift, the time will have been well spent.

[6.5] CLEOMENES. May great Apollo turn it all out for the best! These proclamations, forcing such faults onto Hermione, I don't much like.

[6.6] DION. The violent way it's being driven through will clear it or finish it: when the oracle (sealed up like this by Apollo's great divine hand) reveals what it contains, something extraordinary will then leap into knowledge. Go. Fresh horses! And may the outcome be gracious!

[6.7] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 7: Act 3, Scene 2 — The same. A Court of Justice ##########
[7.0] [Enter Leontes, Lords and Officers appear, properly seated.]

[7.1] LEONTES. This session (which we open with great grief) presses hard against our heart: the party on trial is the daughter of a king, our wife, and one we have loved too much. Let us be cleared of being tyrannous, since we proceed so openly in justice, which shall run its proper course, all the way to guilt or acquittal. Produce the prisoner.

[7.2] OFFICER. It is his highness's pleasure that the queen appear in person here in court. Silence!

[7.3] [Hermione is brought in guarded; Paulina and Ladies attending.]

[7.4] LEONTES. Read the indictment.

[7.5] OFFICER.

[7.6] [Reads.] 'Hermione, queen to the worthy Leontes, king of Sicilia,

[7.7] you are here accused and arraigned of high treason, in committing adultery with Polixenes, king of Bohemia; and in conspiring with Camillo to take away the life of our sovereign lord the king, your royal husband; the pretext for which being by circumstance partly disclosed, you, Hermione, contrary to the faith and allegiance of a true subject, counseled and aided them, for their better safety, to flee away by night.'

[7.8] HERMIONE. Since what I have to say must only contradict my accusation, and the testimony on my side can come from no one but myself, it will scarcely help me to say 'Not guilty.' My integrity, being counted as falsehood, will be received as falsehood no matter how I express it. But this much: if divine powers behold our human actions—as they do, I have no doubt—then innocence will make false accusation blush and tyranny tremble at patience. You, my lord, best know (though you will least seem to know it) that my past life has been as continent, as chaste, as true, as I am now unhappy; which is more than history can match, even if it were invented and staged to draw spectators. For look at me, a fellow occupant of the royal bed, who owns a share of the throne, a great king's daughter, the mother to a hopeful prince, here standing to babble and plead for life and honor in front of whoever cares to come and listen. As for life, I value it as I value grief, which I would gladly spare myself. As for honor, that's something passed from me to my children, and that's all I stand for. I appeal to your own conscience, sir: before Polixenes came to your court, how I stood in your favor, how I deserved to. Since he came, by what improper meeting have I strained myself to appear thus? If by one jot beyond the bounds of honor, in act or in will tending that way, harden the hearts of all who hear me, and let my nearest kin cry shame upon my grave!

[7.9] LEONTES. I never yet heard that any of these bolder kinds of vice had less impudence in denying what they'd done than in doing it in the first place.

[7.10] HERMIONE. That's true enough; though it's a saying, sir, that doesn't apply to me.

[7.11] LEONTES. You won't admit it.

[7.12] HERMIONE. More than I'm mistress of, which comes to me under the name of fault, I must not at all acknowledge. As for Polixenes, with whom I'm accused, I do confess I loved him in the way his rank required, with such a love as became a lady like me; a love no other and no more than what you yourself commanded. Not to have done so, I think, would have been on my part both disobedience and ingratitude to you and toward your friend, whose love had spoken, ever since it could speak, from infancy, freely, that it was yours. Now, as for conspiracy, I don't know how it tastes, though it be served up to me to try; all I know of it is that Camillo was an honest man. And why he left your court the gods themselves, knowing no more than I, are ignorant.

[7.13] LEONTES. You knew of his departure, just as you know what you've undertaken to do in his absence.

[7.14] HERMIONE. Sir, you speak a language I don't understand. My life is in the line of fire of your dreams, and I'll lay it down.

[7.15] LEONTES. Your actions are my dreams. You had a bastard by Polixenes, and I only dreamed it. As you were past all shame (those of your kind are) so past all truth, which it concerns you more to deny than helps; for as your brat has been cast out, like its own kind, with no father to own it (which is, in truth, more criminal in you than in it), so you shall feel our justice; whose easiest sentence is no less than death.

[7.16] HERMIONE. Sir, spare your threats: the bogey you would frighten me with, I welcome. To me, life can be no benefit. The crown and comfort of my life, your love, I give up for lost, because I feel it gone, though I don't know how it went. My second joy, the firstfruits of my body, I am barred from his presence, like someone infectious. My third comfort, born under the unluckiest star, was hauled from my breast (the innocent milk still in its most innocent mouth) to be murdered. Myself proclaimed on every post a whore; with shameless hatred denied the privileges of childbed that belong to women of every rank. Lastly, hurried here to this place, into the open air, before I've recovered any strength. Now, my liege, tell me what blessings I have here alive that I should fear to die. So go ahead. But hear this: don't mistake me—I prize life not a straw, but for my honor, which I would clear. If I'm condemned upon mere suspicions, with all evidence asleep except what your jealousies have awakened, I tell you that's tyranny, not law. To all your honors, I refer my case to the oracle: Apollo be my judge!

[7.17] FIRST LORD. This request of yours is altogether just: therefore bring forth, in Apollo's name, his oracle.

[7.18] [Exeunt certain Officers.]

[7.19] HERMIONE. The Emperor of Russia was my father. Oh, that he were alive and here to see his daughter's trial—that he could only see the bareness of my misery, but with eyes of pity, not of revenge!

[7.20] [Enter Officers with Cleomenes and Dion.]

[7.21] OFFICER. You shall here swear upon this sword of justice that you, Cleomenes and Dion, have both been to Delphos, and from there have brought this sealed-up oracle, delivered by the hand of great Apollo's priest; and that since then you have not dared break the holy seal, nor read the secrets in it.

[7.22] CLEOMENES, DION. All this we swear.

[7.23] LEONTES. Break the seals and read.

[7.24] OFFICER.

[7.25] [Reads.] 'Hermione is chaste; Polixenes blameless; Camillo a true

[7.26] subject; Leontes a jealous tyrant; his innocent baby truly begotten; and the king shall live without an heir, if that which is lost be not found.'

[7.27] LORDS. Now blessed be the great Apollo!

[7.28] HERMIONE. Praised!

[7.29] LEONTES. Have you read truth?

[7.30] OFFICER. Yes, my lord, exactly as it is set down here.

[7.31] LEONTES. There is no truth at all in the oracle: the session shall proceed; this is pure falsehood.

[7.32] [Enter a Servant hastily.]

[7.33] SERVANT. My lord the king, the king!

[7.34] LEONTES. What is the matter?

[7.35] SERVANT. Oh sir, I'll be hated for reporting it. The prince your son, from sheer worry and fear about the queen's fate, is gone.

[7.36] LEONTES. What! Gone?

[7.37] SERVANT. Is dead.

[7.38] LEONTES. Apollo's angry, and the heavens themselves strike at my injustice.

[7.39] [Hermione faints.]

[7.40] What's happening there?

[7.41] PAULINA. This news is mortal to the queen. Look down and see what death is doing.

[7.42] LEONTES. Take her away. Her heart is just overburdened; she'll recover. I have believed my own suspicion too much. I beg you, tenderly apply to her some life-saving remedies.

[7.43] [Exeunt Paulina and Ladies with Hermione.]

[7.44] Apollo, pardon my great impiety against your oracle! I'll reconcile myself to Polixenes, woo my queen again, recall the good Camillo, whom I proclaim a man of truth and mercy; because, being carried away by my jealousies to bloody thoughts and revenge, I chose Camillo to be the minister to poison my friend Polixenes—which would have been done, but that the good mind of Camillo delayed my swift command, though I threatened him with death if he did not act, and promised reward if he did. He, most humane and full of honor, opened my plot to my royal guest, abandoned his great fortunes here, which you knew were great, and committed himself to the certain hazard of every uncertainty, richer in nothing but his honor. How he glitters through my rust! And how his piety makes my deeds look blacker!

[7.45] [Enter Paulina.]

[7.46] PAULINA. Woe the while! Oh, cut my lacing, lest my heart, cracking it, break too!

[7.47] FIRST LORD. What fit is this, good lady?

[7.48] PAULINA. What studied torments, tyrant, have you for me? What wheels? Racks? Fires? What flaying? Boiling in lead or oils? What old or newer torture must I undergo, whose every word deserves to taste the worst you have? Your tyranny, working together with your jealousies—fancies too weak for boys, too green and idle for girls of nine. Oh, think what they have done, and then run mad indeed, stark mad! For all your past follies were only a foretaste of this. That you betrayed Polixenes, that was nothing: that merely showed you, on top of being a fool, fickle and damnably ungrateful. Nor was it much that you would have poisoned good Camillo's honor, by making him kill a king: poor trespasses, with monstrous ones standing alongside. Of which I reckon the casting out to crows of your baby daughter, to be either nothing or a small offense—though a devil would have shed tears out of fire before doing it. Nor is it directly laid to you the death of the young prince, whose honorable thoughts—thoughts noble for one so tender—broke the heart that could conceive a gross and foolish father had blemished his gracious mother. This is not, no, laid to your account. But the last—O lords, when I have said it, cry Woe!—the queen, the queen, the sweetest, dearest creature's dead, and vengeance for it not dropped down yet.

[7.49] FIRST LORD. The higher powers forbid!

[7.50] PAULINA. I say she's dead: I'll swear to it. If word and oath won't convince you, go and see. If you can bring color or shine into her lip, her eye, heat outwardly or breath inside, I'll serve you as I would the gods. But, O you tyrant! Do not repent these things—they're heavier than all your sorrows can stir. So give yourself over to nothing but despair. A thousand knees, ten thousand years together, naked, fasting, upon a barren mountain, in storm perpetual, could not move the gods to look the way you stand.

[7.51] LEONTES. Go on, go on: you cannot say too much; I have deserved every tongue to speak their bitterest.

[7.52] FIRST LORD. Say no more: however this business turns out, you've gone too far in the boldness of your speech.

[7.53] PAULINA. I'm sorry for it: every fault I make, when I come to recognize it, I repent. Alas, I've shown too much the rashness of a woman: he is touched to the noble heart. What's gone and past help should be past grief. Don't take new affliction from my pleading. I beg you, rather let me be punished, who have reminded you of what you should forget. Now, my good liege, sir, royal sir, forgive a foolish woman. The love I bore your queen—look, fool again! I'll speak of her no more, nor of your children. I won't even remind you of my own lord, who is also lost. Take patience to yourself, and I'll say nothing.

[7.54] LEONTES. You spoke well only when you spoke truth, which I welcome much better than your pity. Please, bring me to the dead bodies of my queen and son: one grave shall hold both. On them shall the causes of their deaths appear, to our shame forever. Once a day I'll visit the chapel where they lie, and tears shed there shall be my recreation. As long as nature will bear up with this exercise, so long I daily vow to keep at it. Come, and lead me to these sorrows.

[7.55] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 8: Act 3, Scene 3 — Bohemia. A desert Country near the Sea ##########
[8.0] [Enter Antigonus with the Child and a Mariner.]

[8.1] ANTIGONUS. You're certain, then, that our ship has touched on the wild lands of Bohemia?

[8.2] MARINER. Yes, my lord; and I fear we've landed at a bad time. The skies look grim and threaten an imminent storm. In my conscience, the heavens are angry with the business we're about, and frown upon us.

[8.3] ANTIGONUS. Their sacred wills be done! Go, get aboard; look to your ship. I'll not be long before I call for you.

[8.4] MARINER. Be as quick as you can, and don't go too far inland: it looks like loud weather; besides, this place is famous for the beasts of prey that keep to it.

[8.5] ANTIGONUS. You go: I'll follow at once.

[8.6] MARINER. I'm glad at heart to be rid of this business.

[8.7] [Exit.]

[8.8] ANTIGONUS. Come, poor baby. I have heard, but never believed, that the spirits of the dead may walk again. If such a thing is so, your mother appeared to me last night: no dream was ever so like waking. To me came a figure, sometimes her head to one side, sometimes the other. I never saw a vessel of such grief, so full and so becoming. In pure white robes, like holiness itself, she approached the cabin where I lay. Three times she bowed before me; gasping to begin some speech, her eyes became two fountains. The fury spent, this broke from her: 'Good Antigonus, since fate, against your better nature, has made you the man who must cast out my poor baby, in keeping with your oath, places remote enough exist in Bohemia: weep there, and leave her crying. And because the baby is reckoned lost forever, please call her Perdita. For this unkind task, put upon you by my lord, you shall never see your wife Paulina again.' And so, with shrieks, she melted into the air. Greatly frightened, I in time collected myself, and thought that this was real, and no dream. Dreams are nothing, yet for this once, yes, superstitiously, I will be guided by it. I do believe Hermione has met her death, and that Apollo would have it that, since this is in truth the child of King Polixenes, she should be laid here, for life or death, on the soil of her rightful father. Blossom, fare thee well! There lie; and there, your tokens; there these;

[8.9] [Laying down the child and a bundle.]

[8.10] Which, if fortune pleases, may both feed you, pretty one, and still remain yours. The storm begins: poor wretch, who for your mother's fault are thus exposed to loss and whatever may follow! Weep I cannot, but my heart bleeds, and most accursed am I to be sworn to this. Farewell! The day frowns more and more. You're like to have a lullaby too rough. I've never seen the heavens so dim by day. A savage roar! I had better get aboard! This is the hunt: I'm gone forever.

[8.11] [Exit, pursued by a bear.]

[8.12] [Enter an old Shepherd.]

[8.13] SHEPHERD. I wish there were no age between ten and twenty-three, or that youth would sleep through the rest of it; for there's nothing in between but getting girls pregnant, wronging their elders, stealing, fighting—Listen now! Would anyone but these boiled brains of nineteen and twenty-two hunt in weather like this? They have scared away two of my best sheep, which I'm afraid the wolf will find sooner than the master: if I have them anywhere, it's by the sea-side, nibbling at ivy. Good luck, by your will, what do we have here?

[8.14] [Taking up the child.]

[8.15] Mercy on us, a baby! A very pretty baby! A boy or a girl, I wonder? A pretty one; a very pretty one. Surely some accident. Though I'm not bookish, I can read 'waiting-gentlewoman' in this mishap. This has been some stair-business, some chest-business, some behind-the-door business. Those that got this were warmer than the poor thing is here. I'll pick it up out of pity: but I'll wait till my son comes; he was calling just now. Whoa-ho-hoa!

[8.16] [Enter Clown.]

[8.17] CLOWN. Hilloa, loa!

[8.18] SHEPHERD. What, are you so near? If you want to see a thing to talk about when you're dead and rotten, come here. What's the matter with you, man?

[8.19] CLOWN. I have seen two such sights, by sea and by land! But I shouldn't call it a sea, because it's now the sky: between the heavens and it, you couldn't push the point of a needle.

[8.20] SHEPHERD. Why, boy, how so?

[8.21] CLOWN. I wish you could just see how it chafes, how it rages, how it leaps up the shore! But that's beside the point. Oh, the most pitiful cry of the poor souls! Sometimes seeing them, and sometimes not. Now the ship boring the moon with her mainmast, and the next moment swallowed up with yeast and froth, as you'd thrust a cork into a barrel. And then for the action on land, to see how the bear tore out his shoulder-bone, how he cried out to me for help and said his name was Antigonus, a nobleman. But to finish off the ship: to see how the sea swallowed it like a flap-dragon. But first, how the poor souls roared, and the sea mocked them; and how the poor gentleman roared, and the bear mocked him; both roaring louder than the sea or the storm.

[8.22] SHEPHERD. Name of mercy, when was this, boy?

[8.23] CLOWN. Now, now. I have not blinked since I saw these sights: the men are not yet cold under the water, nor is the bear half done dining on the gentleman. He's at it now.

[8.24] SHEPHERD. I wish I'd been there to help the old man!

[8.25] CLOWN. I wish you'd been by the side of the ship, to help her: your charity would have had nothing to stand on there.

[8.26] SHEPHERD. Heavy matters, heavy matters! But look here, boy. Now bless yourself: you've met with things dying, I with things new-born. Here's a sight for you. Look you, a christening-cloth for a squire's child! Look here; pick it up, pick it up, boy; open it. So, let's see. It was told to me I'd be rich through the fairies. This is some changeling: open it. What's inside, boy?

[8.27] CLOWN. You're a made old man. If the sins of your youth are forgiven, you're set for life. Gold! All gold!

[8.28] SHEPHERD. This is fairy gold, boy, and it will turn out so. Pick it up, keep it close: home, home, the quickest way. We're lucky, boy, and to stay so requires nothing but secrecy. Let my sheep go: come, good boy, the nearest way home.

[8.29] CLOWN. You go the nearest way with your findings. I'll go see if the bear's gone from the gentleman, and how much he's eaten. They're never bad-tempered except when they're hungry: if there's any of him left, I'll bury it.

[8.30] SHEPHERD. That's a good deed. If you can tell by what's left of him who he is, fetch me to the sight of him.

[8.31] CLOWN. Indeed, I will; and you can help me put him in the ground.

[8.32] SHEPHERD. It's a lucky day, boy, and we'll do good deeds on it.

[8.33] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 9: Act 4, Scene 1 ##########
[9.0] [Enter Time, the Chorus.]

[9.1] TIME. I, who please some and put all to the test—both joy and terror of good and bad alike, who makes errors and unmakes them—now take it upon myself, in the name of Time, to use my wings. Don't count it a crime in me or in my swift passage that I slide over sixteen years and leave untested the growth of that wide gap; since it is in my power to overturn law, and in one self-born hour to plant and overwhelm custom. Let me pass the same as I always was, before the most ancient order existed, or what is now accepted. I was witness to the times that brought those in; and so I am to the freshest things now reigning, and I'll make the glitter of this present go stale, as my tale now seems to it. Allowing this much patience, I turn my glass, and give my scene growth as if you had slept through it. Leontes leaving the effects of his foolish jealousies, so grieving that he shuts himself away, imagine me, gentle spectators, that I may now be in fair Bohemia; and remember well, I mentioned a son of the king's, whom I now name Florizel to you; and with speed I move on to speak of Perdita, now grown in grace equal to wonder. What follows of her I won't prophesy; but let Time's news be known when it's brought forth. A shepherd's daughter, and what attaches to her, that follows after, is Time's argument. Of this be content, if you've ever spent time worse before now; if never, yet Time himself says he earnestly wishes you never may.

[9.2] [Exit.]



########## CHAPTER 10: Act 4, Scene 2 — Bohemia. A Room in the palace of Polixenes ##########
[10.0] [Enter Polixenes and Camillo.]

[10.1] POLIXENES. Please, good Camillo, don't keep pressing me on this. It's a sickness to refuse you anything; a death to grant this.

[10.2] CAMILLO. It's been fifteen years since I saw my country. Though I've mostly been living abroad, I want to lay my bones there. Besides, the penitent king, my master, has sent for me; to whose grieving sorrows I might be some comfort, or so I presume to think—which is another spur to my departure.

[10.3] POLIXENES. As you love me, Camillo, don't wipe out the rest of your services by leaving me now. The need I have of you, your own goodness has created. Better not to have had you than to be without you now. You, having taken on tasks for me that no one but you can adequately handle, must either stay to carry them out yourself, or take away with you the very services you've done. If I haven't sufficiently considered them (as I can hardly do too much), to be more thankful to you shall be my study; and my reward in it will be the heaping up of friendships. Of that fatal country Sicilia, please speak no more; the very naming of it punishes me with the memory of that penitent, as you call him, and reconciled king, my brother; whose loss of his most precious queen and children are even now to be freshly lamented. Tell me, when did you last see the Prince Florizel, my son? Kings are no less unhappy in their children not being gracious than in losing them after they've shown their virtues.

[10.4] CAMILLO. Sir, it's been three days since I saw the prince. What his happier affairs may be, I don't know. But I've noticed by his absence that he has lately retired much from court, and is less often at his princely exercises than he used to be.

[10.5] POLIXENES. I've thought about this much, Camillo, and with some concern; so far that I have eyes in my service which watch his withdrawal; from whom I have this intelligence: that he is seldom away from the house of a very plain shepherd, a man who, they say, from absolutely nothing and beyond the imagination of his neighbors, has grown into an extraordinary estate.

[10.6] CAMILLO. I've heard, sir, of such a man, who has a daughter of most rare note: the report of her has spread further than would seem to begin from such a cottage.

[10.7] POLIXENES. That's likewise part of my intelligence: but, I fear, the bait that pulls our son there. You shall come with us to the place, where, not appearing as we are, we'll have some conversation with the shepherd; from whose simplicity I think it not difficult to learn the reason for my son's resorting there. Please, be my partner now in this business, and set aside your thoughts of Sicilia.

[10.8] CAMILLO. I willingly obey your command.

[10.9] POLIXENES. My good Camillo! We must disguise ourselves.

[10.10] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 11: Act 4, Scene 3 — The same. A Road near the Shepherd’s cottage ##########
[11.0] [Enter Autolycus, singing.]

[11.1] AUTOLYCUS. When daffodils begin to peek out, with, hey! the wench tumbling in the dale, why, then comes in the sweetness of the year, for red blood reigns in winter's pale face.

[11.2] The white sheet bleaching on the hedge, with, hey! the sweet birds, O, how they sing! sets my thieving tooth on edge; for a quart of ale is a dish fit for a king.

[11.3] The lark that tirra-lirra chants, with, hey! with, hey! the thrush and the jay, are summer songs for me and my girls, while we lie tumbling in the hay.

[11.4] I have served Prince Florizel, and in my time wore the richest velvet, but now I'm out of service.

[11.5] But shall I go and mourn for that, my dear? The pale moon shines by night: and when I wander here and there, that's when I'm most on the straight road.

[11.6] If tinkers may have leave to live, and carry the pigskin sack, then I can give my account, and own it in the stocks.

[11.7] My trade is sheets: when the kite builds its nest, watch out for smaller linens. My father named me Autolycus, who being, as I am, born under Mercury, was likewise a picker-up of unconsidered trifles. With dice and whoring I bought this outfit, and my income is petty cheating. The gallows and the beating are too dangerous on the highway. Whipping and hanging frighten me. As for the next life, I sleep right through any thought of it. A prize! A prize!

[11.8] [Enter Clown.]

[11.9] CLOWN. Let me see: every eleven wethers make one tod of wool; every tod yields a pound and odd shilling; fifteen hundred sheep shorn—what does the wool come to?

[11.10] AUTOLYCUS.

[11.11] [Aside.] If my snare holds, the cock is mine.

[11.12] CLOWN. I can't do this without counters. Let me see: what am I to buy for our sheep-shearing feast? 'Three pounds of sugar, five pounds of currants, rice'—what will this sister of mine do with rice? But my father has made her mistress of the feast, and she lays it on. She has made me twenty-four nosegays for the shearers, all three-part singers and very good ones; but they're mostly tenors and basses, with only one Puritan among them, and he sings psalms to hornpipes. I must have saffron to color the warden pies; 'mace; dates,' no, that's not on my list; 'nutmegs, seven; one or two roots of ginger,' but I might beg those; 'four pounds of prunes, and as many sun-dried raisins.'

[11.13] AUTOLYCUS.

[11.14] [Groveling on the ground.] Oh, that ever I was born!

[11.15] CLOWN. In the name of me!

[11.16] AUTOLYCUS. Oh, help me, help me! Just pull off these rags; and then, death, death!

[11.17] CLOWN. Alas, poor soul! You need more rags piled on you, not these taken off.

[11.18] AUTOLYCUS. Oh sir, the disgustingness of them offends me more than the lashes I've received, which are mighty ones, and millions.

[11.19] CLOWN. Alas, poor man! A million beatings could amount to something serious.

[11.20] AUTOLYCUS. I'm robbed, sir, and beaten; my money and clothes taken from me, and these detestable rags put on.

[11.21] CLOWN. What, by a horseman or a footman?

[11.22] AUTOLYCUS. A footman, sweet sir, a footman.

[11.23] CLOWN. Indeed, he must be a footman by the clothes he's left with you: if this is a horseman's coat, it's seen very hot service. Lend me your hand, I'll help you up; come, lend me your hand.

[11.24] [Helping him up.]

[11.25] AUTOLYCUS. Oh, good sir, tenderly, oh!

[11.26] CLOWN. Alas, poor soul!

[11.27] AUTOLYCUS. Oh, good sir, gently, good sir. I fear, sir, my shoulder blade is out.

[11.28] CLOWN. How now! Can you stand?

[11.29] AUTOLYCUS. Gently, dear sir! [Picks his pocket.] Good sir, gently. You've done me a charitable service.

[11.30] CLOWN. Do you need any money? I have a little money for you.

[11.31] AUTOLYCUS. No, good sweet sir; no, I beg you, sir. I have a kinsman not three-quarters of a mile from here, to whom I was going. I'll have money or anything I want there. Offer me no money, please; it kills my heart.

[11.32] CLOWN. What kind of fellow was he that robbed you?

[11.33] AUTOLYCUS. A fellow, sir, whom I've known to go around with the loose women. I knew him once as a servant of the prince. I can't say, good sir, for which of his virtues it was, but he was certainly whipped out of the court.

[11.34] CLOWN. His vices, you mean; there's no virtue whipped out of the court. They cherish it to make it stay; and even then it won't do more than just linger.

[11.35] AUTOLYCUS. Vices, I should have said, sir. I know this man well. Since then he's been an ape-keeper, then a process-server, a bailiff. Then he got hold of a puppet-show of the Prodigal Son, and married a tinker's wife within a mile of where my land and living lie; and, having tried many knavish professions, he settled simply into being a rogue. Some call him Autolycus.

[11.36] CLOWN. A curse on him! A thief, by my life, a thief: he haunts wakes, fairs, and bear-baitings.

[11.37] AUTOLYCUS. Very true, sir; he, sir, he; that's the rogue who put me into this outfit.

[11.38] CLOWN. There's not a more cowardly rogue in all Bohemia. If you had only puffed yourself up and spat at him, he'd have run.

[11.39] AUTOLYCUS. I have to confess to you, sir, I'm no fighter. I'm false-hearted that way; and he knew that, I'll wager.

[11.40] CLOWN. How are you now?

[11.41] AUTOLYCUS. Sweet sir, much better than I was. I can stand and walk: I'll just take my leave of you and walk gently toward my kinsman's house.

[11.42] CLOWN. Shall I see you on the way?

[11.43] AUTOLYCUS. No, good-faced sir; no, sweet sir.

[11.44] CLOWN. Then farewell. I must go buy spices for our sheep-shearing.

[11.45] AUTOLYCUS. Prosper you, sweet sir!

[11.46] [Exit Clown.]

[11.47] Your purse isn't hot enough to buy your spices. I'll be with you at your sheep-shearing too. If I don't make this trick lead to another, and turn the shearers into sheep, let me be struck off the rolls and my name put in the book of virtue!

[11.48] [Sings.]

[11.49] Jog on, jog on, the footpath way, and merrily catch the stile-a: a merry heart goes all the day, your sad one tires in a mile-a.

[11.50] [Exit.]



########## CHAPTER 12: Act 4, Scene 4 — The same. A Shepherd’s Cottage ##########
[12.0] [Enter Florizel and Perdita.]

[12.1] FLORIZEL. These unusual clothes of yours give life to every part of you—you're no shepherdess, but Flora peering at the front of April. This sheep-shearing of yours is like a gathering of minor gods, and you are the queen of it.

[12.2] PERDITA. Sir, my gracious lord, it doesn't suit me to scold your excesses; oh, forgive me even naming them! Your high self, the gracious model of the land, you have hidden under a peasant's clothing, and me, a poor lowly girl, you've dressed up like a goddess. If it weren't that our feasts have a bit of folly at every course, and the diners digest it as a custom, I should blush to see you so dressed; faint, I think, if I looked at myself in a mirror.

[12.3] FLORIZEL. I bless the time when my good falcon made her flight across your father's land.

[12.4] PERDITA. May Jove give you cause to bless it! To me the difference between us forges fear. Your greatness isn't used to being afraid. Even now I tremble to think your father, by some accident, might pass this way, as you did. Oh, the Fates! How would he look to see his work, so noble, so basely bound up? What would he say? Or how should I, in this borrowed finery, face the sternness of his presence?

[12.5] FLORIZEL. Imagine nothing but cheer. The gods themselves, humbling their divinity to love, have taken on the shapes of beasts. Jupiter became a bull and bellowed; green Neptune a ram and bleated; and the fire-robed god, golden Apollo, a poor humble peasant, as I seem now. Their transformations were never for a beauty rarer than yours, nor in a way so chaste; since my desires don't run ahead of my honor, nor my lusts burn hotter than my faith.

[12.6] PERDITA. Oh, but, sir, your resolve cannot hold when it's opposed, as it must be, by the king's power: one of two things must happen, that you must change this plan, or I my life.

[12.7] FLORIZEL. My dearest Perdita, please don't darken the mirth of the feast with these forced thoughts. Either I'll be yours, my fair one, or not my father's. Because I cannot be my own, nor anything to anyone, if I am not yours. To this I am most constant, though destiny says no. Be merry, gentle one. Strangle such thoughts as these with anything you happen to see in the meantime. Your guests are coming: lift up your face, as if it were the day of the wedding we two have sworn shall come.

[12.8] PERDITA. Oh, Lady Fortune, stand favorably!

[12.9] FLORIZEL. See, your guests approach: get ready to entertain them brightly, and let's be red with mirth.

[12.10] [Enter Shepherd with Polixenes and Camillo, disguised; Clown, Mopsa, Dorcas with others.]

[12.11] SHEPHERD. For shame, daughter! When my old wife lived, on this day she was pantler, butler, cook, both dame and servant; welcomed everyone; served everyone; would sing her song and dance her turn; now here at the upper end of the table, now in the middle; on his shoulder, and his; her face on fire with labor, and the thing she took to quench it she would sip to each one. You stand back, as if you were a guest, and not the hostess of the gathering. Please, bid these unknown friends of ours welcome, because it's a way to make us better friends, better known. Come, quench your blushes, and present yourself as you are, mistress of the feast. Come on, and bid us welcome to your sheep-shearing, and may your good flock prosper.

[12.12] PERDITA.

[12.13] [To Polixenes.] Sir, welcome.

[12.14] It is my father's will that I take on the hostess role of the day.

[12.15] [To Camillo.] You're welcome, sir.

[12.16] Give me those flowers there, Dorcas. Reverend sirs, for you there's rosemary and rue; these keep their appearance and scent all winter long. Grace and remembrance to you both! And welcome to our shearing!

[12.17] POLIXENES. Shepherdess—a fair one are you—you well suit our ages with flowers of winter.

[12.18] PERDITA. Sir, with the year growing old, not yet at summer's death nor at the birth of trembling winter, the fairest flowers of the season are our carnations and striped gillyflowers, which some call nature's bastards. Of that kind our rustic garden is bare, and I don't care to take cuttings of them.

[12.19] POLIXENES. Why, gentle maiden, do you neglect them?

[12.20] PERDITA. Because I've heard it said there's an art which, in their streaked colors, shares in great creating nature's work.

[12.21] POLIXENES. Say there is. Yet nature is made better by no means but the means nature makes. So, over that art which you say adds to nature, is an art which nature makes. You see, sweet maid, we marry a gentler shoot to the wildest stock, and make a tree of baser kind conceive by a bud of nobler race. This is an art which mends nature, or rather changes it, but the art itself is nature.

[12.22] PERDITA. So it is.

[12.23] POLIXENES. Then make your garden rich in gillyflowers, and don't call them bastards.

[12.24] PERDITA. I'll not put the dibble in the earth to set a single slip of them; no more than, if I were painted with cosmetics, I'd want this youth to say it looked well, and only for that reason desire to breed by me. Here are flowers for you: hot lavender, mints, savory, marjoram, the marigold, that goes to bed with the sun and rises with him weeping. These are flowers of middle summer, and I think they're suited to men of middle age. You're very welcome.

[12.25] CAMILLO. I should leave grazing, were I one of your flock, and live only by gazing.

[12.26] PERDITA. Oh, alas! You'd be so lean that gusts of January would blow you right through. [To Florizel.] Now, my fairest friend, I wish I had some flowers of the spring that might suit your time of day; and yours, and yours, who still wear on your virgin branches your maidenheads, still growing. Oh Proserpina, for the flowers that, frightened, you let fall from Dis's wagon! [12.27] FLORIZEL. What, like a corpse?

[12.28] PERDITA. No, like a bank for love to lie and play on; not like a corpse; or if so, not to be buried, but alive, and in my arms. Come, take your flowers. I think I'm playing a part as I've seen them do in Whitsun pastoral plays. Surely this robe of mine changes my mood.

[12.29] FLORIZEL. What you do keeps bettering what's already done. When you speak, sweet, I'd have you do it forever. When you sing, I'd have you buy and sell that way, give alms that way, pray that way; and, for the ordering of your affairs, sing them too. When you dance, I wish you a wave of the sea, that you might forever do nothing but that—move always, always so—and have no other function. Each thing you do, so singular in every particular, crowns the present action, so that all your acts are queens.

[12.30] PERDITA. Oh, Doricles, your praises are too grand. Except that your youth, and the true blood that peeps fairly through it, plainly declare you an unstained shepherd, I might fear with reason, my Doricles, that you wooed me falsely.

[12.31] FLORIZEL. I think you have as little skill in fearing as I have in trying to put you to it. But come; our dance, please. Your hand, my Perdita. So turtle doves pair that never mean to part.

[12.32] PERDITA. I'll swear that for them.

[12.33] POLIXENES. This is the prettiest low-born girl that ever ran on a green field. Nothing she does or seems but smacks of something greater than herself, too noble for this place.

[12.34] CAMILLO. He's telling her something that makes her blood rise. By my faith, she's the queen of curds and cream.

[12.35] CLOWN. Come on, strike up.

[12.36] DORCAS. Mopsa must be your partner: indeed, garlic, to improve her kissing with!

[12.37] MOPSA. Oh, well timed!

[12.38] CLOWN. Not a word, not a word; we stand on our manners. Come, strike up.

[12.39] [Music. Here a dance of Shepherds and Shepherdesses.]

[12.40] POLIXENES. Please, good shepherd, what fair young man is this who's dancing with your daughter?

[12.41] SHEPHERD. They call him Doricles, and he boasts he has a worthy living. But I have it on his own report, and I believe it. He looks truthful. He says he loves my daughter. I think so too; for the moon never gazed on water as he'll stand and read, as it were, my daughter's eyes. And, to be plain, I think there's not half a kiss between them in deciding who loves the other best.

[12.42] POLIXENES. She dances nicely.

[12.43] SHEPHERD. So she does everything, though I report it who should be silent. If young Doricles ends up with her, she'll bring him what he doesn't even dream of.

[12.44] [Enter a Servant.]

[12.45] SERVANT. Oh master, if you could only hear the peddler at the door, you'd never dance again to a tabor and pipe; no, the bagpipe couldn't move you. He sings several tunes faster than you can count money. He pours them out as if he'd eaten ballads, and every man's ears were glued to his tunes.

[12.46] CLOWN. He couldn't come at a better time: let him come in. I love a ballad pretty well, if it's a doleful matter merrily set, or a very pleasant thing sung lamentably.

[12.47] SERVANT. He has songs for man or woman of every size. No haberdasher can fit his customers with gloves so well. He has the prettiest love songs for maids, so free of bawdry, which is strange; with such delicate refrains of dildos and fadings, 'jump her and thump her'; and where some big-mouthed rascal would, as it were, mean mischief and break a foul gap into the matter, he makes the maid answer 'Whoop, do me no harm, good man'; puts him off, dismisses him, with 'Whoop, do me no harm, good man.'

[12.48] POLIXENES. This is a fine fellow.

[12.49] CLOWN. Believe me, you're talking of an admirable, witty fellow. Has he any unwrapped wares?

[12.50] SERVANT. He has ribbons of all the colors in the rainbow; laces, more than all the lawyers in Bohemia can handle, though they come to him by the gross; tape, threads, cambrics, lawns; why, he sings them over as if they were gods or goddesses; you'd think a smock were a she-angel, the way he chants over the cuff and the embroidery around the bodice.

[12.51] CLOWN. Please bring him in; and let him come in singing.

[12.52] PERDITA. Warn him to use no scurrilous words in his tunes.

[12.53] [Exit Servant.]

[12.54] CLOWN. You have peddlers, sister, that have more in them than you'd think.

[12.55] PERDITA. Yes, good brother, or care to think.

[12.56] [Enter Autolycus, singing.]

[12.57] AUTOLYCUS. Lawn as white as driven snow, cypress black as ever was crow, gloves as sweet as damask roses, masks for faces and for noses, bugle-bracelet, necklace amber, perfume for a lady's chamber, golden caps and stomachers for my lads to give their dears, pins and poking-sticks of steel, what maids lack from head to heel. [12.58] CLOWN. If I weren't in love with Mopsa, you'd get no money from me; but being held captive as I am, I'm also held bondsman for certain ribbons and gloves.

[12.59] MOPSA. I was promised them before the feast; but they don't come too late even now.

[12.60] DORCAS. He's promised you more than that, or there are liars.

[12.61] MOPSA. He has paid you all he promised you. Maybe he has paid you more, which will shame you to give back to him.

[12.62] CLOWN. Is there no manners left among maids? Will they wear their petticoats where they should wear their faces? Is there not milking-time, when you're going to bed, or kiln-hole, to whisper of these secrets, but you must be tattling before all our guests? It's well they're whispering. Quiet your tongues, and not a word more.

[12.63] MOPSA. I've finished. Come, you promised me a tawdry lace and a pair of sweet gloves.

[12.64] CLOWN. Haven't I told you how I was cheated on the road and lost all my money?

[12.65] AUTOLYCUS. And indeed, sir, there are cheats out and about; therefore men should be wary.

[12.66] CLOWN. Don't worry, man. You'll lose nothing here.

[12.67] AUTOLYCUS. I hope so, sir; for I have about me many parcels of value.

[12.68] CLOWN. What have you here? Ballads?

[12.69] MOPSA. Please now, buy some. I love a ballad in print better than anything, for then we're sure they're true.

[12.70] AUTOLYCUS. Here's one to a very doleful tune. How a usurer's wife gave birth to twenty money-bags at one go, and how she longed to eat adders' heads and toads grilled.

[12.71] MOPSA. Is it true, do you think?

[12.72] AUTOLYCUS. Very true, and only a month old.

[12.73] DORCAS. Bless me from marrying a usurer!

[12.74] AUTOLYCUS. Here's the midwife's name on it, one Mistress Taleporter, and five or six honest wives who were present. Why should I carry lies around?

[12.75] MOPSA. Please now, buy it.

[12.76] CLOWN. Come on, set it aside; and let's first see more ballads. We'll buy the other things in a bit.

[12.77] AUTOLYCUS. Here's another ballad, of a fish that appeared off the coast on Wednesday the eightieth of April, forty thousand fathoms above the water, and sang this ballad against the hard hearts of maids. It was thought she was a woman, and was turned into a cold fish because she would not exchange flesh with someone who loved her. The ballad is very pitiful, and as true.

[12.78] DORCAS. Is it true too, do you think?

[12.79] AUTOLYCUS. Five justices' signatures on it, and witnesses more than my pack can hold.

[12.80] CLOWN. Set it aside too: another.

[12.81] AUTOLYCUS. This is a merry ballad; but a very pretty one.

[12.82] MOPSA. Let's have some merry ones.

[12.83] AUTOLYCUS. Why, this is a surpassingly merry one and goes to the tune of 'Two maids wooing a man.' There's hardly a maid westward but sings it. It's in demand, I can tell you.

[12.84] MOPSA. We can both sing it: if you'll take a part, you shall hear; it's in three parts.

[12.85] DORCAS. We had the tune of it a month ago.

[12.86] AUTOLYCUS. I can take my part; you must know it's my profession: have at it with you.

[12.87] SONG.

[12.88] AUTOLYCUS. Get you gone, for I must go where it doesn't suit you to know.

[12.89] DORCAS. Where?

[12.90] MOPSA. Oh, where?

[12.91] DORCAS. Where?

[12.92] MOPSA. It well suits your oath that you should tell me your secrets.

[12.93] DORCAS. Me too! Let me go there.

[12.94] MOPSA. Either you go to the grange or the mill.

[12.95] DORCAS. If to either, you do wrong.

[12.96] AUTOLYCUS. Neither.

[12.97] DORCAS. What, neither?

[12.98] AUTOLYCUS. Neither.

[12.99] DORCAS. You have sworn to be my love.

[12.100] MOPSA. You have sworn it more to me. So where are you going? Say, where?

[12.101] CLOWN. We'll finish this song later by ourselves. My father and the gentlemen are in serious talk, and we'll not trouble them. Come, bring your pack after me. Wenches, I'll buy for both of you. Peddler, let's have first choice. Follow me, girls.

[12.102] [Exit with Dorcas and Mopsa.]

[12.103] AUTOLYCUS.

[12.104] [Aside.] And you shall pay well for them.

[12.105] SONG.

[12.106] Will you buy any tape, or lace for your cape, my dainty duck, my dear-a? Any silk, any thread, any toys for your head, of the newest and finest, finest wear-a? Come to the peddler; money's a meddler that makes all men's wares move-a.

[12.108] [Enter Servant.]

[12.109] SERVANT. Master, there are three carters, three shepherds, three cowherds, three swineherds, who have made themselves all men of hair. [12.110] SHEPHERD. Away! We'll have none of it. There's been too much homely foolery already. I know, sir, we tire you.

[12.111] POLIXENES. You tire the ones who refresh us: please, let's see these four threes of herdsmen.

[12.112] SERVANT. One three of them, by their own report, sir, has danced before the king; and not the worst of the three but jumps twelve and a half feet, measured square.

[12.113] SHEPHERD. Stop your prattling: since these good men are pleased, let them come in; but quickly now.

[12.114] SERVANT. Why, they're waiting at the door, sir.

[12.115] [Exit.]

[12.116] [Enter Twelve Rustics, dressed like Satyrs. They dance, and then exeunt.]

[12.117] POLIXENES. Oh, father, you'll know more of that later.

[12.118] [To Camillo.] Isn't it gone too far? It's time to part them.

You have let him go, and bargained for nothing with him. If your lass should misread that and call it your lack of love or generosity, you'd be hard pressed for a reply, at least if you care about keeping her happy.

This hand, as soft as dove's down and as white as it, or an Ethiopian's tooth, or the wind-blown snow that's twice sifted by northern blasts.

[12.121] POLIXENES. What follows this? How prettily the young swain seems to wash the hand that was fair before! I have interrupted you. But to your declaration. Let me hear what you profess.

[12.122] FLORIZEL. Do, and witness it.

[12.123] POLIXENES. And this neighbor of mine, too?

[12.124] FLORIZEL. And he, and more than he, and men, the earth, the heavens, and all: that even were I crowned the most imperial monarch, most worthy of it, were I the fairest youth that ever made an eye swerve, had I force and knowledge more than ever was man's, I'd not prize them without her love; for her I'd employ them all; commend them and condemn them to her service, or to their own destruction.

[12.125] POLIXENES. Fairly offered.

[12.126] CAMILLO. This shows a sound affection.

[12.127] SHEPHERD. But my daughter, do you say the like to him?

[12.128] PERDITA. I cannot speak so well, nothing so well; no, nor mean better: by the pattern of my own thoughts I cut out the purity of his.

[12.129] SHEPHERD. Take hands, a bargain! And, friends unknown, you shall bear witness to it. I give my daughter to him, and will make her portion equal to his.

[12.130] FLORIZEL. Oh, that must come from the virtue of your daughter: one being dead, I'll have more than you can dream of yet; enough then for your wonder. But come on, contract us before these witnesses.

[12.131] SHEPHERD. Come, your hand; and, daughter, yours.

[12.132] POLIXENES. Wait, swain, a moment, please. Have you a father?

[12.133] FLORIZEL. I have; but what of him?

[12.134] POLIXENES. Does he know of this?

[12.135] FLORIZEL. He neither does nor shall.

[12.136] POLIXENES. I think a father is, at the wedding of his son, a guest who best becomes the table. Please tell me once more, isn't your father grown incapable of reasonable affairs? Isn't he stupid with age and changing humors? Can he speak? Hear? Tell one man from another? Settle his own estate? Isn't he bedridden? And does he do nothing now but what he did as a child?

[12.137] FLORIZEL. No, good sir. He has his health, and ampler strength indeed than most have at his age.

[12.138] POLIXENES. By my white beard, you do him, if this is so, a wrong somewhat unfilial. It's reasonable that my son should choose himself a wife, but it's just as reasonable that the father, whose only joy is in fair posterity, should have some say in such a business.

[12.139] FLORIZEL. I grant all this. But for some other reasons, my grave sir, which it's not fit you know, I do not acquaint my father with this business.

[12.140] POLIXENES. Let him know it.

[12.141] FLORIZEL. He shall not.

[12.142] POLIXENES. Please let him.

[12.143] FLORIZEL. No, he must not.

[12.144] SHEPHERD. Let him, my son: he won't need to grieve at knowing of your choice.

[12.145] FLORIZEL. Come, come, he must not. Mark our contract.

[12.146] POLIXENES.

[12.147] [Revealing himself.] Mark your divorce, young sir,

[12.148] whom I dare not call son; you are too base to be acknowledged: you, a scepter's heir, who lust after a sheep-hook! You, old traitor, I am sorry that, by hanging you, I can only shorten your life by one week. And you, fresh piece of excellent witchcraft, who must of necessity know the royal fool you're coupling with,—

[12.149] SHEPHERD. Oh, my heart!

[12.150] POLIXENES. I'll have your beauty scratched with briers and made homelier than your station. [12.152] PERDITA. Undone, even here. I wasn't much afraid, for once or twice I was about to speak and tell him plainly that the same sun that shines on his court hides not his face from our cottage, but looks on alike. [To Florizel.] Will you please, sir, be gone? I told you what would come of this. I beg you, look to your own state. [12.153] CAMILLO. Why, how now, father! Speak before you die.

[12.154] SHEPHERD. I cannot speak, nor think, nor dare to know that which I know. Oh sir, you have undone a man of eighty-three, who hoped to fill his grave in quiet; yes, to die on the bed my father died on, to lie close by his honest bones. But now some hangman must put on my shroud and lay me where no priest shovels in dust. Oh cursed wretch, that knew this was the prince and would venture to mingle troth with him! Undone, undone! If I might die within this hour, I have lived to die when I desire.

[12.156] FLORIZEL. Why do you look so at me? I am only sorry, not afraid; delayed, but nothing altered: what I was, I am. Pulling harder when held back; not following my leash unwillingly.

[12.158] FLORIZEL. I don't intend to. I think—Camillo?

[12.159] CAMILLO. Even he, my lord.

[12.160] PERDITA. How often have I told you it would be like this! How often said my dignity would last only until it was known!

[12.161] FLORIZEL. It cannot fail except by the violation of my faith; and then let nature crush the sides of the earth together and ruin the seeds within! Lift up your face. From my succession wipe me, father; I am heir to my affection.

[12.162] CAMILLO. Be advised.

[12.163] FLORIZEL. I am, by my fancy. If my reason will be obedient to that, I have reason; if not, my senses, better pleased with madness, bid madness welcome.

[12.164] CAMILLO. This is desperate, sir.

[12.165] FLORIZEL. So call it: but it does fulfill my vow. I must think it honesty. Camillo, not for Bohemia, nor the pomp that might be gleaned there; for all the sun sees, or the close earth wombs, or the deep seas hide in unknown fathoms, will I break my oath to this my fair beloved. Therefore, please, as you have always been my father's honored friend, when he misses me (because, in truth, I don't mean to see him any more), cast your good counsels upon his passion. Let myself and fortune tug for the time to come. This you may know, and so report, that I have put to sea with her whom I cannot hold here on shore; and, most opportunely for her need, I have a vessel anchored close by, but not prepared for this purpose. What course I mean to hold won't benefit your knowing, nor will it concern me to report it.

[12.166] CAMILLO. Oh my lord, I wish your spirit were easier to advise, or stronger for your need.

[12.167] FLORIZEL. Listen, Perdita. [Takes her aside.]

[12.168] [To Camillo.] I'll hear you presently.

[12.169] CAMILLO. He's immovable, resolved on flight. Now would I be happy if I could turn his going to serve my purpose, save him from danger, do him love and honor, gain again the sight of dear Sicilia and that unhappy king, my master, whom I so thirst to see.

[12.170] FLORIZEL. Now, good Camillo, I am so full of pressing business that I leave out ceremony.

[12.171] CAMILLO. Sir, I think you have heard of my poor services, in the love I bore your father?

[12.172] FLORIZEL. Very nobly have you deserved: it is my father's music to speak of your deeds, not a small part of his concern to have them rewarded as he thinks of them.

[12.173] CAMILLO. Well, my lord, if you'll be pleased to think I love the king, and through him, what's nearest to him, which is your gracious self, just embrace my direction, if your weightier and more settled plan may permit alteration. On my honor, I'll point you where you shall have such a reception as becomes your highness; where you may enjoy your mistress (from whom, I see, there's no separation to be made, but by what heaven forbid—your ruin). Marry her, and with my best efforts in your absence I'll strive to soften your discontented father and bring him round to liking.

[12.174] FLORIZEL. How, Camillo, may this, almost a miracle, be done? That I may call you something more than a man, and after that trust in you.

[12.175] CAMILLO. Have you thought of a place to go to?

[12.176] FLORIZEL. Not yet. But as the unthought-of accident is to blame for what we do wildly, so we declare ourselves to be the slaves of chance, and flies of every wind that blows.

[12.177] CAMILLO. She shall be dressed as becomes the partner of your bed. I think I see Leontes opening his free arms and weeping his welcomes; asks you, the son, forgiveness, as if it were in the father's person; kisses the hands of your fresh princess; over and over divides himself between his unkindness and his kindness. The one he chides to hell, and bids the other grow faster than thought or time.

[12.178] FLORIZEL. Worthy Camillo, what excuse for my visit shall I offer him?

[12.179] CAMILLO. Sent by the king your father to greet him and to give him comforts. [12.180] FLORIZEL. I am bound to you: there's some sap in this.

[12.181] CAMILLO. Besides, you know, prosperity is the very bond of love, whose fresh complexion and whose heart together affliction alters.

[12.182] PERDITA. One of these is true: I think affliction may subdue the cheek, but not take in the mind.

[12.183] CAMILLO. Yes, do you say so? There shall not, in your father's house, these seven years be born another such.

[12.184] FLORIZEL. My good Camillo, she is as forward in her breeding as she is behind in our birth.

[12.185] CAMILLO. I cannot say it's a pity she lacks instruction, for she seems a mistress to most that teach.

[12.186] PERDITA. Your pardon, sir; for this I'll blush my thanks.

[12.187] FLORIZEL. My prettiest Perdita! But, oh, the thorns we stand on! Camillo, preserver of my father, now of me, the medicine of our house, how shall we manage? We are not furnished like the son of Bohemia, nor shall appear so in Sicilia.

[12.188] CAMILLO. My lord, fear none of this. I think you know my fortunes all lie there: it shall be so much my care to have you royally appointed, as if the scene you play were mine. For instance, sir, that you may know you'll not want—one word.

[12.189] [They talk aside.]

[12.190] [Enter Autolycus.]

[12.191] AUTOLYCUS. Ha, ha! What a fool Honesty is! And Trust, his sworn brother, a very simple gentleman! I have sold all my trumpery. Not a counterfeit stone, not a ribbon, glass, pomander, brooch, table-book, ballad, knife, tape, glove, shoe-tie, bracelet, horn-ring, to keep my pack from fasting. They throng to see who should buy first, as if my trinkets had been blessed and brought a benediction to the buyer: by which means I saw whose purse looked best; and what I saw, I remembered to my good use. My clown (who needs only a little to be a reasonable man) grew so in love with the wenches' song that he wouldn't stir his feet till he had both tune and words; which so drew the rest of the herd to me that all their other senses stuck in their ears. You might have pinched a placket—it was senseless; gelding a purse from a codpiece was nothing; I would have filed off keys hanging in chains: no hearing, no feeling, but my sir's song, and admiring the nothing of it. So that in this time of lethargy I picked and cut most of their festival purses; and had not the old man come in with a hubbub against his daughter and the king's son, and scared my crows from the chaff, I would not have left a purse alive in the whole army.

[12.192] [Camillo, Florizel and Perdita come forward.]

[12.193] CAMILLO. No, but my letters, by being there as soon as you arrive, shall clear that doubt.

[12.194] FLORIZEL. And those you'll procure from King Leontes?

[12.195] CAMILLO. Shall satisfy your father.

[12.196] PERDITA. Happy you may be! All you say sounds fair.

[12.197] CAMILLO.

[12.198] [Seeing Autolycus.] Who have we here?

[12.199] We'll make an instrument of this; omit nothing that may give us help.

[12.200] AUTOLYCUS.

[12.201] [Aside.] If they've overheard me now,—why, hanging.

[12.202] CAMILLO. How now, good fellow! Why are you shaking so? Don't fear, man; there's no harm intended to you.

[12.203] AUTOLYCUS. I am a poor fellow, sir.

[12.204] CAMILLO. Why, be so still; nobody here will steal that from you: yet, for the outside of your poverty we must make a swap; therefore strip yourself instantly—you must think there's a necessity in it—and change clothes with this gentleman: though the bargain on his side is the worst, yet here, there's a little extra.

[12.205] [Giving money.]

[12.206] AUTOLYCUS. I am a poor fellow, sir: [Aside.] I know you well enough.

[12.207] CAMILLO. No, please be quick: the gentleman is half undressed already.

[12.208] AUTOLYCUS. Are you in earnest, sir? [Aside.] I smell the trick of it.

[12.209] FLORIZEL. Be quick, please.

[12.210] AUTOLYCUS. Indeed, I've had a down-payment; but I cannot in conscience take it.

[12.211] CAMILLO. Unbuckle, unbuckle.

[12.212] [Florizel and Autolycus exchange clothes.]

[12.213] Fortunate mistress—let my prophecy come home to you!—you must withdraw yourself into some hidden spot. Take your sweetheart's hat and pull it down over your brows, muffle your face, take off your finery; and, as best you can, disguise the truth of your appearance; so that you may (for I do fear watchful eyes) get aboard ship undiscovered.

[12.214] PERDITA. I see the play lies so that I must take a part.

[12.215] CAMILLO. No remedy. Have you done there?

[12.216] FLORIZEL. Should I meet my father now, he wouldn't call me son.

[12.217] CAMILLO. No, you shall have no hat. [Giving it to Perdita.] Come, lady, come. Farewell, my friend.

[12.218] AUTOLYCUS. Goodbye, sir.

[12.219] FLORIZEL. Oh Perdita, what have we two forgotten? Please, a word.

[12.220] [They converse apart.]

[12.221] CAMILLO.

[12.222] [Aside.] What I shall do next is tell the king

[12.223] of this escape, and where they are bound; in which my hope is that I shall so prevail as to force him after them. In whose company I shall see Sicilia again; for the sight of which I have a woman's longing.

[12.224] FLORIZEL. Fortune speed us! So we set out, Camillo, to the seaside.

[12.225] CAMILLO. The swifter speed the better.

[12.226] [Exeunt Florizel, Perdita and Camillo.]

[12.227] AUTOLYCUS. I understand the business, I hear it. To have an open ear, a quick eye, and a nimble hand is necessary for a cutpurse; a good nose is needed too, to smell out work for the other senses. I see this is the time when the unjust man thrives. What an exchange this would have been without something extra! What an extra this is along with the exchange! Surely the gods are this year on our side, and we may do anything off the cuff. The prince himself is up to a piece of villainy, sneaking away from his father with his ball-and-chain on his heels: if I thought it were honest to acquaint the king with it, I would not do it: I hold it the greater knavery to conceal it; and in that I am constant to my profession.

[12.228] [Enter Clown and Shepherd.]

[12.229] Aside, aside; here is more matter for a hot brain: every lane's end, every shop, church, session, hanging, gives a careful man work.

[12.230] CLOWN. See, see; what a man you are now! There's no other way but to tell the king she's a changeling, and none of your flesh and blood.

[12.231] SHEPHERD. No, but hear me.

[12.232] CLOWN. No, but hear me.

[12.233] SHEPHERD. Go to, then.

[12.234] CLOWN. She being none of your flesh and blood, your flesh and blood has not offended the king; and so your flesh and blood isn't to be punished by him. Show those things you found on her, those secret things, all but what she has with her: this being done, let the law go whistle, I warrant you.

[12.235] SHEPHERD. I will tell the king all, every word, yes, and his son's pranks too; who, I may say, is no honest man, neither to his father nor to me, to try to make me the king's brother-in-law.

[12.236] CLOWN. Indeed, brother-in-law was the furthest you could have been from him, and then your blood would have been the dearer by I don't know how much an ounce.

[12.237] AUTOLYCUS.

[12.238] [Aside.] Very wisely, puppies!

[12.239] SHEPHERD. Well, let us to the king: there's that in this bundle which will make him scratch his beard.

[12.240] AUTOLYCUS.

[12.241] [Aside.] I don't know what hindrance this complaint may be to the

[12.242] flight of my master.

[12.243] CLOWN. Pray heartily he's at the palace.

[12.244] AUTOLYCUS.

[12.245] [Aside.] Though I'm not naturally honest, I'm so sometimes by

[12.246] chance. Let me pocket up my peddler's stuff. [Takes off his false beard.] How now, rustics! Where are you bound?

[12.247] SHEPHERD. To the palace, if it pleases your worship.

[12.248] AUTOLYCUS. Your affairs there, what, with whom, the contents of that bundle, where you live, your names, your ages, of what property, breeding, and anything that's fit to be known? Disclose!

[12.249] CLOWN. We are only plain fellows, sir.

[12.250] AUTOLYCUS. A lie; you are rough and hairy. Let me have no lying. It becomes none but tradesmen, and they often give us soldiers the lie; but we pay them for it with stamped coin, not stabbing steel; therefore they don't give us the lie.

[12.251] CLOWN. Your worship had like to have given us one, if you hadn't caught yourself in the act.

[12.252] SHEPHERD. Are you a courtier, if you please, sir?

[12.253] AUTOLYCUS. Whether it pleases me or not, I am a courtier. Don't you see the air of the court in this clothing? Doesn't my walk have the measure of the court in it? Doesn't your nose receive court-perfume from me? Don't I cast on your baseness court-contempt? Do you think, because I'm sounding you out, drawing out your business from you, I'm therefore no courtier? I am a courtier from head to foot, and one who will either push on or pull back your business there. So I command you to disclose your affair.

[12.254] SHEPHERD. My business, sir, is with the king.

[12.255] AUTOLYCUS. What advocate do you have for him?

[12.256] SHEPHERD. I don't know, if it pleases you.

[12.257] CLOWN. 'Advocate' is the court-word for a pheasant. Say you have none.

[12.258] SHEPHERD. None, sir; I have no pheasant, cock nor hen.

[12.259] AUTOLYCUS. How blessed are we who are not simple men! Yet nature might have made me as these are; therefore I will not disdain.

[12.260] CLOWN. This cannot be but a great courtier.

[12.261] SHEPHERD. His clothes are rich, but he wears them not handsomely.

[12.262] CLOWN. He seems the more noble for being fantastical: a great man, I'll warrant; I can tell by the picking of his teeth.

[12.263] AUTOLYCUS. That bundle there? What's in the bundle? Why that box?

[12.264] SHEPHERD. Sir, in this bundle and box lie such secrets as none must know but the king; and which he shall know within this hour, if I may come to speak with him.

[12.265] AUTOLYCUS. Old man, you have wasted your effort.

[12.266] SHEPHERD. Why, sir?

[12.267] AUTOLYCUS. The king isn't at the palace; he's gone aboard a new ship to purge melancholy and air himself: because, if you're capable of serious things, you must know the king is full of grief.

[12.268] SHEPHERD. So it's said, sir; about his son, who was to have married a shepherd's daughter.

[12.269] AUTOLYCUS. If that shepherd isn't already under arrest, let him flee. The curses he'll have, the tortures he'll feel, will break the back of a man and the heart of a monster.

[12.270] CLOWN. Do you think so, sir?

[12.271] AUTOLYCUS. Not he alone shall suffer what wit can make heavy and vengeance bitter; but those that are kin to him, even fifty times removed, shall all come under the hangman: which, though it's a great pity, yet it's necessary. An old sheep-whistling rogue, a ram-tender, daring to have his daughter come into favor! Some say he'll be stoned; but that death is too soft for him, I say. Drag our throne into a sheep-pen! All deaths are too few, the sharpest too easy.

[12.272] CLOWN. Has the old man any son, sir, do you hear, if it please you, sir?

[12.273] AUTOLYCUS. He has a son, who shall be flayed alive; then anointed all over with honey, set on the top of a wasp's nest; then made to stand there till he's three-quarters and a dram dead; then revived again with aqua vitae or some other hot infusion; then, raw as he is, on the hottest day the forecast promises, he shall be set against a brick wall, the sun looking with a southward eye upon him, where he is to be watched until flies blow him to death. But what do we talk of these traitorous rascals, whose miseries are to be smiled at, their offenses being so capital? Tell me (for you seem to be honest plain men) what you have to take to the king. Being somewhat gently considered, I'll bring you where he is aboard, present you to his presence, whisper to him on your behalf; and if it's in any man besides the king to grant your suits, here's the man to do it.

[12.274] CLOWN. He seems to be of great authority: close with him, give him gold; and though authority is a stubborn bear, yet it's often led by the nose with gold: show the inside of your purse to the outside of his hand, and no more ado. Remember: 'stoned' and 'flayed alive.'

[12.275] SHEPHERD. If it pleases you, sir, to undertake this business for us, here is what gold I have. I'll make it as much more, and leave this young man in pledge till I bring it to you.

[12.276] AUTOLYCUS. After I have done what I promised?

[12.277] SHEPHERD. Yes, sir.

[12.278] AUTOLYCUS. Well, give me half. Are you a party to this business?

[12.279] CLOWN. In some sort, sir: but though my case may be a pitiful one, I hope I shan't be flayed out of it.

[12.280] AUTOLYCUS. Oh, that's the case of the shepherd's son. Hang him, he'll be made an example.

[12.281] CLOWN. Comfort, good comfort! We must go to the king and show our strange sights. He must know it's not your daughter nor my sister; we're gone otherwise. Sir, I will give you as much as this old man does when the business is performed, and remain, as he says, your pledge till it's brought to you.

[12.282] AUTOLYCUS. I'll trust you. Walk on toward the seaside; take the right-hand way. I'll just look at this hedge, and follow you.

[12.283] CLOWN. We are blessed in this man, as I may say, even blessed.

[12.284] SHEPHERD. Let's go on, as he bids us. He was sent to do us good.

[12.285] [Exeunt Shepherd and Clown.]

[12.286] AUTOLYCUS. If I had a mind to be honest, I see Fortune wouldn't allow it: she drops booty in my mouth. I'm courted now with a double opportunity: gold, and a means to do the prince my master good; which who knows how that may turn back to my advancement? I'll bring these two moles, these blind ones, aboard him. If he thinks fit to ship them back ashore, and that the complaint they have to the king concerns him nothing, let him call me rogue for being so far officious; for I'm proof against that title and what shame else belongs to it. To him I will present them. There may be matter in it.

[12.287] [Exit.]



########## CHAPTER 13: Act 5, Scene 1 — Sicilia. A Room in the palace of Leontes ##########
[13.0] [Enter Leontes, Cleomenes, Dion, Paulina and others.]

[13.1] CLEOMENES. Sir, you have done enough, and have performed a saint-like sorrow: no fault could you make which you have not redeemed; indeed, you have paid down more penitence than wrong committed. Now at last, do as the heavens have done, forget your evil; with them, forgive yourself.

[13.2] LEONTES. While I remember her and her virtues, I cannot forget my blemishes against them; and so I still think of the wrong I did myself: which was so great that it has left my kingdom without an heir, and destroyed the sweetest companion that ever man bred his hopes out of.

[13.3] PAULINA. True, too true, my lord. If, one by one, you wedded all the world, or took something good from all those who are, to make a perfect woman, she you killed would still be unmatched.

[13.4] LEONTES. I think so. Killed! She I killed! I did so: but you strike me sharply, to say I did: it's as bitter on your tongue as in my thought. Now, good now, say so but seldom.

[13.5] CLEOMENES. Not at all, good lady. You might have spoken of a thousand things that would have done the time more good and graced your kindness better.

[13.6] PAULINA. You are one of those who would have him wed again.

[13.7] DION. If you wouldn't have him do so, you pity not the state, nor the remembrance of his most sovereign name. You consider little what dangers may drop upon his kingdom by his highness's lack of issue, and devour those uncertain onlookers. What were more holy than to rejoice the former queen is well? What holier than, for royalty's repair, for present comfort, and for future good, to bless the bed of majesty again with a sweet partner in it?

[13.8] PAULINA. There is none worthy, considering her that's gone. Besides, the gods will have their secret purposes fulfilled; for has not the divine Apollo said, isn't it the gist of his oracle, that King Leontes shall not have an heir till his lost child is found? Which to happen is as monstrous to our human reason as my Antigonus breaking out of his grave and coming back to me; he, on my life, perished with the infant. It's your counsel that my lord should oppose the heavens, set himself against their wills. [To Leontes.] Don't worry about issue; the crown will find an heir. Great Alexander left his to the worthiest; so his successor was likely to be the best.

[13.9] LEONTES. Good Paulina, who keeps the memory of Hermione in honor, I know—oh, that I had ever squared myself to your counsel! Then, even now, I might have looked upon my queen's full eyes, taken treasure from her lips,—

[13.10] PAULINA. And left them richer for what they yielded.

[13.11] LEONTES. You speak truth. No more such wives; therefore, no wife. One worse, and better used, would make her sainted spirit possess her corpse again, and on this stage (where we offenders now appear) come back soul-vexed and begin, 'Why to me?'

[13.12] PAULINA. Had she such power, she would have just cause.

[13.13] LEONTES. She would; and would incense me to murder her I married.

[13.14] PAULINA. I should think so. If I were the ghost that walked, I'd bid you mark her eye, and tell me for what dull part of it you chose her. Then I'd shriek so that even your ears would split to hear me; and the words that followed would be 'Remember mine.'

[13.15] LEONTES. Stars, stars, and all other eyes are dead coals! Don't worry about another wife; I'll have no wife, Paulina.

[13.16] PAULINA. Will you swear never to marry but by my free leave?

[13.17] LEONTES. Never, Paulina; so be blessed my spirit!

[13.18] PAULINA. Then, good my lords, bear witness to his oath.

[13.19] CLEOMENES. You tempt him too much.

[13.20] PAULINA. Unless another, as like Hermione as her picture is, confronts his eye.

[13.21] CLEOMENES. Good madam,—

[13.22] PAULINA. I have done. Yet, if my lord will marry,—if you will, sir, there's no remedy but you will,—give me the office of choosing you a queen. She shall not be so young as was your former, but she shall be such that, if your first queen's ghost walked, she would take joy to see her in your arms.

[13.23] LEONTES. My true Paulina, we shall not marry till you bid us.

[13.24] PAULINA. That shall be when your first queen breathes again; never till then.

[13.25] [Enter a Servant.]

[13.26] SERVANT. One who calls himself Prince Florizel, son of Polixenes, with his princess (the fairest I have yet seen) desires access to your high presence.

[13.27] LEONTES. What's the matter with him? He doesn't come like his father's greatness: his approach, so unforeseen and sudden, tells us it isn't a planned visit, but forced by need and accident. What followers?

[13.28] SERVANT. Just a few, and those modest.

[13.29] LEONTES. His princess, you say, with him?

[13.30] SERVANT. Yes, the most peerless piece of earth, I think, that ever the sun shone bright on.

[13.31] PAULINA. Oh Hermione, as every present time boasts itself above a better one gone, so must your grave give way to what's seen now! Sir, you yourself have said and written so,—but your writing now is colder than its theme,—'She had not been, nor was not to be equaled.' So your verse flowed with her beauty once; it has ebbed sharply, for you to say you have seen a better.

[13.32] SERVANT. Pardon, madam: the one I have almost forgot,—your pardon. The other, when she has taken your eye, will have your tongue too. This is a creature, were she to start a sect, she might quench the zeal of all other believers; make converts of any she just bid follow.

[13.33] PAULINA. What! Not women?

[13.34] SERVANT. Women will love her, that being a woman she is worth more than any man; men, that she is the rarest of all women.

[13.35] LEONTES. Go, Cleomenes; you yourself, with your honored friends to help, bring them to our embrace.

[13.36] [Exeunt Cleomenes and others.]

[13.37] Still, it's strange he should steal upon us like this.

[13.38] PAULINA. Had our prince, jewel of children, seen this hour, he would have paired well with this lord. There was not a full month between their births.

[13.39] LEONTES. Please no more; cease; you know he dies to me again when he's spoken of. Surely, when I see this gentleman, your speeches will bring me to consider what may unfurnish me of reason. They are come.

[13.40] [Enter Florizel, Perdita, Cleomenes and others.]

[13.41] Your mother was most true to her marriage vows, prince; for she printed your royal father off when conceiving you. Were I but twenty-one, your father's image is so caught in you, his very air, that I should call you brother, as I did him, and speak of some wild thing performed by us before. Most dearly welcome! And your fair princess,—goddess! Oh, alas! I lost a couple that, between heaven and earth, might thus have stood, begetting wonder, as you, gracious couple, do! And then I lost,—all my own folly,—the company, the friendship too, of your brave father, whom, though bearing this misery, I desire my life to look on once more.

[13.42] FLORIZEL. By his command I have here touched Sicilia, and from him I give you all greetings that a king, at friend, can send his brother: and, except that the infirmity which attends old age has somewhat seized his wished ability, he himself would have measured the lands and waters between your throne and his, to look upon you. You, whom he loves, he bade me say so,—more than all the scepters and those that bear them living.

[13.43] LEONTES. Oh my brother,—good gentleman!—the wrongs I have done you stir afresh within me; and these your courtesies, so rarely kind, are interpreters of my behind-hand slackness! Welcome here, as is the spring to the earth. And has he too exposed this paragon to the fearful usage, at least ungentle, of dreadful Neptune, to greet a man not worth her pains, much less the venture of her person?

[13.44] FLORIZEL. Good, my lord, she came from Libya.

[13.45] LEONTES. Where the warlike Smalus, that noble honored lord, is feared and loved?

[13.46] FLORIZEL. Most royal sir, from there; from him, whose daughter his tears proclaimed she was, on parting with her. From there, with a prosperous south wind friendly, we have crossed, to execute the charge my father gave me for visiting your highness. My best retinue I have dismissed from your Sicilian shores, who head for Bohemia to signify not only my success in Libya, sir, but my arrival, and my wife's, in safety here, where we are.

[13.47] LEONTES. May the blessed gods purge all infection from our air while you stay here! You have a holy father, a graceful gentleman; against whose person, so sacred as it is, I have sinned, for which the heavens, taking angry note, have left me without issue. And your father is blessed, as he from heaven deserves it, with you, worthy of his goodness. What might I have been, if I might have looked on a son and daughter now, such goodly things as you!

[13.48] [Enter a Lord.]

[13.49] LORD. Most noble sir, what I have to report will bear no credit, were not the proof so close at hand. If you please, great sir, Bohemia greets you from himself by me, and desires you to arrest his son, who has—throwing off his dignity and his duty both—fled from his father, from his hopes, and with a shepherd's daughter.

[13.50] LEONTES. Where's Bohemia? Speak.

[13.51] LORD. Here in your city; I just came from him. I speak in amazement, and it suits my wonder and my message. As he was hastening to your court—in the chase, it seems, of this fair couple—he meets on the way the father of this seeming lady and her brother, both having quit their country with this young prince.

[13.52] FLORIZEL. Camillo has betrayed me; whose honor and whose honesty until now have endured all weathers.

[13.53] LORD. Lay it to his charge so. He's with the king your father.

[13.54] LEONTES. Who? Camillo?

[13.55] LORD. Camillo, sir; I spoke with him; who now has these poor men in questioning. Never have I seen wretches quake so: they kneel, they kiss the earth; perjure themselves with every word. Bohemia stops his ears, and threatens them with various deaths inside death.

[13.56] PERDITA. Oh my poor father! Heaven sets spies upon us, won't have our betrothal celebrated.

[13.57] LEONTES. You are married?

[13.58] FLORIZEL. We are not, sir, nor are we likely to be. The stars, I see, will kiss the valleys first. The odds for high and low are alike.

[13.59] LEONTES. My lord, is this the daughter of a king?

[13.60] FLORIZEL. She is, when once she is my wife.

[13.61] LEONTES. That 'once,' I see by your good father's swiftness, will come on very slowly. I am sorry, most sorry, you have broken from his liking, where you were bound in duty; and as sorry that your choice is not so rich in worth as in beauty, that you might fittingly enjoy her.

[13.62] FLORIZEL. Dear, look up: though Fortune, openly an enemy, should chase us with my father, she has not a jot of power to change our loves. I beg you, sir, remember when you were no older than I am now: with the thought of such affections, step forth as my advocate. At your request my father will grant precious things as trifles.

[13.63] LEONTES. If he would do so, I'd beg your precious mistress, whom he counts but a trifle.

[13.64] PAULINA. Sir, my liege, your eye has too much youth in it. Not a month before your queen died, she was more worth such gazes than what you look on now.

[13.65] LEONTES. I thought of her even in the look I just gave. [To Florizel.] But your petition is yet unanswered. I will go to your father. Your honor not overthrown by your desires, I am friend to them and you: on which errand I now go toward him; therefore follow me, and mark what progress I make. Come, my good lord.

[13.66] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 14: Act 5, Scene 2 — The same. Before the Palace ##########
[14.0] [Enter Autolycus and a Gentleman.]

[14.1] AUTOLYCUS. Please, sir, were you present at this recounting?

[14.2] FIRST GENTLEMAN. I was there at the opening of the bundle, and heard the old shepherd describe how he found it. After a little amazement, we were all ordered out of the chamber. But I did think I heard the shepherd say he found the child.

[14.3] AUTOLYCUS. I would most gladly know the outcome of it.

[14.4] FIRST GENTLEMAN. I can only give a broken account of the business; but the changes I noticed in the king and Camillo were strokes of pure wonder. They seemed almost, by staring at each other, to tear the sockets out of their eyes. There was speech in their silence, language in their very gestures; they looked as if they had heard of a world ransomed, or one destroyed. A remarkable passion of wonder appeared in them; but the wisest watcher, knowing nothing but what he saw, could not say whether the import was joy or sorrow. But it had to be the extremity of one of them. Here comes a gentleman who probably knows more.

[14.5] [Enter a Gentleman.]

[14.6] The news, Rogero?

[14.7] SECOND GENTLEMAN. Nothing but bonfires: the oracle is fulfilled; the king's daughter is found. Such a deal of wonder has broken out within this hour that ballad-makers can't express it. Here comes Lady Paulina's steward; he can tell you more.

[14.8] [Enter a third Gentleman.]

[14.9] How does it go now, sir? This news, which is said to be true, is so like an old tale that the truth of it is under strong suspicion. Has the king found his heir?

[14.10] THIRD GENTLEMAN. Most true, if ever truth were carried full-term by circumstance. What you hear, you'll swear you see, there's such unity in the proofs. The mantle of Queen Hermione's, her jewel about its neck, the letters of Antigonus found with it, which they know to be his handwriting; the majesty of the creature in resemblance of the mother, the affection of nobleness which nature shows above her breeding, and many other pieces of evidence proclaim her with all certainty to be the king's daughter. Did you see the meeting of the two kings?

[14.11] SECOND GENTLEMAN. No.

[14.12] THIRD GENTLEMAN. Then you have lost a sight which was to be seen, can't be spoken of. There you might have beheld one joy crowning another, in such a way that it seemed sorrow wept to take leave of them, because their joy waded in tears. There was casting up of eyes, holding up of hands, with such a countenance of distraction that they could be known by their clothes, not by their faces. Our king, ready to leap out of himself for joy of his found daughter, as if that joy had now become a loss, cries, 'O, your mother, your mother!' Then asks Bohemia forgiveness; then embraces his son-in-law; then again worries his daughter by clasping her; now he thanks the old shepherd, who stands by like a weather-beaten conduit from many kings' reigns. I never heard of such another encounter, which lames report to follow it, and undoes description to do it.

[14.13] SECOND GENTLEMAN. What, please, became of Antigonus, who carried off the child?

[14.14] THIRD GENTLEMAN. Like an old tale still, which has matter to rehearse, even though credit be asleep and not an ear open. He was torn to pieces by a bear: this is vouched for by the shepherd's son, who has not only his innocence (which seems much) to justify him, but a handkerchief and rings of his that Paulina recognizes.

[14.15] FIRST GENTLEMAN. What became of his ship and his followers?

[14.16] THIRD GENTLEMAN. Wrecked the same instant of their master's death, and in the view of the shepherd: so that all the instruments which helped to expose the child were lost in the very moment when she was found. But oh, the noble combat that was fought between joy and sorrow in Paulina! She had one eye cast down for the loss of her husband, another raised because the oracle was fulfilled. She lifted the princess from the earth, and locked her in embracing as if she would pin her to her heart, so she might no more be in danger of being lost.

[14.17] FIRST GENTLEMAN. The dignity of this act was worth the audience of kings and princes; for by such it was acted.

[14.18] THIRD GENTLEMAN. One of the prettiest touches of all, and the one that fished for my eyes (caught the water, though not the fish) was, when at the account of the queen's death (with the manner of it bravely confessed and lamented by the king) the attentiveness wounded his daughter; until, from one sign of grief to another, she did, with an 'Alas!' as I would fain say, bleed tears—for I am sure my heart wept blood. Whoever was most marble there changed color; some swooned, all sorrowed. If all the world could have seen it, the woe would have been universal.

[14.19] FIRST GENTLEMAN. Have they returned to the court?

[14.20] THIRD GENTLEMAN. No: the princess, hearing of her mother's statue, which is in the keeping of Paulina—a piece many years in the making and now newly finished by that rare Italian master, Julio Romano, who, had he himself eternity, and could put breath into his work, would beguile Nature of her trade, he is so perfectly her ape: he has done Hermione so close to Hermione that they say one would speak to her and stand in hope of an answer. There with all greediness of affection they have gone, and there they intend to sup.

[14.21] SECOND GENTLEMAN. I thought she had some great matter in hand there; for she has privately, twice or three times a day ever since Hermione's death, visited that secluded house. Shall we go there, and add our company to the rejoicing?

[14.22] FIRST GENTLEMAN. Who would stay away that has the privilege of access? Every wink of an eye some new grace will be born. Our absence makes us spendthrifts with our knowledge. Let's go.

[14.23] [Exeunt Gentlemen.]

[14.24] AUTOLYCUS. Now, if I didn't have the smudge of my former life on me, advancement would drop on my head. I brought the old man and his son aboard the prince; told him I heard them talk of a bundle, and I don't know what. But he, at that time over-fond of the shepherd's daughter (so he then took her to be), who began to be very seasick, and himself little better, with extremity of weather continuing, this mystery remained undiscovered. But it's all one to me; for had I been the discoverer of this secret, it wouldn't have sat well among my other discredits.

[14.25] [Enter Shepherd and Clown.]

[14.26] Here come those I have done good to against my will, and already appearing in the blossoms of their fortune.

[14.27] SHEPHERD. Come, boy; I am past having more children, but your sons and daughters will all be gentlemen born.

[14.28] CLOWN. You are well met, sir. You refused to fight with me the other day, because I was no gentleman born. Do you see these clothes? Say you don't see them and still think me no gentleman born: you'd best say these robes are not gentlemen born. Give me the lie, do; and try whether I am not now a gentleman born.

[14.29] AUTOLYCUS. I know you are now, sir, a gentleman born.

[14.30] CLOWN. Yes, and have been any time these four hours.

[14.31] SHEPHERD. And so have I, boy!

[14.32] CLOWN. So you have: but I was a gentleman born before my father; for the king's son took me by the hand and called me brother; and then the two kings called my father brother; and then the prince, my brother, and the princess, my sister, called my father father; and so we wept; and those were the first gentleman-like tears that we ever shed.

[14.33] SHEPHERD. We may live, son, to shed many more.

[14.34] CLOWN. Yes; or else it would be hard luck, being in so preposterous a state as we are.

[14.35] AUTOLYCUS. I humbly beg you, sir, to pardon me all the faults I have committed to your worship, and to give me your good report to the prince my master.

[14.36] SHEPHERD. Please, son, do; for we must be gentle, now we are gentlemen.

[14.37] CLOWN. Will you amend your life?

[14.38] AUTOLYCUS. Yes, if it please your good worship.

[14.39] CLOWN. Give me your hand: I will swear to the prince you are as honest and true a fellow as any in Bohemia.

[14.40] SHEPHERD. You may say it, but not swear it.

[14.41] CLOWN. Not swear it, now that I am a gentleman? Let peasants and yeomen say it, I'll swear it.

[14.42] SHEPHERD. What if it's false, son?

[14.43] CLOWN. If it be ever so false, a true gentleman may swear it on behalf of his friend. And I'll swear to the prince that you are a brave fellow with your fists, and that you will not be drunk; but I know you are no brave fellow with your fists, and that you will be drunk. But I'll swear it; and I wish you would be a brave fellow with your fists.

[14.44] AUTOLYCUS. I will prove so, sir, to the best of my power.

[14.45] CLOWN. Yes, by any means, prove a brave fellow: if I do not wonder how you dare venture to be drunk, not being a brave fellow, don't trust me. Listen! The kings and princes, our kin, are going to see the queen's portrait. Come, follow us: we'll be your good masters.

[14.46] [Exeunt.]



########## CHAPTER 15: Act 5, Scene 3 — The same. A Room in Paulina’s house ##########
[15.0] [Enter Leontes, Polixenes, Florizel, Perdita, Camillo, Paulina, Lords and Attendants.]

[15.1] LEONTES. Oh grave and good Paulina, what great comfort I have had from you!

[15.2] PAULINA. What, sovereign sir, I did not do well, I meant well. All my services you have paid for in full: but that you have deigned, along with your crowned brother and these your betrothed heirs of your kingdoms, to visit my poor house, that is a surplus of your grace which my life can never last long enough to answer.

[15.3] LEONTES. Oh Paulina, we honor you with trouble. But we came to see the statue of our queen. Your gallery we have passed through, not without much pleasure in many curiosities; but we did not see that which my daughter came to look upon, the statue of her mother.

[15.4] PAULINA. As she lived peerless, so her dead likeness, I do well believe, excels whatever you ever looked upon, or the hand of man has made. So I keep it secluded, apart. But here it is: prepare to see the life as lively mocked as ever still sleep mocked death. Behold, and say it is well.

[15.5] [Paulina draws back a curtain, and reveals Hermione standing as a statue.]

[15.6] I like your silence; it the more shows off your wonder: but speak. First you, my liege. Does it not come close to her?

[15.7] LEONTES. Her natural posture! Chide me, dear stone, that I may say indeed you are Hermione; or rather, you are she in your not chiding; for she was as tender as infancy and grace. But yet, Paulina, Hermione was not so wrinkled, nothing so aged as this seems.

[15.8] POLIXENES. Oh, not by much!

[15.9] PAULINA. So much the more our carver's excellence, who lets go by some sixteen years and makes her as she would be living now.

[15.10] LEONTES. As she might now have been, as much to my good comfort as it is now piercing to my soul. Oh, thus she stood, even with such life of majesty, warm life, as it now coldly stands, when first I wooed her! I am ashamed: does not the stone rebuke me for being more stone than it is? Oh royal piece, there is magic in your majesty, which has conjured my evils to memory, and from your admiring daughter taken her spirits, standing like stone with you.

[15.11] PERDITA. And give me leave, and do not say it is superstition, that I kneel, and then implore her blessing. Lady, dear queen, that ended when I but began, give me that hand of yours to kiss.

[15.12] PAULINA. Oh, patience! The statue is but newly finished, the color's not dry.

[15.13] CAMILLO. My lord, your sorrow was too heavily laid on, that sixteen winters cannot blow away, so many summers dry. Hardly any joy ever lived so long; no sorrow but killed itself much sooner.

[15.14] POLIXENES. Dear my brother, let him that was the cause of this have power to take off as much grief from you as he will take up in himself.

[15.15] PAULINA. Indeed, my lord, if I had thought the sight of my poor image would have moved you so—for the stone is mine—I would not have shown it.

[15.16] LEONTES. Do not draw the curtain.

[15.17] PAULINA. You shall gaze on it no longer, lest your fancy might think presently that it moves.

[15.18] LEONTES. Let it be, let it be. I wish I were dead, but for the thought that already—Who was it that made it? See, my lord, would you not think it breathed? And that those veins really carried blood?

[15.19] POLIXENES. Masterly done: the very life seems warm upon her lip.

[15.20] LEONTES. The fixed look of her eye has motion in it, as we are mocked with art.

[15.21] PAULINA. I'll draw the curtain: my lord's almost so far transported that he'll think presently it lives.

[15.22] LEONTES. Oh sweet Paulina, make me think so twenty years together! No settled senses of the world can match the pleasure of that madness. Leave it alone.

[15.23] PAULINA. I am sorry, sir, I have stirred you this far: but I could afflict you further.

[15.24] LEONTES. Do, Paulina; for this affliction has a taste as sweet as any cordial comfort. Still I think there is an air comes from her. What fine chisel could ever yet cut breath? Let no man mock me, for I will kiss her!

[15.25] PAULINA. Good my lord, hold back: the red upon her lip is wet; you'll mar it if you kiss it, stain your own with oily painting. Shall I draw the curtain?

[15.26] LEONTES. No, not these twenty years.

[15.27] PERDITA. So long could I stand by, a looker-on.

[15.28] PAULINA. Either hold back, leave the chapel at once, or steel yourself for more amazement. If you can bear to see it, I'll make the statue move indeed, descend, and take you by the hand. But then you'll think (which I protest against) that I am assisted by wicked powers.

[15.29] LEONTES. Whatever you can make her do, I am content to look on; whatever to say, I am content to hear; for it is as easy to make her speak as to move.

[15.30] PAULINA. It is required that you wake your faith. Then all stand still; or those who think the business I am about is unlawful, let them depart.

[15.31] LEONTES. Proceed: no foot shall stir.

[15.32] PAULINA. Music, wake her: strike! [Music.] It is time; descend; be stone no more; approach; strike all that look on you with marvel. Come; I'll fill your grave up: stir; no, come away. Bequeath to death your numbness, for from him dear life redeems you. You see, she stirs.

[15.33] [Hermione comes down from the pedestal.]

[15.34] Don't start; her actions shall be as holy as you hear my spell is lawful. Do not shun her until you see her die again; for then you kill her twice over. No, present your hand: when she was young you wooed her; now in age has she become the suitor?

[15.35] LEONTES.

[15.36] [Embracing her.] Oh, she's warm!

[15.37] If this be magic, let it be an art as lawful as eating.

[15.38] POLIXENES. She embraces him.

[15.39] CAMILLO. She hangs about his neck. If she belongs to life, let her speak too.

[15.40] POLIXENES. Yes, and make it clear where she has lived, or how stolen from the dead.

[15.41] PAULINA. That she is living, were it only told you, would be hooted at like an old tale; but it appears she lives, though she has not yet spoken. Wait a little. Please come forward, fair madam. Kneel and pray your mother's blessing. Turn, good lady; our Perdita is found.

[15.42] [Presenting Perdita who kneels to Hermione.]

[15.43] HERMIONE. You gods, look down, and from your sacred vials pour your graces upon my daughter's head! Tell me, my own, where have you been preserved? Where lived? How found your father's court? For you shall hear that I, knowing by Paulina that the oracle gave hope you were alive, have preserved myself to see the outcome.

[15.44] PAULINA. There's time enough for that; lest they desire on this occasion to trouble your joys with the same kind of telling. Go together, you precious winners all; share your exultation with everyone. I, an old turtle dove, will wing myself to some withered bough, and there my mate, who is never to be found again, lament till I am lost.

[15.45] LEONTES. Oh peace, Paulina! You shall take a husband by my consent, as I shall a wife by yours: this is a match, and made between us by vows. You have found mine; but how, must be inquired into; for I saw her, as I thought, dead; and have in vain said many a prayer over her grave. I'll not seek far—for him, I partly know his mind—to find you an honorable husband. Come, Camillo, and take her by the hand, whose worth and honesty is richly noted, and here testified by us, a pair of kings. Let's leave this place. What! Look upon my brother: both your pardons, that ever I put between your holy looks my ill suspicion. This is your son-in-law, and son to the king, who, heaven directing, is betrothed to your daughter. Good Paulina, lead us from here; where we may at leisure each one ask, and answer for his part performed in this wide gap of time, since first we were separated. Lead away quickly!

[15.46] [Exeunt.]

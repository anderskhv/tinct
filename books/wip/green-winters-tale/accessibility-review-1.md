# Accessibility Review 1 — The Winter's Tale (`winters-tale`), modern-en

Reviewer: fresh blind read of `candidate.json` only (this session, acting as
Reviewer A per `books/prompts/accessibility-review-prompt.md`). Source text
was **not** consulted for this pass.

**Coverage statement:** All 15 chapters (Act 1 Scene 1 through Act 5 Scene
3), all 911 paragraphs, read in full and in order via a generated
line-numbered dump of `candidate.json` (`candidate_readable.txt`), start to
finish, then re-scanned paragraph by paragraph. No sampling — every
paragraph index [1.0]–[15.46] was read.

## Overall verdict

**Substantially accessible.** The prose reads as natural, idiomatic modern
English throughout — Leontes's jealous ravings, the trial scene, the
pastoral comic scenes, and Autolycus's patter all come through clearly
without losing register or intensity. Verse speeches (Time's Chorus,
Perdita's flower speech, Florizel's declarations) are rendered as flowing
prose-like modern sentences rather than choppy line-by-line transcription.
Comic dialect and malapropisms (the Clown's "saltiers" for satyrs, "hodge
podge of capers," the "advocate"/"pheasant" pun) are preserved with their
comic logic intact — a reader can still follow the joke.

## Specific accessibility notes

None of the following are blocking; they are places a general reader might
pause slightly, mostly because the material itself (mythological allusion,
period agricultural/legal vocabulary) is inherently dense, not because the
wording is needlessly obscure.

- **[2.43]** "we're as alike as eggs" — a proverb ("as like as two eggs")
  a modern reader may not immediately recognize, but the sense (close
  resemblance) is clear from context. No fix needed.
- **[9.1]** (Time's Chorus) is the densest single speech in the play —
  abstract personification, inverted syntax rebuilt into forward-order
  sentences. It reads coherently as one continuous paragraph of exposition;
  a reader may need to read it twice to track "I" (=Time) across clauses,
  but that's inherent to the device (a single actor personifying an
  abstraction), not a translation defect.
- **[12.26]** Perdita's flower catalogue ("Proserpina," "Dis's wagon,"
  "Cytherea's breath," "Phoebus") assumes classical-mythology familiarity
  the play itself assumes of its audience. The surrounding English is
  plain; only the proper nouns require outside knowledge, which is
  appropriate to preserve (these are the source's own allusions, not
  invented difficulty).
- **[2.13]** "we'll drive him out with our distaffs" — "distaff" (a
  spinning tool, used here as a symbol of women's domestic weapons) is left
  unglossed. A reader unfamiliar with the word can still follow the
  sentence's comic threat from context.
- **[5.29], [5.36]** "Dame Partlet" (a stock name for a hen, from the
  Reynard/Chanticleer tradition) is kept as the source's own joke-name; the
  surrounding line ("henpecked, knocked off your perch") makes the barnyard
  meaning clear without needing the reader to know the literary reference.
- **[11.9], [11.12]** The Clown's sheep-counting/shopping-list scene uses
  period specifics ("tod" of wool, "warden pies") — "tod" is glossed
  in-line by the surrounding math ("every eleven wethers make one tod of
  wool"), which is sufficient.

## What reads unusually well

- The Leontes jealousy speeches in Act 1 Scene 2 and Act 2 Scene 1 land
  with full force in modern syntax — nothing softened, register preserved.
- The trial scene (Act 3 Scene 2) reads as compelling courtroom drama;
  Hermione's long defense speech [7.8] is rebuilt into clear modern
  sentences without losing its rhetorical build.
- The sheep-shearing scene's comic business (Autolycus's ballads, the
  Clown/Mopsa/Dorcas byplay, the peddler patter) is lively and easy to
  follow, including the bawdy material, which is not euphemized away.
- Paulina's confrontations with Leontes (Act 2 Scene 3, Act 3 Scene 2) keep
  her rhetorical force and specificity intact.

**Verdict: substantially accessible.** No targeted accessibility fixes are
required as a result of this pass.

## Packet 1 findings (paragraphs 0–9 read, 0–8 certified)

**Paragraph 0** — Source is a single long sentence building to "is the
foresight of their own preservation..." The candidate restructures this into
several sentences and inserts a sentence not present in the source: *"Why,
then, do they accept the restraint we see them living under in
commonwealths?"* This is an invented rhetorical question. It introduces no
new factual claim (it restates, as a question, material the source states
directly), so it is not a meaning-level fidelity defect, but it is an
addition not licensed by the source text and is flagged as such per the
review method. Non-blocking.

**Paragraph 1** — Faithful. All clauses present: the list of natural laws,
"covenants without the sword," the small-families/robbery passage, the
cities-and-kingdoms analogy. "instruments of husbandry" → "farming tools" and
"art" → "skill" are acceptable modernizations that preserve meaning.

**Paragraph 2** — Faithful. The "multitude sufficient to confide in" passage
and the comparison-to-the-enemy logic are both preserved intact.

**Paragraph 3** — Faithful. One structural note: source's "not onely
subdued... but also... make warre" parallel construction is split into two
independent clauses in the candidate ("As a result they are easily
subdued... and even when there is no common enemy, they make war..."). Both
claims survive; the explicit "not only/but also" linkage is softened but not
lost. Non-blocking style note.

**Paragraph 4** — Faithful. "unanimous endeavour" → "united effort," "he that
by one part is held for an enemy, is by another part held for a friend" is
rendered correctly and does not flip which party holds which view.

**Paragraph 5** — Faithful. The Aristotle attribution, the hedge "some man
may perhaps desire to know," and "why Man-kind cannot do the same" are all
preserved with the hedge intact ("someone may perhaps want to know").

**Paragraph 6** — Faithful. Actors and causal chain (competition → envy/hatred
→ war) preserved.

**Paragraph 7** — Faithful. One phrase worth flagging for calibration, not
correction: "can relish nothing but what is eminent" → "can relish nothing
but what sets him above them." This makes explicit a comparison that the
source leaves more general ("eminent" = prominent/outstanding, not
necessarily "above them" specifically). Given the immediately preceding
clause about comparing himself to other men, this is a defensible gloss
rather than an invented claim. Non-blocking.

**Paragraph 8** — Faithful. "Distraction and Civill warre" → "disorder and
civil war" — reasonable synonym, no claim lost.

## Packet 2 findings (paragraphs 7–15 read, 9–15 certified)

**Paragraph 9** — Faithful. The good-in-likeness-of-evil / evil-in-likeness-
of-good construction and "augment, or diminish" are both preserved correctly
and in the right direction (not flipped).

**Paragraph 10** — Faithful. "controule the Actions" → "check the actions" —
correct sense (restrain/call to account), not "control" in the modern sense
of commandeer.

**Paragraph 11** — Faithful. Causal structure ("no wonder... if there be
somewhat else required... which is a Common Power") intact.

**Paragraph 12** (the long mechanism/definition paragraph) — Mostly faithful
and carefully handled: the capitalized terms of art (Person, Author) are
preserved through the modernized prose; the famous social-contract formula —
*"I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe..."* — is left
**verbatim**, unmodernized, exactly matching the source character-for-
character. This is the correct choice for a quoted performative formula and
is a fidelity strength, not a defect.

One terminology point flagged for review: source's *"reduce all their
Wills, by plurality of voices, unto one Will"* becomes *"who can reduce all
their wills, by majority of voices, to one will."* "Plurality" and "majority"
are near-synonyms in general 17th-century usage but are technically distinct
in modern political vocabulary (plurality = largest share among 3+ options;
majority = more than half). Because this is Hobbes's own technical
description of how a multitude's wills are reduced to one — a mechanism this
chapter treats precisely — I recommend restoring "plurality" or glossing it,
even though the substitution is defensible as an accessibility choice and
does not change the paragraph's actual argument (one man/assembly ends up
authorized either way). Flagged as non-blocking but worth a deliberate
decision rather than a silent swap.

**Paragraph 13** — The definition-of-commonwealth quote — *"One Person, of
whose Acts a great Multitude..."* — is preserved **verbatim**, matching the
source exactly. Correct.

**Paragraph 14** — Faithful. Source's "as called SOVERAIGNE" (an apparent
period typo for "is called") is correctly normalized to "is called" — this
is an obvious transcription-level fix, not a meaning change, and is not
flagged as a silent correction of Hobbes's actual content.

**Paragraph 15** — Faithful. Both routes to sovereign power (natural
force/paternal and by war; voluntary institution) are preserved with the
same actors and the same conditions ("since he is able to destroy them if
they refuse," "trusting to be protected by him against all others"). The
Institution/Acquisition distinction and closing sentence ("I shall speak
first of a commonwealth by institution") are intact.

## Defects

### Blocking
None found.

### Non-blocking

1. **¶0** — Source: single sentence, "The finall Cause, End, or Designe of
   men... is the foresight of their own preservation..." Candidate inserts:
   *"Why, then, do they accept the restraint we see them living under in
   commonwealths?"* — a rhetorical question not present in the source.
   **Fix:** optional; if a stricter accessibility standard is wanted, remove
   the inserted question and render the paragraph as declarative sentences
   only, e.g. "Men naturally love liberty... The final cause, end, or design
   behind [the restraint they accept] is this: they foresee their own
   preservation..."

2. **¶7** — Source: "can relish nothing but what is eminent." Candidate:
   "can relish nothing but what sets him above them." **Fix:** optional; if
   a closer register is wanted, "can relish nothing but what is eminent" or
   "what stands out" preserves the source's more general term without
   asserting the specific comparative "above them" (which, while
   contextually supported, is an interpretive step beyond the source word).

3. **¶12** — Source: "reduce all their Wills, by plurality of voices, unto
   one Will." Candidate: "reduce all their wills, by majority of voices, to
   one will." **Fix:** restore "plurality of voices" (Hobbes's own term of
   art) or, if "majority" is kept for accessibility, add a brief gloss
   noting the terms are used interchangeably in this 17th-century sense, so
   a modern reader doesn't read in the technical plurality/majority
   distinction that didn't exist for Hobbes.

4. **¶3** — Source's "not onely subdued... but also... make warre" parallel
   construction is split into two independent clauses. **Fix:** optional
   only; no claim is lost, but restoring the "not only X but also Y" framing
   would track the source's rhetorical emphasis more closely, e.g. "As a
   result, they are easily subdued not only by a very few who act in
   agreement, but they also make war on one another over their private
   interests even when there is no common enemy."

## Verdict

**ACCEPT AS-IS**

No blocking defects — no actor swaps, no dropped or flipped negations, no
causality reversals, no dropped hedges, no dropped conditions, no omissions
of clauses/examples/numbers, no unlicensed factual additions, and no silent
"corrections" of names, dates, or citations. The two set-piece quotations
(the contract formula and the definition of commonwealth) are both preserved
verbatim and exactly. The four items above are optional polish, not required
fixes, and none of them changes what the chapter claims or who it attributes
actions to.

# Style note: *Poetics* (Aristotle), Tinct Modern English

- **Book:** `poetics`
- **Edition:** `modern-en`

This note governs every renderer, reviser and reviewer of this book. The book is short (about 14,800 words), so every sentence counts.

**Fidelity anchor:** `served-original-en.json`, the served `original-en`, S. H. Butcher's English translation (1895). The task is a modernization of that English, not a new translation. Consult no other translation and no Greek.

## What this edition is

Butcher's *Poetics* rendered sentence by sentence into plain contemporary English. It removes archaic diction and over-long sentences, and keeps every step of Aristotle's argument, every definition, qualification, example, name and quoted line. It does not explain the book and it does not make a compressed, difficult treatise easy. Do not reword a sentence that is already clear just to lower the similarity score.

## Voice and sentences

- Aristotle writes as a lecturer: compact, definitional, moving step by step ("Now...", "Again...", "Hence...", "For..."). Keep that. The register is clear, direct, slightly formal modern prose. No contractions.
- Break a long sentence only where the logic allows, and keep the connectives (for, hence, therefore, but, whereas, again, since) so the steps stay visible. Keep "I mean", "I call", "we say", "let us" where he defines or announces; do not turn them into impersonal statements.
- Replace Butcher's period words with natural modern ones ("it is to be observed", "whereas", "inasmuch as", "hath", "doth", "unto"). Keep the numbered or "first... second... third..." order of lists exactly.
- Do not smooth away his deliberate repetition of a term (see the table).

## Force

Keep hedges and judgments exactly as strong or mild as served ("perhaps", "it would seem", "as it appears", "must", "should", "generally", "the best", "the least artistic"). Do not soften a rule into advice or sharpen a doubt into a law. A "should" stays "should"; a "must" stays "must".

## No additions

No commentary, notes, headings, glosses, Greek words, scholarly framing or modern comparisons. A period reference may be clarified only inside the sentence, in a few words, where the text supports it. Never add the word "catharsis", "hamartia", "mimesis", "tragic flaw", "denouement" (except where served), or any other scholarly label that is not in the served text.

## Structure 1:1

26 chapters, 130 paragraphs, with chapter numbers and `title` fields exactly as served (including "Chapter N — ..."). Paragraph counts per chapter: 7, 2, 3, 6, 4, 10, 3, 1, 3, 2, 2, 2, 4, 5, 4, 6, 4, 5, 2, 8, 9, 7, 1, 8, 17, 5. Output paragraph N renders source paragraph N and opens with equivalent content. Never merge, split, reorder, drop or invent a paragraph. Output length is normally at least 80% of the source paragraph's words. The live modern-en is a convenience only: keep a good rendering, fix drift, and fully render any paragraph it left verbatim.

## Technical terms: one rendering, kept stable

| Served term | Fixed rendering and rule |
|---|---|
| imitation, imitate, imitator | Keep "imitation" / "imitate" / "imitator" everywhere. Never "representation", "mimicry", "depiction" or "copy" as a replacement. Butcher's own "represent" and "representation" (e.g. "imitate and represent", "Tragedy is an imitation... of life", "the representation of character") stay as served and are not interchanged with "imitate". |
| Plot | Keep "plot" (Butcher defines it: "the arrangement of the incidents", ch6). Never "story", "narrative", "fable" or "myth". "The Plot... is the first principle, and as it were the soul of a tragedy" keeps "first principle" and "soul" exactly; "the end of a tragedy" keeps "end". "Incidents" stays "incidents" ("structure of the incidents"). |
| Character | Keep "character" (what makes agents have certain qualities); "characters" for the persons. Never "personality" or "characterization". "Moral purpose" and "ethical" stay as served. |
| Thought | Keep "Thought" / "thought" (the faculty of saying what is possible and pertinent, ch6; the effects produced by speech, ch19). Never "reasoning", "theme" or "idea" for the part. |
| Diction | Keep "Diction". Butcher's definition ("the mere metrical arrangement of the words", ch6) stays. Never "style", "vocabulary" or "language" for the part. "Language" stays "language" wherever he wrote it (e.g. "language embellished", ch6). |
| Spectacle, Spectacular | Keep "Spectacle" and "spectacular" ("spectacular equipment", "the spectacular element"). Never "staging", "visual effects" or "production values". |
| Song | Keep "Song" for the part of Tragedy; "Choric song", "songs of actors" stay. Never "music", "melody" or "lyrics" for it. Where he says "music of the flute and of the lyre" (ch1), keep "music". |
| The six parts | Order and capitalization as served: Plot, Character, Diction, Thought, Spectacle, Song. |
| definition of tragedy (ch6) | "Tragedy is an imitation of an action that is serious, complete, and of a certain magnitude; in language embellished with each kind of artistic ornament...; in the form of action, not of narrative; through pity and fear effecting the proper purgation of these emotions." Keep every clause, in this order, and keep "serious, complete, and of a certain magnitude", "embellished", "artistic ornament", "action" vs "narrative". Only syntax and archaic words around these may change. Its explanatory sentences ("By 'language embellished' I mean...") stay with their quotation marks. |
| pity and fear | Always "pity" and "fear", in that order. Never "compassion", "terror", "horror" or "sympathy". |
| purgation | Keep Butcher's "purgation" ("the proper purgation of these emotions"). Never "catharsis", "purification", "cleansing", "release" or "emotional discharge". No Greek word is added anywhere. |
| error or frailty (ch13) | Keep "error or frailty" and "great error or frailty" (also "vice or depravity" as the contrast). Never "tragic flaw", "mistake", "weakness", "hamartia" or "sin". Elsewhere "error" stays "error" in its own sense (the critics' "error", ch25). |
| Reversal of the Situation | Keep this full phrase with capitals; "Reversal" alone only where served. The single served "Peripeteia or Reversal of the Situation" (ch6) keeps both words; do not introduce "Peripeteia" anywhere else. |
| Recognition | Keep "Recognition" (capital where served as a part), "recognition" for the act. Never "discovery" or "realization". "Scene of Suffering" stays as served. |
| Complication and Unravelling | Keep "Complication" and "Unraveling" (American spelling). The served "Unravelling or Denouement" keeps both words; "tie the knot... unravel it" stays. "Denouement" is not used elsewhere. |
| Simple, Complex (plots) | Keep as capitalized in the served definitions (ch10, ch18); likewise "the Pathetic", "the Ethical" and "the Simple" of ch18. |
| Epic, Tragedy, Comedy, Dithyrambic | Keep the served capitalization pattern; "Epic poetry", "Epic poem", "Epic canon" stay. "Epic" is never "epic poem" unless served so. The comparison (ch24-26: unity, length, meter, spectacle, "which is higher") keeps every point and the superiority conclusion as served. |
| magnitude, probability or necessity | Keep "magnitude" and "probability or necessity", always in that order. |
| Deus ex Machina | Keep as served, with its quotation marks. |

## Quotations and citations

One decision: quoted verse lines, titles and Butcher's quoted terms (in single quotation marks) stay in the served wording, with only typography and spelling normalized. Aristotle's lines from Homer and the tragedians (e.g. 'Sing, goddess, of the wrath', 'Upright upon their butt-ends stood the spears') are not paraphrased and not re-metered. Everything around a quotation is modernized. Words he defines or discusses in quotation marks ('harmony', 'language embellished', 'doing') keep both the word and its quotation marks. There are no footnotes or citation data in this edition; add none.

## Notes, markers and special elements

- Three whole paragraphs are bracketed editorial links: ch12 p0, ch20 p0 and ch21 p8. Keep the opening and closing square brackets; modernize the text inside. Keep the lists inside them in served order (Prologue, Episode, Exode, Choric song, Parode, Stasimon, Commoi; Letter, Syllable, Connecting word, Noun, Verb, Inflexion or Case, Sentence or Phrase).
- Angle-bracketed text (<...>) is Butcher's restoration of a damaged passage (ch14 p3, ch16 p4, ch18 p1, ch25 p1, ch26 p2). Keep the angle brackets and the words within them, exactly around the same stretch of text. Do not drop or "complete" them.
- Parenthetical clarifications and explanatory remarks by Butcher, in round brackets, stay as served and in place.
- Greek in the source appears as transliterated letter-names inside braces ({kappa omega mu alpha iota}, {gamma eta}) in ch3, ch20, ch21, ch22, and as lowercase names in round brackets in ch3. Keep each brace group character for character; never convert it to Greek or Latin letters, never translate or add to it. The surrounding gloss ("to revel", "doing", "village") is modernized.

## Names and spellings

Keep every name and title exactly as served: Oedipus, Thyestes, Iphigenia, Medea, Homer, Sophocles, Euripides, Aeschylus, Agathon, Polygnotus, Aristophanes, Epicharmus, Theodectes, Heracles, Odysseus, Achilles, Hector, the *Iliad*, the *Odyssey*, the *Margites*, Lynceus, Phorcides, Prometheus, the Dorians, Megarians, Massilian, and the rest. Play and poem titles stay as printed and in the forms served (e.g. "the Oedipus", "the Odysseus Disguised as a Messenger"). Examples naming plays and poets are never replaced, shortened or updated. Spelling: American, as in the live modern-en (color, recognize, honor, "any one" stays as served).

## Typography

Match the live modern-en: straight single quotes ' for quotation and for the apostrophe (the source has no double quotes; add none); a spaced em dash ` — ` where Butcher has `--` or `,--`; no `--` remaining; no ellipses except the three the source has (keep "..." as served); no newline inside a paragraph; one space after a colon or period; contractions none. Repair the source's stray punctuation silently: "one: another" becomes "one another", "emotional: interest" becomes "emotional interest", "finish: of" becomes "finish of", and an unpaired ">" after a quotation ("... Father Zeus>.") is dropped.

## Things that must not happen

1. "Catharsis", "purification", "mimesis", "hamartia", "tragic flaw" or any outside label introduced.
2. "Plot" rendered "story", "narrative" or "myth"; "pity and fear" rendered "compassion and terror".
3. Any one of the six parts renamed (Spectacle to "staging", Song to "music", Diction to "style").
4. Plays, poets, characters and quoted verse lines altered, dropped or replaced.
5. A bracketed paragraph losing its brackets, angle brackets removed, or brace-Greek converted or invented.
6. Aristotle's "should" and "must" judgments about the best form of tragedy (ch13-14) or the higher art (ch26) softened or sharpened.
7. A paragraph merged, split, reordered or given a heading or note.

While Gilgamish tells his vision in this way, Enkidu sits before the hetaera. As she bares her breasts and shows her beauty (?), he forgets where he was born. So Enkidu spends six days and seven nights with the courtesan, lingering in their lovemaking.
Then the nymph began speaking to Enkidu: “As I look at you, Enkidu, I see that you shall be like a god. Why do you keep roaming the desert among the wild beasts? Rise, and I will take you to Erech with its broad markets, to the sacred Temple where Anu dwells. Come, Enkidu; let me guide you to E-Anna, Anu’s home. Gilgamish lives there, the foremost being in creation. You will embrace him as you would a woman, yes, and love him as much as yourself. Come, lift yourself up off the ground. It is only a shepherd’s sleeping place.”
He heard what she said and welcomed her guidance; her counsel touched him deeply. Taking off one of her garments, she dressed him in it and kept the other on herself. She led him, his hand in hers as a brother’s would be, to the shepherds’ huts (?) beside their sheepfolds. On seeing him, the shepherds came together . . .

Until then he had been accustomed to suck milk from wild creatures! He broke the bread she placed in front of him, yet looked at it and stared. Enkidu did not understand how to eat bread, and he had never learned how to drink mead!
The woman answered him: “Enkidu, try the bread. It is indeed a necessity for living. Drink the mead as well; people throughout this country commonly do so.”
Enkidu ate the bread until he had stuffed himself. He drank seven brimming cups of mead, and his spirits lifted. He felt joyful and triumphant, and his face brightened. He rubbed himself (?) and spread oil over the hair of his body. In this way he became human. He dressed in a garment to resemble a man, then took up his weapon. He hunted down the lions that troubled the shepherds at night and caught the jackals. With the lions under his control, the shepherds could sleep peacefully. Enkidu guarded them: he had become a man in his full strength.
One of the warriors now speaks to Gilgamish (?) . . .

While enjoying himself, he raised his eyes and noticed the man. He said to the woman, “Mistress, bring this fellow to me. What has he come for? I want to find out his purpose.”
The woman called the man over so that Enkidu could see him. “Sir, why are you searching? Tell me, which way leads to the place where you are staying?”
The man addressed Enkidu: “Gilgamish summons you to the House of Community. This follows the custom of men and pays honour to the mighty. Come and pile up the offerings owed to the city. Come, bring the city’s provisions for the benefit of everyone. The king of Erech with its broad markets is to receive your greeting; Gilgamish, king of that broad-marketed city, is to receive it. First he sleeps with the woman assigned by fate. Afterwards, following divine counsel and the forms of the omens, he speaks the pronouncement of his destiny.” At the fellow’s words they went ahead of him.

Enkidu entered Erech and its broad markets first, with the courtesan following him. The townspeople collected behind him. When he halted in the street of broad-marketed Erech, those crowding behind cried out, “He really does resemble Gilgamish. He is a little shorter, but more strongly built. . . . once he sucked wild creatures’ milk like a helpless infant! Erech’s bread-cakes always bring a man to splendid maturity! A mere savage becomes a warrior with a fitting appearance. Now his build is the equal of godlike Gilgamish’s.”
The bed is spread for the rites of love. Gilgamish comes at night to sleep and take pleasure in the woman. But Enkidu comes into the roadway and stands across his path, menacing Gilgamish with his strength . . .

Gilgamish . . . behind him . . . His anger swelled, and he charged to attack. They confronted one another in the roadway. Enkidu planted his foot against the door and refused to let Gilgamish enter. They wrestled and snorted (?) like bulls, breaking the threshold. The very wall shook as Gilgamish and Enkidu grappled. Like bulls they snorted (?) and smashed the threshold, and the wall itself trembled.
Gilgamish lowered his leg to the ground. His fury subsided, and his ardour was subdued. As soon as that ardour had settled, Enkidu spoke to him: “Your mother truly bore you as a unique son, the one and only. Nin-sun, that finest cow of the cattle-folds, has raised your head higher than other warriors’, and Enlil has granted you the right to reign over men.”

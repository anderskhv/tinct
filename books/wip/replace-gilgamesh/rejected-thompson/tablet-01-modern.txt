The Immortals listened to the appeal of their cries. The heavenly gods spoke to Anu, the lord of Erech: “You have fathered a son who is altogether a tyrant, his head rearing like an aurochs’. No one can match the impact of his weapons. His fellows are driven into the snares, while Erech’s warriors cower, un- . . . Gilgamish allows no father to keep his son. Day after day, night after night, his arrogance increases. Yes, he is the shepherd of Erech with its high walls; he is their shepherd . . . domineering, powerful, crafty . . . Gilgamish leaves no girl with her mother, no daughter with a warrior, nor any wife with her husband.”
Anu heard the appeal of their lamentation and summoned the lady Aruru. “Aruru, you were the one who fashioned the first seed of humanity (?). Now fashion its counterpart, so that he may encounter Gilgamish, yes, on the day of his pleasure. Let the two contend against each other, and let him bring Erech some relief.”
On hearing this, the goddess Aruru immediately shaped Anu’s idea in her mind. She washed her hands, then worked some clay with her fingers and moulded it out in the desert. There she made Enkidu, a warrior, as though born and begotten, yes, a second Ninurta. Hair grew out over his whole body. He bound his locks in a band as a woman does. His hair sprang up thickly like the bristles of barley. He knew neither a people nor a country, and his clothing was like Sumuqan’s. He grazed on plants beside the gazelles, drank his fill alongside the cattle, and took pleasure in the water together with the beasts.
Then a hunter who set traps came face to face with him. He encountered him at the animals’ watering place on one day, then a second, then a third. At the sight of him, terror spread over the hunter’s face. He and his cattle returned to his homestead, alarmed and frightened. He cried out, his heart troubled and his expression dark . . . distress in his belly . . . Yes, his face looked like that of someone who had travelled a great distance.
The hunter began to speak to his father. “Father, an immense fellow has come out of the mountains. Oh, his strength surpasses everyone’s throughout the country. His enormous power makes him like another Anu himself. Always (?) he roams freely over the mountains; always he grazes with the cattle and makes his way to the water. I am afraid to go near him. He has filled in the pits I dug with my own hands and ripped up the traps I laid. He has helped all the cattle, the wild creatures of the desert, get away from my grasp. He will not let me practise my skills as a hunter.”
His father spoke in reply to the hunter. “My son, Gilgamish lives in Erech, and no one has ever defeated him. His strength is the greatest anywhere in the country. His vast power is like that of a second Anu himself. Go towards Erech. When he hears about this monster, he will say, ‘Hunter, go and take a courtesan, a hetaera, with you . . . like a strong one. When he brings the cattle together again at their watering place, let her remove her cloak and show her lovely beauty. He will see her, and surely he will hold her in his arms. From that moment the cattle reared in his own deserts will reject him.’”
The hunter listened to his father’s advice and at once set off for Gilgamish. He took the road to Erech, came before Gilgamish and addressed him: “An enormous fellow has emerged from the mountains. Oh, no one in the whole country can rival his strength. His tremendous power is like that of another Anu himself. Always (?) he wanders over the mountains at will, grazes on plants with the cattle, and goes to the water. I am afraid to approach him. He has filled back in the pits I dug with my own hands and torn up the traps I set. All the cattle, the creatures of the desert, he has helped escape from my clutches. He prevents me from carrying on my work as a hunter.”
Gilgamish gave the hunter this answer: “My good hunter, go and bring a courtesan, a hetaera, along with you. When he gathers the cattle at their watering place once more, she must take off her cloak and reveal her beautiful body. He will catch sight of her and surely take her in his arms. Then the cattle that grew up in his very own deserts will immediately refuse to have him among them.”
The hunter set out, taking a courtesan, a hetaera, along. Together they began their travels and went on their journey. After three days they reached the pleasant place they had chosen. The hunter and the hetaera sat down in hiding (?). For a day, then a second day, they waited beside the place where the animals drank. At last the cattle arrived and began drinking their fill.
The animals came there to enjoy the water. Enkidu was among them, the man born of the mountains. He grazed on plants with the gazelles and drank all he wanted with the cattle, taking pleasure in the water beside the other beasts. The courtesan saw him, that huge, vigorous fellow, a completely wild creature from the desert’s depths! “Girl, that is him! Uncover your beauty and show him how lovely you are, so he can possess you. Do not feel shy at all. Captivate his soul. As soon as he sees you he will certainly come towards you. Then loosen your cloak, let him embrace you, and use a woman’s arts on him. The animals born in his desert will immediately reject him, because he has held you against his chest.”
The girl bared her breasts and displayed her beauty so that he could possess her. She felt no shyness: she captivated his soul and loosened her cloak. He embraced her, and she used a woman’s arts as he held her against his breast. In this way Enkidu spent six days and seven nights lingering with the courtesan, making love to her.
At last satisfied with her charms, he turned towards his cattle. Oh, how the gazelles ran off the moment they noticed him! The desert animals fled from him, yes, from Enkidu! He had lost his innocence. As the cattle ran away, his knees gave out, and he could no longer run as swiftly as before. Yet through this he had reached his full maturity and gained a wider understanding.
He sat down once more at the woman’s feet. She studied his face, and he listened to what she said. “Enkidu, you are handsome; you shall be just like a god. Why do you keep wandering through the desert with the wild animals? Get up! I will take you to high-walled Erech, to the sacred Temple, the home of Anu and Ishtar. There Gilgamish holds the greatest power and overcomes men as an aurochs does.”
Her advice pleased him as soon as he heard it, for he felt his longing to find a companion. He answered the courtesan: “Then come, girl, invite me into that holy, sacred Temple, into the dwelling of Anu and Ishtar. There Gilgamish has supreme power and dominates men like an aurochs. But I too, I myself, will call him out. I will challenge him fearlessly and shout through Erech, ‘I have strength too!’ Yes, I myself will change even destiny. The one born in the desert truly has the greatest vigour! . . . I will give you pleasure . . . whatever is there, I want to know it.”
“Then come to Erech, Enkidu, the city with high walls. Its people dress in splendid festival clothes, and every day is a celebration. Eunuch-priests strike their cymbals, and dancing-girls . . . carried away by their merriment and desire, happily keeping the noblemen from bed! Enkidu, you shall savour all the joy your life can hold. I will show you a happy man, Gilgamish! Look at him, look at his face and how handsome he is in his manhood! He is full of vitality; power fills his entire body. His vigour exceeds yours, and he is restless through every day and night! Enkidu, curb your pride. Shamash loves Gilgamish, and Anu, Enlil and Ea have generously endowed him with wisdom. Before you ever left your mountains, Gilgamish had already seen you in a dream in Erech. He went to his mother to tell her what he had dreamed. ‘Mother, I saw a vision during the night. There were stars in heaven; then something resembling Anu himself fell onto my shoulders. I tried to lift him, but he was too strong for me. I loosened his grip, yet I could not shake him off. Meanwhile the people of Erech stood around him. Craftsmen pressed in from behind, and warriors crowded round. My own companions kissed his feet. I held him to my breast as I would a woman. Then I brought him down before your feet so that you could regard him as my equal.’ She who has all wisdom answered her lord; she who has all wisdom replied to Gilgamish: ‘The heavenly stars stand for your companions. That being like Anu himself fell on your shoulders. You tried to lift him and found him too powerful; you freed yourself from his grip, yet could not shake him off. Then you brought him to my feet so that I could acknowledge him as your equal, and held him to your breast as you would a woman. This is a brave man, a friend prepared to support a companion. Because you held him against your breast as you would a woman, the dream shows that you are the person he will never desert. That is what your dream means.’ He spoke to his mother again: ‘Mother, I saw another dream. An axe came hurtling into high-walled Erech, and people assembled around it. People from Erech stood about it; crowds thronged in front of it, with craftsmen pushing behind.

They stood looking at the forest and the towering cedars, studying the avenue that led among the trees. Where Humbaba strode, a path ran straight; his tracks made a good way through. They also saw the Cedar Mountain, dwelling of the Immortals and sanctuary of Irnini. The Cedar raised its proud height against the mountain. Its lovely shade was full of delight, and bushes spread around it. Spread also the . . . the Cedar, the incense . . .
(After several damaged lines the column breaks off. About twenty lines at the top of the second column are badly damaged; its lower portion is better preserved and begins with visions given to the warrior.)
“Companion, a further dream came to me. This second vision was indeed pleasant to see. We two (?) were standing together high on (?) a mountain summit, when the peak fell over. It left the two of us (?) resembling . . . (?) that are born in the desert.”
Enkidu spoke to his companion to explain the dream (?): “My companion, that vision of yours foretells good luck for us. Yes, you have seen a dream of great advantage. Consider it, my friend: surely the mountain you saw must represent Humbaba (?). It means we shall take him captive and cast down his body, leaving his corpse humbled on the ground. I will show you what tomorrow brings.”
At forty leagues they stopped to eat a little food. At sixty they rested, digging a hollow in the sunlight . . . Gilgamish went up above it . . . and poured out his grain-meal as an offering to the mountain. “Mountain, send a dream . . . breathe upon him . . .”
The mountain gave a dream . . . breathed on him . . . A cold blast of wind rose, and a gust passed over . . . making him crouch in fear, and . . . he swayed like mountain grain . . .
Gilgamish squatted with his knees bent, resting his haunches, and the sleep that comes to human beings descended upon him. At midnight he woke abruptly and hurried to speak to his companion. “My friend, did you call me? Why have I awakened? Did you touch me? Why am I frightened (?)—has a spirit gone past me? Why does my whole body tremble? Companion, I have seen a third dream, and this one was altogether terrible. The sky roared loudly, and the earth echoed in reply. Day grew gloomy, darkness rose, and lightning flashed. Flames caught fire, and Pestilence (?) was there, full to overflowing; Death had eaten its fill! Then the brightness died away, and the fires faded. Burning pieces fell and became ash. Come down into the desert with me, so we can take counsel together.”
Enkidu now begins telling Gilgamish the meaning of his dream . . .

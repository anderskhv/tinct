# Coriolanus — PLAY-NOTE (supplements SHAKESPEARE-BRIEF.md; this note wins on play-specific points)

Source: 29 chapters (scenes, Act 1 sc.1 to Act 5 sc.6). Ids below are chapter:paragraph (0-based paragraph index, same as the slices).

## 1. Speaker prefixes and names inside speeches

Copy prefixes exactly. Keep each name form as the source uses it; never merge "Martius" and "Coriolanus".

| Prefix (source) | Who / how to name inside speeches |
|---|---|
| MARTIUS (1:51 to 9:8) | Caius Martius, before the title. Others say "Martius", "Caius Martius". Never "Marcius". |
| CORIOLANUS (9:14 on) | Same man, after the name "Coriolanus" is given at Corioles. Others say "Coriolanus"; Aufidius, Menenius and the women still say "Martius" at times (21:58, 26) — keep as written. |
| MENENIUS | Menenius Agrippa, patrician friend and "father" figure; he is not a tribune or consul. |
| COMINIUS | Cominius, the consul and general; "the General". |
| LARTIUS (and 2 x TITUS LARTIUS) | Titus Lartius, Roman commander; use "Lartius" or "Titus Lartius" as the source does. |
| VOLUMNIA | Coriolanus's mother; he says "mother", she says "son" / "boy". |
| VIRGILIA | His wife ("gracious silence", 11:32 ff). |
| VALERIA | Roman lady, friend of Virgilia. |
| AUFIDIUS | Tullus Aufidius, Volscian general; "Tullus" in direct address. |
| SICINIUS, BRUTUS | The two tribunes (Sicinius Velutus, Junius Brutus). Always "tribune(s)" in their title; BRUTUS is not the Julius Caesar Brutus. BOTH TRIBUNES / TRIBUNES also occur. |
| FIRST/SECOND/THIRD/FOURTH... CITIZEN, ALL, ALL PLEBEIANS, ALL CITIZENS, CITIZEN | Roman commoners. "Citizens" stays "citizens"; do not turn into "people" except where the source says people. |
| FIRST/SECOND SENATOR, SENATORS, PATRICIAN(S), AEDILE(S) | Patrician side; the Aediles are the tribunes' officers. |
| VOLSCE, FIRST/SECOND/THIRD LORD, CONSPIRATORS, FIRST-THIRD SERVINGMAN, WATCH, ROMAN(S), OFFICER(S), MESSENGER, SOLDIER, LIEUTENANT, HERALD, GENTLEWOMAN, YOUNG MARTIUS | As named; "Lords" are Volscian lords in Act 5. |

Quirk: ALL PEOPLE at 29:45 has no period after the prefix in the source (the live edition added one). Copy the source exactly, then follow the validator.

## 2. Fixed period terms (use identically throughout)

- patricians (the nobles), plebeians (commoners; keep "plebeians" where used), tribunes (of the people, officers who defend the commoners; "the people's tribunes"), aediles, the Senate, the Capitol (Rome's hill and senate house; keep "the Capitol"), the Forum, the Tarpeian rock (execution cliff; keep, add "the cliff" if needed), consul, consulship.
- voices = votes, always. "Your voices" = "your votes". Where it also means speech (12:27 "I shall lack voice"; 13:41, 13:51), let the reader see both ("votes", with "voice" kept where speaking is meant).
- gown of humility (13:12, 13:13): the plain worn gown a candidate wore to ask for votes; keep the phrase "gown of humility", gloss as "plain gown of a humble candidate" once. "Napless vesture of humility" (11:96) is the same garment.
- the belly fable (1:32 to 1:46): keep as "the belly" and "the body's parts"; Menenius makes Rome's senators the belly.
- Corioles (spelled so everywhere in this edition; city of the Volsces), Antium (Aufidius's city), the Volsces / Volscians / Volscian (use each as the source does), Rome, the Tiber, Tarquin (the exiled king).
- Titles: "consul", "general", "Lord", "noble", "worthy". Keep "Hail" and "noble Martius" recognizable.
- Mars, Jupiter, Juno ("jealous queen of heaven"), Hercules, Hector: keep names.

## 3. Hardest passages

- 1:11 First Citizen's dense prose on the poor and the rich ("What authority surfeits on...", "pikes" and "rakes"): unpack, keep colloquial tone.
- 1:32 to 1:46 Menenius's fable of the belly: keep the body-part images (head, eye, heart, arm, tongue) and the belly's reply ("cupboard", "storehouse", "shop"); say clearly who is who in 1:46; the citizens interrupt mid-sentence-fragments (1:35, 1:37).
- 1:53, 1:59 Martius's scorn: insults ("curs", "scabs", hares, geese) stay harsh. 1:59 "Five tribunes": name the figures.
- 3:1 and 3:9 Volumnia on honour and wounds (Hecuba, Hector): keep her violent pride, do not soften.
- 11:25, 11:27, 11:29 Menenius: his joking self-description and insults to the tribunes ("humorous patrician" = a moody, odd-tempered one); keep the wit.
- 11:90 and 11:96 Brutus's mocking account of the crowd's adoration; "napless vesture" here.
- 12:27 Cominius's praise speech (331 words): long periodic sentences; split heavily; keep each deed.
- 12:38 to 12:40 Coriolanus refuses the gown and the showing of wounds; Menenius's coaxing.
- 13:3 to 13:13 the citizens' prose on whether to give their voices, with 13:51 ("Most sweet voices!... wolvish toge") and 13:53 ("Here come more voices"): sarcasm must show; the citizens are simple but not stupid; "toge" is the gown, "wolvish" means wolf-like.
- 13:86 and 13:97 the tribunes coach the citizens to withdraw their votes: the tribune's craft in plain words.
- 14:53 and 14:55 "Triton of the minnows", "Hydra" and "shall": name the images (a sea-god over tiny fish; many-headed monster); keep "shall" as the word he spits at.
- 14:61 to 14:64 Coriolanus on mixing the people into government; the oath at 14:64. Argument must stay stepwise.
- 15:26 to 15:40 Volumnia persuades him to the people: "bonnet in thy hand", "mildly"; keep her argument and his "unbarbed sconce" (bare head, 15:37) reluctance.
- 16:62 the banishment curse: "You common cry of curs... There is a world elsewhere." Keep the end line recognizable ("There is a world elsewhere"); curs and the rotten fens stay.
- 17:5 and 18:34 farewell speeches (Hercules; Volumnia's curse on the gods' leisure).
- 21:57 and 21:58 Coriolanus reveals himself to Aufidius; Aufidius's welcome (274 words): keep the lover-like imagery of reunion, the "bride" and the "wars" images.
- 23:7 Aufidius on his rival; 26:7 to 26:13 the family approaches (kiss, "virgined"); 26:36 and 26:41 Volumnia's plea (340 words): the women's plea; keep the choice she puts (mother or country), "tread upon thy mother's womb"; do not summarize.
- 29:14, 29:37, 29:42 Aufidius's final accusation, "boy of tears", Coriolanus's reply ("Cut me to pieces, Volsces... Boy?"; eagle in the dove-cote image).

## 4. Oddities in the served text

- Split speeches: a paragraph with only a prefix ("FIRST SENATOR.", "MARTIUS.", "CORIOLANUS.", "COMINIUS.", "VIRGILIA.", "AUFIDIUS.") is followed by a stage-direction paragraph, then an UNPREFIXED paragraph carrying the words (e.g. 1:81 to 1:83, 4:5 to 4:6, 11:82 to 11:84, 14:77 to 14:80, 21:49 to 21:51, 26:51 to 26:53). Keep the prefix-only paragraph as is (do not merge or fill). Render the unprefixed paragraph without adding a prefix.
- Other unprefixed paragraphs are speech continuations after a direction, with the speaker the one before (e.g. 1:50, 11:84, 26:13, 29:3). Do not add a prefix.
- The source writes directions as `[_To Messenger_.]` (period outside the underscore). Copy exactly. The LIVE modern edition normalized these to `[To Messenger.]`, bracketed bare directions (e.g. 1:0 "[A company ... enters ...]") and changed wording ("[He kneels.]"). Do not follow the live edition on this: the brief says copy verbatim.
- Bare stage directions without brackets (1:0, 7:0, 11:89, 14:0, 21:0, 21:62) are copied verbatim.
- Joint prefixes occur: "VALERIA, VIRGILIA.", "FIRST and SECOND SERVINGMAN."; copy as is.
- The source uses curly quotes and apostrophes; write straight ones in the output.
- Mixed verse and prose in single paragraphs; the citizens and the servingmen (ch. 21) are prose, so keep them colloquial.

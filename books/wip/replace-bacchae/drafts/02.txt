Chorus. From the soil of Asia, far across Tmolus' sacred ridges, I hasten to the work I love, the labour that brings me no pain, serving the King of Clamour and singing my welcome to the Bacchanals' God.

Chorus. Who is blocking the road? Who is still inside the house? Come out! Let everyone make their lips holy by closing them to irreverent speech. Now I will sing his praises, sounding the hymn that Dionysus himself commands.

Chorus. Happy the person granted the blessing of instruction in heaven's mysteries, whose life is pure and whose soul is swept by the ever-wakeful sacred dance! Holy cleansing fits him to join the Bacchanals racing over the mountains and the secret ceremonies of Cybelê, the ancient Mother. Crowned with sprays of ivy, he lifts his thyrsus high and sings to honour the God of the Vine. Come, you Bacchanals, come! Bring the King of Clamour, a God's child who walked the Phrygian mountains, home to the broad roads of Hellas, bring him home!

Chorus. His mother bore this God as she died. The irresistible pain of childbirth tore her, carrying her towards death on the wings of Zeus's descending thunder. He was born before his time as her life went out, struck by the falling flame. But Zeus Kronion received his child into another chamber of birth. Hidden in a fold of his thigh, fastened shut with golden clasps, the baby lay concealed from Hera's gaze until the day appointed by the Fates. Then Zeus brought forth a God with the horns of a bull and wound snakes through his hair. That is why his Maenads, too, wear serpents among their locks.

Chorus. Thebes, the town that nursed Semelê, put an ivy crown upon your forehead. Blossom inside the rich green bryony with its star-shaped flowers, and weave oak and pine among your hair in the rapture of Bacchus. Trim your spotted fawnskin with shining white wool, hanging in silvery tassels. Servants of Bacchus, swing your wild wands high! The whole country will become a single company of dancers when the King of Clamour leads his revellers into the mountains, the mountains where your women wait for him, driven from their shuttles and looms by the Vine-god who pursued them in their frenzied flight.

Chorus. O cave that echoed with the Curetes' songs, chamber where the infant Zeus was born! There the Corybants, their helmet-crests flashing as they danced through the dark underground halls, discovered this timbrel with its circle of stretched hide that our hands beat. They joined its Bacchic joy to the sweet, high call of Phrygian flutes and placed its thunder in Rhea's hands, lifting it above the roar of celebration. But the gift passed from the Mother to the raving band of Satyrs. Those wild woodland creatures gave Semelê's child the worship he delights in, joining the clash of timbrels to the flash of white dancing feet at every third year's festival.

Chorus. What a transport of delight when someone swerves away from the Bacchanals racing through the mountains and sinks down on the ground, covered by the holy garment of a spotted fawnskin! Wearing it, he has hunted the goat to death for its blood, for a taste of the steaming raw feast, while wild feet hurry across the hills of Lydia and Phrygia. The King of Clamour leads us, thrilling our hearts with his cry of "Evoë!" The earth streams with milk, with wine, with the nectar of bees, and smoke rises like the incense of Araby. The Bacchanal raises his flaming pine torch and sweeps it through the air, shouting to the scattered revellers as fire pours from its tip. On he runs, on he dances, shaking his curls loose to the wind, and his voice cuts clearly through the roaring company: "Forward, you Bacchanals! Forward, Bacchanal girls, glory of Tmolus with its springs of gold. Join your singing voices to the thunder of the timbrels. Ring out the happy God's happy praises in Phrygian cries and song, while that fountain of melody rises, the sacred flutes pouring their music far and wide, their notes keeping time with the feet that climb the pilgrim road into the mountains!" Enraptured, the Bacchanal woman races onwards, her feet skipping swiftly like foals gambolling about mares that graze in the meadows. (Teiresias enters.)

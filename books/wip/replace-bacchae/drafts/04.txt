Chorus. O Holiness, ruling even over the Gods, yet bending your golden wing down towards us on the earth, do you hear the king's words? Do you hear Pentheus blasting his defiance against the King of Clamour, his blasphemous abuse of Semelê's son, foremost of the blessed Gods at the beautiful feast? It is his special privilege to call our feet into the dance, where light laughter mingles with flutes and the children of sorrow forget their tears. Whenever the glory of the grape appears at the banquet people offer to the high Gods, the wine bowl spreads the cloak of sleep over ivy-crowned revellers drinking deeply.

Chorus. Unbridled mouths that acknowledge no restraint and stubborn folly that strays beyond the bounds of law share a single end: disaster. But a life as peaceful as a summer day, and footsteps guided by good judgement, stand firm and steady; they remain the support of the household. Though the heavenly beings live far away, they look down on humanity from their cloudy doorways. Knowledge cannot purchase wisdom. The spirit that rises beyond a mortal's proper height has few days to live. Anyone who grasps at things beyond human reach will lose even the blessings already secured. Such paths, it seems to me, belong to wandering, witless madness.

Chorus. If only I could escape to Aphroditê's home in Cyprus, that island paradise surrounded by the sea, where the Love-gods weave their powerful enchantments over the soul; or to the fields of uncountable golden harvests, watered by the seven-mouthed river, where rain has never fallen! Better still, to the land of incomparable beauty, Pieria, where the Muses make their home and sing! Lead me there, King of Clamour, to stand without fear on holy Olympus! God of Revels, guide me to the dwelling of the Graces and Desire, where nobody forbids the Bacchanals to dance in their circle.

Chorus. Our God, Zeus's offspring, delights in the happy feast where his cups gleam. He loves Peace, the giver of riches, the divine mother who nurses Youth. His generous gift of wine brings joy that ends sorrow to the lowly and the great alike. But he hates the person who scorns a life whose days and nights fly on the wings of happiness and refuses to choose its blessings. Wisdom tells us to keep our distance from excessive cleverness. For me the people's heartfelt faith, alive in their daily dealings in the marketplace, is enough. (Pentheus returns. A Servant and attendants enter with Dionysus in bonds.)

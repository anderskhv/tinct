Servant. Pentheus, we have returned with the prey you sent us to pursue; our mission succeeded. The wild creature proved tame. He made no dash for freedom, but willingly held out his hands. He did not turn pale; the rosy colour stayed in his cheeks. Smiling, he told us to tie him up and take him away, and waited there to make my job easy. Ashamed, I told him, "Stranger, I am taking you against my own wishes, under Pentheus' orders." The Bacchanals you captured and chained in the public prison have escaped their bonds and run into the meadows. There they dance and call upon the God Bromius. Their fetters fell apart by themselves; the doors unbarred themselves without a human hand. This man has certainly brought many wonders to Thebes! What happens next is for you to decide.

Pentheus. Untie his hands. Now he is caught in the net, he will not be quick enough to get away from me. Ha! You are certainly handsome enough to tempt women, stranger, which is why you have come to Thebes. Those flowing locks falling so seductively down your cheeks show you are no wrestler. Your carefully tended skin is white, untouched by sunlight, kept pale in the shade while you hunt for desire using beauty as your bait. First tell me which country you come from.

Dionysus. There is no need to boast; the answer is simple. Perhaps you know flower-covered Tmolus.

Pentheus. Yes; it surrounds the city where the Sardians live.

Dionysus. That is where I come from. My native country is Lydia.

Pentheus. Why have you brought these ceremonies into Hellas?

Dionysus. Zeus's son Dionysus initiated me into them.

Pentheus. Is there a Zeus over there fathering a new set of gods?

Dionysus. No, the very Zeus who married Semelê in this place.

Pentheus. Did you become his servant awake or in a dream?

Dionysus. We were face to face when he gave me his mysteries.

Pentheus. Well, what exactly are these mysteries like?

Dionysus. Only people who have been initiated may know.

Pentheus. What benefit do his worshippers gain from them?

Dionysus. You are forbidden to hear, though knowing would be worthwhile.

Pentheus. A clever sham to make me eager to hear more!

Dionysus. His ceremonies reject anyone who acts without reverence.

Pentheus. You saw the God: what did he look like?

Dionysus. Whatever he chose it to be. That was no decision of mine.

Pentheus. Another clever dodge; you have told me nothing again.

Dionysus. To foolish ears, a sensible answer sounds like nonsense.

Pentheus. Were you the first to bring his divine presence here?

Dionysus. The people of all Asia take part in these mystic dances.

Pentheus. Yes, and they have far less wisdom than the Hellenes.

Dionysus. In this they have much more. Their customs differ from yours.

Pentheus. Do you celebrate his ceremonies at night or during daylight?

Dionysus. Usually after dark; darkness deepens their solemnity.

Pentheus. And it sets traps of lust for women as well.

Dionysus. Schemes of lust can be planned in daylight too.

Pentheus. Your disgusting evasions will now earn their punishment.

Dionysus. As will your foolishness and your lack of reverence.

Pentheus. What a bold Bacchant, so skilful at fencing with words!

Dionysus. What sentence awaits me? How do you intend to take revenge?

Pentheus. I shall begin by shearing off those pretty curls of yours.

Dionysus. My hair is sacred; I have grown it for the God.

Pentheus. Next you will surrender the thyrsus you are holding in your hands.

Dionysus. Take it yourself; that staff belongs to Dionysus.

Pentheus. I shall keep your body locked inside my prison.

Dionysus. Whenever I choose, the God himself will set me free.

Pentheus. Yes, when you call on him among your Bacchanals!

Dionysus. Indeed, he is close by now, observing this insult.

Pentheus. Where, then? He certainly isn't visible to my eyes.

Dionysus. Here beside me. Your impiety keeps you from seeing him.

Pentheus. Arrest him! The man is making a mockery of me and Thebes.

Dionysus. Don't tie me up! That is reason giving folly a warning.

Pentheus. My order is to bind him; my authority outweighs yours.

Dionysus. You understand neither the life you lead nor who you truly are.

Pentheus. I am Pentheus, son of Agavê and Echion.

Dionysus. A fitting name for someone who will be pent up in misery.

Pentheus. Take him away! Lock him in the nearby horses' stalls, where he will see nothing but darkness. He can dance there! These women you brought as accomplices to your crimes I shall sell into slavery or put to work as my weavers. That will silence their hands, their clashing cymbals and their beating drums.

Dionysus. I am going. No fate that Fate has forbidden can ever reach me. Dionysus, whose existence you denied, will punish you for these insults. When you abuse me, it is he whom you drag into bondage. (Dionysus leaves under guard; Pentheus also leaves.)

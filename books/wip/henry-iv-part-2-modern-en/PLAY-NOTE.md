# Henry IV, Part 2 (henry-iv-part-2): play note

Supplements SHAKESPEARE-BRIEF.md. Where this note differs from the brief on a point specific to this play, this note wins. Ids below are chapter:paragraph (0-based, as in slices.json). Chapters are scenes 1-19 (ch 7 is the Boar's Head, 178 paragraphs; ch 9 is Shallow's house, 133).

COPY THE PREFIX EXACTLY FROM served-original-en.json, not from the live modern file. The validator checks against the source, so keep PRINCE. and KING.

## 2. Fixed terms

- sack: the wine, keep "sack"; also "sherris" in 12:34 (keep "sherry-sack" once, then "sack"). Malmsey-nose (4:14) = "wine-red nose".
- Bardolph's nose: the joke is its red glow. 7:141 "whose zeal burns in his nose": keep the burning image. The "red lattice" (5:26) is a tavern window; keep.
- vizard / visor: Davy's "William Visor" is a name; keep it (15:17).
- tester = sixpence (9:125); "pound", "mark", "angel" keep as coins.
- press and impress (conscription): Shallow's recruiting scene (ch 9). Render "press" as "draft" or "conscript"; "prick" a name on the list = "mark down".
- drawer = tavern waiter; keep "drawer" (the Prince plays on it, 7:124-125). Jordan (7:14) = chamber pot.
- ancient = ensign (standard-bearer). Corporal, captain: Bardolph and Pistol are mock-ranked.
- Eastcheap tavern: "the Boar's Head Tavern in Eastcheap", "Eastcheap".
- Rebels: the Archbishop of York, Lord Mowbray, Lord Hastings, Lord Bardolph, the Earl of Northumberland. Their field is Gaultree Forest. Royal side: Prince John of Lancaster and the Earl of Westmoreland. "Shrewsbury" (the battle), "Hotspur" and "Harry Percy" are the same man, Northumberland's dead son.
- "Jerusalem Chamber" keeps its name (ch 13 title). King's "nightgown" (8:0) is a dressing gown.
- Keep the Italian and Latin tags (7:66 "Si fortune me tormente, sperato me contento", "Samingo"), with a short plain gloss only where the line needs it.

## 3. Hardest passages

1. 1:34-35 Morton and Northumberland: the news of Hotspur's death; heavy periodic verse. Keep the simile chain; split sentences.
2. 2:21, 2:23-25 Falstaff and the Servant: quibbling on "honest", "knighthood aside", "leave to tell me so".
3. 2:60, 2:64 Falstaff to the Chief Justice: wit on his own age, "white head", "ill angel is light" (coin pun, 2:58), and the sly begging at 2:66.
4. 4:14, 4:34 Mistress Quickly: malapropisms ("infinitive" for infinite, "honeysuckle" for homicidal, "rampallian", "fustilarian", 4:23). Keep them as comic mistakes. Do not correct them silently.
5. 5:42-43 the letter read by Poins: one speech split across paragraphs, prose, comic "Japhet" and "King's poor cousin" jokes.
6. 7:61-69, 7:77 Pistol: bombast; stage-quotations and Marlowe tags ("Hiren", "Calipolis", "King Cerberus", "Atropos"); keep the roar, plain sense underneath.
7. 7:54, 7:91 Doll and the tavern bawdy (sack, "sweat", "ape"): keep the sexual edge; do not soften.
8. 7:122-141 the eavesdropping: Falstaff's insults of the disguised Prince; "zeal burns in his nose".
9. 8:3 the King's sleeplessness soliloquy ("O sleep, O gentle sleep") and 8:12 ("the book of fate"): dignified, plain words; the shipboy and the crown are the key images.
10. 9:9, 9:94-100, 9:126 Shallow's reminiscences: rambling prose, "Jane Nightwork", "bona-roba", "Clement's Inn". 9:131 Falstaff's long soliloquy on lying, with embedded stage directions (see section 4).
11. 10:16-17, 10:23-24 the Archbishop and Westmoreland: legal and political argument in verse; keep the steps visible.
12. 12:34 Falstaff on sherry and the cold blood of Prince John: the longest prose set piece (371 words); keep the logic of the medical joke.
13. 14:19 Hal speaking to the crown, 14:43-46 the King's death-bed (372, 348 words) and Hal's answer: central. Keep the "wish was father" image.
14. 16:25, 16:30-31 King Henry V and the Chief Justice: "this new and gorgeous garment"; his speech turns on "the balance and the sword".
15. 19:30 the rejection ("I know thee not, old man"): keep it bare and cold. 19:52-54 the Epilogue: the legs and the dancing pun ("use my legs", "dance out of your debt").

Prose and verse: Falstaff, the tavern, the recruits and Shallow are prose; kings, rebels and Warwick are verse. Prose stays loose and funny; verse speeches may run together but keep their rhythm and dignity.

## 4. Oddities in the served text

- There is NO Induction. The play opens at Act 1, Scene 1 with Lord Bardolph; Rumour does not appear as a speaker. The only "Rumour" in the text is an ordinary noun (8:17). There is no presenter paragraph to render.
- The Epilogue is the last chapter's last four paragraphs: 19:51 is the bare label "EPILOGUE." and 19:52-54 are speech paragraphs with no prefix. Copy "EPILOGUE." unchanged; render 52-54 as one speaker, a dancer or the author's voice. Note Falstaff's promised return and the Oldcastle remark (19:54).
- Speeches split by stage directions: the source often turns the rest of a speech into an unprefixed paragraph, and sometimes a bare "SPEAKER." paragraph precedes the speech (5:41, 7:13, 7:164, 7:171, 7:174, 15:30, 17:13, 17:24, 17:30, 17:44, 17:47, 17:66). Keep these shapes exactly. Unprefixed paragraphs stay unprefixed, and bare prefix paragraphs stay bare.
- Mid-speech paragraph breaks: 5:42-43, 7:165-166 and 17:45-46 break one sentence across paragraphs ("as / oft as", "Farewell, hostess; / farewell, Doll", "have done me / right."). Render each part so that it reads naturally on its own, e.g. 17:46 stays the single word "right." or a short equivalent.
- Embedded directions inside speeches (copy verbatim): 2:73, 4:53, 9:131 (two), 15:24 and 17:33 (these two are lowercase "[_to the Page_]"), 17:6.
- Whole-line stage directions without brackets ("Enter ...", "The Porter opens the gate.", "Trumpets sound, and the King and his train pass over the stage. ...") are paragraphs: copy verbatim.
- Italics: songs in ch 17 (17:7, 17:15, 17:26, 17:32, 17:49) are wholly wrapped in underscores; keep the underscores. Mixed song or direction paragraphs (7:14-15, 17:67) keep their markers. Note the odd placement "[_Reads_.]" with the period outside.
- Duplicate paragraph text is legitimate: 9:107 and 9:109 are the same line from Bardolph; the repeated "[_Exeunt._]" are fine. No damaged or missing paragraphs found; counts match the live edition in every scene.
- The live edition keeps stage directions without underscores and with plain brackets (e.g. "[Exit Porter.]" for "[_Exit Porter._]"); this brief says to copy verbatim from the source including underscores. Source form wins.
- Curly apostrophes are used in the source; output uses the straight apostrophe per the brief.

## Ambiguities: resolved or kept

- **¶23 "frelses ud af det Dæmoniske i Angeren".** This could mean "saved, through repentance, out of the demonic" or "saved out of the demonic-in-repentance". ¶20 introduced "Det Dæmoniske i Angeren" as a phrase, so I resolved it grammatically: "saved out of the demonic that lies in repentance". A reviewer should check this against the ¶20 drafter's wording.
- **¶23 "Havmanden staaer paa en dialektisk Spidse".** Rendered "on a dialectical knife-edge". ¶13 has the related image "holder jeg dem paa Spidsen". I do not know how the ¶13 drafter rendered it, so the book-level review should harmonize the two if needed.
- **¶23 "hjemfalden til Klosteret" and ¶25 "hjemfalden til det Comiske".** The Danish is a legal term: property forfeit or escheated to someone. I rendered it "forfeit to" in both places so the echo survives: "the merman is plainly forfeit to the monastery" and "no age has been so forfeit to the comic".
- **"aabenbart / Aabenbar" (¶23, ¶28).** These are the ordinary adverb "obviously". They share a root with *aabenbar* ("disclosed"), which may be a quiet pun. I rendered them "plainly" and "Obviously" and did not force the pun.
- **¶25 "at tage Tiden paa sin Samvittighed, at give den Tid til …".** "den" could refer to conscience or to time. I resolved it as conscience ("giving conscience time"), which is grammatically the more natural reading. I kept "taking time upon one's conscience" literal: *Tiden* could also carry the sense "the age", and I left that open.
- **¶25, the long "Alene Forestillingen om … — alene denne Forestilling …" period.** I split it into two sentences: "Consider merely the thought … This thought alone …". Every clause is kept, including the "whereas" clause about living in company with others.
- **¶26 "tungt".** In the epigrams it is rendered literally as "heavy" ("It is heavy not to find someone to give oneself to").
- **¶26 "bedragen" / "bedragen for".** This is one Danish word. It is rendered "deceived" in the epigrams ("deceived before she ever gave herself") and "cheated of" where it takes an object ("cheated of the most blessed thing of all"; "cheat her mother of what is most beautiful"). A reviewer may prefer a single English verb.
- **¶26 "Hengivelsens … Fortabelse".** Rendered "self-abandon of giving herself". *Fortabelse* also means perdition, and it echoes "den Fortabte" in ¶25 ("the lost one"). The echo is only partly kept.
- **¶26 "hendes uhyre Prioritets Obligation i Tilværelsen".** A financial image, a first-priority (first-mortgage) bond. Rendered "her enormous first-priority bond on existence".
- **¶27 "at paatage sig det Ansvar".** Sarah is the grammatical subject in context, so it is rendered "take upon herself".
- **¶28 "Der ligger en Krænkelse deri".** "deri" is resolved to pity: "There lies in pity an injury".
- **¶28 "eller om Forklaringen".** Rendered "or of their explanation", meaning the explanation of the horrors of existence. This is a grammatical resolution.
- **¶22 "hans Kunst".** Rendered "his art". It refers to the demonic stratagems of ¶21.
- **¶22 "Jo mere Agnete har været selvisk udviklet, desto mere blændende vil Selvbedraget være".** The Danish does not say whose self-deception is meant, so the English keeps "the self-deception" unattributed.

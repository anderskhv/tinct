## Terminology decisions

- **urimelig / det Urimelige → "preposterous"** (¶2, ¶3, ¶6, ¶8). The Danish here is *not* *det Absurde*, so I kept it distinct from "the absurd". **Reviewer:** the word itself is open; "unreasonable" or "beyond reason" are the alternatives. Do not merge it into "absurd".
- **Afmagt → "powerlessness"; Kraft → "power"; Styrke → "strength"**: "great by the power whose strength is powerlessness". **Daarskab → "foolishness"** in ¶1 and ¶6. **Vanvid → "madness"** in ¶1 and ¶13.
- **Prøvelse / prøve → "test" / "testing"** (¶6, ¶10, ¶11). **forsøges → "put to the proof"** (¶5). **fristede → "tempted"** in both Genesis quotations. **Fristelsen → "the temptation"** (¶10). **offre → "sacrifice"** throughout, including Gen. 22:2.
- **Erindringens Genius → "the presiding spirit of recollection"** (¶0). *Genius* in the Latin sense of a tutelary spirit; the plain "genius" would read as "brilliance".
- **Heltens bedre Væsen → "the hero's better being"** (¶0, both occurrences).
- **Ængstede → "the anxious"** (¶8), in line with Angest = anxiety.
- **Tilskikkelser → "dispensations"** (¶10). I avoided "trials" so that it cannot be confused with "test" or "spiritual trial".

## "misundelig paa det Betroede" (¶0) — reconsidered

This now reads **"he is jealous of what was entrusted to him"** (literal). The previous draft had "guards jealously", which interpreted the phrase. The literal wording keeps the Danish open. Possible readings:

1. **Protective jealousy (my reading):** zealously possessive of the trust, as in "a jealous God". This fits the next clause about his faithful service.
2. **Envy:** *misundelig* ordinarily means "envious". The poet envies the hero the deeds he has only been entrusted to remember.
3. **Scriptural echo:** "han tager Intet af sit Eget" recalls John 16:14–15, where the Spirit takes what is Christ's. That pushes toward the steward who possesses nothing and so guards the deposit.

"Jealous of" in English carries both sense 1 and sense 2, which is why I chose it.

The companion clause "han tager Intet af sit Eget" is rendered "He takes nothing that is his own".

## Ambiguities kept or resolved

- **¶0 "Denne … hiin" / "Dog er ogsaa han lykkelig".** I resolved these to "the poet" and "the hero". This is only a grammatical ambiguity.
- **¶0 "Dersom …".** Rendered "Suppose …" so the anaphora is kept. "Dersom" means "if"; the conditional sense is unchanged.
- **¶3 "Saa var det jo bedre …?" and "Er det at blive negtet … i Alderdommen."** The second ends with a full stop in the Danish. Both are rendered as questions, because both are questions grammatically.
- **¶3 "Hvis Abraham havde vaklet, da havde han opgivet den. Han havde sagt til Gud: …".** I joined these as "… he would have given it up, saying to God: …". The paragraph count is unchanged.
- **¶2 and ¶3: the exile and the one "who also had an expectation".** Both are left unnamed, as in the Danish. The exile is often taken to be Ovid; I added no gloss.
- **¶6 "Hvo havde vovet at være denne Sorgens Udsending."** A full stop in the Danish; rendered as a question. In the "Who is this …" pairs, the first sentence takes "?" and the second keeps the Danish "!".
- **¶6 "Slægtens herlige Ihukommelse".** Rendered "the glorious remembrance of the generations" and left open: it could mean remembrance *by* his posterity or the memory *of* his line.
- **¶7 "Jacob havde 12 Sønner og een han elskede".** Rendered "Jacob had twelve sons, and one he loved", which keeps the Danish word order and its slight double reading.
- **¶8 "Lad Isaak aldrig faae det at vide, at han maa trøste sig ved sin Ungdom".** Rendered "Let Isaac never learn of it, so that he can find comfort in his youth". I read *at* as "so that". The other reading is "never learn that he must console himself with his youth". **Reviewer should check.**
- **¶10 "svarede Du da, eller svarede Du ikke, maaskee sagte, og hviskende?"** Kept open: "did you answer, or did you not — perhaps quietly, in a whisper?"
- **¶13 "Slægtens anden Fader".** Rendered "Second father of our kind!" without resolving whether Adam or Noah is the first father.
- **¶13 "ikke kom videre end til Troen".** Rendered "got no further than faith". "Further" is kept deliberately to echo the book's "go further" refrain.

## Bible and allusions (fresh renderings of Kierkegaard's Danish wording)

- **¶2–3** "It was by faith that Abraham …" (Heb. 11:8–9); *Fremmed* → "alien"; "weeping with the one who weeps" (Rom. 12:15).
- **¶3** "every family on earth would be blessed in his seed" (Gen. 12:3 / 22:18); "the fullness of time" (Gal. 4:4); the sun halted (cf. Josh. 10); "Moses struck the rock with his rod, but he did not have faith" (Num. 20).
- **¶5** Gen. 22:2, freshly rendered from the Danish: "Take Isaac, your one and only son, whom you love; go off to the land of Moriah, and there sacrifice him as a burnt offering on a mountain I will point out to you."
- **¶6** Sarah's laughter (Gen. 18).
- **¶9** Sodom and Gomorrah (Gen. 18).
- **¶10** "Abraham, Abraham, where are you?" is **Kierkegaard's own conflation** of Gen. 22:1 with Gen. 3:9. It is kept as written, not corrected.
- **¶10** "Hide me … Fall on me" (Luke 23:30 / Hos. 10:8).
- **¶10** "whose hand took it" echoes Job 1:21; it is not an explicit quotation.
- **¶10** The Gen. 22 sequence is rendered "He chopped the firewood, he bound Isaac, he set the pyre alight, he drew the knife". ¶8 uses the same wording.
- **¶12** Ararat (Gen. 8).
- **¶13** "blessed in your bosom" (Luke 16:22); *Paulun* → "tent"; *hisset* → "in the beyond"; the figures 100 and 130 years are Kierkegaard's.

Proper names: Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Eliezer, Jacob, Moses, Mount Moriah, Sodom and Gomorrah, Ararat.

Kierkegaard's capitalized *Du/Din* (addressing God, Abraham and the reader) is rendered with lowercase "you".

Two 12-word runs remain at minrun 12. Both are kept as forced:

1. **3.1 "hope whose form is madness, great by the love that is hatred [of oneself]".** This is the closing paradox series, "stor ved det Haab, hvis Form er Vanvid, stor ved den Kjærlighed, der er Had til sig selv". It is a word-for-word rendering of an exact paradox that the brief requires to be kept exact. Each element (hope / form / madness; love / hatred) has only one accurate English noun, and "great by …" is the refrain.
2. **3.8 "But Abraham had faith and did not doubt; he believed the preposterous".** This renders a very short, plain Danish sentence: "Men Abraham troede og tvivlede ikke, han troede det Urimelige." "Abraham had faith" is the chosen refrain wording. Any other word order would distort the plain statement.

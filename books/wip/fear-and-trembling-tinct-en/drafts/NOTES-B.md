## Terminology decisions

- **urimelig / det Urimelige → "preposterous" / "the preposterous"** (¶2, ¶3, ¶6, ¶8). The Danish is *not* *det Absurde*, so I kept it distinct from "the absurd", which the standard fixes for *det Absurde*. I used it consistently in all four places. **Reviewer:** check whether the book-level review would rather have "unreasonable" or "beyond reason". Do not merge it into "absurd".
- **Afmagt → "powerlessness"; Kraft → "power"; Styrke → "strength"** (¶1): "great through that power whose strength is powerlessness". **Daarskab → "foolishness"** in both ¶1 and ¶6. **Vanvid → "madness"** in ¶1 and ¶13.
- **stor ved → "great through"** throughout ¶1. I chose "through" over "by" for *ved* and applied it to every member of the series, so that the parallelism is exact. The first version used "by"; see the screen section below. Both are accurate.
- **stride med → "strive with"** (Jacob's wrestling, Gen. 32). "Saaledes blev der stridt" is rendered "Thus there was strife". In ¶13, "stride med Gud" is "strive with God", which ties it back to ¶1.
- **Prøvelse / prøve → "test" / "tested"** (¶6, ¶10, ¶11). **forsøges → "put to the proof"** (¶5). **fristede → "tempted"** in the Genesis quotations (¶5, ¶10). **Fristelsen → "the temptation"** (¶10). The ordinary verb "fristede … til veemodig Længsel" (¶2) is "tempted … to wistful longing".
- **Troen / troe → "faith" / "believe"; den Troende → "the believer"**. "det er større at troe" is "it is greater to have faith". "han modtog den troende" (¶3) is "he received it believing". "den, der troede Gud" (¶1) is "the one who believed God", kept without "in" as in Gen. 15:6.
- **betragte den Troende → "behold the believer"** (¶2, ¶3, refrain). **saligere → "more blessed"** (Salighed = blessedness).
- **Glemsel → "oblivion"** throughout (¶0, ¶13).
- **Forjættelse → "promise"**: "the land of promise", "the child of promise".
- **veemodig → "wistful"**. The refrain words (*Klagesang*, *Sørgesang*, *Qvad*) are "lament", "song of grief" and "song".
- **Væsen (Heltens bedre Væsen) → "better self"** (¶0, both occurrences).
- **Erindringens Genius → "the genius of recollection"** (¶0).
- **Ængstede → "the anxious"** (¶8), in line with Angest = anxiety.
- **Anfægtelse** does not occur in ch3.

## Ambiguities kept or resolved

- ¶0 "Denne … hiin". "The latter … the former" is resolved to "The poet … the hero", which is a grammatical resolution. "glad ved, at det dog ikke er ham selv" refers to the poet, so I made the subject explicit: "and he is glad that it is not … he himself".
- ¶0 "misundelig paa det Betroede" becomes "guards jealously what has been entrusted to him". *Misundelig* is literally "envious". I took it in the sense of jealous guarding of a trust. Reviewer may check.
- ¶1 "i Forhold til dets Storhed, han elskede" becomes "according to the greatness of what he loved". The repeated "each shall be remembered, but …" structure and every paradox are kept verbatim in form.
- ¶3 "Er det at blive negtet … i Alderdommen." The Danish ends with a full stop but is grammatically a question. I gave it a question mark.
- ¶3 "Der var den i Verden, der ogsaa havde en Forventning" and ¶2 "Der var i Verden ogsaa den, der levede forviist" are left unnamed, as in the Danish. The ¶2 exile is often taken as Ovid; I did not name him and added no gloss.
- ¶6 "Hvo havde vovet at være denne Sorgens Udsending." The Danish has a full stop. I rendered it as a question ("Who would have dared …?"). The exclamation marks on the other "Who is it …" sentences are kept as in the Danish; the first of each pair has a question mark, because the Danish uses "!" after the pair.
- ¶6 "Slægtens herlige Ihukommelse" becomes "the glorious remembrance of the generations". I kept it open: it could mean the remembrance by his posterity or the memory of his line.
- ¶7 "Jacob havde 12 Sønner og een han elskede" becomes "Jacob had twelve sons and one whom he loved". The Danish word order is kept, together with its slight double reading.
- ¶8 "Lad Isaak aldrig faae det at vide, at han maa trøste sig ved sin Ungdom" becomes "Let Isaac never find out, so that he may take comfort in his youth". I read *at* as final ("so that"). Another reading is "never learn that he must console himself with his youth". **Reviewer should check.**
- ¶10 "svarede Du da, eller svarede Du ikke, maaskee sagte, og hviskende?" is kept open: "did you answer, or did you not answer — perhaps softly, in a whisper?"
- ¶10 "Tilskikkelser" becomes "visitations of fate". I avoided "trials" so that it cannot be confused with "test" or "spiritual trial".
- ¶13 "Slægtens anden Fader" becomes "Second father of the race". I did not resolve it to Adam or Noah.
- ¶13 "ikke kom videre end til Troen" becomes "got no further than faith". I kept "further" deliberately to echo the book's "go further" refrain.

## Bible and allusions (fresh renderings, Kierkegaard's wording kept)

- ¶2–3 "By faith Abraham …" (Heb. 11:8–9). "Fremmed" is "foreigner".
- ¶2 "weep with the one who weeps" (Rom. 12:15).
- ¶3 "in his seed all the families of the earth" (Gen. 12:3 / 22:18).
- ¶3 "the fullness of time" (Gal. 4:4).
- ¶3 "Moses struck the rock … but he did not believe" (Num. 20).
- ¶3 "he did not halt the course of the sun" (cf. Josh. 10).
- ¶5 Gen. 22:2, following Kierkegaard's Danish.
- ¶6 Sarah's laughter (Gen. 18). "his face would shine like the Lord's" is left as it stands.
- ¶9 Sodom and Gomorrah (Gen. 18).
- ¶10 "Abraham, Abraham, where are you?" is **Kierkegaard's own misquotation** (it conflates Gen. 22:1 with Gen. 3:9). I kept it as written and did not correct it. "Hide me … Fall upon me" is Luke 23:30 / Hos. 10:8. "His hand took it" echoes Job 1:21; there is no explicit quotation.
- ¶12 Ararat (Gen. 8).
- ¶13 "blessed in your bosom" (Luke 16:22). "Paulun" is "tent". "hisset" is "in the world beyond". The ages 100 and 130 are kept as Kierkegaard gives them.
- Proper names: Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Eliezer, Jacob, Moses, Moriah (Mount Moriah), Sodom and Gomorrah, Ararat.
- Capitalized Danish *Du/Din* (for God, Abraham and the reader) is rendered with lowercase "you".

## First-use definitions introduced
- **the leap** (¶12): "the great springboard leap by which I pass over into infinity" (*Tramplin-Spring*). No gloss beyond the text.
- **the double movement** (¶12): "through a double movement he had come back to his first condition". No gloss beyond the text. The surrounding sentences give the logic: he gives Isaac up, and by virtue of the absurd he receives him back.
- **infinite resignation** (¶13): "infinite resignation — the movement by which one gives up the finite and finds one's peace in the eternal." Grounded in ¶11 ("the infinite movement to find myself and rest in myself again") and ¶16.
- **knight of infinite resignation** (¶15): bare ("The knights of infinite resignation are easy to recognize…"), as the table asks.
- **philistine / philistinism** (¶15): "to philistinism, the outlook of the philistine, the narrow, comfortable townsman." The Danish has *Spidsborgerlighed*, which gives the abstract noun, so the definition attaches the table's wording to "the philistine".
- **knight of faith** (¶16): bare, with no gloss.
- Used bare, as already defined in ¶0–11: the absurd, by virtue of the absurd, paradox, incommensurable, the movement(s) of infinity / the infinite movement, the outcome, mediation (in n4.22a).
- Also used, following the table: "the knight(s) of infinity" (*Uendelighedens Ridder[e]*, ¶16), "the movements of finitude" (¶15), "the movement of resignation" (n4.21a), and "go further" / "stop at" (¶12, ¶13, ¶14, ¶15).

## Footnotes (both in ¶17)

## Ambiguities and decisions
- **"Troede" (¶12)**: "had faith" where it stands alone ("All that time he had faith"). Elsewhere it is "believed" ("he believed that…", "Abraham believed"), following the table (faith / have faith, believe).
- **"lade sig bevæge til at troe" (¶14)**: "let themselves be moved to have faith". This keeps the pun on *Bevægelse*.
- **"hvert andet Menneske" (¶16)**: "every other person". It keeps the Danish double reading ("every second person" / "any other person at all"), which I take to be deliberate irony.
- **"i min Praxis" (¶16)**: "in my practice". This keeps the mock-professional (doctor's or naturalist's) joke.
- **"normalt" (n4.22a anchor sentence)**: "in the proper way". "Normally" would read as "usually" in English. The Danish means "as it should be done", "according to the norm".
- **"Sluttethed" (¶17, twice)**: "self-containment", the same both times.
- **"Livets Indhold"**: "the content of (his) life" throughout ¶17.
- **"Forstand" (¶12)**: "understanding" throughout, including "for which the understanding is the exchange broker" (*Vexel-Mægler*). "Sindssvag" is "out of his mind".
- **"salig hisset / lyksalig her i Verden" (¶12)**: "blessed in the world beyond / happy here in this world". *Salighed* stays "blessedness" elsewhere (¶16).
- **"Vidunder / det Vidunderlige"**: "marvel / the marvelous" throughout. "det eneste Vidunder" is "the only marvel" (¶12, ¶16).
- **"forbauses"**: "be astonished", "stand amazed", "astonishment".
- **"Held den, der…" (¶15)**: "Happy is the one who…". I avoided "blessed", which is reserved for *salig*.
- **"Vaderne" (¶15)**: "the waders". This keeps the bird and wading joke.
- **"Spring"**: "leap" throughout ¶16, including Johannes's own comic "make a leap backward" when he first sees the knight, and the dancer's leap.
- **"Oversidderne" (¶16)**: "the ones who sit out and never join the dance".
- **"en ikke usalig Tidsfordriv"**: "a pastime not without its bliss". The litotes is kept.
- **"det Sublime i det Pedestre"**: "the sublime … in the pedestrian". The pun is kept.
- **"Elevation"**: kept as the ballet term "elevation" (¶12, ¶16).
- **"lader 5 være lige" (¶16)**: an idiom, rendered "lets things take their course".
- **"kjøber … den beleilige Tid til den dyreste Priis"**: "buys up the opportune time at the dearest price". This keeps the echo of Eph. 5:16 / Col. 4:5 ("buying up the time").
- **"en ny Skabning"**: "a new creation" (echo of 2 Cor. 5:17).
- **"den, der staaer, saae til, at han ikke faldt" (¶15)**: "for the one who stands to take care that he does not fall" (1 Cor. 10:12). Fresh wording.
- **Love (¶15)**: "Kjærligheden" is "love" (the 1 Cor. 13 register), left without comment.
- **Tax collector**: "Rodemester" is "a tax collector". A *Rodemester* was the Copenhagen district official who, among other things, collected taxes.
- **"Kræmmersjæl, der slog sig løs"**: "a petty shopkeeper out on the town for once".
- **"Skriverkarl … italienske Bogholderi"**: "a clerk who had lost his soul in Italian double-entry bookkeeping". I added "double-entry" so that "Italian" is understood; it is the dictionary sense of the term.
- **"Giftbægeret" (¶17)**: "the cup of poison". The Socratic allusion is left implicit, as in the Danish.
- **"slagvante Duer"**: "well-trained doves". *Slag* is the dovecote; the doves are used to coming home.
- **"Æslet"**: "the donkey" (modern). **Reviewer: check consistency with the other parts (Attunement, Eulogy), which may have "ass".**
- **"latterlige Menneskeracer" (¶16)**: "ridiculous races of mankind". It is kept, because the satire targets the gawking tourist. It is not a slur term in the §E sense, but **flag for review under §E**.

## Doubts about the Danish text
- ¶16: "…varm Mad til ham. naar han kommer hjem, f. Ex. et stegt Lammehoved…". In the raw OCR (l. 1590–1593) the period falls at a page break, before the lowercase "naar". I read it as one sentence ("…waiting for him when he gets home — a roast lamb's head…"). This resolves grammar only.
- "Fresberg" (¶16) is the colloquial form of Frederiksberg; rendered "Frederiksberg".
- "4 Sk." is rendered "four skilling". It is a period coin, kept as it is.

## Proper names and places
Frederiksberg, the woods (*Skoven*, i.e. Dyrehaven, not named), the Sound, the Strandvej, Østerport and Esau are kept as in Johannes, without gloss, per §A.9. Moriah is Moriah.

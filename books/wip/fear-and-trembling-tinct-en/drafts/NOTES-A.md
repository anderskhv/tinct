## Ambiguities resolved or kept

- ch1 ¶1 **"hiin Angst og Bævelse"**: rendered "that anxiety and trembling", following the hard rule that *Angst* is always anxiety and never fear. **Flag:** the phrase echoes the book's title (*Frygt og Bæven*, Phil. 2:12). Kierkegaard's words here are *Angst og Bævelse*, not *Frygt og Bæven*, so the echo is only partial in the Danish too. It is **kept as "that anxiety and trembling" per policy**. The English reader loses the title echo ("fear and trembling"), and a reviewer or the onboarding copy may want to point it out; the text itself does not.
- ch1 ¶2 the **Begreb/begribe pun** ("the form of the concept … comprehended faith"): rendered "concept" / "comprehended". The etymological link is partly lost.
- ch1 ¶2 **"forskriver sig paa Systemet eller til Systemet"**: *forskrive sig* means both "make a slip of the pen" and, with *til*, "sign oneself away" (as to the devil). I split the pun: "neither makes a slip of the pen on the System’s account nor signs himself away to it".
- ch1 ¶2 **"Extra-Skriver"**: rendered "an extra clerk", a supernumerary copyist. This keeps the writing pun with "writes the System".
- ch1 ¶2 **"staae Skoleret"** (a military running-the-gauntlet punishment): rendered "run the gauntlet".
- ch1 ¶2 **"Posekigger"**: rendered "customs-snooper", i.e. one who peeks into bags.
- ch1 ¶0 **"spekulativ — Marqueur … pointerer"**: *Marqueur* is the billiard-marker who keeps score. Rendered "speculative billiard-marker who conscientiously keeps score of…".
- ch1 ¶0 **"Udflytter og Indsidder"**: rendered "cottager and lodger on the estate of philosophy". These are rural social categories, and "on the estate of" is added to make the image readable. That is the one small expansion in this paragraph.
- ch1 ¶0 **"Gadevægter"**: rendered "night watchman in the street", the watchman who called out alarms.
- ch2 ¶5 **"det var jo og Synd"**: here *Synd* means "a pity" or "a shame", not "sin". Rendered "it would surely be a shame".
- ch2 ¶5 **"Moderen hun er den samme"**: I kept the resumptive pronoun ("the mother, she is the same") for tenderness.
- ch2 ¶4 **"Det er min Lyst"**: rendered "it is what I want". *Lyst* covers desire, will and pleasure. I avoided "pleasure" because it adds sadism the Danish does not force.
- ch2 ¶4 **"Umenneske"**: rendered "monster". "an inhuman creature" is the alternative.
- ch2 ¶4 **"forbarm Dig over mig"**: rendered "take pity on me" (×2), not the liturgical "have mercy". Both are accurate. *Formaning/formanende* is "exhortation" at both occurrences.
- ch2 ¶4 **"troer Du …"**: rendered "do you believe …", not "think", to keep the *tro* thread.
- ch2 ¶6 **"Væderen, som Gud havde udseet"**: rendered "the ram God had provided", since *udsee* is the verb of Gen. 22:8 in the Danish Bible.
- ch2 ¶8 **"Hagar og Sønnen"**: rendered "Hagar and the son", not "her son". Ishmael is Abraham's son too, and the Danish leaves this open.
- ch2 ¶9 **"hvis det var en Synd, hvis han ikke havde elsket Isaak saaledes"**: kept knotted, with the two conditions side by side ("And supposing it was a sin — supposing he had not loved Isaac like that — then …"). This is a deliberate ambiguity and is not resolved.
- ch2 ¶11 **"indtil han atter vendte tilbage"**: a merely grammatical ambiguity, resolved by making Eliezer the subject ("the faithful servant Eliezer went with him a stretch of the way and then turned back").
- ch2 ¶12 **"Isaak havde tabt Troen"**: rendered "Isaac had lost the faith". The definite article is kept, as in "kept the faith" (¶0).
- ch2 ¶14 **"Vandring"**: rendered "pilgrimage", for the repeated walks to Moriah. A reviewer could prefer "walk".
- ch8 ¶0 **"begynder … primitivt"**: rendered "begins from the very beginning". The Danish technical sense (originally, at first hand) is conveyed without the misleading English "primitively".
- ch8 ¶0 **"ængster Menneskene til at ville forraske sig i det Høieste"**: rendered "does not frighten people into an anxious rush to take the highest by storm".
- ch8 ¶1 **"forkeert"** ×3: rendered "topsy-turvy" ×3 to keep the triple repetition. The tailor is Grimm's "The Tailor in Heaven", kept as "the tailor in the fairy tale" with no further gloss.
- ch8 ¶1 **"den Aand, der styrer Verden"**: rendered "the spirit who governs the world", lower-case, as the Danish is ambiguous between God and world-spirit.
- ch8 ¶2 **"mig selv, der ikke dølger, at det har lange Udsigter med ham"**: the Danish shifts from "myself" to third person ("him"). I kept the shift in a softened form: "to myself — to one who does not hide that he has a long way to go". The lover's reply ("I am not standing still in the least — my life is in it") stays as unquoted direct speech, as in the Danish.
- ch8 ¶2 **"naar han opdager dette, da har han en anden Forklaring"**: kept as is ("if he discovers that, then he has another explanation"). This is a deliberate ambiguity.
- ch8 ¶3 **"Gudens Tempel"** (Diana's temple): rendered "the temple of the deity". This keeps the Danish generic noun without making Diana "the god".
- **komme videre vs gaae videre** (ch8): *gaae videre* is always "go further", per the standard. *komme videre* is "get further", keeping the two verbs distinguishable.
- ch2 ¶1 **"komme ud over Troen"**: rendered "get beyond faith". This is a different verb from *gaae videre*, so "go further" is not used here.

It keeps "God tempted Abraham". Elsewhere *opløftede sit Øie* (singular) is rendered "raised his eyes".

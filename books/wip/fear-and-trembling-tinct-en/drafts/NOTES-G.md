## Terminology decisions

- **tie / Taushed:** always "keep silent" / "silence". **tale:** "speak". The numbered options keep Johannes's figures exactly: "1) Should he keep silent and go ahead with the wedding…", "2) Should he keep silent and call off the wedding?", "3) Should he speak?"
- **Ulykke / ulykkelig:** "misfortune" / "unhappy", following part F.
- **Høimod / høimodig / Høimodighed:** "magnanimity" / "magnanimous". **Heltemod:** "heroic courage". This keeps the Danish pairing of Heltemod and Høimod in ¶16.
- **fornærme / Fornærmelse (mod Pigen):** "wronged the girl" / "a wrong against the girl" (¶15, n7.19a).
  - ¶17 has Elizabeth's "personlig Fornærmelse", rendered "personal offense".
  - ¶18 has "Fornærmelse mod Qvindekjønnet", rendered "an offense against womankind".
  - The girl passages use "wrong" because the point there is injustice done to her, not insult.
- **hæve:** "cancel", per the fixed table.
  - ¶15: "cancel that ill-fated prophecy".
  - ¶16: "having had its presupposition and having cancelled it" (*havt og hævet*).
- **det Mellemliggende** (¶16): "the middle term", per the fixed table.
- **Anger:** "repentance". **Qval:** "torment". **martre:** "torture" (¶20, ¶21). The two are kept distinct.
- **Latterlighed** (¶21): "make her love ridiculous", never "absurd".
- **det Uendelige** (n7.27a): "the infinite".
- **Nød** (¶15): "distress". **Udfaldet** (¶17): "the outcome".
- **friste** (n7.27a): "tempted men with her songs".
- **troende** (¶18, Agnete's look): "in absolute faith". This keeps the faith vocabulary audible.
- **den Enkelte** (¶17): "the single individual", per the fixed table, including the augur sentence. There the Danish uses it in a near-ordinary sense three times: "any individual can understand … that an augur announces heaven's decision to the individual".
  - I kept the fixed term because the passage turns on the single individual versus the universal a few lines later.
  - The result reads a little heavily. **Reviewer check.**
- **enhver Enkelt** (¶17): "every single person". It is a different Danish phrase and is not the technical term.
- **spinkle Elskere og Elskerinder** (¶15): "those slender lovers, men and women alike". This matches part F's "slender heroes" (ch7 ¶7), which the phrase refers back to.

## Ambiguities kept or resolved

- **¶15 "fordi de ere hinanden lige nær"** (Axel and Valborg): rendered "since they stand equally near to each other".
  - In Oehlenschläger's play the lovers are kept apart by kinship: "near" in the kin sense.
  - The phrase also means that the obstacle applies equally to both.
  - English "near" can carry both senses, so the ambiguity is kept.
- **¶15, option 1:** the bridegroom's reasoning is given as unmarked inner speech ("thinking: perhaps the misfortune… — but I must keep silent…?"). The Danish has no quotation marks, so none are added.
- **¶18 "hvorefter hun stirrede ned paa Havsens Bund":** rendered "what she used to stare down at the bottom of the sea for". I read *hvorefter* as "for which", not "after which". This resolves a grammatical ambiguity only.
- **¶19 "Angeren, og Agnete og Angeren":** Johannes's odd enumeration is kept exactly: "repentance; and Agnete and repentance."
- **¶17 "arbeide lige i Haanden paa det Religieuse":** rendered "playing straight into the hands of the religious", which is the sense of the Danish idiom.
- **¶18, the demonic gloss:** "which, like the divine, works through silence". This makes explicit that silence belongs to both, as ¶12 says. It avoids making "which" refer to one term only.
- **n7.19a "en Laban":** in Danish *laban* is a colloquial word for a lout or rascal. It probably also echoes the biblical Laban, who gave Jacob the wrong sister, and the note's own example is the sister who "is the ideal".
  - I kept the name and glossed it: "a Laban — a lout —".
  - **Reviewer check:** whether the gloss is wanted, or whether the name should stand bare.
- **n7.19a "ved Lys":** rendered "by lamplight", meaning artificial light as opposed to daylight.
- **n7.19a "paa 2den Sal":** rendered literally as "on the second floor". Danish floor numbering differs from US numbering, but the detail is only colour.

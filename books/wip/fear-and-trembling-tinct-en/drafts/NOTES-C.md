## Foreign-language insertions

- **¶10.** *jam tua res agitur*, glossed "now it is your own affair that is at stake". This is Horace, *Epistles* I.18.84, where the line begins *nam*; Kierkegaard writes *jam*, and I kept that. The allusion is not glossed in the text.
- **¶10.** *Schäfer-Historier* is a German-Danish hybrid. I kept it with "(shepherds' idylls)". A reviewer may prefer to render it outright as "pastoral idylls" if it is not counted as a foreign insertion.
- **¶11.** *proprio motu et propriis auspiciis*, glossed "of his own motion and on his own authority". *Auspicia* is the Roman commander's right to take the auspices, meaning acting under one's own command.
- **¶4.** *Nul og Nichts* is an idiomatic Danish doublet with German *Nichts*. I rendered it "nil and nothing" without keeping the German, because it works as a Danish idiom, not a quotation. Flag it if the policy wants it kept.

## Allusions and proper names

- **¶0.**
  - Noureddin and Aladdin come from Oehlenschläger's *Aladdin*. They are kept, with no gloss.
  - The rain and sun on just and unjust is Matt. 5:45.
  - "Abraham for one's father" is Matt. 3:9.
  - "Seventeen noble ancestors" renders *17 Ahner* (noble lineage).
  - The virgins of Israel who give birth to wind is Isa. 26:18.
  - Orpheus: *kjelen* is rendered "soft" and *Citharspiller* "cithara player".
- **¶1.** Miltiades: the unnamed "only one" who was made sleepless is Themistocles. That is left implicit, as in the Danish. The Midas allusion ("everything turns to gold") is left implicit too.
- **¶2.**
  - The rich young man is Matt. 19.
  - The cherub with the flaming sword is Gen. 3:24.
  - *Præsten* is "the pastor", and *geistlig* is "clerical" or "clergyman".
  - *Taler* is "speaker", except in the pastor's own boast *Jeg er Taler*, which is "I am an orator".
  - *Sjælesørger* is "shepherd of souls".
- **¶7.** *Kongens Riger og Lande* is kept as "the King's realms and lands", a Danish formula for the whole realm. The place name is "Mount Moriah".
- **¶9.** "Theology sits painted at the window" keeps the Jezebel image (2 Kings 9:30) without a gloss. Theology is "her" in "her charms" because the image is of a woman. The Danish *sin* is neutral.
- **¶10.** "Married with the left hand" is a morganatic marriage. It is kept bare, because the contrast with "the right" makes the sentence followable. It is a candidate for a short gloss if the reviewer thinks it is essential.
- **¶11.** *Kongereise* ("royal journey") is a journey one is summoned to supply in the king's service (*tilsige til Kongereise*). It is rendered "summoned for such an extraordinary royal journey". The duty-summons sense is only partly carried.

## Ambiguities resolved or kept

- **¶2, the tense of the sermon anecdote.** The Danish moves from present to a narrative past ("Han gik hjem … gik han maaskee til ham"). I kept the past as the Danish has it. The anecdote's last sentence ("Eller hvis Synderen … at det ikke gaaer til …") is an unfinished conditional with no main clause. I made it a complete sentence with "Or suppose …", with no content added.
- **¶4.** *den ethiske Forglemmelse, at Isaak var Sønnen* is rendered "the ethical forgetting of the fact that Isaac was the son". The parallel is kept: the speaker forgot faith, just as he forgot, ethically, that Isaac was the son.
- **¶6.** *en Enkelt* ("someone") is deliberately **not** "the single individual". *Bevægelse* in "a passing movement of feeling" keeps "movement", as the standard requires, with "of feeling" added so the emotional sense is clear.
- **¶7.** *elskede han ikke som Abraham* has an unclear "he", which I resolved as "a father". That is grammatical only.
- **¶7.** *i Tiden bliver han det ikke* is rendered "but not in time". It is kept terse and unexplained.
- **¶8.** The final clause (*den Vanskelighed … der bragte det kløgtige Hoved videre ved at overvinde den, end til det Punkt …*) is rendered as "as if overcoming it brought the shrewd head any further than the point that the simplest … person reaches more easily". This is the most compressed syntax in the part.
- **¶10.**
  - *det Enkelte bekymrer mig ikke* is rendered "particular things do not concern me". It is neuter, so it is kept distinct from "the single individual".
  - In *men jeg troer ikke, dette Mod mangler mig*, *troe* is "have faith": "But I do not have faith; this is the courage I lack."
- **¶11.**
  - *min Samtid* is "my time", and the following *den* refers to it.
  - Johannes's counterfactual past tense (*var*) in "my enormous resignation was far more ideal … was the substitute for faith" is kept as "was".
- **¶9.** *At gaae ud over Hegel … at komme ud over Abraham* is rendered "going beyond … getting beyond". This is **not** the *gaae videre* refrain, which is "Philosophy goes further" in the same paragraph.

## Terminology checks

- Angest/Angst is always "anxiety" (¶0, ¶2, ¶4).
- Prøve (¶7) is "test", and *forsøges* (¶7) is "put to the proof".
- *salig* is "blessed" or "attain blessedness" (¶7, ¶10).
- *Nød* (¶10) is "distress".
- *latterlig* (¶2) is "ridiculous".
- *offre* is "sacrifice".
- *Virkelighed* is "actuality" (¶3 "so-called actuality", ¶10).
- *Timeligheden* (¶11) is "temporality".

## For the reviewer

1. **"Mediation" at ¶2.** The brief assigns it, but the Danish says *meditere*. I left it out; please confirm. Kierkegaard might intend a pun on *mediere*, but nothing in the text requires it.
2. **The ¶8 closing clause.** Its syntax is compressed and should be checked.
3. **The bare period references in ¶10–11.** "Married with the left hand" and *Kongereise* ("royal journey") carry no gloss. Decide whether either needs one.

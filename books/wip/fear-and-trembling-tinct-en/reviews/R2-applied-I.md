## [39] *desultorisk*: why not "stand out more sharply"

The Danish word goes back to Latin *desultor*, the rider who leaps from horse to horse. Its dictionary sense is "leaping, disconnected, shifting". "Stand out more sharply" is not a sense of the word; it guesses at the effect. So I did not use it.

"Come out by leaps, piece by piece" keeps both parts of the lexical sense in plain English:
- "by leaps" renders *springende*;
- "piece by piece" renders *usammenhængende*.

It also fits the method the sentence describes: a run of separate cases, none of them an analogy.

## [29] Shakespeare's lines against the German (Schlegel–Tieck), line by line

| German | Shakespeare (Richard III I.i) | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| … Ich, roh geprägt, und aller Reize baar. | I, that am rudely stamp’d, and want love’s majesty | Close. *aller Reize baar* ("bare of all charms") stands for "want love's majesty". |
| Vor leicht sich dreh'nden Nymphen mich zu brüsten; | To strut before a wanton ambling nymph; | Close. The German has plural nymphs that "lightly turn"; the English has one "wanton ambling" nymph. |
| Ich, so verkürzt um schönes Ebenmass, | I, that am curtail’d of this fair proportion, | Exact. |
| Geschändet von der tückischen Natur, | Cheated of feature by dissembling nature, | Close. German "defaced by treacherous nature"; English "cheated of feature". |
| Entstellt, verwahrlost, vor der Zeit gesandt | Deform’d, unfinish’d, sent before my time | Close. *verwahrlost* ("neglected") stands for "unfinish'd". |
| In diese Welt des Athmens, halb kaum fertig | Into this breathing world, scarce half made up, | Exact in sense. The German breaks mid-phrase ("fertig / Gemacht"). |
| Gemacht, und zwar so lahm und ungeziemend, | And that so lamely and unfashionable | Exact. |
| Dass Hunde bellen, hink' ich wo vorbei. | That dogs bark at me as I halt by them. | Exact in sense ("halt" means "limp"). |

- There are eight lines on each side. The only divergences are the translator's small shifts noted above, and none changes the sense Johannes uses: deformity, and being sent unfinished into the world.
- The bracket keeps the opening "…".
- Apostrophes are typographic (’).
- The last line ends with a full stop to close the quotation, matching the German "vorbei.".
- Shakespeare's text is public domain, and the screen treats it as a forced quotation.

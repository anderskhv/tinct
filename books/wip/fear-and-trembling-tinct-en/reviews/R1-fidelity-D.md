## ¶12 — "Men hvad gjorde Abraham?"

Overall faithful and complete. The lyrical and self-deprecating passage (rope-dancer, "walk on my head") keeps its voice. All defects are MINOR.

1. **MINOR — MEANING SHIFT (slight modality).**
   - Danish: "medens han dog var villig til at offre ham, naar det forlangtes"
   - English: "if it was demanded"
   - *Naar* here is temporal ("when"). "If" adds a note of doubt that the Danish does not stress.
   - Fix: "when it was demanded".
2. **MINOR — ENGLISH / hedge.**
   - Danish: "Han blev da vel overrasket ved Udfaldet"
   - English: "He was certainly surprised by the outcome, then;"
   - *Vel* is concessive ("no doubt", "to be sure"), not an assertion. The trailing "then" is also awkward.
   - Fix: "No doubt he was surprised by the outcome, then;" or "He was, to be sure, surprised by the outcome;".
3. **MINOR — ADDITION (interpretive, at a first use).**
   - Danish: "han havde gjennem en Dobbelt-Bevægelse naaet hen til sin første Tilstand"
   - English: "had come back to his first condition"
   - *Naaet hen til* means "reached" or "arrived at". "Come back" glosses the double movement as a return, and the brief says "no gloss beyond the text".
   - Fix: "through a double movement he had arrived at his first condition".
4. **MINOR — consistency (drafter's own policy).**
   - Danish: "Abraham troede."
   - English: "Abraham believed."
   - NOTES-D says a stand-alone *troede* is "had faith" ("All that time he had faith"). This one is also stand-alone.
   - Fix: "Abraham had faith."
5. **MINOR — lexical echo lost.**
   - Danish: "salig hisset … lyksalig her i Verden"
   - English: "blessed in the world beyond … happy here in this world"
   - The Danish climbs from *salig* to *lyksalig*. "Happy" drops the echo.
   - Fix: "blessed in the world beyond … blissfully happy here in this world".
6. **MINOR — ADDITION.**
   - Danish: "min Ryg er som en Liniedandsers, vreden i min Barndom"
   - English: "wrung into shape when I was a child"
   - "Into shape" is added; *vreden* means "twisted".
   - Fix: "my back is like a rope-dancer's, twisted when I was a child".
7. **MINOR — connective lost.**
   - Danish: "men det næste kan jeg ikke; thi det Vidunderlige kan jeg ikke gjøre; men kun forbauses ved"
   - English: "The next step, though, I cannot take; the marvelous is beyond my doing — I can only stand amazed at it."
   - *Thi* ("for") is dropped. The brief asks for connectives to be made explicit, not removed.
   - Fix: "…I cannot take; for the marvelous I cannot do — I can only stand amazed at it."
8. **MINOR — tone shift.**
   - Danish: "saa behøver jeg ikke Abraham"
   - English: "then Abraham is no use to me"
   - The Danish says "I do not need Abraham". "No use to me" is more dismissive and utilitarian.
   - Fix: "then I have no need of Abraham".
9. **MINOR — connective shifted.**
   - Danish: "Dette har han nemlig ikke gjort"
   - English: "That is exactly what he did not do."
   - *Nemlig* is explanatory ("for, you see"). It is not emphatic "exactly".
   - Fix: "For this he did not do, as I can prove from the fact that…" (or keep the split, as "But this he did not do.").

(Accepted: "surely it was the absurd" for *jo*; "sail so close to the wind" for *knibe … op mod Vinden*; "the exchange broker" for *Vexel-Mægler*; "win back exactly the same finitude" for *vinde netop den samme*, which is defensible; "seven times … seventy times" for *halvfjerdsindstyve*, which is correct at 70.)

## ¶14 — "Abraham kan jeg ikke forstaae"

1. **MINOR — AMBIGUITY FLATTENED / form changed.**
   - Danish: "jeg kan i en vis Forstand Intet lære af ham uden at forbauses"
   - English: "in a certain sense, all I can learn from him is astonishment"
   - *Intet … uden at forbauses* can be read as "nothing except to be astonished" or as "nothing without being astonished". The draft turns the negative-with-exception into a positive statement and turns the verb into a noun ("astonishment"), so it commits fully to one reading.
   - Fix: "in a certain sense I can learn nothing from him except to be astonished". This keeps the negation and the verbal form.
2. **MINOR — ENGLISH.**
   - Danish: "suge Leve-Viisdom ud af Paradoxet"
   - English: "suck a practical wisdom for living out of the paradox"
   - "A practical wisdom for living" is clumsy.
   - Fix: "suck worldly wisdom out of the paradox" or "suck wisdom for living out of the paradox".

(Accepted: "let themselves be moved to have faith", which keeps the *bevæge* pun; "cheat God of the first movement of faith"; the water/wine inversion, which is complete.)

## ¶15 — "Var det dog ikke bedst…"

1. **MINOR — archaism and inconsistency.**
   - Danish: "Trællen i Abrahams Huus"
   - English: "the bondsman in Abraham's household"
   - "Bondsman" reads as archaic. In ¶17 the same word *Træl* ("Elendighedens Trælle") is rendered "slaves".
   - Fix: "the slave in Abraham's household", to match ¶17.
2. **MINOR — lexical.**
   - Danish: "deres Gang er svævende, dristig"
   - English: "soaring and bold"
   - *Svævende* means "hovering", "floating" or "gliding", not "soaring", which suggests rising. That matters because ¶16 contrasts the knights' "elevation" with their landing.
   - Fix: "their gait is gliding, bold" or "their gait is light as if floating, and bold".
3. **MINOR — definition phrasing / ENGLISH.**
   - Danish: "— med Spidsborgerlighed."
   - English: "— to philistinism, the outlook of the philistine, the narrow, comfortable townsman."
   - The chain of three appositions grammatically equates *philistinism* with the *townsman*.
   - "Townsman" is then used again in ¶16 for *Borgermand* ("no spruced-up townsman"). That blurs *Borgermand* and *Spidsborger* in the very sentence where Johannes distinguishes them ("ingen pyntet Borgermand … ingen Spidsborger").
   - Fix: "— to philistinism, the outlook of the philistine: the narrow, comfortable townsman." In ¶16, render *Borgermand* as "burgher" or "citizen".

(Accepted: "Happy is the one who can make these movements!" for *Held den*. The exclamation mark is a light touch, and "blessed" is rightly avoided. Also accepted: "I am not one of the waders"; the swimming-harness image, complete; "they are the movements of infinity, while faith does the reverse: having made the movements of infinity, it makes those of finitude", which is exact; "for the one who stands to take care that he does not fall", fresh wording of 1 Cor 10:12.)

## ¶17 — The young man and the princess

1. **MAJOR — DISTINCTION BLUR (fixed rendering violated; a key concept weakened).**
   - Danish: "dog er Forholdet et saadant, at den **umulig** lader sig realisere, **umulig** lader sig oversætte fra Idealiteten til Realiteten"
   - English: "this love can **by no means** be realized — can **by no means** be translated out of ideality into reality"
   - The table fixes *Umulighed / umulig* → **impossibility / impossible**. The same paragraph turns on that word: the thoughts return "and explain to him that it is an **impossibility**" (*Umulighed*). The Eulogy ("expected the impossible") and ¶10 ("it is an impossibility", in C) set up the impossible as the object of faith.
   - "By no means" breaks the lexical chain from the premise (the love is impossible to realize) to the verdict (it is an impossibility) that triggers the movement of resignation. The reader loses the concept on which resignation and faith divide.
   - Fix: "…and yet the situation is such that it is impossible for this love to be realized, impossible for it to be translated out of ideality into reality."
   - Then update n4.21a `anchorAfterEn` to "impossible for it to be translated out of ideality into reality."
2. **MINOR — distinction lost.**
   - Danish: "skrige naturligviis … Lad dem uforstyrret **qvække** i Sumpen"
   - English: "naturally croak … Let them croak undisturbed"
   - *Skrige* means "shriek" or "screech". Only the second verb is *qvække*.
   - Fix: "naturally shriek: …" and keep "croak" for *qvække*.
3. **MINOR — ADDITION.**
   - Danish: "for sund og for stolt til at ødsle det Mindste paa en Ruus"
   - English: "to waste the smallest part of itself on a passing intoxication"
   - "Of itself" and "passing" are both added.
   - Fix: "too sound and too proud to squander the least thing on an infatuation" (or "on a drunken fancy").
4. **MINOR — terminology consistency / weakening.**
   - Danish: "Han føler en **salig Vellyst**"
   - English: "a blissful delight"
   - *Salig* should be "blessed", as elsewhere. *Vellyst* is stronger and more sensual than "delight" ("voluptuous pleasure", "rapture").
   - Fix: "He feels a blessed rapture in letting the love thrill through every nerve of his".
5. **MINOR — hedge/intensifier shifted.**
   - Danish: "thi **selv** i det Øieblik, da han er nærmest derved"
   - English: "for at the very moment he is nearest to it"
   - *Selv* means "even".
   - Fix: "for even at the moment he is nearest to it, he will suddenly discover that…". *Opdage* is "discover", not "it will occur to him".

## Overall judgment

This is a high-quality, complete and faithful draft. Johannes's voice is well preserved: the self-mockery, the lyricism of the dancer passage, and the comic realism of the knight-of-faith portrait. The terminology policy is followed almost everywhere ("movement", "by virtue of the absurd", "go further" / "stop at", "infinite resignation", "knight of infinity", "finitude", "anxious"). The first-use definitions are in the right places and within limits. The notes are correct, separate and properly anchored.

There is **one MAJOR** defect: *umulig* is rendered "by no means" in ¶17, against the fixed "impossible". It is a one-sentence fix plus an anchor update.

There are 20 MINOR defects. The most worth fixing:
- the *salig* renderings ("blissful", "bliss") against the book-wide "blessed";
- the philistine gloss chain and its "townsman" collision with *Borgermand*;
- "bondsman" versus "slaves" for *Træl*;
- the dropped *thi* and the shifted *nemlig* in ¶12.

After the MAJOR fix the part is ready for style review.

## ch7 ¶42 (text changed)

**before**

> The real tragic hero sacrifices himself and everything that is his for the universal. Whatever he does, every emotion that stirs in him, belongs to the universal. He is disclosed, and in being disclosed he is ethics’ beloved son. That is not how it is with Abraham: he does nothing for the universal, and he is concealed.

**after**

> The real tragic hero sacrifices himself and everything that is his for the universal. Whatever he does, every emotion that stirs in him, belongs to the universal. He is disclosed, and in being disclosed he is the beloved son of ethics. That is not how it is with Abraham: he does nothing for the universal, and he is concealed.

## ch7 ¶58 (text changed)

**before**

> So it appears here again that one can indeed understand Abraham, but only understand him in the way one understands the paradox. For my part, I can indeed understand Abraham, but I also comprehend that I do not have the courage to speak like that, any more than I have the courage to act like Abraham. Yet I by no means say, on that account, that it is something slight; on the contrary, it is the one thing that is wondrous.

**after**

> So it appears here again that one can indeed understand Abraham, but only understand him in the way one understands the paradox. For my part, I can indeed understand Abraham, but I also comprehend that I do not have the courage to speak like that, any more than I have the courage to act like Abraham. Yet I by no means say, on that account, that it is something lowly; on the contrary, it is the only marvel.

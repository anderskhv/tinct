# R2: readability items applied to Part D (ch4 ¶12–17, notes n4.21a and n4.22a)

Source review: `reviews/R1-readability-ch04.md`. The draft before these edits is saved as `drafts/history/D-ch4.r2.json`.
I changed wording only, and re-checked every change against `source/original-da-final.json`.

## Items

1. **¶12 — "through a double movement he had arrived at his first condition"**
   - **CHANGED:** "…he had arrived at the state he was in at the start,"
   - Danish: *naaet hen til sin første Tilstand*.
   - I kept "arrived at" (*naaet hen til*), as the R1 fidelity review required. I avoided "back where he started", which glosses the double movement.
   - *første Tilstand* is now concrete: "the state he was in at the start".

2. **¶12 — "the whole of finitude, for which the understanding is the exchange broker"**
   - **CHANGED:** "to lose one's understanding — and with it the whole of finitude, since the understanding is the broker who handles all its exchanges — and then…"
   - Danish: *hvis Vexel-Mægler den er*.
   - The metaphor is Kierkegaard's and it stays. The new syntax puts it in its own clause and spells out *Vexel-* as "exchanges", so it can be read at once.

3. **¶12 — "reflects on himself / reflects on God"**
   - **CHANGED:** "For the one who loves God without faith turns his reflection on himself; the one who loves God in faith turns his reflection on God."
   - Danish: *reflekterer paa sig selv / paa Gud*.
   - Johannes's category of reflection stays; n4.22a sets reflection against passion. "Turns his reflection on" gives the sense of direction the reviewer read, rather than "thinks about".

4. **¶13 — "The last stage he loses sight of"**
   - **CHANGED:** "The last stage to vanish from his sight is infinite resignation"
   - Danish: *Det sidste Stadium, han taber af Sigte*.
   - The picture is of the summit (*Spidse*), with the stages disappearing from view behind the climber. The old "loses sight of" read as "forgets".
   - I did not take the reviewer's "the last one in sight". It would say the opposite of *taber af Sigte*.

5. **¶14 — "cheat God of the first movement of faith"**
   - **CHANGED:** "cheat God out of the first movement of faith"
   - Danish: *bedrage Gud for*.
   - This is the idiomatic English. I did **not** add "(resignation)": the Danish does not name it here. The identification is the reader's, from ¶13 and ¶15 on, and it is idea-level.

6. **¶14 — "Perhaps one or two may succeed at it; for…"**
   - **CHANGED:** "Perhaps one or two may even manage this; for our age does not stop at faith…"
   - Danish: *Maaskee lykkes det En og Anden; thi…*
   - "This" points back to what was just described (sucking wisdom out of the paradox). "Even" signals the irony.
   - *thi* is **KEPT** as "for". It carries Johannes's sarcastic logic: such success fits an age that turns wine into water. The leap in that logic is the idea, not the wording.

7. **¶16 — "his staying in finitude bore no trace of a cowed and anxious drilling"**
   - **CHANGED:** "for his remaining in finitude bore no trace of the cowed, anxious drill of a trained animal,"
   - Danish: *hans Forbliven i Endeligheden havde intet Spor af en forknyt beængstet Dressur*.
   - *Dressur* is the breaking-in and training of animals (dressage), so "of a trained animal" is its dictionary sense, not an addition.
   - I did not take the reviewer's "routine". It loses *Dressur*.
   - "Remaining" is more natural for *Forbliven* than "staying".

8. **¶17 — "he does not give up his love" vs. resignation**
   - **KEPT.**
   - *opgiver* has a fixed rendering: "give up" (STYLE-AND-TERMINOLOGY §C, *offre / opgive*).
   - The apparent contradiction with "resignation" is Johannes's own. He resolves it in ¶18 (renouncing fulfilment is not abandoning the love). That makes it idea-level.
   - "Abandon" would break the fixed term and pre-empt ¶18.

9. **¶17 — two conditions whose failure descriptions repeat**
   - **PARTLY CHANGED.** The second condition now reads "concentrate the whole result of his thinking it through in one act of consciousness". The old wording was "the whole result of his operations of thought". The new wording links to the thoughts he has just sent out and called back (the doves), so the pair now reads as *one wish* against *one conclusion of thought*.
   - The repeated failure clause ("if his soul is scattered from the start among the many things") is **KEPT verbatim**, because the Danish repeats it word for word (*er hans Sjæl fra Begyndelsen adsplittet i det Mangfoldige*). The consequences already differ: "never get as far as making the movement" (prudent investor) against "never find the time to make the movement" (running errands).
   - Any further difference between the two conditions is idea-level.

10. **n4.21a — "the whole reality of actuality"**
    - **CHANGED:** "…has concentrated for himself the whole reality of what is actual"
    - Danish: *hele Virkelighedens Realitet*.
    - The ruling is kept: *Virkelighed* is actuality and *Realitet* is reality. The phrase no longer reads as a tautology.

11. **n4.22a — mediation, first use in the book**
    - **CHANGED:** "whereas mediation is a chimera:" is now "while mediation (Hegel's term for reconciling opposites in a higher unity) is a chimera: in Hegel it is meant to account for everything…"
    - I used a parenthesis because the sentence already has a colon and dashes.
    - This is the only added explanation.
    - Part C's notes confirm that ch4 ¶2 has *meditere*, not *mediere*, so this note is the book's first occurrence of the term.
    - **Consequence:** the brief's first-use table, which lists ch4 ¶2, should be updated. The pilot's ch5 ¶4 gloss ("cannot be mediated — that is, it cannot be brought under the universal") should be checked against this new gloss. That is the editor's call.

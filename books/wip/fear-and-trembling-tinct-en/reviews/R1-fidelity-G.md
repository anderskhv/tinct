## ¶18 — MAJOR ×1, MINOR ×6

### MAJOR — ADDITION / DISTINCTION BLUR / ENGLISH: the first-use gloss of "the demonic"

- **Danish:** "Nu vil jeg lade en Skizze følge i Retning af det Dæmoniske."
- **Draft:** "Now I will follow this with a sketch in the direction of the demonic **— the dark counterpart of the divine, which, like the divine, works through silence.**"

The Danish of ¶18 does not support this gloss. The paragraph never mentions the divine,
silence or any counterpart. It moves straight to the Agnete legend. Taking the gloss piece by piece:

1. **"counterpart" (Modstykket)** is taken from ¶22. There, Johannes concludes that the demonic
   is "Analogien, Modstykket til hiint Paradox": the counterpart **to the paradox of faith**,
   not to "the divine".
   - Importing the word here gives away, four paragraphs early, the conclusion that ¶19–22
     argue towards.
   - It also changes what the demonic is a counterpart *of*.
2. **"dark"** has no source anywhere. It is an evaluative addition, and it pre-judges a
   figure that Johannes treats ambivalently. In ¶21 a demonic person has "in a certain sense
   infinitely more good" in him than trivial people. In ¶22 the demon "can mean very well by a person".
3. **"works through silence"** overstates ¶12, which says only "Taushed er begge Dele"
   (silence is both) and "Taushed er Dæmonens Besnærelse" (silence is the demon's snare).
   - "Works through" is a claim about agency.
   - For the divine, ¶12 says silence is "Guddommens Samvidne med den Enkelte"
     (a shared knowing), not a means of working.
4. **English:** "which, like the divine" grammatically attaches "which" to the nearest noun,
   "the divine". The result reads "the divine, which, like the divine, works…", which is
   nonsensical. The drafter's own note says the phrase was meant to avoid exactly this.
5. **Placement:** the reader has already met "the demonic" bare in ¶12 (part F): "the divine and
   the demonic; for silence is both". So ¶18 is not the true first occurrence. ¶12 already
   supplies the source's own characterisation of the term, in Johannes's words.

**Fix:** delete the gloss and use the bare term:

> "Now I will follow this with a sketch in the direction of the demonic."

If the book-level editors insist on a gloss, it belongs at the true first use, ch7 ¶12.
There the Danish sentence itself already defines the term, so no extra words are needed.
Record a correction to the brief's first-use table: "the demonic | ch7 ¶12 | defined by the
text itself; no gloss". If a gloss must stay at ¶18, it must use only ¶12's own content. For example:
"…in the direction of the demonic (which, as I said, shares silence with the divine)". Even
this adds "as I said", so the bare term is preferred.

3. **MINOR — OMISSION (anaphora).**
   - Danish: "**men** absolut troende, **men** absolut ydmygt".
   - Draft: "but in absolute faith, in absolute humility".
   - Fix: "but in absolute faith, **but** in absolute humility, like the lowly flower…".
4. **MINOR — MEANING SHIFT.**
   - Danish: "Da **synker** Havmanden **sammen**".
   - Draft: "Then the merman sinks down".
   - *Synke sammen* means to collapse or break down. "Sinks down" can be misread as sinking back into the sea, which contradicts the next sentences.
   - Fix: "Then the merman **collapses**" or "**breaks down**".
5. **MINOR — MEANING SHIFT.**
   - Danish: "Nonsens og **Lefleri** og Fornærmelse mod Qvindekjønnet".
   - Draft: "nonsense and fawning".
   - In this context, *Lefleri* is flirtatious gallantry towards women: flattering them by declaring them wholly guiltless. "Fawning" is within range but misses the gallant-flirting sense.

   - Draft: "what she used to stare down at the bottom of the sea for".
   - The drafter's reading *hvorefter* = "for which" is correct, but the stranded preposition makes the clause clumsy, and "used to" adds a habitual sense that is only implied.

## Overall judgment

The draft is **accept after one MAJOR fix**.

- It is faithful and complete, with no omissions of substance.
- Johannes's voice is well kept: "I am no poet", "Let us…", "I have allowed myself", "ni fallor", the satirical footnote, and the ballad cadence of the Agnete retelling.
- The key Problema III distinctions are clean:
  - concealed/disclosed;
  - keep silent/speak;
  - aesthetic/ethical/religious;
  - the interesting;
  - repentance;
  - torment/torture.

The ¶18 gloss on "the demonic" must be removed or reduced to the source's own words. It imports ¶22's conclusion ("counterpart"), retargets it from "the paradox" to "the divine", and adds an unsupported evaluation ("dark"). Its relative clause is also grammatically misattached. The brief's first-use slot for "the demonic" should also be corrected to ch7 ¶12, where the term actually first appears.

The 22 MINOR items are quick edits. The most worthwhile are:
- *foregøgle sig* (¶17);
- *synker sammen* (¶18);
- *Susen* versus *Suk* (¶18 and n7.27a);
- the dropped *selv* and the "surely" hedge (¶15);
- the added "word by word" (¶17).

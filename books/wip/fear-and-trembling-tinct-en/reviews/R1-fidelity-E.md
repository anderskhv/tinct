## MAJOR findings

### M1 — ¶24: a conditional is turned into an incredulous question (MEANING SHIFT, MAJOR)

- **Danish:** "I Kraft af det Absurde skal Du faae hver en Hvid igjen, kan Du troe det."
- **Draft:** "By virtue of the absurd you will get every last penny back — can you believe that?"

The clause *kan Du troe det* has its verb first, after a comma, and the sentence ends with a full stop. That is the ordinary Danish inverted conditional: "if you can believe it". The promise is therefore **conditional on faith**, which is the whole point of the knight of faith's words to the rich young man.

"Can you believe that?" does two things wrong:
- it removes the condition;
- in colloquial English it reads as astonishment ("can you believe it!"), which turns the knight's promise into a joke.

**Fix:** "By virtue of the absurd you will get every last penny back, if you can believe it." (Use "if you can believe that" if you prefer.)

### M2 — Virkelighed rendered "reality" (¶18 ×2, ¶28): collides with ch4 C/D (DISTINCTION BLUR, MAJOR, book level)

- ¶18: "som ingen **Virkelighed** kan fratage ham" → "no reality can take from him"; "Ønsket, der vilde føre ham ud i **Virkeligheden**" → "led him out into reality".
- ¶28: "Individet er incommensurabelt for **Virkeligheden**" → "incommensurable with reality".

In the same chapter:
- C ¶10 renders the same formula, *incommensurabel* + *Virkelighed*, as "incommensurable with the whole of actuality".
- D ¶17 uses "actuality" for *Virkelighed*, and "reality" for *Realiteten* ("translated out of ideality into reality").

The ch4 reader therefore meets two effects:
- the same Danish concept under two names, in ¶10 and ¶28, which are deliberately parallel;
- "reality" standing for *Realitet* in ¶17 and for *Virkelighed* in ¶18. That falsely identifies the two terms one paragraph apart.

Elsewhere in the book the drafts split:
- the pilot, F and I use "reality" for *Virkelighed*;
- C and D use "actuality".

**Fix:** this needs a book-level decision, recorded in STYLE §C.

My recommendation is *Virkelighed* → **"actuality"** throughout. Because ¶17 pairs *Idealitet*/*Realitet*, "reality" needs to stay free for *Realitet*. On that basis, E changes to:
- ¶18 "no actuality can take from him";
- ¶18 "led him out into actuality";
- ¶28 "incommensurable with actuality".

The pilot, F and I should then be aligned.

If "reality" is chosen instead, D ¶17 must render *Realiteten* differently, and C must be aligned. Either way, E cannot stay as it is while C/D stand.

### M3 — ¶27 (and ¶18): *fatte* and *gribe* both rendered "grasp" (DISTINCTION BLUR, MAJOR)

**Danish ¶27:**
- "en Mand, der har **fattet** Livets Rædsel"
- "havde Sjæls Kraft til at **fatte**"
- "**greb** hele Tilværelsen i Kraft af det Absurde"
- "Dette kan jeg ikke **fatte**."

**Draft:**
- "who has grasped the horror of life"
- "had the strength of soul to grasp the thought"
- "grasped the whole of existence by virtue of the absurd"
- "This I cannot grasp."

NOTES-E §4 makes "grasp" the fixed echo for *gribe*: faith's act of seizing existence and temporality (¶20, ¶21, ¶23, ¶24, ¶27). Using the same verb for *fatte* (to comprehend) blurs the two. In ¶27 the collision is acute:
- The paragraph moves from comprehending the horror and the impossibility (*fatte*) to seizing existence by faith (*gribe*). The English makes these one act.
- Johannes's closing confession, "Dette kan jeg ikke fatte" (I cannot *understand* it), now reads as "I cannot seize it by faith". That is a different confession, and a plausible-sounding one, because he has just said he cannot make the movement.

¶18 "Han har fattet den dybe Hemmelighed" → "He has grasped the deep secret" has the same problem to a lesser degree.

**Fix:** keep "grasp" for *gribe* only. Render *fatte* as "comprehend" or "understand":
- ¶27: "who has comprehended the horror of life"; "had the strength of soul to comprehend the thought that the wish was an impossibility"; "This I cannot comprehend."
- ¶18: "He has understood the deep secret."

Also check C/D ¶2 (*greben*) and ¶7 (*opfattes*) so they agree.

## Overall judgment

This is an accurate, complete and well-voiced draft.
- There are no omissions of clauses, hedges, examples or allusions.
- Johannes's confessions and irony are intact: "I cannot make", "This movement … I cannot make", "the least among them", and the coach-and-four joke.
- The anacoluthon in ¶25 and the refrains are handled with care.
- The fixed terms are used correctly: infinite resignation, knight of faith / of resignation, movement, the absurd, test / put to the proof, anxiety, ridiculous.

Three MAJOR issues must be fixed before acceptance:
1. the conditional "kan Du troe det" (¶24);
2. the book-level *Virkelighed* rendering, which currently collides with ch4 C/D and with ¶17's *Realitet*;
3. the *fatte / gribe* merge under "grasp" (¶27, ¶18).

The 16 MINOR items are quick edits. After the MAJOR fixes, E is fit for integration.

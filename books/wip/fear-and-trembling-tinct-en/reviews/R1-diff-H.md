# Part H: before → after R1 fixes (for re-verification)

## ch7 ¶22 (text changed)

**before**

> By means of the demonic, then, the merman would be the single individual who as the single individual was higher than the universal. The demonic has the same property as the divine: the single individual can come into an absolute relation to it. Here we have the analogy, the counterpart, of that paradox we are discussing. It therefore has a certain likeness, and the likeness can deceive. Thus the merman apparently has this proof that his silence is justified: in it he suffers all his pain. Nevertheless there is no doubt that he can speak. So he can become a tragic hero — in my view a grandiose tragic hero — if he speaks. Only a few, perhaps, would comprehend where the grandeur lies. Then he will have the courage to wrest from himself every self-deception that he can make Agnete happy by his art, and the courage — in human terms — to crush Agnete. Incidentally, I will just make a psychological observation here. The more selfish Agnete’s development has been, the more dazzling the self-deception will be. Indeed, it is not unthinkable that it could happen in reality that a merman, by his demonic cunning, had — humanly speaking — not only saved an Agnete but drawn something extraordinary out of her. A demon, after all, knows how to torment strength out of even the weakest human being, and in his own way he can mean very well by a person.

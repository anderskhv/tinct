## 2. New-defect check on the changed text, sentence by sentence

### ¶15

Each changed sentence was re-read against the Danish:
- "1) Should he keep silent and hold the wedding, thinking: …" (*tie og holde Bryllup, og tænke*). Accurate.
- "presumably" (*vel*), "are" (*styres*), "the lovers themselves" (*dem selv*). Accurate.
- "contains an offense against the girl and the reality of her love". This renders "en Fornærmelse mod Pigen og Realiteten af hendes Kjærlighed" with a single *mod*, and *Realitet* → reality follows the ruling. Accurate.

**N1 — MINOR (English/voice, optional).**
- Danish: "thi i saa Fald har han **fornærmet** Pigen."
- Draft: "he has **offended against** the girl."
- The problem: in modern English "offend against" normally takes a law or principle, not a person, so with a person as object it sounds stilted or biblical.
- The consistency fix X.1 required only the noun to be uniform. The verb does not have to match it.
- Fix, either of:
  - "he has **wronged** the girl" (the r0 verb, which gives the injury sense);
  - "he has **offended** the girl" (closer to the noun).
- Either one is fine. Changing it does not create a new run with the Danish.

**N2 — MINOR (English, optional).**

**C1 — ¶17: "Every single person" for *enhver Enkelt*.**
- This text is unchanged since r0, and R1 passed it.
- The ruling for ordinary *en Enkelt* is "an individual / someone", and "single" is kept for the fixed term.
- Here "Every single person" sits among six uses of "the single individual" (*den Enkelte*) in the same paragraph. The shared word "single" invites the reader to link the two terms, which is exactly what the ruling aims to prevent.
- Fix: "**Any individual** will be able to understand the outcome just as well as the hero", or "**Anyone** will be able to understand…".

**C2 — informational only (no action).**
- *hidse* has two renderings:
  - n7.27a "inflames the merman";
  - ¶21 "stir up all the dark passions".
- *hidse* is not in the fixed table, and the two contexts differ (a person versus passions), so this is acceptable.

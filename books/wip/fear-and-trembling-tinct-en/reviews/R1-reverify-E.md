## 2. New defects in changed paragraphs (sentence by sentence)

¶19, ¶21, ¶22, ¶26 and ¶27 are clean. Every sentence in them matches the Danish, with no omissions, additions or shifts.

### N1 — ¶24, "if you can believe it" (MINOR, ambiguity / voice)

- **Danish:** "I Kraft af det Absurde skal Du faae hver en Hvid igjen, kan Du troe det."
- **Draft:** "By virtue of the absurd you will get every last penny back, if you can believe it."
- **Problem:** at the end of a sentence, "if you can believe it" is an everyday English aside meaning "believe it or not". A reader can still take the knight of faith as marvelling at his own promise rather than stating its condition. That is the misreading M1 was raised to prevent. The Danish has only the conditional sense.
- **Fix:** "By virtue of the absurd you will get every last penny back, provided you can believe it." This is unambiguously conditional and keeps *troe* as "believe", as the standard allows.

### N2 — ¶33, "I would recall that…" / "Then I would point out…" (MINOR, meaning shift + lost repetition)

- **Danish:** "Jeg vilde minde om, at Reisen varede 3 Dage…" / "Da vilde jeg minde om, hvad der er min Mening, at…"
- **Draft:** "I would recall that the journey took…" / "Then I would point out — for this is my opinion — that…"
- **Problems:**
  - In modern English, "I would recall" means "I would remember". *Minde om* is outward: it means to draw attention to something or remind of it. The speaker is planning what he would *say*.
  - The Danish repeats the same verb as a structural anaphora for the two points of the imagined sermon. The draft uses two different verbs.
- **Fix:** "I would point out that the journey took three days and a good way into the fourth…" / "Then I would point out — for this is my opinion — that…". This adds no audience, keeps the repetition and restores the outward sense.

### NITs (optional)

- **NIT-1, ¶18, *fatte*.** In ¶18, *fatte* is "understood", while ¶27 uses "comprehend" three times and keeps "understood" for *forstaae*. Both renderings are allowed, but "He has comprehended the deep secret" would keep *fatte* uniform across E.
- **NIT-2, ¶24, *Endelighed*.** "something finite" renders *nogen Endelighed*; the term table has "finitude". "every time some bit of finitude threatens to get the better of me" would keep the term.
- **NIT-3, ¶28, *lader … dømme*.** "which I let my deed, not my word, pass judgment on" is grammatical and follows the natural reading of *som jeg ikke lader mit Ord men min Gjerning dømme*, with deed as the judge. Contrary to R1-applied-E, though, it does *not* keep an ambiguity: the causative-passive reading ("by which I let my deed be judged") is closed off. This is acceptable, since that reading is the less likely one. No change is required, but the applied log overstates the case.
- **¶18, m1 wording.** "to forget all that one's life contained" loses the verbal echo of *Livs Indhold* ("the whole content of his life" later in the same paragraph). The meaning is intact and the change was made to break a screen run. Acceptable.

- **23-1 Consistency / image, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "paa en dialektisk Spidse"
  - English: "on a dialectical knife-edge"
  - Problems:
    - Draft F renders the explicitly related image in ¶13 ("holder jeg dem paa Spidsen") as "on the point".
    - "Knife-edge" adds a precariousness that *Spidse* (point, tip) does not state.
  - Fix: "The merman stands on a dialectical point." Alternatively, harmonize both ¶13 and ¶23 at book level.
- **23-2 AMBIGUITY FLATTENED / consistency, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "frelses ud af det Dæmoniske i Angeren"
  - English: "saved out of the demonic that lies in repentance"
  - Problems:
    - Draft G renders the source phrase in ¶20 ("Det Dæmoniske i Angeren") as "The demonic in repentance".
    - "that lies in" is a gloss. It also closes off the reading "saved, in repentance, out of the demonic".
  - Fix: "If he is saved out of the demonic in repentance". This matches ¶20 and keeps the Danish's openness.
- **23-3 MEANING SHIFT (hedge strengthened), MINOR.**
  - Danish: "Saaledes vilde **vel** Middelalderen gjøre Bevægelsen"
  - English: "This, **no doubt**, is how the Middle Ages would make the movement"
  - Problem: *vel* is a softening "presumably" or "I suppose". "No doubt" asserts certainty.
  - Fix: "This is presumably how the Middle Ages would make the movement".
- **23-4 ENGLISH, MINOR.** The same issue occurs in ¶25.
  - Danish: "hjemfalden til Klosteret" and "hjemfalden til det Comiske"
  - English: "plainly forfeit to the monastery" and "no age has been so forfeit to the comic"
  - Problem: the drafter's aim of keeping the echo is sound, but "forfeit to" is not idiomatic English, especially with "so". The sense is "falls by right to" or "has fallen prey to".
  - Fix: "the merman plainly falls by right to the monastery" and "no age has fallen so completely to the comic". Alternatively, use "belongs by forfeit to" in both places.
- **23-5 OMISSION (connective), MINOR.**

  - English: "…merman's life); on that score he has been saved."
  - Problem: the *thi* gives the reason the reading is excluded, and it is dropped.
  - Fix: "…the merman's life) — for in that respect he is already saved."
- **23-6 DISTINCTION BLUR, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "et **æsthetisk** Redningsforsøg"
  - English: "**aesthetics'** way of attempting a rescue"
  - Problem: the standard keeps the adjective "aesthetic" apart from "aesthetics", the discipline. The English turns the adjective into a possessive of the discipline.
  - Fix: "this is an aesthetic attempt at rescue, which always sidesteps the main point: the continuity of the merman's life".
- **23-7 ADDITION (gloss) / AMBIGUITY, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "conditio sine qua non"
  - English: "— the condition without which it cannot be met"
  - Problems:
    - "it cannot be met" introduces an unclear referent ("it" is the requirement? the universal?).
    - The Latin means only "the condition without which not".
    - Separately, "det er en Modsigelse af det Almene" is resolved to "the universal contradicts itself". That is defensible, but "a contradiction on the part of the universal" would keep the genitive's openness.
  - Fix: "— the indispensable condition". Optionally also: "because it is a contradiction on the part of the universal to want to demand itself of someone who lacks …".
- **23-8 ENGLISH / AMBIGUITY, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "Dersom Philosophien … ogsaa **tænkte paa**, at det kunde falde et Menneske ind …"
  - English: "If philosophy … also **thought that** it might occur …"
  - Problem: "thought that" reads as "believed that". *tænke paa* means "consider" or "bear in mind".
  - Fix: "If philosophy, among other things, also gave a thought to the possibility that it might occur to someone to want to act on its teaching …".

- **26-1 Terminology / echo, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "fordi Angerens Lidenskab **ikke** blev sat i **Bevægelse**"
  - English: "since in it the passion of repentance is **never** set in **motion**"
  - Problems:
    - The Danish deliberately echoes ¶25's "Angerens Bevægelse". The standard says to keep "movement" even where "move" or "motion" would be idiomatic.
    - "never" intensifies *ikke*.
  - Fix: "since in it the passion of repentance is not set in movement". Alternatively: "since no movement of the passion of repentance is set going". At minimum, change "never" to "not".
- **26-2 OMISSION (Greek gloss), MINOR.**
  - Danish: "πάντως γὰρ οὐδεὶς …"
  - English: "for no one has ever escaped Eros …"
  - Problem: the gloss drops πάντως ("assuredly", "at all events").
  - Fix: "for assuredly no one has ever escaped Eros, or ever will, …".
- **26-3 ENGLISH, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "Det er **tungt** …", "uudsigeligt **tungt**", "Det er **tungt**, om en Pige hengav sig"
  - English: "It is heavy …"
  - Problem: "heavy" used predicatively for a situation is unidiomatic (and slangy in modern English).
  - Fix: "It is hard …; it is unspeakably hard …". Alternatively "grievous", used consistently.
- **26-4 OMISSION (connectives), MINOR.**
  - Danish: "…ikke at finde den, til hvem man kan hengive sig, **men** det er uudsigeligt tungt …" and "…nu er hun ikke fri mere, **men** Sara var aldrig fri …"
  - English: a semicolon in the first case and a sentence break in the second.
  - Problem: both *men* are dropped. The epigram chain depends on "but" (the third *men* is kept).
  - Fix: "It is hard not to find someone to give oneself to, but it is unspeakably hard …" and "…Now she is no longer free. But Sarah was never free …".
- **26-5 VOICE (rhetorical question) + MEANING SHIFT, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "Hvilken **Verdens Sorg** ligger der **ikke** i det Følgende, da Tobias endelig vil ægte hende?"
  - English: "How much of the world's sorrow lies in what follows …!"
  - Problems:
    - The rhetorical question has become an exclamation, which the standard forbids ("keep every rhetorical question").
    - *en Verdens Sorg* is the idiom "a world of sorrow", not "the world's sorrow".
  - Fix: "What a world of sorrow is there not in what follows, when Tobias at last means to marry her?" For more natural English: "Is there not a world of sorrow in what follows, when Tobias at last means to marry her?"
- **26-6 Wordplay loss, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "Ingen Pige er bleven **bedragen** som Sara; thi hun blev **bedragen for** …, **bedragen for** …", then "**bedrage** Moderen for …"
  - English: "deceived", then "cheated of".
  - Problem: the *thi* clause is meant to explain the "deceived", and the verb switch cuts the link the reader needs.
  - Fix: either "cheated" throughout this sentence ("No girl has been cheated as Sarah was; for she was cheated of …"), or "deceived … deceived of …".
  - Also: "For first there had to be the smoking" drops *jo* ("after all", "you see").
  - Optional: "for after all, first there had to be the smoking".

  - English: "“Thank you, I am no friend of …”"
  - Problem: *Tak* here is the ironic refusal "No, thanks". "Thank you" can read as gratitude.
  - Fix: "“No, thank you — I am no friend of ceremonies and fuss …”". The shift from the Danish's "who has *my* delight" to "his delight" is acceptable normalisation.
- **28-5 AMBIGUITY FLATTENED, MINOR.**
  - Danish: "eller om **Forklaringen**"
  - English: "or of their explanation"
  - Problem: the Danish has the definite article only. It may mean the explanation of the horrors, or *the* explanation (of existence). The drafter's resolution closes this off.
  - Fix: "or of the explanation".

## X. Cross-part observations (outside H; not counted)

1. **Draft G ¶18: an ADDITION likely to be flagged MAJOR in G's review.**
   - G's text reads: "a sketch in the direction of the demonic — the dark counterpart of the divine, which, like the divine, works through silence."
   - The Danish has only "en Skizze … i Retning af det Dæmoniske". The gloss is interpretive ("works through silence" is not in the source).
   - It also pre-empts, in different terms, the analogy that ¶22 itself states ("The demonic has the same property as the divine …"). If the first-use definition of "the demonic" must stay at ¶18, it should come from the text.
2. **¶13 (F) "on the point" versus ¶23 (H) "knife-edge".** Harmonize (see 23-1).
3. **¶20 (G) "The demonic in repentance" versus ¶23 (H) "the demonic that lies in repentance".** Harmonize (see 23-2).
4. ***martre*: "torture" in G and I, "tormented" in H.** *Qval* is fixed as "torment", so *martre* should be "torture" book-wide (see 25-8).
5. ***Virkelighed*: "reality" or "actuality".** This needs a book-level decision (see 25-3).
6. **Tobit's "Sarah".** The single form is fine. I confirm there is no confusion in ¶26–28, because the Tobit context is explicit.

## Overall judgment

**Accept after minor revisions. There are no BLOCKER or MAJOR defects.**

The draft is complete and faithful, and the altered-legend material and Johannes's voice come through intact:

- his first person ("in my view", "I would bet a hundred to one", "not even I … can utter her name");
- his irony about "our age", which has "arrived at the highest";
- the Tobit re-casting, with Sarah as the heroine.

The round-2 anti-overlap rewrites introduced no meaning drift of substance. Where they cost something, they cost connectives (*thi*/*men* dropped in ¶23, ¶25, ¶26 and ¶27) and a few literalisms ("forfeit to", "heavy", "held up", "Where he has sinned").

Priority fixes are:
- 25-1 and 23-5 (the dropped *thi*);
- 25-6 ("many a single individual");
- 26-5 (question → exclamation; "world of sorrow");
- 23-3 (*vel* → "no doubt");
- n34-1 ("precisely as such");
- 28-2;
- the harmonization items 23-1, 23-2 and 25-8.

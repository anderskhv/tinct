## 2. Changed paragraphs: new or residual defects

Severity scale: BLOCKER > MAJOR > MINOR > NIT. **New** means the R1 edit introduced the defect. **Residual** means it was already in r0 and R1 did not catch it.

### ¶0 (changed)

The paragraph is clean against the Danish. The four *dersom*/"suppose" beats are intact, and the chiasmus "as it were" / "as it were" is exact. "struggling … with" and "shall" are right.

- **N6 — NIT (new; this is what R1 asked for).** "the longer time went on, the more faithfully he clings to him." The Danish tense mix (*gik … hænger*) is kept on purpose to carry the "Time went on" refrain. An English reader may take it for a typo. **Fix:** accept it as it stands. If it is to be smoothed, use "the longer time goes on", which gives up the refrain.
- **N9 — NIT (new).** "He takes nothing from what is his own." This can be misread as "he deprives his own property of nothing". The sense is right, and the Johannine "take of" echo is weak but present. **Optional fix:** "He takes nothing of his own; he is jealous, rather, of what was entrusted to him."
- **N11a — NIT (residual, cross-paragraph).** *minde om* (¶0) → "call to mind", while *ethvert Tungemaal minder Dig* (¶13) → "every language remembers you". The ¶13 line hands the eulogist's ¶0 office to every tongue, and the echo is lost. **Optional fix, ¶13:** "for every language calls you to mind".

### ¶1 (changed)

This paragraph is now exact. "greatness" (*Storhed*) and "magnitude" (*Størrelse*) are kept apart, and "grew greater" and "was greater" follow *blev* and *var*.

- **N3 — MINOR, refrain (residual).** "No! **Not one** who was great in the world shall be forgotten" answers ¶0 "So **no one** who was great shall be forgotten". The Danish repeats *Ingen … der var stor* on purpose: the "Nei!" takes up the previous sentence. **Fix:** "No! No one who was great in the world shall be forgotten."
- **N7 — NIT (new wording, which R1 permitted).** "the one who loved himself grew great by himself". Idiomatically, "by himself" reads as "alone, unaided", while the Danish *ved sig selv* means "through himself, the object he loved". The context softens the problem but does not remove it. **Optional fix:** leave it as it is. "grew great through himself" is better only if all the "great by" phrases change with it, and that would be worse.

### ¶3 (changed)

The refrains are intact:
- "Time went on; the possibility was there; Abraham had faith. Time went on; it became unreasonable; Abraham had faith."
- "held fast to the promise" → "hold it fast" → "hold fast to the temporal".
- "young enough" appears 3 times.
- "fulfilled" leads into "the fulfillment of the promise".
- "deceive" and "deceived" match.

- **N4 — MINOR, voice/parallel (new).** Danish: "I udvortes Henseende **ligger det Vidunderlige i, at** det skete efter deres Forventning, i dybere Forstand **ligger Troens Vidunder i, at** Abraham og Sara vare unge nok til at ønske, **og at** Troen havde bevaret …". English: "Outwardly, the wonder is that it happened as they had expected. In a deeper sense, faith's wonder is this: Abraham and Sarah were young enough to wish, and faith had preserved their wish, and so their youth."
  - The recast was made to avoid an echo flagged by the independence screen, and it breaks the matched "lies in … that" pair. That pair is the whole point of the outward/deeper contrast.
  - The colon also turns the two *at* clauses into bare assertions.
  - **Fix:** "Outwardly, the wonder lies in its having happened as they expected; in a deeper sense, faith's wonder lies in this: that Abraham and Sarah were young enough to wish, and that faith had preserved their wish, and with it their youth." Re-run the screen after the change. A matched "lies in" pair is a forced rendering.
- **N8 — NIT (new).** The Danish "for med stor Besværlighed at **faae det opfyldt**" means "to get it fulfilled": the person whose wish was refused is the one who receives it. "so that with great difficulty it may be fulfilled in old age" drops that person. **Optional fix:** "so that with great difficulty you may have it fulfilled in old age?". This mirrors "refused you".
- **N12 — NIT (residual).** The third "Time went on" in "Time went on, evening came near, and he was not so wretched …" uses commas and "and", while the Abraham pair uses semicolons. The Danish uses commas throughout. This is acceptable, because the sentence continues differently.
- **Hedge note.** *vel* is "probably" here, as R1 proposed, and "presumably" in ¶2 and ¶7. The difference is acceptable and is not counted.

  **Optional fix:** "if … he had by chance spotted the ram … his salvation a matter of chance, his reward disgrace, and his future, perhaps, perdition." A lighter version keeps "rescue" and changes only "an accident" to "chance".

The Danish mirrors *Ingen skal glemmes*: no one shall be forgotten, and he shall never forget. Now that "shall" is fixed for the *skal glemmes* refrain, "he shall never forget" would keep the reversal. This fix is optional.

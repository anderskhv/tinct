# Richard III — PLAY-NOTE (supplements SHAKESPEARE-BRIEF.md)

Chapters are scenes: ch1 = 1.1 ... ch4 = 1.4, ch5-8 = 2.1-2.4, ch9-15 = 3.1-3.7, ch16-20 = 4.1-4.5, ch21-25 = 5.1-5.5. Ids below are `chapter:paragraph` (0-based). Work from `served-original-en.json`, never from `served-live-modern-en.json` for stage directions or structure (see section 4).

## 1. Speakers and naming

Copy each prefix exactly as the source has it. Inside speeches use one name form per person.

Family, as speeches rely on it:
- **York:** the late Duke of York (killed at Wakefield; the Duchess's husband) fathered Edward IV, Clarence and Richard, plus Rutland (the youngest son, killed in the wars; the Duchess names him in 19:24). "Son of York" in 1:1 = King Edward. Warwick ("Warwick", Clarence's father-in-law) briefly fought for Lancaster. Edward's children: Edward V, Richard of York, young Elizabeth.
- **Lancaster:** Henry VI ("Harry") and his wife Margaret; their son Edward, Prince of Wales, killed at Tewksbury, was Anne's first husband. Anne's father-in-law Henry VI was killed in the Tower by Richard.
- **Woodville kin** (Queen Elizabeth's side): Rivers, Grey, Dorset. Rivers and Grey, with Vaughan, die at Pomfret (ch11).
- **Richmond** is the Lancastrian heir; Stanley is his stepfather.

## 2. Fixed terms

- **protector** (Lord Protector, 2:2 onward): keep "Protector"; Richard rules for the boy king.
- **the Tower**: "the Tower" or "the Tower of London", never "the prison"; "Tower" appears in Clarence's death, Henry's murder, the princes' fate. Pomfret = "Pomfret Castle", keep.
- **York and Lancaster**: "house of York", "house of Lancaster"; the white rose and red rose if named. Keep "Plantagenet" (the family name, also a pun in 1:2:57).
- **the boar** (Richard's emblem, a white boar; Hastings's and Stanley's dreams, 10:13, 12:34, 23:97): keep "boar", never replace with "pig"; "the wretched, bloody, usurping boar" is Richmond's name for Richard (22:1). Always "boar", with "hog" only when the source says it.
- **curse and prophecy**: curse, prophesy, "Margaret's curse"; the repeated payoffs (Hastings 12:34, Rivers 11:6-7) must still echo her earlier words, so render "curse" the same way each time.
- **sanctuary**: keep "sanctuary" (the Queen's refuge at Westminster); a place where one cannot be seized.
- **Bosworth Field**: keep as a name; "Bosworth" in the title of ch23.
- Also fixed: "crown", "coronation", "Majesty / Grace / Highness" as forms of address (keep "Your Grace" for Dukes and for Richard); "Lord Chamberlain"; "Marquess"; "bastard" (Richard's slur on Edward's sons); "Iwis" = indeed; "Jesu" = Jesus.

## 3. Hardest passages

1. **1:1 (paragraph 1)** "Now is the winter of our discontent": the famous opening. Say "son of York" = King Edward; "this" sun/son pun; unpack "barbed steeds" (armored horses), "descant on my deformity", "determined to prove a villain". Keep the winter/summer image. Do not summarize.
2. **1:1 Clarence and Richard** (ch1 around 3-12): the Tower arrest, "Because my name is George", prophecy (G), and "this is what happens when men are ruled by women" (1:9).
3. **2:40-105 the wooing of Lady Anne**: stichomythia, puns ("Where is he?" "Here", 2:61; "Plantagenet", 2:57; "I would they were [basilisks]"). Anne's insults (toad, devil, homicide) stay strong. Keep 2:103 "Was ever woman in this humour wooed? Was ever woman in this humour won?" recognizable.
4. **3:36-101 Margaret and the court**: asides split over several paragraphs (3:38-66); the long curse (3:67 and 3:78-101) must name each target: Richard, Elizabeth, Dorset, Rivers, Hastings.
5. **3:87-88** Richard's "'Tis done by me, and ends in Margaret" and Elizabeth's "Thus have you breathed your curse against yourself": the curse-trap pun on the name.
6. **4:4-10 Clarence's dream**: drowning at sea, the Tower, Gloucester stumbling; "wedges of gold", "sour ferryman" (Charon), "Warwick" and "Prince Edward's" ghost. The dream is the richest imagery of the play; keep every image.
7. **4:15-103 the murderers** (prose, colloquial): the Second Murderer's conscience ("Zounds, he dies!"), the "judgement day" joke, "my voice is now the King's, my looks mine own." Soldier-register, short and blunt; keep the jokes. Zounds becomes a strong oath. Clarence's pleading speeches (4:63, 71, 79) are formal and long.
8. **9:25-39 the Prince and Richard's asides**: "formal Vice, Iniquity", "I moralize two meanings in one word" (the morality-play Vice who says one thing and means another). "So wise so young, they say, do never live long."
9. **12:27-33 Hastings accused**: "If? Thou protector of this damned strumpet, Talk'st thou to me of ifs?" Keep Richard's fury and the "withered arm".
10. **15:40-58 Richard "refuses" the crown**: hypocrisy: he holds a prayer book between two bishops. Say so plainly only where the text does.
11. **19:17-36 Margaret and the queens** and **19:23-33**: prophecy fulfilled ("I called thee then, vain flourish of my fortune"). Keep the repeated "thou hadst ... I had" structure.
12. **19:84-157 Richard woos Queen Elizabeth for her daughter**: single-line stichomythia; oaths ("by my George", "by the world", "by myself", "by God") stripped one by one (19:133-146), and "I bury them in your daughter's womb" (19:153). Keep every turn of the exchange 1:1. Elizabeth's "But how long shall that title 'ever' last?" must stay clear.
13. **23:50-109 the ghost scene**: ghosts alternate "Despair and die!" (refrain) with a blessing for Richmond; each ghost addresses Richard first, then Richmond. Keep the refrain identical in every ghost speech. "Think on the Tower and me" (23:61).
14. **23:114 Richard wakes**: "Give me another horse! Bind up my wounds!"; "coward conscience", "I am I", "Richard loves Richard" soliloquy. The self-accusing lines ("What do I fear? Myself?") must stay a sequence of short questions and answers.
15. **24:4 "A horse! A horse! My kingdom for a horse!"** Leave nearly as is. Surrounding battle prose stays short and urgent.

## 4. Oddities in the served text

- **Speeches split across paragraphs.** The source has "bare prefix" paragraphs (just `QUEEN MARGARET.` or `GHOST OF EDWARD.`) followed by a separate paragraph with the speech. Bare-prefix ids: 3:40,44,48,51,54,57,63; 8:36; 9:28,37; 10:3,6,61; 15:57; 16:34; 17:18,20; 19:5,9,13,19; 23:52,59,66,73,76,78,81,86,93,100,107,153. Keep each bare prefix paragraph exactly as is (it is only the prefix) and render the next paragraph as its speech; do not add a prefix to continuation paragraphs.
- **Unprefixed continuation paragraphs** also occur after a stage direction inside a speech (e.g. 2:63 "Why dost thou spit at me?", 3:39, 3:42, 3:59, 23:142, 23:152). Render them as speech without a prefix.
- **Orations and stage-direction paragraphs without brackets**: "Enter Richard ..." (1:0), "Alarum. ...", "His oration to his soldiers." (23:132) and "His oration to his army." (23:156) are copied verbatim; the oration text follows in its own paragraph (23:133).
- **Missing periods after prefix** in ch11:3-4: `VAUGHAN You live ...`, `RATCLIFFE Dispatch ...`. Copy the prefix exactly as written, no period added.
- **Bracket styles differ from the live modern.** The source uses `[_Aside._]`, `[_Exit._]`; the live modern edition uses `[Aside.]` and capitalized names. Follow the source (verbatim, underscores kept), not the live edition.
- Some paragraphs hold two speakers' lines run together by the source's line breaks; some stage directions sit in the middle of a verse line (e.g. 3:66 `[_Coming forward._]` is its own paragraph). Keep them as they stand.
- Spellings: "Jesu", "Iwis", "'sblood" absent; "Zounds" appears at 4:39 and 23:117.

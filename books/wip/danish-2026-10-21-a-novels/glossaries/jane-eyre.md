# Jane Eyre: Danish glossary (new edition)

> **Batch rules override anything below:** speech-tag comma goes OUTSIDE the closing quote (»Victor«, sagde han); English miles → »engelsk(e) mil«, feet → »fod«, yards → »alen«/»skridt«, never metric; »alle sammen« in two words; dashes are spaced en dashes ( – ), not em dashes.


Source: modern English edition (38 ch., 4,034 ¶). Compared with the old Danish edition (4,047 ¶, mostly ¶-aligned). Alignment drifts in ch. 12–13, 28, 33–34 and 37, where the old Danish follows Brontë's original wording or adds sentences (e.g. 37: "sagde en stemme. Det var John."). **Translate the modern English, never the old Danish.** Ch. 34 of the English source is unmodernised original text with curly quotes and `_emphasis_`, so give it the same modern register as the rest.

## 1. Conventions: what the old Danish did and the rule to use

| Item | Old Danish | Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mr./Mrs. | `Mr.` ×483, `Mrs.` ×248, plus `hr.`/`Hr.`/`herr` ×340; mixed even for one person (hr. Rivers / Mr. Rivers, hr. St. John) | Inconsistent; capitals wrong | `mr.`, `mrs.` in lowercase before a name; capital only at sentence start. Never `hr.` or `herr` before English names |
| Miss | `Miss` ×113 and `frøken` ×214, mixed (Miss Temple / frøken Temple) | Inconsistent | `miss` + name (miss Eyre, miss Temple, miss Ingram). Bare vocative "Miss" → `frøken` |
| sir (vocative) | `sir` ×200 and `hr.` (»Ja, hr.«, »Hr., jeg …«) | `hr.` used alone is not Danish | Keep `sir` in lowercase. Never `hr.` without a name, never `herre` |
| Sir + name | `Sir George` | Capital | `sir George Lynn` |
| Lady / Lord | `Lady Ingram` | Capital | `lady Ingram`, `lord Ingram`. "the Dowager" → enkefruen |
| madam, ma'am | `frue` | OK | `frue` (vocative) |
| Master John / Master Reed | `hr. John`, `hr. Reed` | Wrong: he is 14 | `unge hr. John`, `unge hr. Reed` (1.22: »Sig: ›Hvad ønsker De, unge hr. Reed?‹«) |
| the Missus / the Misses Reed / Miss Jane | fruen / frøknerne Reed / Miss Jane | Mixed | `fruen` / `frøknerne Reed` / `miss Jane` (Bessie's word for Jane) |
| Madame, Mademoiselle | Madame Pierrot; Adèle's »Mademoiselle« | OK | `madame Pierrot`. Adèle's French vocative stays `mademoiselle` in lowercase |
| Quotation marks | »…« in 34 chapters, straight "…" in ch. 10, 16, 19 | Inconsistent | »…« for dialogue. Inner quotes use ›…‹ (old used '…') |
| Emphasis | All `_x_` dropped (34 in English, all in ch. 34) | Loss | Keep `_…_` on the matching Danish word (_skal_, _din_) |
| Dashes | — with spaces | OK | Keep the em dash. Use "——" for blanked names (——shire, Martha G——) |
| Possessive -s | Rochesters, St. Johns | OK | Danish -s, no apostrophe (Rochesters, Adèles). Names ending in s/x/z take an apostrophe: Burns', Briggs' |
| Units of distance | »halvtreds mil«, »to mil« | **False friend**: a Danish mil is 10 km, so 50 mil reads as 500 km | `engelske mil` on first mention in a chapter, then `mil` may be used. Never convert to km |
| yards, feet | Often turned into »meter« | Anachronistic | Vague distances → `et par skridt` / `få skridt`. Exact measures → `fod`, `tommer`, `alen` |
| Money | pund, shilling, penny, pence | OK | `pund`, `shilling` (pl. `shilling`: "fem shilling", not "shillings"), `penny`/`pence`, `guinea(s)`. "a sovereign instead of ten pounds" → "et enkelt pund". £20,000 → `tyve tusind pund` |
| French (Adèle, Céline, Rochester) | Kept French where the English keeps it. Dropped accents (»C'est la ma …«) | Mostly OK | Copy the English source exactly: keep the French it keeps (with accents: C'est là, Revenez bientôt, n'est-ce pas), translate what it translates. Add no new glosses |
| Dialect (Hannah, Bessie, Leah, the farmer) | Plain Danish | OK | The modern English has already flattened the dialect. Use plain, slightly colloquial Danish (»Nå, pige«, »ikke«). Never a Danish regional dialect (jysk, fynsk) |
| Archaic register | Some stiff calques (»Meget vel, sir«, »forlangte han at vide«, »han fo'r sammen«) | Stiff | Use modern but not slangy Danish. Avoid »thi«, »eder«, »I« as polite plural, and verb plurals |
| Spelling | Adele ×11, Adela ×4 | Adele is wrong | Always `Adèle`. `Adela` only in mrs. Fairfax's own speech (11.60 »miss Adela«), as the English has it |

## 2. Forms of address (du/De)

The old edition switches between du and De mid-scene: St. John says De/du in one paragraph (30.19–20, 31.12), and Jane says »du, Mr. Rivers« (32.22). Fix it with this table. De marks distance and rank; the change to du is a plot event.

| Speaker → addressee | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mrs. Reed ↔ Jane (child, ch. 1–4) | du ↔ du | Family. Jane says »tante« / »mrs. Reed« |
| Mrs. Reed ↔ adult Jane (ch. 21) | du ↔ du | Unchanged: aunt and niece |
| Eliza, Georgiana ↔ adult Jane | De ↔ De | They call her »miss Eyre« (21.119). Cold on purpose |
| John Reed → Jane | du. Jane → John: De | He demands »De, unge hr. Reed« |
| Bessie, Abbot → child Jane | du. Child Jane → Bessie: du | |
| Bessie ↔ adult Jane (ch. 10) | du ↔ du + »miss Jane« | Old familiarity, kept on both sides |
| Robert Leaven → Jane / Jane → Robert | De + »frøken« / du | Coachman; Jane knew him as a child |
| Brocklehurst → Jane | du. Jane → him: De | |
| Brocklehurst ↔ Mrs. Reed, Miss Temple | De | |
| Helen Burns ↔ Jane | du | Schoolgirls |
| Miss Temple → Jane | du (pupil). Later as colleague, either way: du | Jane → Temple: De + »miss Temple« |
| Teachers → pupils | du. Pupils → teachers: De | |
| Mrs. Fairfax ↔ Jane | De ↔ De | Housekeeper and governess are equals in rank |
| Jane → Adèle / Adèle → Jane | du / De + »mademoiselle« | Adèle's French: `vous` → De |
| Jane ↔ Leah, John, Mary (servants) | De ↔ De | Adult strangers. Servants add »frøken«/»frue« |
| Rochester → Jane, ch. 12–22 | De | Employer to governess. The old used du from ch. 20; change it to De |
| Rochester as the gypsy (ch. 19) | du | The fortune-teller's familiarity is part of the disguise |
| Rochester → Jane, from ch. 23 | du | Switch at his first »Janet« in the orchard (23.45–48). Keep du for the rest of the book |
| Jane → Rochester, whole book | De + »sir« | Jane never stops saying "sir", even at Ferndean (37.31–41). The asymmetry is intended. Do not move her to du |
| Jane → »Edward« (24, 37) | De with the first name | Only where the English has it |
| Rochester ↔ Blanche Ingram, guests, Mason | De | |
| St. John, ch. 28–32 (stranger, then employer) | De ↔ De | Jane says »mr. Rivers« |
| St. John ↔ Jane, from the cousin reveal (33) | du ↔ du | Switch at the reveal »I tre er altså mine fætre og kusiner?« (33.107). Jane then says »St. John« |

| Rosamond Oliver ↔ Jane / St. John | De / De | |

| "Reader, I married him" (38.0) | **»Læser, jeg giftede mig med ham.«** | Keep it short and plain. It is the most quoted line |

## 3. Names, places, terms

**People** (keep the English spelling; Danish genitive -s): Jane Eyre · Edward Fairfax Rochester · Bertha Antoinetta Mason · Richard Mason · Grace Poole · Adèle Varens · Céline Varens · Sophie · Mrs. Fairfax → mrs. Fairfax · Leah · John and Mary (Ferndean) · Pilot (dog) · Mesrour (horse) · Mrs. Reed · John, Eliza, Georgiana Reed · Bessie Lee, later Leaven (mrs. Leaven) · Abbot · Mr. Lloyd · Robert Leaven · Brocklehurst (Naomi, Augusta) · Miss Temple · Miss Scatcherd · Miss Miller · Miss Smith · Helen Burns · Mr. Wood, Mr. Briggs · Blanche, Mary and Lady Ingram · Dent, Eshton, Lynn · St. John, Diana, Mary Rivers · Hannah · Carlo (St. John's dog) · Rosamond Oliver · Alice Wood · John Eyre (uncle).
- "St. John": write `St. John` with the full stop. Genitive is `St. Johns`.
- `Janet` (Rochester's pet name) → keep **Janet**; do not turn it into "Jane". `Jeannette` (Adèle's French) → keep.
- "my pale little elf / mustard-seed" → »min blege lille alf / mit sennepsfrø« (the old wrote »min sennepsfrø«: wrong gender).

**Places**: keep all English names: Gateshead (Hall) · Lowood · Brocklebridge (Church) · Thornfield (Hall) · Hay · Millcote · the Leas → Leas · Whitcross · Morton · Moor House = Marsh End (keep both; they are the same house) · Vale Hall · Ferndean (Manor) · Madeira, Funchal · Jamaica, Spanish Town · Bitternutt Lodge, Connaught · Marseille (not "Marseilles") · India → Indien · county → `grevskab` · ——shire → ——shire.

## 4. Verse, songs, Bible, letters

General rule: each stanza is its own paragraph in the source; keep one stanza per paragraph and the same line order, and never join verse lines with `/` (the `/` in the table below only shows line breaks). Translate as verse (metre plus rhyme where possible), not as prose. The old rendered Bessie's ballad as prose with grammar errors (»den stakkels forældreløse barns«) and **left Rochester's song in English** (24.156–165). Both must be redone.

## 5. Chapter titles

- English `Chapter N` → **`Kapitel N`** for all 38 (the old matches this).
- The only exception: `Chapter 38 — Conclusion` → **`Kapitel 38 — Afslutning`** (old OK; keep the spaced em dash).
- There are no other titled chapters.

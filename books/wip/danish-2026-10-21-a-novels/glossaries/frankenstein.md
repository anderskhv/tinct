# Frankenstein: Danish glossary (new edition)

> **Batch rules override anything below:** speech-tag comma goes OUTSIDE the closing quote (»Victor«, sagde han); English miles → »engelsk(e) mil«, feet → »fod«, yards → »alen«/»skridt«, never metric; »alle sammen« in two words; dashes are spaced en dashes ( – ), not em dashes.


Source: modern English edition (28 chapters: Letter 1–4, Chapter 1–24), compared paragraph by paragraph with the old Danish edition. Old = old Danish edition. Rec. = recommended rule.

## 1. Conventions

| Topic | Old Danish did | Rec. rule |
|---|---|---|
| English honorifics | `Mrs. Saville`, `Mr. Kirwin` (capitalised) | Lowercase in running text: mrs. Saville, mr. Kirwin. Capitalise only at sentence start. |
| French honorifics | `Monsieur Clerval/Frankenstein`, `Madame Moritz` (capitalised) | monsieur Clerval, monsieur Frankenstein, madame Moritz. If an abbreviation is needed: m. Krempe. |
| Miss | frøken Mansfield | Keep frøken Mansfield. It reads well and is good Danish. |
| Professor | Mixed `Professor Krempe` (7) and `professor Krempe` (18) | professor Krempe, professor Waldman (lowercase title). |
| Address line | `_Til Mrs. Saville, England._` | `_Til mrs. Saville, England._` Keep it as an emphasised line. |
| Datelines | `Sankt Petersborg, 11. december, 17—.` | Sankt Petersborg den 11. december 17—. Use Danish date order with "den" and no comma before the year. Same for Arkhangelsk den 28. marts 17—. and Genève den 18. marts 17—. A line with no place, such as "July 7th, 17—.", becomes 7. juli 17—. |
| Salutations, sign-offs | Min kære søster, / Din hengivne bror, | Fine as they are. Also Min elskede søster, / Min kære ven, / Din hengivne og bedrøvede far, |
| "Walton, _in continuation._" | Walton, _i fortsættelse._ (stiff) | Walton, _fortsat._ |
| Dialogue marks | Mostly »…«, but straight "…" throughout Ch 6, 8, 11, 13, 14, 15. Nested quotes use '…'. Continuation paragraphs open with « (wrong). | Use »…« for all dialogue and ›…‹ for quotes inside quotes. In a multi-paragraph quote (letters, the creature's tale in Ch 11–16), start every paragraph with » and close with « only at the end. Never open a paragraph with «. Put dialogue-tag commas inside: »Kom ind,« sagde han. |
| Source lines under verse | `[Coleridges »Den gamle sømand«.]`, which adds brackets | Follow the English: Fra Coleridges »Den gamle sømand«. / Fra Wordsworths »Tintern Abbey«. |
| Emphasis | `_word_` mostly kept | Always keep `_…_`, for example »… at selv _du_ vendte dig bort«, »_Jeg skal være hos dig på din bryllupsnat!_« |
| Miles | `mil` for English miles (false friend). | **Batch rule (overrides earlier note): do not convert to metric.** English mile → »engelsk mil« / »engelske mil« (after first mention in a paragraph »mil« alone only if clear). Vague "many miles" → »mange mil« or »langt« is fine. |
| Leagues | `mange mil` | League → »mil« (the traditional Danish rendering of a league/lieue as a long old-style unit); keep the number, do not convert to km. |
| Feet, yards | old DA converted to meter | **Do not convert.** feet → »fod«, inches → »tommer«, yards → »alen« or »skridt« when vague. Keep the body sense of "foot/feet" (fod, fødder). |

**Du/De.** Use the 1790s social register, but make it consistent. The old edition already uses this pattern; it slips in a few places, flagged below.

| Pair | Rec. | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Walton → Margaret (letters) | du | Siblings. Old is correct. |
| Victor ↔ Walton | du both ways | Old mostly du. Fix the stray »Er De vanvittig, min ven? … Deres …« (Ch 24 ¶30) to du. |
| Victor ↔ creature | du both ways | The 1818 "thou" register: intimacy and contempt. Old is correct. |
| Walton ↔ creature (Ch 24) | De both ways | Strangers at the deathbed. Old is correct and consistent. |
| Victor ↔ Elizabeth, father, Ernest, William, Clerval | du | Family and friends. Old is correct. |
| Elizabeth ↔ Justine | du | Raised together. |
| Justine → Victor | De ("Kære herre, De …") | Servant to son of the house. Victor → Justine: du. |
| Krempe, Waldman → Victor | **De** (old: du, which is wrong for professor to new student, c. 1790) | Victor → professors: De. Krempe → Clerval: De (old has this already). |
| Kirwin, the Irish townsmen, the nurse → Victor | De | Victor → Kirwin: De. Old is correct. |
| Genevan magistrate ↔ Victor | De | Old is correct. |
| De Lacey ↔ creature | De | Fix Ch 15 ¶36 »Hvem er du?« to »Hvem er De?«. |
| Felix, Agatha, Safie, De Lacey (family) | du | |
| William ↔ creature | du | |
| Victor or Walton → the crew; invocation of spirits | I/jer (plural) | Not "De". |

"sir" in direct address: old has bare `herre` ("Kom, herre."), which is stiff. Leave it out where De already carries the politeness. In servile or rough speech use min herre. Exclamations: Good God / Great God → Gud i himlen! / Du store Gud! (old "Store Gud!" is stiff).

**The creature's designations** (keep one Danish word per English word so the shifting tone survives; old blurred them):

- creature → skabning. Old split between væsen and skabning. "I am your creature" = Jeg er din skabning (old "dit væsen" loses the theology).
- being → væsen. Keep it apart from creature.
- monster → uhyre. Old is consistent; keep.
- demon (1818 "daemon") → dæmon. Old varied (djævelsk, skabningen).
- fiend → djævel; for the vocative »Djævel!«. Old mostly gave uhyre (merging it with monster) or flattened it to skabningen; fix both.
- devil (literal) → djævel; "the devil himself" = Djævelen selv.
- wretch → usling when contemptuous (old has this 13×, good); stakkel when pitying ("poor wretch").
- creator / my creator → skaber, lowercase. Capitalise Skaber only for God.
- "spark of existence" → livsgnisten.

## 4. Quotations and verse

Keep the line breaks if the format allows them. Otherwise run the lines on as the English does, with a capital at the start of each line. Rhyme is optional; keep the rhythm and the imagery.

**Coleridge, Ancient Mariner** (Letter 2): "the land of mist and snow" → »tågens og sneens land«. Title → »Den gamle sømand«.

**Coleridge, Ancient Mariner** (Ch 5 ¶7; old was flat prose with inverted word order):
> Som en, der ad en øde vej / går frem i angst og gru, / og som har vendt sig én gang om / og ej tør vende sig nu, / fordi han ved, en grufuld djævel / går tæt i hælene på ham.

**P. B. Shelley, "Mutability"** (Ch 10 ¶3). Intro: Som Percy Shelleys digt »Foranderlighed« udtrykker det:
> Vi hviler; en drøm kan forgifte vor søvn. / Vi står op; én vildfaren tanke besmitter vor dag. / Vi føler, vi tænker, vi grunder; ler eller græder, / favner vor kære kval, slår vore sorger hen; / Det er det samme: thi hvad enten glæde eller smerte, / dens vej bort står altid åben. / Menneskets i går bliver måske aldrig som dets i morgen; / intet består uden foranderligheden — forandringen selv!

**Wordsworth, "Tintern Abbey"** (Ch 18 ¶19; old left it **untranslated**):
> Det brusende vandfald / hjemsøgte ham som en lidenskab: den høje klippe, / bjerget og den dybe, dystre skov, / deres farver og deres former var for ham dengang / en hunger, en følelse og en kærlighed, / der ikke trængte til nogen fjernere tiltrækning, / skænket af tanken, eller nogen interesse, / der ikke var lånt af øjet.

**Byron, Childe Harold** (Ch 7 ¶22): "the palaces of nature" → »naturens paladser«. Restore the quote marks, which old dropped.

**Milton, Paradise Lost** and the books the creature reads (Ch 13, 15):
- _Paradise Lost_ → _Det tabte paradis_. Old "Det Tabte Paradis" is title-cased against Danish orthography.
- _Plutarch's Lives_ → _Plutarks levnedsbeskrivelser_. _The Sorrows of Werter_ → _Den unge Werthers lidelser_. Werter → Werther.
- Volney's _Ruins of Empires_ → Volneys _Rigernes ruiner_. Old "_Imperieernes Ruiner_" is stiff.
- "I should be your Adam, but instead I'm the fallen angel" → Jeg burde være din Adam, men i stedet er jeg den faldne engel.
- "I bore a hell within me" (Ch 8, Ch 16) → jeg bar et helvede i mig. "Evil became my good" (Ch 24) → Det onde blev mit gode.
- "Adam's plea to his Creator" → Adams bøn til sin Skaber (capitalised here: God). Eve → Eva.
- 1818 title-page epigraph, if front matter is added (it is not in the JSON): »Bad jeg dig, Skaber, om af ler / at forme mig til menneske? Bad jeg dig / at løfte mig af mørket?« (_Det tabte paradis_, X, 743–745).

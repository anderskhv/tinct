# Jekyll and Hyde: Danish glossary (new edition)

Source: modern English edition (10 chapters, 340 paragraphs), compared paragraph by paragraph with the old Danish edition. The old Danish is fluent but literal in places: it drops the modern edition's inline glosses and breaks the house rules on honorifics and address. Translate from the English, not from the old Danish.

## 1. Conventions

| Item | Old Danish | Verdict | Rule for the new edition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mr. | `Mr.` capitalised (121 times) | Breaks the house rule | `mr. Utterson`, `mr. Hyde`; `Mr.` only at the start of a sentence |
| Dr. / Doctor | `Dr.` capitalised (38 times); `doktor` in the ch. 6 title | Breaks the house rule | `dr. Jekyll`, `dr. Lanyon`; spell out `doktor` in titles and in direct address (»Doktor!«) |
| Sir (title) | `Sir Danvers` | Breaks the house rule | `sir Danvers Carew`, `sir Danvers` |
| sir (vocative) | lowercase `sir`, about 50 times | Good | Keep `sir` from servants and police. Translate »Mr. Utterson, sir« as »mr. Utterson« only, without doubling |
| Messrs. | `hr. Maw` / `hr. M.` | Wrong: it is a firm, and `hr.` clashes with `mr.` | `firmaet Maw` / `firmaet M.`; never use `hr.` for English names |
| Master (ironic, 2.49) | `Mester Hyde` | Stiff, and reads as "master craftsman" | `den unge herre Hyde` or `lille Hyde` |
| Inspector | `Inspektør Newcomen` | Should be lowercase | `kriminalinspektør Newcomen`, then `inspektøren` |
| Mrs. / Miss / Lady | none in the text | n/a | If one turns up: `mrs.`, `miss`, `lady` lowercase |
| Dialogue marks | `»…«`, inner `›…‹` | Good | Keep `»…«` and `›…‹` (Enfield's quoting of Hyde in 1.7 is the main nested case) |
| Comma at a speech tag | `»…,« sagde han` | Old-fashioned | `»…«, sagde han` (Retskrivningsordbogen). Apply it the same way in every book of the batch |
| Dashes | spaced em dash ` — ` | Not Danish | Spaced en dash ` – `; year `18–` |
| Emphasis | none in either file | n/a | Keep any `_word_` underscores exactly |
| Currency | `pund`, `ti pund i guld` | Good | `pund`; `hundrede pund`. Never convert to kroner |
| Cheque | `check` | Correct current spelling | `check`, `checken`; `trukket på Coutts' bank`, `udstedt til ihændehaver` |
| Units | `fod`, `gård` | Fine | Keep imperial units if they appear; `yard` (courtyard) → `gård` |
| Hansom / cab | `hansom`; `droske` | `hansom` is opaque | `cab` → `droske`; `hansom` → `hyrevogn` or `droske` |

### Forms of address (du/De)
The old Danish is mostly sound (1880s gentlemen: `du` between old friends, `De` to strangers, officials and superiors). Fix these slips:
- Jekyll's last letter to Utterson (8.92–93) uses `De`/`Deres`, but they say `du` everywhere else. Use `du`/`din`.
- Hyde says `din` to Enfield in 1.7 (›Læg din uro fra dig‹). A stranger in a standoff would use `De`. His remark to the whole group stays plural: `I`/`jeres`.
- Guest says `du` to his employer Utterson (5.24–34). Use `De` both ways: a head clerk and his principal.

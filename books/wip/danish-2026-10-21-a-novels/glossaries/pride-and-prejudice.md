# Pride and Prejudice: Danish glossary (Stolthed og fordom)

Source: modern English edition (61 ch., 2053 paras). The old Danish (2060 paras) is a recent, fairly modern translation, not a 19th-century one, but its conventions are inconsistent. It has one extra paragraph in ch. 3, 14, 22, 30, 36, 46 and 48, so alignment drifts by one there.

## 1. Conventions

| Item | Old Danish | Rule for the new edition |
|---|---|---|
| Mr. / Mrs. | `Mr.`, `Mrs.` capitalised (786 / 346) | **mr. Darcy, mrs. Bennet**; `Mr.`/`Mrs.` only at sentence start. `Mr. and Mrs. Gardiner` → mr. og mrs. Gardiner |
| Miss | `Miss Bennet` capitalised (265); servants say `frøken` | **miss Bennet, miss Elizabeth, miss Eliza, miss Bingley, miss de Bourgh**. A servant's bare vocative "Miss" → *frøken* |
| Sir (title) | `Sir William` | **sir William, sir William Lucas** |
| Lady (title) | `Lady Catherine`, `Lady Lucas` | **lady Catherine (de Bourgh), lady Lucas, lady Anne** |
| her Ladyship | *hendes nåde* (good) | **hendes nåde**; Collins to her face: **Deres nåde** |
| Colonel | mixed: English `Colonel Forster/Fitzwilliam` (ch. 6–7, 30+), `oberst Forster` (ch. 9+), once `Oberst Miller` | **oberst Forster, oberst Fitzwilliam, oberst Miller**; *mrs. Forster* |
| Captain | English `Captain Carter` kept | **kaptajn Carter** |
| Vocative "sir" | `hr.` alone (24×, stiff), sometimes `sir` | Use the name (*mr. Collins*) or leave it out. Servant to master: *herre*. Never a bare *hr.* |
| Vocative "madam" | *frue* (17×) | Servant or tradesman: *frue*. Elizabeth to lady Catherine: leave it out or *lady Catherine*. Collins to mrs. Bennet: *frue* is fine |
| "Lord!" / "Good Lord!" (Lydia, Mrs. B.) | mixed | **Gud!**, **Du godeste!**, **Herregud!** Never the English *Lord* |
| Dialogue quotes | `»…«` mostly, but ch. **13, 26, 47, 49, 50, 51** use straight `"…"` (458×); 7 paras open with a reversed `«` (e.g. 61.7) | **»…«** everywhere; **›…‹** inside quotes (e.g. Elizabeth's words quoted back in 58.13). Fix both old defects |
| Emphasis | `_…_` mostly kept (482 vs 494 EN) | Keep every `_word_`; move it onto the Danish word that carries the stress (`_Du_ danser…`) |
| De / Dem / Deres | capitalised (correct); *deres* (their) lowercase | Keep. Plural familiar "you" → **I / jer / jeres** (capital I) |
| Money | *tyve tusind pund*, *ti tusind om året* (good) | **pund** (never kroner/rigsdaler). "ten thousand a year" → **ti tusind om året**; add *pund* only where it is ambiguous. "four per cents / funds" → *statsobligationer*. "pin money" → *nålepenge* |
| Numbers | words | Spell out amounts in dialogue (*fem tusind pund*); use digits only for dates |

## 2. Forms of address (du/De)

Old Danish: du inside the family and between close friends, De everywhere else; Darcy and Elizabeth switch to du only in ch. 60. It is mostly sound. Rule: **De is the default between adults who are not family or close friends.** du is for family, for sisters' close friends and for engaged couples. Titles and surnames stay alongside du (the comedy of *»Min kære mr. Bennet, har du hørt…«* is Austen's).

| Pair | Rule | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mr. ↔ Mrs. Bennet | **du** both ways; she calls him *mr. Bennet* | Same as old. Keep the formal vocative with du |
| Parents → daughters | **du** (I/jer to the group) | Same as old |
| Daughters → parents | **du**; *Mama* → **mor**, *Papa* → **far** | Old kept *Mama* (stiff); use *mor/far* in direct address too |
| Sisters | **du** | Same as old |
| Charlotte ↔ Elizabeth | **du**; *Eliza* is Charlotte's name for her | Same as old |
| Gardiners ↔ Elizabeth/Jane | **du** both ways; *tante*, *onkel* | Same as old (Mrs. Gardiner's letter, ch. 52) |
| Mrs. Philips ↔ nieces | **du** | |
| Darcy ↔ Elizabeth | **De** until she accepts in 58.7; **du** from 58.8 on | Old kept De to ch. 59 and switched in ch. 60, which jars. Change it at the acceptance so *kæreste, dejligste Elizabeth* (58.23) comes with du. She may still call him *mr. Darcy* (Austen does) |
| Bingley ↔ Jane | **De** until the engagement (ch. 55); **du** after | |
| Darcy ↔ Bingley | **du** (old friends) | Same as old. They use surnames: *Darcy*, *Bingley* |
| Darcy ↔ Georgiana; Darcy ↔ Fitzwilliam | **du** (siblings, cousins) | |
| Miss Bingley / Mrs. Hurst ↔ Darcy | **De** | Her pursuit stays formal, which is the point |
| Bingley sisters ↔ Elizabeth/Jane | **De** | Miss Bingley's *miss Eliza* is a deliberate put-down |
| Lady Catherine ↔ Elizabeth | **De** both ways | Ch. 56: keep it cold; no *frue* from Elizabeth |
| Lady Catherine → Collins, Charlotte, Maria | **De** | Collins → her: **De / Deres nåde** |
| Collins ↔ Elizabeth | **De** both ways (cousins, but he is a stranger) | Same as old. He says *min kære miss Elizabeth*; *my fair cousins* → *mine smukke kusiner* |
| Collins ↔ Mr./Mrs. Bennet | **De** | Mr. Bennet's irony works best in De |
| Wickham ↔ Elizabeth | **De** before his marriage; after it he says **du** + *kære søster* and she answers **du** | Same as old (52.8). His presumption is the point; her coolness comes from the wording |
| Lydia ↔ Wickham | **du** after the elopement | |
| Colonel Fitzwilliam ↔ Elizabeth | **De** | |
| Sir William, Lady Lucas ↔ Bennets | **De** (neighbours) | |
| Servants → family | **De** + *frøken / frue / herre* | Same as old (Hill, mrs. Reynolds) |
| Family → servants | **du**, with the surname alone: *Hill* | Old used De to Hill; du marks the hierarchy and sounds natural now. Narrative: *mrs. Hill* / *Hill* as in EN |
| Officers ↔ the girls | **De** | Lydia's familiarity shows in what she says, not in du |

## 3. Names, places, institutions, terms

**People.** Keep English spellings; no Danish forms (no *Lise*, *Kathrine*).
- Elizabeth: **Lizzy** (family, Jane), **Eliza** (Charlotte, Sir William, Miss Bingley). Keep as in the EN. Never use *Liza*.
- Jane = miss Bennet (eldest); Mary; Catherine = **Kitty**; Lydia. "the Miss Bennets" → *frøknerne Bennet* or *Bennet-pigerne*.
- Fitzwilliam Darcy; Georgiana Darcy (*miss Darcy*); lady Anne Darcy (dead mother).
- Charles Bingley; Caroline Bingley (*miss Bingley*); Louisa and mr. Hurst (*mrs. Hurst*).
- Lady Catherine de Bourgh; Anne = *miss de Bourgh* (lowercase *de*); mrs. Jenkinson.
- William Collins; Charlotte Lucas (*miss Lucas*) → *mrs. Collins*; Maria Lucas; sir William and lady Lucas; Lucas Lodge.
- Edward and mrs. Gardiner; mrs. Philips; mrs. Long; mrs. Hill; mrs. Reynolds (housekeeper → *husholderske*).
- Wickham; Denny (*mr. Denny*); oberst Forster and **mrs. Forster** (Harriet); kaptajn Carter; mrs. Younge.

**Places.** Leave English names untranslated: Longbourn, Netherfield (Park), Meryton, Pemberley, Rosings (Park), Hunsford, Lambton, Brighton, Gracechurch Street, Cheapside, Hertfordshire, Derbyshire, Kent, Bath, Ramsgate, Epsom, Barnet, Clapham, Newcastle, Gretna Green, Matlock, Chatsworth, Dovedale, Kympton, Oxford, Cambridge.
- Scotland → **Skotland**; London; the North of England → **Nordengland**; the Lakes → **Lake District** (or *søerne i Nordengland*).
- St. James's (the court) → **hoffet i St. James's**. The *——shire* regiment: keep the dash.
- Hunsford Parsonage → **præstegården i Hunsford** (old: *præstebolig* / capitalised *Præstegård*, which is inconsistent).

**Institutions and terms.**
- the entail / entailed → **fideikommis** / **båndlagt** (to male heirs). Explain it once: *godset var båndlagt til nærmeste mandlige arving*. Old 7.0 *arveretsklausul* is invented: replace it.
- militia → **milits**, **militsregiment**. Old 7.2 *militærregiment* loses the sense. regiment → *regiment*; officers → *officerer*; red coat(s) → *rød uniform / de røde uniformer*.
- the living → **sognekald(et)**, **præsteembede** (old *sognekald* is good). rector → *sognepræst*; clergyman → *præst*. patroness → **velynderinde**.
- parsonage → **præstegård**.
- assembly (Meryton ball) → **ballet (i forsamlingshuset)**. Old *sammenkomst* is too vague. ball → *bal*.
- accomplished → **dannet** for women's accomplishments (8.4x debate: *en dannet kvinde*); *talentfuld* only for a skill (old used *talentfuld* throughout, which flattens the debate).
- agreeable → *behagelig*, *elskværdig*; amiable (not in this edition) → *elskværdig*.
- handsome → *smuk*, *køn* (women), *flot* (men).
- sensible → **fornuftig**. Never *sensibel*: it is a false friend.
- civility → *høflighed*; condescension (Collins, admiring) → **nådige venlighed / imødekommenhed**. Modern *nedladenhed* is only negative and makes Collins's praise of her an insult.
- pride → **stolthed**; prejudice → **fordom(me)**; prejudiced → *forudindtaget*; vanity → *forfængelighed*. Title: **Stolthed og fordom**.
- connections → **forbindelser**; where it means relatives (*low connections*), use *slægt / familie*.
- fortune → **formue**; estate → **gods** (Pemberley), *ejendom* (Longbourn); steward → *godsforvalter*.
- settlement(s) (marriage) → **ægtepagt / ægteskabskontrakt**; financial settlement → *økonomisk ordning*.
- establishment (marriage prospect) → *et godt parti*, *at blive godt gift*.
- come out / be out (in society) → **være introduceret i selskabslivet**. Never a bare *komme ud*.
- governess → *guvernante*; whist → *whist*; nerves (Mrs. B.) → **mine stakkels nerver**.

## 4. Famous lines (suggested Danish)

- 1.0 *It is a truth universally acknowledged…* → **Det er en almindeligt anerkendt sandhed, at en ugift mand, der er i besiddelse af en god formue, må mangle en hustru.** Old *nødvendigvis må være på udkig efter en kone* loses the irony: "be in need of" = *mangle*.
- 3.12 *She is tolerable, but not attractive enough to tempt _me_.* → **Hun er tålelig, men ikke køn nok til at friste _mig_.** Old *acceptabel* sounds bureaucratic. Use *tålelig* again in 5.8: *kun lige _tålelig_*.
- 11.20 *Follies and nonsense, whims and inconsistencies _do_ amuse me* → **Dumheder og vrøvl, luner og selvmodsigelser _morer_ mig**.
- 11.27 *My good opinion, once lost, is lost forever.* → **Min gode mening er, når den først er tabt, tabt for bestandig.** Old is fine.
- 34.3 *I have struggled with this in vain… how ardently I admire and love you.* → **Forgæves har jeg kæmpet imod. Det går ikke. Mine følelser lader sig ikke undertrykke. De må tillade mig at sige Dem, hvor inderligt jeg beundrer og elsker Dem.** Old *hvor dybt* is too weak for *ardently*.
- 34.23 *had you behaved in a more gentlemanly fashion* → **hvis De havde opført Dem mere som en gentleman**. Echo it word for word in 58.13 inside ›…‹.
- 34.27 *the last man in the world I could ever be persuaded to marry* → **den sidste mand i verden, jeg nogensinde ville lade mig overtale til at gifte mig med**.
- 35.3 letter: *Do not be alarmed, madam…* → **Bliv ikke foruroliget, frøken, når De modtager dette brev, af frygt for at det skulle indeholde en gentagelse af de følelser eller en fornyelse af det tilbud, der i aftes var Dem så modbydeligt.**
- 56.60 *Are the grounds of Pemberley to be so polluted?* → **Skal Pemberleys jord besudles på den måde?**
- 58.6 *You are too generous to play games with me.* → **De er for ædel til at lege med mig.** (Still De: this comes before her acceptance.)
- 58.23 *dearest, loveliest Elizabeth* → **kæreste, dejligste Elizabeth** (du).
- 60.3 *For the liveliness of your mind, I did.* → **For dit livlige sinds skyld, ja.**

## 5. Chapter titles

All 61 EN titles are `Chapter N`, with no named chapters; the old Danish uses `Kapitel N` for all 61. **Use `Kapitel N`.**

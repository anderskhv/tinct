# R10 fidelity and accessibility review: Nos. 49-55

**Scope.** Federalist Nos. 49, 50, 51, 52, 53, 54, 55 (batches ch49-ch55) against `source-original-en.json`. Paragraphs checked, side by side: 11 + 11 + 10 + 8 + 11 + 11 + 9 = **71 of 71**. Paragraph counts match the source in every paper, and each paragraph N starts with content equivalent to source N. Mechanical sweeps run on all 71: numbers and dates, all-caps emphasis words, non-ASCII or curly quotes, residual "--", and counts of federal, national, faction, democra*, republic*, Union, liberty, magistrate, emolument, sovereign, energy, check, balance and Convention.

Sweep results: no figure or date differs. No curly quotes or leftover "--". Emphasis caps are all preserved (50 p1 ENFORCING becomes ENFORCE; see below). No "judicial review" label. Fixed-term counts are unchanged except the intended magistrate to official or chief executive, emolument to pay, and the items listed below. ## Separation-of-powers vocabulary, Nos. 49-51

- **Kept as printed.** "partition of power" (51 p0), "departments", "constitutional equilibrium" (49 p7), "will of its own" (51 p1), "distinct and separate departments" (51 p8), "checks"/"check" (51 p4 "each may be a check on the other"), "controls" (51 p3, unchanged), "auxiliary precautions", "ambition must be made to counteract ambition", "the weaker/stronger department". "Judiciary" and "executive" stay. "Magistracies" becomes "offices" and "executive magistrate" becomes "chief executive", per the style note.
- **Drift found.** 50 p1 renders "restraint on power" as "check" (MINOR). 51 p0 drops "necessary" and strengthens "laid down in" to "requires" (MINOR). 51 p1 recasts "separate and distinct exercise" as adverbs (NIT). 51 p3 drops "necessary" before "constitutional means" (NIT). 51 p5 renders "weight of the legislative authority" as "is heavy" and flattens the leading question "May not...supplied" (MINOR).
- **Not found.** No swap of democracy and republic, and no use of "party" for faction. 51 p9 keeps "faction(s)", "federal republic", "compound republic" (p8), "the Union" and "Confederacies" as printed, and keeps the capitalized emphasis REPUBLICAN CAUSE and FEDERAL PRINCIPLE. Nos. 49 and 50 use "party/parties" only where the source does.

## Open issues (suspected source misprints and silent repairs; the lead decides)

1. **ch54 p0 and p1: "appointment" for "apportionment".** Source p0 "the appointment of its members to the several States", p1 "the appointment of taxes". Candidate silently renders "apportionment of its members among the individual States" and "apportion taxes". This is the repair the brief says a misprint must not receive, but the source's own chapter title reads "The Apportionment of Members Among the States", so the repair is evidently right. I recommend accepting it, or recording it as a deliberate, documented exception. No fix entry is written.
2. **ch55 p3: "the public safely might otherwise require".** Misprint for "safety". Candidate has "the public safety might otherwise demand". Same status as item 1; recommend accepting.
3. **ch52 p6 and ch55 p6: missing sentence breaks in the source.** ch52 p6 "its proper enlargement This remark" and "most frequent Virginia was". ch55 p6 "every class of citizens I am unable". Candidate inserts periods. This is a punctuation-level repair, so accept and note it.
4. **ch49 p2: "declare its true meaning".** Source "its" (the commission) is kept as printed. Fine.
5. **ch52 p4, p7: "three-year" and "seven-year" figures and Charles II/William III chronology** are kept as printed, with no added dates.

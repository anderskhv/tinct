## Corrected ch66 p11

```
The security the Constitution essentially intends against corruption and treachery in making treaties is to be sought in the numbers and characters of those who are to make them. The JOINT AGENCY of the chief executive of the Union and of two thirds of the members of a body selected by the collective wisdom of the legislatures of the individual States is designed to be the pledge for the fidelity of the national councils in this matter. The Convention might properly have intended the punishment of the Executive for departing from the instructions of the Senate, or for lack of integrity in conducting the negotiations entrusted to him. It might also have had in view the punishment of a few leading individuals in the Senate who had prostituted their influence in that body as the mercenary instruments of foreign corruption. But it could not, with more or even equal propriety, have contemplated the impeachment and punishment of two thirds of the Senate for consenting to an improper treaty, any more than of a majority of that branch or of the other branch of the national legislature for consenting to a harmful or unconstitutional law. That is a principle which, I believe, has never been admitted into any government. How, in fact, could a majority in the House of Representatives impeach themselves? Not better, it is evident, than two thirds of the Senate could try themselves. And yet what reason is there that a majority of the House of Representatives, sacrificing the interests of society by an unjust and tyrannical act of legislation, should escape with impunity any more than two thirds of the Senate, sacrificing the same interests in an injurious treaty with a foreign power? The truth is that in all such cases it is essential to the freedom and the necessary independence of the body's deliberations that its members be exempt from punishment for acts done in a collective capacity. The security of society must depend on the care taken to place the trust in proper hands, to make it their interest to execute it faithfully, and to make it as difficult as possible for them to combine in any interest opposed to the public good.
```

## Corrected ch69 p10

```
The President of the United States would be an officer elected by the people for four years; the king of Great Britain is a perpetual and hereditary prince. The one would be liable to personal punishment and disgrace; the person of the other is sacred and inviolable. The one would have a qualified negative on the acts of the legislative body; the other has an absolute negative. The one would have the right to command the military and naval forces of the nation; the other has this right and in addition holds that of declaring war and of raising and regulating fleets and armies by his own authority. The one would share with a branch of the legislature the power of making treaties; the other is the sole holder of that power. The one would share in the same way in appointing to offices; the other is the sole source of all appointments. The one can grant no privileges whatever; the other can make denizens of aliens (foreigners granted residence and some of the rights of citizens) and noblemen of commoners, and can create corporations with all the rights that belong to corporate bodies. The one can set no rules about the nation's commerce or currency; the other is in several respects the arbiter of commerce, and in that capacity can establish markets and fairs, regulate weights and measures, lay embargoes for a limited time, coin money, and authorize or prohibit the circulation of foreign coin. The one has not a particle of spiritual jurisdiction; the other is the supreme head and governor of the national church! What answer shall we give to those who would persuade us that things so unlike resemble each other? The same answer that ought to be given to those who tell us that a government whose entire power would be in the hands of the elected servants of the people, who are chosen at regular intervals, is an aristocracy, a monarchy and a despotism.
```

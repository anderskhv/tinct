# Act 2, Scene 1 — Padua. A room in Baptista’s house

[0] Enter Katherina and Bianca.

[1] BIANCA. Good sister, don't wrong me — and don't wrong yourself — by making a bondmaid and a slave of me. I won't stand for that. But as for these other trinkets, untie my hands and I'll pull them off myself — yes, all my clothes, down to my petticoat. Or whatever you order me to do, I will, since I know my duty to my elders.

[2] KATHERINA. Of all your suitors here, I order you to tell me which one you love best. See that you don't pretend.

[3] BIANCA. Believe me, sister, of all the men alive, I have never yet seen that special face that I could care for more than any other.

[4] KATHERINA. Hussy, you're lying. Isn't it Hortensio?

[5] BIANCA. If you fancy him, sister, then I swear right here, I'll plead your case for you so that you can have him.

[6] KATHERINA. Oh! Then maybe you fancy riches more — you'd take Gremio to keep you in fine things.

[7] BIANCA. Is it on his account that you envy me so? No, then you're joking. And now I clearly see you've been joking with me all this time. Please, sister Kate, untie my hands.

[8] KATHERINA. If that was a joke, then all the rest was too.

[9] [_Strikes her._]

[10] Enter Baptista.

[11] BAPTISTA. Why, what's all this, madam! Where does this insolence come from? Bianca, stand aside. Poor girl — she's weeping. Go work on your needlework. Don't meddle with her. For shame, you wretch with a devilish spirit — why do you wrong her, who never wronged you? When did she ever cross you with a bitter word?

[12] KATHERINA. Her silence mocks me, and I'll be avenged.

[13] [_Flies after Bianca._]

[14] BAPTISTA. What — in my sight? Bianca, get inside.

[15] [_Exit Bianca._]

[16] KATHERINA. What — you won't allow me anything? No, now I see: she's your treasure, she must have a husband. I shall dance barefoot on her wedding day, and, for your love of her, lead apes in hell. Don't talk to me. I'll go sit and weep until I can find an occasion for revenge.

[17] [_Exit._]

[18] BAPTISTA. Was ever a gentleman so grieved as I? But who comes here?

[19] Enter Gremio, with Lucentio in the dress of a humble man; Petruchio, with Hortensio as a musician; and Tranio, with Biondello carrying a lute and books.

[20] GREMIO. Good morning, neighbour Baptista.

[21] BAPTISTA. Good morning, neighbour Gremio. God save you, gentlemen!

[22] PETRUCHIO. And you, good sir! Tell me — don't you have a daughter called Katherina, fair and virtuous?

[23] BAPTISTA. I have a daughter, sir, called Katherina.

[24] GREMIO. You're too blunt — go about it properly.

[25] PETRUCHIO. You wrong me, Signior Gremio — let me speak. I'm a gentleman of Verona, sir, who, having heard of her beauty and her wit, her friendliness and bashful modesty, her remarkable qualities and mild behaviour, am bold enough to present myself as a forward guest in your house — to let my eye witness the report I've so often heard. And as an entrance fee for my reception, I present you with a man of mine,

[26] [_Presenting Hortensio._]

[27] skilled in music and mathematics, to instruct her fully in those sciences, in which I know she is not ignorant. Accept him, or else you wrong me. His name is Licio, born in Mantua.

[28] BAPTISTA. You're welcome, sir, and he for your good sake. But as for my daughter Katherine — this much I know: she's not for your purpose, the more's my grief.

[29] PETRUCHIO. I see you don't mean to part with her. Or else you don't enjoy my company.

[30] BAPTISTA. Don't mistake me — I speak only as I find. Where are you from, sir? What may I call you?

[31] PETRUCHIO. Petruchio is my name, Antonio's son — a man well known throughout all Italy.

[32] BAPTISTA. I know him well — you're welcome for his sake.

[33] GREMIO. With respect to your tale, Petruchio, please — let those of us who are poor petitioners speak too. _Backare!_ You're remarkably pushy.

[34] PETRUCHIO. Oh, forgive me, Signior Gremio — I'd like to be doing.

[35] GREMIO. No doubt about it, sir — but you'll curse your wooing. Neighbour, this gift is very welcome, I'm sure. To show similar kindness, I myself, who have been more kindly obliged to you than anyone, freely give you this young scholar,

[36] [_Presenting Lucentio._]

[37] who has long been studying at Rheims — as skilled in Greek, Latin, and other languages as the other in music and mathematics. His name is Cambio — please accept his service.

[38] BAPTISTA. A thousand thanks, Signior Gremio — welcome, good Cambio. [_To Tranio._] But, kind sir, I think you walk like a stranger. May I be so bold as to ask the reason for your visit?

[39] TRANIO. Forgive me, sir — the boldness is my own. Being a stranger in this city, I am making myself a suitor to your daughter — to Bianca, fair and virtuous. Nor is your firm resolution unknown to me, concerning the preference for the eldest sister. This liberty is all I ask: that, given my parentage, I may be welcome among the others who woo, with the same free access and favour as the rest. And, toward the education of your daughters, I here present a simple instrument and this small bundle of Greek and Latin books. If you accept them, then their worth is great.

[40] BAPTISTA. Lucentio is your name — from where, please?

[41] TRANIO. From Pisa, sir — son to Vincentio.

[42] BAPTISTA. A man of consequence in Pisa — by reputation I know him well. You are very welcome, sir.

[43] [_To Hortensio._] You take the lute,

[44] [_To Lucentio._] and you take the set of books.

[45] You'll go see your pupils right away. Hey, inside!

[46] Enter a Servant.

[47] Fellow, lead these gentlemen to my daughters, and tell them both that these are their tutors. Tell them to treat them well.

[48] [_Exeunt Servant with Hortensio, Lucentio and Biondello._]

[49] We'll go take a little walk in the orchard, and then to dinner. You are exceedingly welcome — so consider yourselves so, all of you, please.

[50] PETRUCHIO. Signior Baptista, my business demands haste, and I can't come to woo every day. You knew my father well, and through him me — sole heir to all his lands and goods, which I've improved rather than reduced. So tell me: if I win your daughter's love, what dowry shall I have with her in marriage?

[51] BAPTISTA. After my death, half my lands — and in immediate possession, twenty thousand crowns.

[52] PETRUCHIO. And for that dowry, I will assure her — her widow's portion, should she survive me — in all my lands and leases whatsoever. So let detailed contracts be drawn between us, so that the agreements may be kept on both sides.

[53] BAPTISTA. Yes — once the special thing is well secured. That is, her love. For that's the whole of it.

[54] PETRUCHIO. Why, that's nothing — for I tell you, father, I am as firm as she is proud-minded. And where two raging fires meet, they consume the very thing that feeds their fury. Though a little fire grows large with a little wind, extreme gusts will blow fire and all clear out. So I am to her, and so she yields to me — for I am rough, and don't woo like a baby.

[55] BAPTISTA. May your wooing go well, and your speed be happy! But arm yourself for some unhappy words.

[56] PETRUCHIO. Yes — proof against them, like mountains against winds: they don't shake, though winds blow forever.

[57] Re-enter Hortensio, with his head bashed.

[58] BAPTISTA. What now, my friend! Why do you look so pale?

[59] HORTENSIO. From fear, I promise you — if I look pale.

[60] BAPTISTA. What — will my daughter make a good musician?

[61] HORTENSIO. I think she'll sooner make a soldier. Iron may hold up with her — but never lutes.

[62] BAPTISTA. Why — so you can't break her in on the lute?

[63] HORTENSIO. Why, no — for she has broken the lute on me. I just told her she'd mistaken her frets and bent her hand to teach her fingering, when, with a most impatient and devilish spirit, 'Frets, do you call these?' she said — 'I'll fret with them!' And with that word she struck me on the head, and my skull went through the instrument. There I stood amazed for a while, as in a pillory, looking through the lute — while she called me rascal fiddler and twanging Jack, with twenty other vile names, as if she'd studied how to abuse me.

[64] PETRUCHIO. By all the world, she's a lively wench! I love her ten times more than I ever did. Oh, how I long to have a chat with her!

[65] BAPTISTA.

[66] [_To Hortensio._] Well, come along with me, and don't be so put out.

[67] Carry on practising with my younger daughter. She's quick to learn, and grateful for kindness. Signior Petruchio, will you come with us, or shall I send my daughter Kate to you?

[68] PETRUCHIO. Please do.

[69] [_Exeunt Baptista, Gremio, Tranio and Hortensio._]

[70] I'll wait for her here, and woo her with some spirit when she comes. Say that she rails — why, then I'll tell her plainly she sings as sweetly as a nightingale. Say she frowns — I'll say she looks as bright as morning roses freshly washed with dew. Say she stays silent and won't speak a word — then I'll praise her fluency and say she utters piercing eloquence. If she tells me to clear off, I'll thank her as though she begged me stay with her a week. If she refuses to marry, I'll demand the date when I'll cry the banns, and when we'll be married. But here she comes — now, Petruchio, speak.

[71] Enter Katherina.

[72] Good morning, Kate — that's your name, I hear.

[73] KATHERINA. You've heard correctly — but a little hard of hearing. They call me Katherine, those who speak of me.

[74] PETRUCHIO. You lie, in fact, for you're called plain Kate, and bonny Kate, and sometimes Kate the shrew. But, Kate, the prettiest Kate in Christendom — Kate of Kate Hall, my super-dainty Kate — for all dainties are Kates — and therefore, Kate, take this from me, Kate of my consolation: hearing your mildness praised in every town, your virtues spoken of, and your beauty rumoured — yet not as deeply as you deserve — I am moved to woo you for my wife.

[75] KATHERINA. Moved! In good time. Let whoever moved you here remove you again. I knew you the first moment — you're a moveable.

[76] PETRUCHIO. Why — what's a moveable?

[77] KATHERINA. A joint-stool.

[78] PETRUCHIO. You've hit it. Come, sit on me.

[79] KATHERINA. Asses are made to bear — and so are you.

[80] PETRUCHIO. Women are made to bear — and so are you.

[81] KATHERINA. No such jade as to bear you, if you mean me.

[82] PETRUCHIO. Alas, good Kate — I won't burden you. For, knowing you to be only young and light—

[83] KATHERINA. Too light for such a swain as you to catch. And yet as heavy as my weight should be.

[84] PETRUCHIO. Should be! Should buzz!

[85] KATHERINA. Well caught — and like a buzzard.

[86] PETRUCHIO. Oh, slow-winged turtle-dove — shall a buzzard take you?

[87] KATHERINA. Yes — for a turtle, just as he takes a buzzard.

[88] PETRUCHIO. Come now, wasp — honestly, you're too angry.

[89] KATHERINA. If I'm waspish, you'd best beware my sting.

[90] PETRUCHIO. My remedy then is to pluck it out.

[91] KATHERINA. Yes — if the fool could find where it lies.

[92] PETRUCHIO. Who doesn't know where a wasp wears its sting? In its tail.

[93] KATHERINA. In its tongue.

[94] PETRUCHIO. Whose tongue?

[95] KATHERINA. Yours, if you go telling tales. And so farewell.

[96] PETRUCHIO. What — with my tongue in your tail? No, come back, good Kate. I'm a gentleman.

[97] KATHERINA. I'll test that.

[98] [_Striking him._]

[99] PETRUCHIO. I swear I'll cuff you if you strike again.

[100] KATHERINA. So you may lose your arms — your coat of arms. If you strike me, you're no gentleman. And if no gentleman, then no arms.

[101] PETRUCHIO. A herald, Kate? Oh — enter me in your books.

[102] KATHERINA. What's your crest — a coxcomb?

[103] PETRUCHIO. A combless cock, so long as Kate will be my hen.

[104] KATHERINA. No cock of mine — you crow too like a coward.

[105] PETRUCHIO. No, come, Kate, come — you mustn't look so sour.

[106] KATHERINA. That's my fashion when I see a crab.

[107] PETRUCHIO. Why, there's no crab here — so don't look sour.

[108] KATHERINA. There is, there is.

[109] PETRUCHIO. Then show it to me.

[110] KATHERINA. If I had a mirror, I would.

[111] PETRUCHIO. What — you mean my face?

[112] KATHERINA. Sharp aim for such a young one.

[113] PETRUCHIO. Now, by Saint George, I'm too young for you.

[114] KATHERINA. Yet you're withered.

[115] PETRUCHIO. With cares.

[116] KATHERINA. I don't care.

[117] PETRUCHIO. No, listen, Kate — truly, you won't escape so.

[118] KATHERINA. I rile you if I stay. Let me go.

[119] PETRUCHIO. No, not in the slightest — I find you exceedingly gentle. I was told you were rough, and coy, and sullen — and now I find that report is a complete liar. For you're pleasant, playful, exceedingly courteous, but slow in speech — yet sweet as springtime flowers. You can't frown, you can't look askance, nor bite your lip as angry wenches do, nor do you take pleasure in being contrary in talk. No — you entertain your wooers with mildness, with gentle conversation, soft and friendly. Why does the world report that Kate limps? Oh, slanderous world! Kate is straight and slender like the hazel-twig, and as brown in colour as hazel-nuts, and sweeter than the kernels. Oh — let me see you walk: you don't limp at all.

[120] KATHERINA. Go on, fool — and command whoever you keep.

[121] PETRUCHIO. Did ever Diana so grace a grove as Kate this room with her princely gait? Oh, you be Diana, and let her be Kate — and then let Kate be chaste, and Diana sportive!

[122] KATHERINA. Where did you study all this elegant speech?

[123] PETRUCHIO. It's improvised — from my mother-wit.

[124] KATHERINA. A witty mother! Witless otherwise her son.

[125] PETRUCHIO. Am I not wise?

[126] KATHERINA. Yes — keep yourself warm.

[127] PETRUCHIO. Indeed, that's what I intend, sweet Katherine — in your bed. So setting all this chat aside, plainly: your father has consented that you shall be my wife. Your dowry is agreed. And whether you will or not, I will marry you. Now, Kate, I'm a husband for your purpose — for, by this light, by which I see your beauty — your beauty that makes me like you so well — you must be married to no man but me. For I am the one born to tame you, Kate, and bring you from a wild Kate to a Kate as conformable as other household Kates.

[128] Re-enter Baptista, Gremio and Tranio.

[129] Here comes your father. Don't deny it — I must and will have Katherine as my wife.

[130] BAPTISTA. Now, Signior Petruchio, how do you fare with my daughter?

[131] PETRUCHIO. How but well, sir? How but well? It would be impossible for me to fare badly.

[132] BAPTISTA. Why, what's this, daughter Katherine — in the dumps?

[133] KATHERINA. You call me daughter? Now I assure you, you have shown a tender fatherly regard, wishing me wed to one half lunatic — a mad-cap ruffian and a swearing Jack, who thinks he can bluff his way through with oaths.

[134] PETRUCHIO. Father, the situation is this: you, and all the world that has talked about her, have talked of her wrongly. If she is shrewish, it's a deliberate strategy — for she's not stubborn, but as modest as a dove. She's not hot-tempered, but as temperate as the morning. For patience she'll prove a second Griselda, and Roman Lucrece for her chastity. And to conclude, we have agreed so well together that Sunday is the wedding day.

[135] KATHERINA. I'll see you hanged on Sunday first.

[136] GREMIO. Listen, Petruchio — she says she'll see you hanged first.

[137] TRANIO. Is this your success? Well — goodnight to our part of it!

[138] PETRUCHIO. Be patient, gentlemen. I'm choosing her for myself. If she and I are pleased, what's that to you? It's been bargained between the two of us, in private, that she'll still play the shrew in company. I tell you, it's incredible to believe how much she loves me. Oh, the sweetest Kate — she hung about my neck, and kiss on kiss she came at me so fast, swearing oath on oath, that in a moment she won me to her love. Oh, you are novices. It's a sight to see, when men and women are alone, how tame a milksop wretch can make the worst shrew. Give me your hand, Kate — I'm off to Venice, to buy clothes for the wedding day. Prepare the feast, father, and invite the guests. I'll make sure my Katherine is finely dressed.

[139] BAPTISTA. I don't know what to say — but give me your hands. God send you joy, Petruchio! It's a match.

[140] GREMIO, TRANIO. Amen, say we — we'll be witnesses.

[141] PETRUCHIO. Father, and wife, and gentlemen, farewell. I'm off to Venice — Sunday is coming fast. We'll have rings and things, and fine clothes. And kiss me, Kate — we'll be married on Sunday.

[142] [_Exeunt Petruchio and Katherina, separately._]

[143] GREMIO. Was ever a match closed so suddenly?

[144] BAPTISTA. Honestly, gentlemen, now I'm playing a merchant's part, and venturing recklessly on a desperate market.

[145] TRANIO. The merchandise was sitting and spoiling on your hands. It'll bring you gain, or be lost at sea.

[146] BAPTISTA. The gain I'm after is peace in the match.

[147] GREMIO. No doubt he's got himself a quiet catch. But now, Baptista, to your younger daughter — this is the day we've long looked forward to. I am your neighbour, and was suitor first.

[148] TRANIO. And I am one who loves Bianca more than words can tell or your thoughts can guess.

[149] GREMIO. Youngster, you can't love as dearly as I.

[150] TRANIO. Greybeard, your love is freezing.

[151] GREMIO. But yours fries. Skipper, stand back — it's age that nourishes.

[152] TRANIO. But it's youth that flourishes in ladies' eyes.

[153] BAPTISTA. Settle down, gentlemen — I'll resolve this dispute. It's deeds that must win the prize: whichever of you can guarantee my daughter the largest settlement shall have my Bianca's love. Say, Signior Gremio, what can you guarantee her?

[154] GREMIO. First, as you know, my house in town is richly furnished with plate and gold — basins and ewers to wash her dainty hands. My hangings are all Tyrian tapestry; in ivory coffers I've stuffed my crowns. In cypress chests, my counterpane tapestries, costly clothes, tents, and canopies, fine linen, Turkish cushions embossed with pearl, valances of Venetian gold needlework, pewter and brass, and everything that belongs to a house or housekeeping. Then, at my farm, I have a hundred milk-cows at the pail, six score fat oxen standing in my stalls, and everything else proportionate to this. I'm getting on in years, I must admit. And if I die tomorrow, all this is hers — provided that while I live, she'll be mine alone.

[155] TRANIO. That 'alone' was well placed. Sir, listen to me: I am my father's heir and only son. If I may have your daughter as my wife, I'll leave her three or four houses, every one as good within rich Pisa's walls as any old Signior Gremio has in Padua — plus two thousand ducats a year from fruitful land, all of which shall be her settlement. What — have I pinched you, Signior Gremio?

[156] GREMIO. Two thousand ducats a year from land! My total land doesn't come to that much. She shall have that, plus an argosy now lying in the Marseilles roads. What — have I choked you with an argosy?

[157] TRANIO. Gremio, it's known my father has no less than three great argosies, plus two galliasses, and twelve tight galleys. I'll guarantee her these — and twice as much as whatever you offer next.

[158] GREMIO. No, I've offered all I have — no more. And she can have no more than all I have. If you like me, she shall have me and mine.

[159] TRANIO. Why, then the girl is mine against all the world, by your firm promise. Gremio is outbid.

[160] BAPTISTA. I must admit your offer is the best. And let your father guarantee her this — and she's your own. Otherwise, you'll have to forgive me. If you were to die before him, where would her settlement be?

[161] TRANIO. That's just a quibble — he's old, I'm young.

[162] GREMIO. And can't young men die as well as old?

[163] BAPTISTA. Well, gentlemen, this is what I've decided. Next Sunday, as you know, my daughter Katherine is to be married. Now, on the following Sunday, Bianca shall be your bride, if you provide this guarantee. If not, to Signior Gremio. And so I take my leave, and thank you both.

[164] GREMIO. Farewell, good neighbour.

[165] [_Exit Baptista._]

[166] Now, I don't fear you. Look here, young gamester — your father would be a fool to give you everything, and in his old age sit at the foot of your table. Pah, nonsense! An old Italian fox is not so kind, my boy.

[167] [_Exit._]

[168] TRANIO. A curse on your crafty withered hide! Yet I've faced him down with a card worth ten. It's in my head to do my master good. I see no reason why supposed Lucentio shouldn't get himself a father — called supposed Vincentio. And that's a wonder: fathers ordinarily beget their children — but in this case of wooing, a child shall beget a sire, if my cunning doesn't fail me.

[169] [_Exit._]

# Act 4, Scene 3 — A room in Petruchio’s house

[0] Enter Katherina and Grumio.

[1] GRUMIO. No, no, truly — I dare not for my life.

[2] KATHERINA. The more I'm wronged, the more his spite shows. What — did he marry me to starve me? Beggars that come to my father's door get an alms on request. If not, they meet charity elsewhere. But I, who never knew how to beg, nor ever needed to, am starved for food, dizzy for lack of sleep — kept awake with oaths and fed with brawling. And what spites me most beyond all these needs — he does it in the name of perfect love, as if to say that if I should sleep or eat, it would be deadly sickness or present death. Please, go and get me something to eat. I don't care what, as long as it's wholesome food.

[3] GRUMIO. What do you say to a calf's foot?

[4] KATHERINA. That's exceedingly good — please let me have it.

[5] GRUMIO. I'm afraid it's too choleric a meat. What do you say to a fat tripe finely grilled?

[6] KATHERINA. I like that well — good Grumio, fetch it for me.

[7] GRUMIO. I can't say — I'm afraid it's choleric. What do you say to a piece of beef and mustard?

[8] KATHERINA. A dish I love to eat.

[9] GRUMIO. Yes, but the mustard is a little too hot.

[10] KATHERINA. Why then, the beef — and let the mustard rest.

[11] GRUMIO. No, then I won't. You shall have the mustard, or else you get no beef from Grumio.

[12] KATHERINA. Then both, or one, or whatever you like.

[13] GRUMIO. Why then, the mustard without the beef.

[14] KATHERINA. Go, get out — you false deluding slave,

[15] [_Beats him._]

[16] you who feed me with just the name of meat. Sorrow upon you and all the pack of you that triumph like this over my misery! Go, get out, I say.

[17] Enter Petruchio with a dish of meat, and Hortensio.

[18] PETRUCHIO. How is my Kate? What, sweetheart — all dejected?

[19] HORTENSIO. Mistress, how are you?

[20] KATHERINA. Honestly — as cold as can be.

[21] PETRUCHIO. Pluck up your spirits. Look at me cheerfully. Here, love — you see how diligent I am, dressing your meat myself and bringing it to you.

[22] [_Sets the dish on a table._]

[23] I'm sure, sweet Kate, this kindness deserves thanks. What — not a word? No, then you don't like it, and all my efforts have come to nothing. Here — take this dish away.

[24] KATHERINA. Please — let it stand.

[25] PETRUCHIO. The smallest service is repaid with thanks. And so shall mine be, before you touch the meat.

[26] KATHERINA. I thank you, sir.

[27] HORTENSIO. Signior Petruchio, for shame — you are to blame. Come, Mistress Kate — I'll keep you company.

[28] PETRUCHIO.

[29] [_Aside._] Eat it all up, Hortensio, if you love me.

[30] Much good may it do your gentle heart! Kate, eat fast. And now, my honey love, we'll return to your father's house and revel there as splendidly as the best, with silken coats and caps and golden rings, with ruffs and cuffs and farthingales and things, with scarves and fans and double change of finery, with amber bracelets, beads, and all this knavery. What — have you dined? The tailor is waiting on your leisure, to deck your body with his ruffling treasure.

[31] Enter Tailor.

[32] Come, tailor, let's see these ornaments. Lay out the gown.

[33] Enter Haberdasher.

[34] What news with you, sir?

[35] HABERDASHER. Here is the cap your worship ordered.

[36] PETRUCHIO. Why, this was moulded on a porringer — a velvet dish. Fie, fie! It's vulgar and filthy. Why, it's a cockle or a walnut-shell, a knack, a toy, a trick, a baby's cap. Away with it! Come, let me have a bigger one.

[37] KATHERINA. I'll have no bigger. This fits the fashion, and gentlewomen wear caps like these.

[38] PETRUCHIO. When you are gentle, you shall have one too — and not until then.

[39] HORTENSIO.

[40] [_Aside._] That won't be soon.

[41] KATHERINA. Why, sir, I trust I may have leave to speak — and speak I will. I'm no child, no babe. Your betters have allowed me to speak my mind, and if you can't, you'd best stop your ears. My tongue will tell the anger of my heart, or else my heart, holding it in, will break. And rather than have that happen, I'll be free, even to the utmost, to speak as I please.

[42] PETRUCHIO. Why, you speak true — it's a paltry cap, a custard-coffin, a trinket, a silken pie. I love you the more for not liking it.

[43] KATHERINA. Love me or love me not, I like the cap. And this one I'll have, or none at all.

[44] [_Exit Haberdasher._]

[45] PETRUCHIO. Your gown? Why, yes — come, tailor, let's see it. Oh mercy, God! What masquerade stuff is here? What's this? A sleeve? It's like a demi-cannon. What — carved up and down like an apple tart? Here's snip and nip and cut and slish and slash, like a censer in a barber's shop. Why, what in the devil's name, tailor, do you call this?

[46] HORTENSIO.

[47] [_Aside._] I see she's likely to have neither cap nor gown.

[48] TAILOR. You told me to make it orderly and well, according to the fashion and the times.

[49] PETRUCHIO. Indeed, I did — but if you remember, I didn't tell you to ruin it to the times. Go, hop over every gutter on your way home — you'll hop without my custom, sir. I'll have none of it. Off with you! Make the best of it.

[50] KATHERINA. I never saw a better-made gown — more elegant, more pleasing, nor more commendable. It seems you mean to make a puppet of me.

[51] PETRUCHIO. Why, true — he means to make a puppet of you.

[52] TAILOR. She says your worship means to make a puppet of her.

[53] PETRUCHIO. Oh, monstrous arrogance! You lie, you thread, you thimble, you yard, three-quarters, half-yard, quarter, nail! You flea, you nit, you winter-cricket you! Braved in my own house with a skein of thread! Away! You rag, you scrap, you remnant — or I shall so measure you with your own yard-stick that you'll think on prating as long as you live! I tell you — you, you've ruined her gown.

[54] TAILOR. Your worship is mistaken. The gown was made just as my master had directed. Grumio gave the order for how it should be done.

[55] GRUMIO. I gave him no order — I gave him the material.

[56] TAILOR. But how did you want it to be made?

[57] GRUMIO. Indeed, sir, with needle and thread.

[58] TAILOR. But didn't you request it to be cut?

[59] GRUMIO. You've trimmed many things.

[60] TAILOR. I have.

[61] GRUMIO. Don't trim me. You've faced down many men — don't face me. I'll neither be trimmed nor faced. I tell you — I told your master to cut out the gown. But I did not tell him to cut it to pieces. Therefore, you lie.

[62] TAILOR. Why, here's the note of the fashion to testify.

[63] PETRUCHIO. Read it.

[64] GRUMIO. The note lies in his throat, if he says I said so.

[65] TAILOR. '_Imprimis_, a loose-bodied gown.'

[66] GRUMIO. Master, if ever I said 'loose-bodied gown,' sew me in the skirts of it and beat me to death with a ball of brown thread. I said: a gown.

[67] PETRUCHIO. Proceed.

[68] TAILOR. 'With a small compassed cape.'

[69] GRUMIO. I admit the cape.

[70] TAILOR. 'With a trunk sleeve.'

[71] GRUMIO. I admit two sleeves.

[72] TAILOR. 'The sleeves curiously cut.'

[73] PETRUCHIO. Yes — there's the villainy.

[74] GRUMIO. Error in the bill, sir — error in the bill. I ordered that the sleeves should be cut out, and sewn up again. And that I'll prove on you, though your little finger were armed with a thimble.

[75] TAILOR. This is true, what I say — and I had you in a place where you'd know it.

[76] GRUMIO. I'm ready for you straightaway. You take the bill, give me your yard-stick, and don't spare me.

[77] HORTENSIO. God help us, Grumio! Then he shall have no advantage.

[78] PETRUCHIO. Well, sir, in short — the gown is not for me.

[79] GRUMIO. You're right, sir — it's for my mistress.

[80] PETRUCHIO. Go, take it up for your master's use.

[81] GRUMIO. Villain, not for your life! Take up my mistress's gown for your master's use!

[82] PETRUCHIO. Why, sir — what's your meaning in that?

[83] GRUMIO. Oh, sir, the meaning is deeper than you think. Take up my mistress's gown for his master's use! Oh fie, fie, fie!

[84] PETRUCHIO.

[85] [_Aside._] Hortensio, say that you'll see the tailor paid.

[86] [_To Tailor._] Go, take it away — be off, and say no more.

[87] HORTENSIO.

[88] [_Aside to Tailor._] Tailor, I'll pay you for your gown tomorrow.

[89] Don't take offence at his hasty words. Away, I say — give my regards to your master.

[90] [_Exit Tailor._]

[91] PETRUCHIO. Well, come, my Kate — we'll go to your father's, even in these honest plain clothes. Our purses shall be proud, our garments poor — for it's the mind that makes the body rich. And as the sun breaks through the darkest clouds, so honour shows in the poorest dress. Is the jay more precious than the lark because his feathers are more beautiful? Or is the adder better than the eel because his painted skin pleases the eye? Oh no, good Kate — neither are you the worse for this poor furniture and plain array. If you count it a shame, lay it on me. And so be cheerful — we'll go right away to feast and amuse ourselves at your father's house. Go call my men, and let's straight to him. And bring our horses to the end of Long-lane. There we'll mount, and walk to it on foot. Let's see — I think it's now about seven o'clock, and we may well come there by dinner-time.

[92] KATHERINA. I dare assure you, sir, it's nearly two — and it will be supper-time before you arrive.

[93] PETRUCHIO. It shall be seven before I get on the horse. Whatever I say, or do, or think to do — you're still crossing it. Sirs, leave it alone — I won't go today. And before I do, it shall be whatever time of day I say it is.

[94] HORTENSIO. Why, this gallant will command the sun next.

[95] [_Exeunt._]

# Act 4, Scene 1 — A hall in Petruchio’s country house

[0] Enter Grumio.

[1] GRUMIO. Curse it, curse it on all tired nags, on all mad masters, and all foul roads! Was ever a man so beaten? Was ever a man so spattered? Was ever a man so weary? I'm sent ahead to make a fire, and they're coming after to warm themselves. Now, if I weren't a little pot that boils up fast, my very lips might freeze to my teeth, my tongue to the roof of my mouth, my heart in my belly, before I came near a fire to thaw me. But blowing on the fire will warm me — for, considering this weather, a taller man than me will catch cold. Hello, ho! Curtis!

[2] Enter Curtis.

[3] CURTIS. Who's that calling so chillily?

[4] GRUMIO. A piece of ice — if you doubt it, you may slide from my shoulder to my heel with no greater run-up than my head and neck. A fire, good Curtis.

[5] CURTIS. Are my master and his wife coming, Grumio?

[6] GRUMIO. Oh yes! Curtis, yes — so fire, fire, and don't throw any water on it.

[7] CURTIS. Is she such a hot shrew as she's reputed to be?

[8] GRUMIO. She was, good Curtis, before this frost. But you know winter tames man, woman, and beast — it has tamed my old master, and my new mistress, and myself, fellow Curtis.

[9] CURTIS. Away with you, you three-inch fool! I am no beast.

[10] GRUMIO. Am I just three inches? Why, your horn is a foot — and so long am I at the very least. But will you make a fire, or shall I complain about you to our mistress, whose hand — she being now at hand — you shall soon feel, to your cold comfort, for being slow at your hot duty?

[11] CURTIS. Please, good Grumio, tell me — how goes the world?

[12] GRUMIO. A cold world, Curtis, in every department but yours — so fire! Do your duty, and have your due, for my master and mistress are nearly frozen to death.

[13] CURTIS. There's fire ready. So, good Grumio — the news.

[14] GRUMIO. Why — 'Jack boy! Ho, boy!' and as much news as you want.

[15] CURTIS. Come on — you're so full of con tricks.

[16] GRUMIO. Why, therefore, fire — for I've caught an extreme cold. Where's the cook? Is supper ready, the house tidied, rushes strewn, cobwebs swept, the servingmen in their new fustian and white stockings, every officer in his wedding clothes? Are the cups clean inside, the girls neat outside, and carpets laid, everything in order?

[17] CURTIS. All ready. So please — the news.

[18] GRUMIO. First, know that my horse is exhausted, and my master and mistress have fallen out.

[19] CURTIS. How?

[20] GRUMIO. Out of their saddles into the dirt — and there hangs a tale.

[21] CURTIS. Let's have it, good Grumio.

[22] GRUMIO. Lend me your ear.

[23] CURTIS. Here.

[24] GRUMIO.

[25] [_Striking him._] There.

[26] CURTIS. This is to feel a tale, not to hear a tale.

[27] GRUMIO. That's why it's called a sensible tale — and this cuff was just to knock at your ear and beg for a listen. Now I begin: _Imprimis_, we came down a foul hill, my master riding behind my mistress—

[28] CURTIS. Both on one horse?

[29] GRUMIO. What's that to you?

[30] CURTIS. Why — a horse.

[31] GRUMIO. You tell the tale yourself. But if you hadn't interrupted me, you'd have heard how her horse fell, and she under her horse; you'd have heard in how miry a place, how she was filthy with mud; how he left her with the horse on top of her; how he beat me because her horse stumbled; how she waded through the mud to pull him off me; how he swore; how she prayed, who never prayed before; how I cried; how the horses ran away; how her bridle was broken; how I lost my crupper — with many other things worth remembering, which now shall die in oblivion, and you'll return inexperienced to your grave.

[32] CURTIS. By this reckoning he's more of a shrew than she is.

[33] GRUMIO. Yes — and that you and the proudest of you all shall find out when he comes home. But why am I talking about this? Call out Nathaniel, Joseph, Nicholas, Philip, Walter, Sugarsop, and the rest. Let their hair be smoothly combed, their blue coats brushed, and their garters of matching knit. Let them curtsy with their left legs, and not presume to touch a hair of my master's horse-tail until they kiss their hands. Are they all ready?

[34] CURTIS. They are.

[35] GRUMIO. Call them out.

[36] CURTIS. Do you hear? Ho! You must meet my master to support my mistress.

[37] GRUMIO. Why, she has a face of her own.

[38] CURTIS. Who doesn't know that?

[39] GRUMIO. You, it seems, since you call for company to support her.

[40] CURTIS. I call them out to do her credit.

[41] GRUMIO. Why, she doesn't come to borrow anything of them.

[42] Enter four or five Servants.

[43] NATHANIEL. Welcome home, Grumio!

[44] PHILIP. How now, Grumio!

[45] JOSEPH. What, Grumio!

[46] NICHOLAS. Fellow Grumio!

[47] NATHANIEL. How now, old lad!

[48] GRUMIO. Welcome, you; how now, you; what, you; fellow, you — and that's enough for greetings. Now, my smart companions, is everything ready and neat?

[49] NATHANIEL. Everything is ready. How near is our master?

[50] GRUMIO. Right at hand — dismounted by this time. So don't — by God's passion, silence! I hear my master.

[51] Enter Petruchio and Katherina.

[52] PETRUCHIO. Where are these knaves? What — no man at the door to hold my stirrup or to take my horse? Where is Nathaniel, Gregory, Philip?

[53] ALL SERVANTS. Here, here, sir; here, sir.

[54] PETRUCHIO. Here, sir! Here, sir! Here, sir! Here, sir! You logger-headed and unpolished grooms! What — no attendance? No regard? No duty? Where is the foolish knave I sent on ahead?

[55] GRUMIO. Here, sir — as foolish as I was before.

[56] PETRUCHIO. You peasant lout! You whoreson malt-horse drudge! Didn't I tell you to meet me in the park, and to bring these rascal knaves along with you?

[57] GRUMIO. Nathaniel's coat, sir, wasn't fully made. Gabriel's pumps were unpinked at the heel. There was no link to dye Peter's hat black. Walter's dagger hadn't come back from the sheath-maker. There were none decent except Adam, Ralph, and Gregory. The rest were ragged, old, and beggarly. Yet, as they are, here they are, come to meet you.

[58] PETRUCHIO. Go, rascals, go and bring my supper in.

[59] [_Exeunt some of the Servants._]

[60] Where is the life that late I led? Where are those—? Sit down, Kate, and welcome. Food, food, food, food!

[61] Re-enter Servants with supper.

[62] Why, when, I say? — No, good sweet Kate, be merry. Off with my boots, you rogues, you villains — when? 'It was the friar of orders grey, as he forth walked on his way:' — Out, you rogue! You pull my foot askew.

[63] [_Strikes him._]

[64] Take that, and mind how you pull off the other one. Be merry, Kate. Some water here — what, ho! Where's my spaniel Troilus? Fellow, get yourself out and tell my cousin Ferdinand to come here.

[65] [_Exit Servant._]

[66] One, Kate, that you must kiss and get acquainted with. Where are my slippers? Shall I have some water? Come, Kate, and wash, and welcome with all my heart.

[67] [_Servant lets the ewer fall. Petruchio strikes him._]

[68] You whoreson villain! Will you drop it?

[69] KATHERINA. Patience, please — it was an unwilling fault.

[70] PETRUCHIO. A whoreson, beetle-headed, flap-eared knave! Come, Kate, sit down — I know you're hungry. Will you give thanks, sweet Kate, or shall I? What's this — mutton?

[71] FIRST SERVANT. Yes.

[72] PETRUCHIO. Who brought it?

[73] PETER. I did.

[74] PETRUCHIO. It's burnt — and so is all the meat. What dogs are these! Where is the rascal cook? How dared you, villains, bring it from the kitchen and serve it thus to me, who can't stand it?

[75] [_Throws the meat, etc., at them._]

[76] There — take it back, plates, cups, and all. You heedless blockheads and unmannered slaves! What — do you grumble? I'll deal with you in a moment.

[77] KATHERINA. Please, husband, don't be so worked up. The meat was fine, if you'd just have been content.

[78] PETRUCHIO. I tell you, Kate, it was burnt and dried out — and I'm specifically forbidden to touch it. It breeds bile, plants anger. Better that we both fast — since by our own nature we're choleric — than feed our temper with over-roasted flesh. Be patient — tomorrow it will be amended. And for tonight we'll fast together. Come — I'll bring you to your bridal chamber.

[79] [_Exeunt Petruchio, Katherina and Curtis._]

[80] NATHANIEL. Peter, did you ever see the like?

[81] PETER. He outdoes her in her own humour.

[82] Re-enter Curtis.

[83] GRUMIO. Where is he?

[84] CURTIS. In her chamber, preaching a sermon on continence to her. He rails, and swears, and scolds, until she, poor soul, doesn't know which way to stand, look, or speak — and sits like one just risen from a dream. Away, away — for he's coming this way.

[85] [_Exeunt._]

[86] Re-enter Petruchio.

[87] PETRUCHIO. Thus I have politicly begun my reign — and it is my hope to end it successfully. My falcon now is keen and exceedingly empty — and till she stoops she must not be fully fed, for then she'll never look at her lure. Another method I have for taming my haggard, to make her come and know her keeper's call — that is, to watch her, as we watch these kites that flutter and beat their wings, refusing to be obedient. She ate no meat today, and shall eat none. Last night she didn't sleep, and tonight she shall not either. As with the meat, I'll find some undeserved fault about the making of the bed. Here I'll fling the pillow, there the bolster, this way the coverlet, another way the sheets — yes, and in the middle of this chaos I'll pretend that all is done in reverent care of her. And, in conclusion, she shall stay awake all night. If she happens to nod off, I'll rail and brawl, and with the clamour keep her awake. This is a way to kill a wife with kindness — and thus I'll curb her mad and headstrong humour. Whoever knows a better way to tame a shrew, let him speak now — it's charity to share.

[88] [_Exit._]

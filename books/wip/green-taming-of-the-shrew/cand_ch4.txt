# Act 3, Scene 1 — Padua. A room in Baptista’s house

[0] Enter Lucentio, Hortensio and Bianca.

[1] LUCENTIO. Fiddler, hold off — you're getting too forward, sir. Have you so soon forgotten the welcome her sister Katherine treated you to?

[2] HORTENSIO. But, quibbling pedant, this lady is the patroness of heavenly harmony. So let me have priority — and when we've spent an hour on music, your lecture shall have the same time.

[3] LUCENTIO. Ridiculous ass — you never read far enough to know why music was invented! Wasn't it to refresh a man's mind after his studies or his usual labour? So let me read philosophy, and while I pause, serve up your harmony.

[4] HORTENSIO. Fellow, I won't put up with these provocations of yours.

[5] BIANCA. Why, gentlemen, you wrong me twice over, by quarrelling over what lies in my choice. I'm not a schoolboy under threat of the rod — I won't be tied to set hours or appointed times. I'll learn my lessons as I please. And, to cut off all strife, here let us sit down. You take your instrument, play in the meantime — his lecture will be finished before you have tuned.

[6] HORTENSIO. You'll leave his lecture when I'm in tune?

[7] [_Retires._]

[8] LUCENTIO. That'll be never. Tune your instrument.

[9] BIANCA. Where did we leave off?

[10] LUCENTIO. Here, madam: _Hic ibat Simois; hic est Sigeia tellus; Hic steterat Priami regia celsa senis._

[11] BIANCA. Translate it.

[12] LUCENTIO. _Hic ibat_, as I told you before; _Simois_, I am Lucentio; _hic est_, son to Vincentio of Pisa; _Sigeia tellus_, disguised this way to win your love; _Hic steterat_, and that Lucentio who comes wooing; _Priami_, is my man Tranio; _regia_, taking my place; _celsa senis_, so that we might fool the old pantaloon.

[13] HORTENSIO. [_Returning._] Madam, my instrument is in tune.

[14] BIANCA. Let's hear.

[15] [_Hortensio plays._]

[16] Oh, ugh! The treble jars.

[17] LUCENTIO. Spit in the hole, man, and tune it again.

[18] BIANCA. Now let me see if I can translate it: _Hic ibat Simois_, I don't know you; _hic est Sigeia tellus_, I don't trust you; _Hic steterat Priami_, take care he doesn't hear us; _regia_, don't presume; _celsa senis_, don't despair.

[19] HORTENSIO. Madam, it's in tune now.

[20] LUCENTIO. All but the bass.

[21] HORTENSIO. The bass is right — it's the base knave that jars.

[22] [_Aside_] How fiery and forward our pedant is!

[23] Now, on my life, the rascal is courting my love. Little pedant, I'll watch you better from now on.

[24] BIANCA. In time I may believe — but I still mistrust.

[25] LUCENTIO. Don't mistrust it — for sure, Æacides was Ajax, named so from his grandfather.

[26] BIANCA. I must believe my master — otherwise, I promise you, I'd still be arguing about that doubt. But let it rest. Now, Licio, to you. Good master, please don't take it amiss that I've been so playful with both of you.

[27] HORTENSIO.

[28] [_To Lucentio_] You may go take a walk and give me some space.

[29] My lessons don't make music in three parts.

[30] LUCENTIO. Are you so strict, sir? Well, I must wait.

[31] [_Aside_] And keep an eye out too. Unless I'm mistaken,

[32] our fine musician is growing amorous.

[33] HORTENSIO. Madam, before you touch the instrument, to learn the order of my fingering, I must begin with the basics of the art. I'll teach you the gamut in a quicker fashion — more pleasant, pithy, and effective than has been taught by anyone in my trade. And there it is in writing, neatly drawn out.

[34] BIANCA. Why, I'm past the gamut long ago.

[35] HORTENSIO. Yet read the gamut of Hortensio.

[36] BIANCA. _Gamut_ I am, the foundation of all harmony; _A re_, to plead Hortensio's passion; _B mi_, Bianca, take him for your lord; _C fa ut_, who loves with all his heart; _D sol re_, one key, two notes I have; _E la mi_, show pity or I die. You call this a gamut? Pah, I don't like it. Old fashions please me best — I'm not so fastidious as to swap true rules for odd inventions.

[37] Enter a Servant.

[38] SERVANT. Mistress, your father asks you to leave your books and help to dress up your sister's chamber. You know tomorrow is the wedding day.

[39] BIANCA. Farewell, sweet masters, both of you. I must be off.

[40] [_Exeunt Bianca and Servant._]

[41] LUCENTIO. Truly, mistress, then I have no reason to stay.

[42] [_Exit._]

[43] HORTENSIO. But I do have reason to pry into this pedant. He looks to me as though he were in love. Yet if your thoughts, Bianca, are so cheap as to cast your wandering eyes on every commoner — let anyone who wants you seize you. If I once catch you straying, Hortensio will be quits with you by switching too.

[44] [_Exit._]

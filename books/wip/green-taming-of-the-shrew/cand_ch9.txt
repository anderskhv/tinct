# Act 4, Scene 4 — Padua. Before Baptista’s house

[0] Enter Tranio and the Pedant dressed like Vincentio.

[1] TRANIO. Sir, this is the house — would you like me to call?

[2] PEDANT. Yes, of course. And unless I'm mistaken, Signior Baptista may remember me from nearly twenty years ago in Genoa, where we were both lodgers at the Pegasus.

[3] TRANIO. That's good. Now hold your own, in any case, with the gravity that befits a father.

[4] PEDANT. I'll guarantee it. But, sir, here comes your boy — he ought to be schooled in his part.

[5] Enter Biondello.

[6] TRANIO. Have no fear about him. Sirrah Biondello, do your duty thoroughly now, I advise you. Imagine he were the real Vincentio.

[7] BIONDELLO. Tut! Don't worry about me.

[8] TRANIO. But have you finished your errand to Baptista?

[9] BIONDELLO. I told him your father was in Venice, and that you expected him in Padua this very day.

[10] TRANIO. You're a fine fellow — here, take this to buy yourself a drink. Here comes Baptista. Compose your face, sir.

[11] Enter Baptista and Lucentio.

[12] Signior Baptista, well met indeed!

[13] [_To the Pedant._] Sir, this is the gentleman I told you of.

[14] I beg you, stand a good father to me now — give me Bianca for my inheritance.

[15] PEDANT. Easy, son! Sir, by your leave: having come to Padua to collect some debts, my son Lucentio informed me of a serious matter — a love between your daughter and himself. And because of the good reputation I hear of you, and because of the love he bears your daughter and she him, I'm content — so as not to delay him too long — as a good father should, to see him matched. And if you're inclined to think no worse of it than I do, then upon some agreement you'll find me ready and willing, with single consent, to have her so bestowed. I cannot be picky with you, Signior Baptista, of whom I hear so much good.

[16] BAPTISTA. Sir, pardon what I'm about to say. Your plainness and brevity please me well. It's quite true that your son Lucentio here loves my daughter, and she loves him — or both of them are deeply concealing their feelings. And so, if you'll say no more than this — that you'll deal with him as a father should, and settle on my daughter a sufficient dowry — the match is made, and everything is decided: your son shall have my daughter with my consent.

[17] TRANIO. Thank you, sir. Now where do you think it best we conclude the betrothal, and have such assurances exchanged as will satisfy both parties?

[18] BAPTISTA. Not in my house, Lucentio — you know that pitchers have ears, and I have many servants. Besides, old Gremio is always listening, and we could easily be interrupted.

[19] TRANIO. Then at my lodging, if it suits you. My father is staying there, and there tonight we'll handle the business privately and well. Send for your daughter by your servant here. My boy will fetch the scrivener at once. The worst part is that, on such short notice, you're likely to have only a thin and meager supper.

[20] BAPTISTA. That suits me well. Cambio, hurry home and tell Bianca to make herself ready right away. And, if you like, tell her what's happened: Lucentio's father has arrived in Padua, and she's likely to become Lucentio's wife.

[21] LUCENTIO. I pray the gods she may — with all my heart!

[22] TRANIO. Don't waste time on the gods — get going. Signior Baptista, shall I lead the way? Welcome! One simple course is likely to be your fare. Come, sir — we'll make it up to you in Pisa.

[23] BAPTISTA. I'll follow you.

[24] [_Exeunt Tranio, Pedant and Baptista._]

[25] BIONDELLO. Cambio!

[26] LUCENTIO. What are you saying, Biondello?

[27] BIONDELLO. Did you see my master wink and laugh at you?

[28] LUCENTIO. Biondello, what about it?

[29] BIONDELLO. Faith, nothing — except he's left me here behind to explain the meaning or moral of his signs and signals.

[30] LUCENTIO. Please, then, interpret them for me.

[31] BIONDELLO. Like this, then: Baptista is safe, busy talking with the false father of a deceitful son.

[32] LUCENTIO. And what of him?

[33] BIONDELLO. His daughter is to be brought by you to the supper.

[34] LUCENTIO. And then?

[35] BIONDELLO. The old priest at Saint Luke's church is at your command at any hour.

[36] LUCENTIO. And what does all this mean?

[37] BIONDELLO. I can't say for certain — except that they're busy with a counterfeit betrothal. Take your assurance of her, _cum privilegio ad imprimendum solum_ — to the church! Take the priest, clerk, and some sufficient honest witnesses. If this isn't what you're looking for, I've nothing more to say, except — bid Bianca farewell for ever and a day.

[38] [_Going._]

[39] LUCENTIO. Do you hear me, Biondello?

[40] BIONDELLO. I can't linger. I once knew a girl who was married in an afternoon, while she was going into the garden for parsley to stuff a rabbit — and so it may be with you, sir. So farewell, sir. My master has ordered me to go to Saint Luke's and tell the priest to be ready when you arrive with your appendix.

[41] [_Exit._]

[42] LUCENTIO. I may, and will, if she's willing. She'll be pleased — so why should I doubt? Come what may, I'll go straight to her. It would be a hard thing if Cambio went without her.

[43] [_Exit._]

# Act 4, Scene 2 — Padua. Before Baptista’s house

[0] Enter Tranio and Hortensio.

[1] TRANIO. Is it possible, friend Licio, that Mistress Bianca cares for any man other than Lucentio? I tell you, sir, she gives me strong encouragement.

[2] HORTENSIO. Sir, to satisfy you about what I've said, stand aside and observe the manner of his teaching.

[3] [_They stand aside._]

[4] Enter Bianca and Lucentio.

[5] LUCENTIO. Now, mistress, are you profiting from what you read?

[6] BIANCA. What is it, master, that you read? Tell me that first.

[7] LUCENTIO. I read what I profess — _The Art of Love_.

[8] BIANCA. And may you prove, sir, master of your art!

[9] LUCENTIO. While you, sweet dear, prove mistress of my heart.

[10] [_They retire._]

[11] HORTENSIO. Fast workers, indeed! Now tell me, please — you, who dared swear that your Mistress Bianca loved no one in the world as well as Lucentio.

[12] TRANIO. Oh, spiteful love! Inconstant womankind! I tell you, Licio, this is astonishing.

[13] HORTENSIO. Don't be mistaken any longer — I am not Licio. Nor a musician, as I seem to be. I'm one who scorns to live in this disguise, for a woman who leaves a gentleman and makes a god of such a low fellow. Know, sir, that my name is Hortensio.

[14] TRANIO. Signior Hortensio, I have often heard of your wholehearted affection for Bianca. And since my own eyes are now witness to her fickleness, I will, if you're willing, join you and forswear Bianca and her love forever.

[15] HORTENSIO. See, how they kiss and court! Signior Lucentio, here is my hand, and here I firmly vow never to woo her again — and I forswear her, as someone unworthy of all the former attentions I've foolishly lavished on her.

[16] TRANIO. And here I take the same unfeigned oath — never to marry her, even if she pleaded with me. Shame on her! See how shamelessly she courts him!

[17] HORTENSIO. I wish all the world but him had quite forsworn her! As for me, so that I may surely keep my oath, I'll be married to a wealthy widow before three days pass — one who has loved me as long as I have loved this proud, disdainful haggard. And so farewell, Signior Lucentio. Kindness in women, not their beautiful looks, shall win my love. And so I take my leave, in the same resolution as I swore before.

[18] [_Exit Hortensio. Lucentio and Bianca come forward._]

[19] TRANIO. Mistress Bianca, may you be blessed with such grace as belongs to a lover's happy state! No — I've caught you napping, gentle love, and I've forsworn you along with Hortensio.

[20] BIANCA. Tranio, you're joking — but have you both really forsworn me?

[21] TRANIO. Mistress, we have.

[22] LUCENTIO. Then we are rid of Licio.

[23] TRANIO. Honestly, he'll have a lively widow now, who shall be wooed and wedded in a day.

[24] BIANCA. God give him joy!

[25] TRANIO. Yes — and he'll tame her.

[26] BIANCA. So he says, Tranio.

[27] TRANIO. Truly, he's gone off to taming-school.

[28] BIANCA. Taming-school! What — is there such a place?

[29] TRANIO. Yes, mistress — and Petruchio is the master. He teaches eleven and twenty long tricks for taming a shrew and charming her chattering tongue.

[30] Enter Biondello, running.

[31] BIONDELLO. Oh master, master! I've watched so long I'm dog-tired — but at last I've spied an ancient sort of angel coming down the hill who will serve our turn.

[32] TRANIO. What is he, Biondello?

[33] BIONDELLO. Master, a merchant or a pedant, I don't know which — but formal in dress, and in gait and countenance very much like a father.

[34] LUCENTIO. And what of him, Tranio?

[35] TRANIO. If he's credulous and believes my story, I'll make him glad to play Vincentio and give assurances to Baptista Minola as if he were the real Vincentio. Take in your love, and then leave me to it.

[36] [_Exeunt Lucentio and Bianca._]

[37] Enter a Pedant.

[38] PEDANT. God save you, sir!

[39] TRANIO. And you, sir! You are welcome. Are you travelling on far, or are you at the end of your journey?

[40] PEDANT. Sir, at the end for a week or two. But then on further — as far as Rome. And so to Tripoli, if God grants me life.

[41] TRANIO. What country are you from, please?

[42] PEDANT. From Mantua.

[43] TRANIO. From Mantua, sir? Indeed — God forbid! And come to Padua, regardless of your life?

[44] PEDANT. My life, sir! How so, please? That sounds serious.

[45] TRANIO. It's death for anyone from Mantua to come to Padua. Don't you know the cause? Your ships are detained at Venice. And the Duke — for a private quarrel between your Duke and him — has published and proclaimed it openly. It's a wonder — but, being only newly arrived, you might otherwise have heard it proclaimed.

[46] PEDANT. Alas, sir! It's worse for me than that — for I have bills of exchange from Florence and must deliver them here.

[47] TRANIO. Well, sir, to do you a courtesy, this is what I'll do — and this is what I'd advise you. First, tell me — have you ever been to Pisa?

[48] PEDANT. Yes, sir — in Pisa I have often been. Pisa, famous for its grave citizens.

[49] TRANIO. Among them, do you know one Vincentio?

[50] PEDANT. I don't know him, but I have heard of him — a merchant of incomparable wealth.

[51] TRANIO. He is my father, sir. And, truth to say, in countenance he somewhat resembles you.

[52] BIONDELLO.

[53] [_Aside._] As much as an apple resembles an oyster — same thing entirely.

[54] TRANIO. To save your life in this extremity, this is the favour I'll do you for his sake. And don't count it the worst of all your fortunes that you happen to look like Sir Vincentio. You shall take on his name and credit, and in my house you shall be lodged as a friend. See that you act the part as you should! You understand me, sir. So you shall stay until you've concluded your business in the city. If this is a courtesy, sir, accept it.

[55] PEDANT. Oh, sir, I do — and I shall always regard you as the patron of my life and liberty.

[56] TRANIO. Then come with me, and we'll set the matter up. This, by the way, I should let you understand: my father is expected here any day to pass the bond for a dowry in the marriage between me and one Baptista's daughter here. I'll instruct you in all these circumstances. Come with me to dress as befits you.

[57] [_Exeunt._]

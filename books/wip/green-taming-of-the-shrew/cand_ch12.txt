# Act 5, Scene 2 — A room in Lucentio’s house

[0] Enter Baptista, Vincentio, Gremio, the Pedant, Lucentio, Bianca, Petruchio, Katherina, Hortensio and Widow. Tranio, Biondello, Grumio and Others, attending.

[1] LUCENTIO. At last — though long in coming — our jarring notes agree. And it is time, when raging war is done, to smile at narrow escapes and dangers past. My fair Bianca, bid my father welcome, while I with the same kindness welcome yours. Brother Petruchio, sister Katherina, and you, Hortensio, with your loving widow — feast with the best, and welcome to my house. My banquet is to settle our stomachs after our great good cheer. Please, sit down — for now we sit to chat as well as to eat.

[2] [_They sit at table._]

[3] PETRUCHIO. Nothing but sit and sit, and eat and eat!

[4] BAPTISTA. Padua offers this kindness, son Petruchio.

[5] PETRUCHIO. Padua offers nothing that is not kind.

[6] HORTENSIO. For both our sakes, I wish that word were true.

[7] PETRUCHIO. Now, on my life, Hortensio is afraid of his widow.

[8] WIDOW. Then never trust me, if I am afraid.

[9] PETRUCHIO. You're very quick, and yet you miss my meaning: I mean Hortensio is afraid of you.

[10] WIDOW. He who is dizzy thinks the world turns round.

[11] PETRUCHIO. Roundly replied.

[12] KATHERINA. Mistress, what do you mean by that?

[13] WIDOW. This is what I gather from him.

[14] PETRUCHIO. Conceives from me! How does Hortensio like that?

[15] HORTENSIO. My widow says this is how she conceives her meaning.

[16] PETRUCHIO. Very well patched. Kiss him for that, good widow.

[17] KATHERINA. 'He who is dizzy thinks the world turns round' — please tell me what you meant by that.

[18] WIDOW. Your husband, being troubled with a shrew, measures my husband's sorrow by his own woe. And now you know my meaning.

[19] KATHERINA. A very mean meaning.

[20] WIDOW. Right — I mean you.

[21] KATHERINA. And I am mean, indeed, by comparison with you.

[22] PETRUCHIO. At her, Kate!

[23] HORTENSIO. At her, widow!

[24] PETRUCHIO. A hundred marks, my Kate puts her down.

[25] HORTENSIO. That's my office.

[26] PETRUCHIO. Spoken like an officer — here's to you, lad.

[27] [_Drinks to Hortensio._]

[28] BAPTISTA. How does Gremio like these quick-witted folk?

[29] GREMIO. Believe me, sir, they butt heads well together.

[30] BIANCA. Head and butt! A hasty-witted person would say your head and butt were head and horn.

[31] VINCENTIO. Aye, mistress bride — has that woken you up?

[32] BIANCA. Aye, but it hasn't frightened me — therefore I'll sleep again.

[33] PETRUCHIO. No, you shan't. Since you've begun, prepare for a sharp jest or two.

[34] BIANCA. Am I your bird? I mean to change my bush — then pursue me as you draw your bow. You are welcome, all of you.

[35] [_Exeunt Bianca, Katherina and Widow._]

[36] PETRUCHIO. She's gotten ahead of me. Here, Signior Tranio — this is the bird you aimed at, though you didn't hit her. So a toast to all who shot and missed.

[37] TRANIO. O, sir! Lucentio slipped me like his greyhound, who runs by himself and catches for his master.

[38] PETRUCHIO. A good swift simile — but somewhat doggish.

[39] TRANIO. It's just as well, sir, that you hunted for yourself — it's thought your deer holds you at bay.

[40] BAPTISTA. O ho, Petruchio! Tranio hits you now.

[41] LUCENTIO. I thank you for that thrust, good Tranio.

[42] HORTENSIO. Confess, confess — hasn't he hit you here?

[43] PETRUCHIO. He's chafed me a little, I admit. And as the jest glanced off me, it's ten to one it wounded you two outright.

[44] BAPTISTA. Now, in good earnest, son Petruchio, I think you have the biggest shrew of all.

[45] PETRUCHIO. Well, I say no — and therefore, for proof, let each of us send for his wife. And the one whose wife is most obedient — who comes first when he sends for her — shall win the wager we'll propose.

[46] HORTENSIO. Agreed. What's the wager?

[47] LUCENTIO. Twenty crowns.

[48] PETRUCHIO. Twenty crowns! I'd venture that much on my hawk or hound — but twenty times that much on my wife.

[49] LUCENTIO. A hundred, then.

[50] HORTENSIO. Agreed.

[51] PETRUCHIO. A match! It's done.

[52] HORTENSIO. Who shall begin?

[53] LUCENTIO. I will. Go, Biondello — tell your mistress to come to me.

[54] BIONDELLO. I go.

[55] [_Exit._]

[56] BAPTISTA. Son, I'll go halves with you that Bianca will come.

[57] LUCENTIO. I'll have no halves — I'll bear it all myself.

[58] Re-enter Biondello.

[59] How now! What news?

[60] BIONDELLO. Sir, my mistress sends word that she is busy and cannot come.

[61] PETRUCHIO. What! She's busy, and she cannot come! Is that an answer?

[62] GREMIO. Aye, and a kind one too — pray God, sir, your wife send you no worse.

[63] PETRUCHIO. I hope better.

[64] HORTENSIO. Sirrah Biondello, go and entreat my wife to come to me at once.

[65] [_Exit Biondello._]

[66] PETRUCHIO. O, ho! Entreat her! No — then she must come.

[67] HORTENSIO. I'm afraid, sir, that whatever you may do, yours will not be entreated.

[68] Re-enter Biondello.

[69] Now, where is my wife?

[70] BIONDELLO. She says you have some fine jest in hand. She will not come — she bids you come to her.

[71] PETRUCHIO. Worse and worse — she will not come! O vile, intolerable, not to be endured! Sirrah Grumio, go to your mistress — say I command her to come to me.

[72] [_Exit Grumio._]

[73] HORTENSIO. I know her answer.

[74] PETRUCHIO. What?

[75] HORTENSIO. She will not.

[76] PETRUCHIO. The worse luck for me — and there's an end.

[77] Re-enter Katherina.

[78] BAPTISTA. Now, by my faith — here comes Katherina!

[79] KATHERINA. What is your will, sir, that you send for me?

[80] PETRUCHIO. Where is your sister, and Hortensio's wife?

[81] KATHERINA. They are sitting and talking by the parlour fire.

[82] PETRUCHIO. Go fetch them here. If they refuse to come, beat them soundly out to their husbands. Away, I say, and bring them here at once.

[83] [_Exit Katherina._]

[84] LUCENTIO. Here's a wonder, if you talk of wonders.

[85] HORTENSIO. And so it is. I wonder what it foretells.

[86] PETRUCHIO. Indeed, it foretells peace — and love, and quiet life, awe-inspiring rule, and rightful authority. And, in short, everything that is sweet and happy.

[87] BAPTISTA. Now, good fortune to you, good Petruchio! You have won the wager — and I'll add to their losses twenty thousand crowns: another dowry to another daughter, for she is changed, as if she had never been the same.

[88] PETRUCHIO. No, I'll win my wager better still, and show more proof of her obedience — her newly built virtue and obedience. See where she comes, bringing your contrary wives as prisoners to her womanly persuasion.

[89] Re-enter Katherina with Bianca and Widow.

[90] Katherine, that cap of yours doesn't suit you. Off with that bauble — throw it underfoot.

[91] [_Katherina pulls off her cap and throws it down._]

[92] WIDOW. Lord, may I never have cause to sigh till I'm reduced to such a silly state!

[93] BIANCA. Fie! What a foolish duty do you call this?

[94] LUCENTIO. I wish your duty were as foolish too. The wisdom of your duty, fair Bianca, has cost me a hundred crowns since suppertime!

[95] BIANCA. The more fool you, for wagering on my duty.

[96] PETRUCHIO. Katherine, I charge you — tell these headstrong women what duty they owe their lords and husbands.

[97] WIDOW. Come, come, you're mocking — we'll have no lecturing.

[98] PETRUCHIO. Come on, I say — and begin first with her.

[99] WIDOW. She shall not.

[100] PETRUCHIO. I say she shall — and begin first with her.

[101] KATHERINA. Fie, fie! Unknit that threatening, unkind brow, and don't dart scornful glances from those eyes to wound your lord, your king, your governor. It blots your beauty as frost bites the meadows, ruins your reputation as whirlwinds shake fair buds — and in no way is it fitting or amiable. A woman moved is like a troubled fountain: muddy, ugly, thick, stripped of beauty. And while it is so, no one — however dry or thirsty — will deign to sip or touch a drop of it. Your husband is your lord, your life, your keeper, your head, your sovereign — one who cares for you, and for your maintenance commits his body to painful labour both by sea and land, to keep watch by night in storms, by day in cold, while you lie warm at home, secure and safe. And he asks no other tribute from you but love, kind looks, and true obedience — too small a payment for so great a debt. The same duty a subject owes the prince, a woman owes her husband. And when she is contrary, peevish, sullen, sour, and not obedient to his honest will, what is she but a foul, contentious rebel, and a graceless traitor to her loving lord? I am ashamed that women are so simple as to offer war where they should kneel for peace, or seek rule, supremacy, and command, when they are bound to serve, love, and obey. Why are our bodies soft and weak and smooth, unfit for toil and trouble in the world, except that our gentle natures and our hearts should match our outward form? Come, come, you contrary and incapable creatures! My spirit has been as big as any of yours, my heart as great, my reason perhaps more — to trade word for word and frown for frown. But now I see our lances are only straws, our strength as weak, our weakness beyond compare — seeming most what we are in truth the least. Then lower your prides, for it's no use, and place your hands beneath your husband's foot. As a token of that duty, if he please, my hand is ready — may it bring him ease.

[102] PETRUCHIO. Why, there's a woman! Come on, and kiss me, Kate.

[103] LUCENTIO. Well, carry on, old lad — for you've won it.

[104] VINCENTIO. It's a fine thing to hear, when children are dutiful.

[105] LUCENTIO. But a harsh thing to hear, when women are contrary.

[106] PETRUCHIO. Come, Kate, let's go to bed. We three are married — but you two are bested. It was I who won the wager,

[107] [_To Lucentio._] though you hit the bullseye —

[108] and being a winner, God give you good night!

[109] [_Exeunt Petruchio and Katherina._]

[110] HORTENSIO. Now go your ways — you've tamed a cursed shrew.

[111] LUCENTIO. It's a wonder, by your leave, that she will be tamed so.

[112] [_Exeunt._]

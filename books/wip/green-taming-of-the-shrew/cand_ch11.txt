# Act 5, Scene 1 — Padua. Before Lucentio’s house

[0] Enter on one side Biondello, Lucentio and Bianca; Gremio walking on the other side.

[1] BIONDELLO. Softly and swiftly, sir — the priest is ready.

[2] LUCENTIO. I'll fly, Biondello — but they may need you at home, so leave us.

[3] BIONDELLO. No, truly, I'll see you safely into the church and back, and then return to my master's as soon as I can.

[4] [_Exeunt Lucentio, Bianca and Biondello._]

[5] GREMIO. I'm amazed Cambio hasn't come all this while.

[6] Enter Petruchio, Katherina, Vincentio and Attendants.

[7] PETRUCHIO. Sir, here's the door — this is Lucentio's house. My father's lies more toward the marketplace; that's where I must go, and so here I leave you, sir.

[8] VINCENTIO. You shan't go without drinking with us first. I think I can secure your welcome here, and by all likelihood some good cheer is on the way.

[9] [_Knocks._]

[10] GREMIO. They're busy inside — you'd best knock louder.

[11] Enter Pedant above, at a window.

[12] PEDANT. Who is it that knocks as if he'd beat down the gate?

[13] VINCENTIO. Is Signior Lucentio inside, sir?

[14] PEDANT. He's inside, sir, but not to be spoken to.

[15] VINCENTIO. What if a man brought him a hundred pound or two to make merry with?

[16] PEDANT. Keep your hundred pounds to yourself — he'll need none as long as I live.

[17] PETRUCHIO. No, I told you your son was well loved in Padua. Do you hear, sir? To set frivolous matters aside, please tell Signior Lucentio that his father has come from Pisa, and is here at the door to speak with him.

[18] PEDANT. You lie — his father has come from Padua, and is here looking out the window.

[19] VINCENTIO. Are you his father?

[20] PEDANT. Yes, sir — so his mother says, if I may believe her.

[21] PETRUCHIO.

[22] [_To Vincentio._] Why, how now, gentleman! This is plain knavery — to

[23] take on you another man's name.

[24] PEDANT. Lay hands on the villain — I believe he means to cheat someone in this city under cover of my identity.

[25] Re-enter Biondello.

[26] BIONDELLO. I've seen them in the church together — God send them good sailing! But who is here? My old master, Vincentio! Now we are undone and brought to nothing.

[27] VINCENTIO.

[28] [_Seeing Biondello._] Come here, gallows-bird.

[29] BIONDELLO. I hope I have a choice, sir.

[30] VINCENTIO. Come here, you rogue. What — have you forgotten me?

[31] BIONDELLO. Forgotten you! No, sir — I couldn't have forgotten you, for I've never seen you before in my life.

[32] VINCENTIO. What, you notorious villain! Have you never seen your master's father, Vincentio?

[33] BIONDELLO. What, my old worshipful master? Yes indeed, sir — look where he's looking out of the window.

[34] VINCENTIO. Is that so, really?

[35] [_He beats Biondello._]

[36] BIONDELLO. Help, help, help! Here's a madman who'll murder me.

[37] [_Exit._]

[38] PEDANT. Help, son! Help, Signior Baptista!

[39] [_Exit from the window._]

[40] PETRUCHIO. Please, Kate, let's stand aside and see how this quarrel ends.

[41] [_They retire._]

[42] Re-enter Pedant, below; Baptista, Tranio and Servants.

[43] TRANIO. Sir, who are you to dare to beat my servant?

[44] VINCENTIO. Who am I, sir! No — who are you, sir? O immortal gods! O fine villain! A silken doublet, velvet hose, a scarlet cloak, and a sugar-loaf hat! O, I'm ruined! I'm ruined! While I play the thrifty householder at home, my son and my servant spend everything at the university.

[45] TRANIO. How now! What's the matter?

[46] BAPTISTA. What — is the man a lunatic?

[47] TRANIO. Sir, you seem a sober elderly gentleman by your dress, but your words mark you as a madman. Why, sir, what concern is it of yours if I wear pearl and gold? I thank my good father — I am able to maintain it.

[48] VINCENTIO. Your father! O villain! He's a sailmaker in Bergamo.

[49] BAPTISTA. You're mistaken, sir — you're mistaken. Pray, what do you think is his name?

[50] VINCENTIO. His name! As if I didn't know his name! I've brought him up ever since he was three years old, and his name is Tranio.

[51] PEDANT. Away, away, mad ass! His name is Lucentio — and he is my only son, and heir to my lands, I, Signior Vincentio.

[52] VINCENTIO. Lucentio! O, he's murdered his master! Lay hold of him, I charge you, in the Duke's name. O, my son, my son! Tell me, you villain — where is my son Lucentio?

[53] TRANIO. Call an officer.

[54] Enter one with an Officer.

[55] Take this mad rogue to the jail. Father Baptista, I charge you to see that he be produced when required.

[56] VINCENTIO. Take me to the jail!

[57] GREMIO. Wait, officer — he shall not go to prison.

[58] BAPTISTA. Hold your tongue, Signior Gremio — I say he shall go to prison.

[59] GREMIO. Take care, Signior Baptista, lest you be taken in by this scheme. I dare swear this is the real Vincentio.

[60] PEDANT. Swear it, if you dare.

[61] GREMIO. No, I dare not swear it.

[62] TRANIO. Then you'd best say I'm not Lucentio.

[63] GREMIO. Yes, I know you to be Signior Lucentio.

[64] BAPTISTA. Away with the old fool! To the jail with him!

[65] VINCENTIO. So strangers may be hauled about and abused — O monstrous villain!

[66] Re-enter Biondello, with Lucentio and Bianca.

[67] BIONDELLO. O! We're done for — and there he is. Deny him, disown him, or else we're all undone.

[68] LUCENTIO.

[69] [_Kneeling._] Pardon, sweet father.

[70] VINCENTIO. Does my sweetest son still live?

[71] [_Biondello, Tranio and Pedant run out._]

[72] BIANCA.

[73] [_Kneeling._] Pardon, dear father.

[74] BAPTISTA. How have you offended? Where is Lucentio?

[75] LUCENTIO. Here is Lucentio — true son of the true Vincentio — who has by marriage made your daughter mine, while counterfeit pretenses blinded your eyes.

[76] GREMIO. Here's a plot indeed — proof positive of how we've all been deceived!

[77] VINCENTIO. Where is that damned villain Tranio, who faced and defied me in this matter so brazenly?

[78] BAPTISTA. Why, tell me — is this not my Cambio?

[79] BIANCA. Cambio is changed into Lucentio.

[80] LUCENTIO. Love worked these miracles. Bianca's love made me exchange my position with Tranio, while he took on my appearance in town. And happily I have at last arrived at the longed-for haven of my bliss. What Tranio did, I forced him to do — so pardon him, sweet father, for my sake.

[81] VINCENTIO. I'll slit the villain's nose — the one who would have sent me to jail!

[82] BAPTISTA.

[83] [_To Lucentio._] But do you hear, sir? Have you married my daughter

[84] without asking my consent?

[85] VINCENTIO. Don't worry, Baptista — we'll satisfy you, never fear. But I'll go inside, to take revenge for this villainy.

[86] [_Exit._]

[87] BAPTISTA. And I'll go in to sound the depth of this knavery.

[88] [_Exit._]

[89] LUCENTIO. Don't look pale, Bianca — your father won't frown.

[90] [_Exeunt Lucentio and Bianca._]

[91] GREMIO. My cake is dough — but I'll go in with the rest, having lost all hope of anything but my share of the feast.

[92] [_Exit._]

[93] Petruchio and Katherina come forward.

[94] KATHERINA. Husband, let's follow them to see the end of this commotion.

[95] PETRUCHIO. First kiss me, Kate, and then we will.

[96] KATHERINA. What! In the middle of the street?

[97] PETRUCHIO. What! Are you ashamed of me?

[98] KATHERINA. No, sir — God forbid; but ashamed to kiss in public.

[99] PETRUCHIO. Why, then, let's go home again. Come, sirrah, let's away.

[100] KATHERINA. No, I will give you a kiss. Now please, love, stay.

[101] PETRUCHIO. Isn't this good? Come, my sweet Kate. Better once than never — for never is too late.

[102] [_Exeunt._]

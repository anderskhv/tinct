
=== Chapter 1: Act 1, Scene 1 — Britain. The garden of Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter two Gentlemen.]

[1] FIRST GENTLEMAN. You won't meet a man here who isn't frowning. Our moods obey the heavens no more than our courtiers do — they always mirror the King's expression.

[2] SECOND GENTLEMAN. But what's the matter?

[3] FIRST GENTLEMAN. His daughter, the heir to his kingdom, whom he intended for his wife's only son — a widow he recently married — has given herself instead to a poor but worthy gentleman. She's married him. Her husband is banished, and she is imprisoned. Outwardly everyone shows sorrow, though I think the King is genuinely heartsick.

[4] SECOND GENTLEMAN. Only the King?

[5] FIRST GENTLEMAN. The Queen too — she most wanted the match. And the courtiers, although they wear their faces to match the King's looks, have hearts that are secretly glad about the very thing they scowl at.

[6] SECOND GENTLEMAN. And why is that?

[7] FIRST GENTLEMAN. The man who missed out on the Princess is too despicable to even speak of. And the man who married her — alas, the poor soul, banished for it — is the kind of creature that, if you searched the whole earth for his equal, anyone you compared him to would come up short. I don't believe so fine an outward man and such substance within exists in anyone else.

[8] SECOND GENTLEMAN. You speak of him in lofty terms.

[9] FIRST GENTLEMAN. I describe him only within his own measure, sir. If anything I understate rather than overstate him.

[10] SECOND GENTLEMAN. What's his name and lineage?

[11] FIRST GENTLEMAN. I can't trace him back to the root. His father was called Sicilius, who fought with honor against the Romans alongside Cassibelan, though he received his titles from Tenantius, whom he served with glory and remarkable success. That earned him the added name Leonatus. Besides this gentleman in question, he had two other sons, who died sword in hand during the wars of that time. His father, then old and fond of his children, took such sorrow at the loss that he simply stopped living. And his gentle lady, pregnant with the man we're talking about, died as her son was born. The King took the baby into his protection, named him Posthumus Leonatus, raised him as a member of his bed-chamber, and gave him every education the time could offer — which he absorbed as readily as we breathe air. In his youth he was already a harvest, lived at court — which is rare — most praised, most loved, a model for the youngest, a mirror for the more mature, and to the elders a child who could guide old men. To his lady, for whose sake he is now banished, her own choice proves how she valued him and his virtue. By her election one can truly read what kind of man he is.

[12] SECOND GENTLEMAN. I honor him just based on your account. But tell me — is she the King's only child?

[13] FIRST GENTLEMAN. His only child now. He had two sons — if this is worth your hearing, mark it: the eldest at three years old, the other still in swaddling clothes, were stolen from their nursery, and to this hour no one has the slightest idea where they went.

[14] SECOND GENTLEMAN. How long ago was this?

[15] FIRST GENTLEMAN. About twenty years.

[16] SECOND GENTLEMAN. That a king's children should be so easily taken, so slackly guarded, and the search so slow that no one could track them!

[17] FIRST GENTLEMAN. However strange it is, and however much the negligence might be laughed at, it's true, sir.

[18] SECOND GENTLEMAN. I do believe you.

[19] FIRST GENTLEMAN. We must stop talking. Here comes the gentleman, the Queen, and the Princess.

[20] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 2: Act 1, Scene 2 — The same ===
[0] [Enter QUEEN, POSTHUMUS, and IMOGEN.]

[1] QUEEN. No, daughter, rest assured you'll not find me, like the slander against most stepmothers, looking at you with an evil eye. You're my prisoner, but your gaoler herself will give you the keys that lock your restraint. As for you, Posthumus — as soon as I can win over the offended King, I'll openly be your advocate. To be sure, the fire of rage is still in him, and it would be wise to bend to his sentence with whatever patience your good sense can muster.

[2] POSTHUMUS. If it please your Highness, I'll leave today.

[3] QUEEN. You know the danger. I'll take a turn around the garden, pitying the pangs of forbidden affection, even though the King has ordered that you two not speak together.

[4] [Exit.]

[5] IMOGEN. Oh what a dissembling courtesy! How elegantly this tyrant can tickle where she wounds! My dearest husband, I do somewhat fear my father's wrath, but — apart from my sacred duty to him — I fear nothing his rage can do to me. You must go. And I shall stay here under the hourly fire of angry eyes, finding no comfort in living except that this jewel exists in the world, and that I may yet see you again.

[6] POSTHUMUS. My queen! my mistress! Oh lady, weep no more, or I'll be suspected of more tenderness than becomes a man. I shall remain the most loyal husband that ever swore an oath. I'll lodge in Rome with one Philario, who was a friend to my father, and known to me only by letter. Write to me there, my queen, and I will drink your words with my eyes, even if the ink were made of gall.

[7] [Enter QUEEN.]

[8] QUEEN. Be brief, I beg you. If the King comes, I'll incur I don't know how much of his displeasure. [Aside.] Yet I'll lure him to walk this way. I never do him wrong but he ends up paying for my injuries to make peace — pays dearly for my offenses.

[9] [Exit.]

[10] POSTHUMUS. If we were to take as long to say goodbye as we still have to live, the reluctance to part would only grow. Farewell!

[11] IMOGEN. No, stay a little. Even if you were just riding out to take some air, such a parting would be too small. Look here, love: this diamond was my mother's. Take it, heart — but keep it until you court another wife, after Imogen is dead.

[12] POSTHUMUS. What, what? Another? You gentle gods, give me only what I have, and seal up my embraces from any next woman with bonds of death! Stay, stay here —

[13] [Puts on the ring.]

[14] — while my senses can keep it on. And, sweetest, fairest one — as I, my poor self, was exchanged for you to your infinite loss, so in our small gifts I still get the better of you. For my sake wear this. It is a manacle of love. I'll place it upon this fairest prisoner.

[15] [Puts a bracelet on her arm.]

[16] IMOGEN. O the gods! When shall we see each other again?

[17] [Enter CYMBELINE and Lords.]

[18] POSTHUMUS. Alas, the King!

[19] CYMBELINE. You vilest thing, get out — out of my sight. If after this command you burden the court with your unworthiness, you die. Away! You are poison to my blood.

[20] POSTHUMUS. The gods protect you, and bless the good ones who remain at court! I'm gone.

[21] [Exit.]

[22] IMOGEN. No pang of death can be sharper than this.

[23] CYMBELINE. Oh disloyal creature — you who should restore my youth — you pile a year of age on me!

[24] IMOGEN. I beg you, sir, don't harm yourself with your vexation. I'm numb to your wrath. A rarer feeling subdues all my pangs, all my fears.

[25] CYMBELINE. Past grace? past obedience?

[26] IMOGEN. Past hope, and in despair — that way past grace.

[27] CYMBELINE. You might have had the Queen's only son!

[28] IMOGEN. How blessed that I didn't! I chose an eagle, and avoided a kite.

[29] CYMBELINE. You took a beggar, and would have made my throne a seat for baseness.

[30] IMOGEN. No — I rather added a luster to it.

[31] CYMBELINE. Oh you vile thing!

[32] IMOGEN. Sir, it is your fault that I love Posthumus. You raised him as my playmate, and he is a man worth any woman. He overpays for me by almost the sum he gives.

[33] CYMBELINE. What, are you mad?

[34] IMOGEN. Almost, sir. Heaven restore me! I wish I were a cowherd's daughter, and my Leonatus our neighbor shepherd's son!

[35] [Enter QUEEN.]

[36] CYMBELINE. You foolish thing!

[37] [To the QUEEN.] They were together again. You have not done as we commanded.

[38] Take her away, and lock her up.

[39] QUEEN. I beg your patience. Peace, dear lady daughter, peace! — Sweet sovereign, leave us to ourselves, and find some comfort in your own best counsel.

[40] CYMBELINE. No — let her waste a drop of blood a day, and grow old, and die of this folly.

[41] [Exit with Lords.]

[42] [Enter PISANIO.]

[43] QUEEN. Come now, you must yield. Here is your servant. — How now, sir! What news?

[44] PISANIO. My lord your son drew his sword on my master.

[45] QUEEN. Ha! No harm done, I trust?

[46] PISANIO. There might have been, except that my master played rather than fought, and had no anger to spur him on. They were parted by gentlemen on hand.

[47] QUEEN. I'm very glad of it.

[48] IMOGEN. Your son is my father's friend — and takes his part to draw a sword on an exile! O brave sir! I wish they were in Africa together, with me beside them, needle in hand, to prick the one who tried to retreat. — Why did you leave your master?

[49] PISANIO. On his command. He would not allow me to bring him to the harbor. He left me these notes telling me what orders I should follow whenever you saw fit to employ me.

[50] QUEEN. This man has been your faithful servant. I'd stake my honor that he'll remain so.

[51] PISANIO. I humbly thank your Highness.

[52] QUEEN. Please, walk with me a while.

[53] IMOGEN. In about half an hour, please speak with me. At the very least, go see my lord aboard ship. For now, leave me.

[54] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 3: Act 1, Scene 3 — Britain. A public place ===
[0] [Enter CLOTEN and two Lords.]

[1] FIRST LORD. Sir, I'd advise you to change your shirt. All this exercise has made you reek like a sacrifice. Wherever air goes out, air comes back in — and none of the air around here is as wholesome as the air you're venting.

[2] CLOTEN. If my shirt were bloody, then I'd change it. Did I hurt him?

[3] SECOND LORD.

[4] [Aside.] No, honestly — not so much as his patience.

[5] FIRST LORD. Hurt him! His body's a perfectly serviceable corpse if he isn't hurt. It's a thoroughfare for steel if he isn't hurt.

[6] SECOND LORD.

[7] [Aside.] His steel was in debt — it went around the back of town to avoid being seen.

[8] CLOTEN. The villain wouldn't stand and face me.

[9] SECOND LORD.

[10] [Aside.] No — he kept moving forward, right toward your face.

[11] FIRST LORD. Stand his ground against you? You already own enough land — but he was generous and gave you some more ground.

[12] SECOND LORD.

[13] [Aside.] About as many inches as you have oceans.

[14] Puppies!

[15] CLOTEN. I wish they hadn't come between us.

[16] SECOND LORD.

[17] [Aside.] So do I — at least until you'd measured how long a fool you were

[18] lying flat on the ground.

[19] CLOTEN. And to think she'd love that fellow and refuse me!

[20] SECOND LORD.

[21] [Aside.] If making a true choice is a sin, she's damned.

[22] FIRST LORD. Sir, as I've always told you — her beauty and her brain don't match. She's a fine sign hanging out front, but I've seen little reflection of her wit.

[23] SECOND LORD.

[24] [Aside.] She doesn't shine on fools — in case the reflection should hurt

[25] her.

[26] CLOTEN. Come, I'll go to my chamber. I wish there had been some real damage done!

[27] SECOND LORD.

[28] [Aside.] I don't wish that — unless it had been an ass falling over,

[29] which is no great damage.

[30] CLOTEN. You'll come with us?

[31] FIRST LORD. I'll attend your lordship.

[32] CLOTEN. No, come, let's all go together.

[33] SECOND LORD. Very well, my lord.

[34] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 4: Act 1, Scene 4 — Britain. Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter IMOGEN and PISANIO.]

[1] IMOGEN. I wish you had grown roots on the shores of the harbor and questioned every sail. If he wrote and I didn't get the letter, it would be like mercy offered but lost. What was the last thing he said to you?

[2] PISANIO. It was: my queen, my queen!

[3] IMOGEN. Then waved his handkerchief?

[4] PISANIO. And kissed it, madam.

[5] IMOGEN. Senseless linen — happier in that than I am! And that was all?

[6] PISANIO. No, madam. As long as he could make me out with his eye, or my eye distinguish him from others, he kept the deck — with glove, or hat, or handkerchief still waving — showing by every motion of his mind how slow his soul sailed away, even as the ship moved fast.

[7] IMOGEN. You should have watched him until he was no bigger than a crow, or smaller, before you looked away.

[8] PISANIO. Madam, I did.

[9] IMOGEN. I would have strained my eyes to breaking just to look at him, until the distance had narrowed him down sharp as the point of my needle — no, followed him till he had shrunk from the smallness of a gnat into air, and only then turned my eye away and wept. But, good Pisanio, when shall we hear from him?

[10] PISANIO. Be assured, madam — at his very next opportunity.

[11] IMOGEN. I didn't get to say goodbye to him, but I had so many lovely things to say. Before I could tell him how I'd think of him at certain hours, with such and such thoughts; or make him swear the women of Italy would not betray my interest and his honor; or arrange with him that at the sixth hour of morning, at noon, and at midnight he should meet me in prayer — for at those times I am in heaven for him; or even before I could give him the parting kiss I had set between two charming words — in comes my father, and like the tyrannous wind from the north shakes all our buds from blooming.

[12] [Enter a LADY.]

[13] LADY. The Queen, madam, requests your Highness's company.

[14] IMOGEN. Those things I asked you to do — get them done. I'll attend the Queen.

[15] PISANIO. Madam, I shall.

[16] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 5: Act 1, Scene 5 — Rome. Philario’s house ===
[0] [Enter PHILARIO, IACHIMO, a Frenchman, a Dutchman, and a Spaniard.]

[1] IACHIMO. Believe me, sir, I have seen him in Britain. He was then on the rise, expected to grow into the kind of reputation he's since been allowed. But I could have looked at him then without any admiration, even if the list of his qualities had been laid out beside him for me to go through item by item.

[2] PHILARIO. You're describing him from a time before he had developed all that he has now — both inside and out.

[3] FRENCHMAN. I've seen him in France — and we had plenty of men there who could look at the sun with eyes as steady as his.

[4] IACHIMO. This business of marrying his king's daughter — where he's weighed by her value rather than his own — inflates his reputation, I have no doubt, well beyond the truth.

[5] FRENCHMAN. And then his banishment.

[6] IACHIMO. Yes — and those who weep for this miserable divorce on her side wildly exaggerate his merits, if only to defend her judgment, which an easy attack could otherwise demolish for taking up with a beggar of so little quality. But how does he come to be staying with you? How did the acquaintance arise?

[7] PHILARIO. His father and I were soldiers together — a man to whom I've often been indebted for no less than my life.

[8] [Enter POSTHUMUS.]

[9] Here comes the Briton. Treat him among yourselves as gentlemen of your standing should treat a stranger of his quality. I beg you all, get better acquainted with this gentleman, whom I commend to you as a noble friend of mine. How worthy he is, I'll let you discover for yourselves rather than recite it in his own hearing.

[10] FRENCHMAN. Sir, we've known each other in Orleans.

[11] POSTHUMUS. Ever since which time I've been in your debt for courtesies I'll always be paying off and yet keep on paying.

[12] FRENCHMAN. Sir, you overstate my small kindness. I was glad to reconcile you and my countryman. It would have been a pity if you'd been pitted against each other with such mortal intent as you both had then, over a matter of so slight and trivial a nature.

[13] POSTHUMUS. With your pardon, sir — I was then a young traveler. I rather avoided going along with everything I heard, instead of being guided by the experience of others. But on my matured judgment (if it doesn't offend to call it matured) my quarrel wasn't entirely slight.

[14] FRENCHMAN. Honestly, it was, if it was going to be settled by swords — and by two such men as in all likelihood would have killed each other, or both fallen.

[15] IACHIMO. May we politely ask what the disagreement was?

[16] FRENCHMAN. Safely, I think. It was a public dispute — fit to be reported without contradiction. It was much like an argument that broke out last night, where each of us got to praising the women of our country. This gentleman at that time vouching — and on the warrant of bloody affirmation — that his lady was more fair, virtuous, wise, chaste, constant, and accomplished, and less open to temptation, than the rarest of our ladies in France.

[17] IACHIMO. Either that lady is no longer living, or this gentleman's opinion of her has by now worn out.

[18] POSTHUMUS. She holds her virtue still, and I hold my opinion.

[19] IACHIMO. You must not prefer her so far above our Italian women.

[20] POSTHUMUS. Being as provoked as I was in France, I would yield her not a whit, though I profess myself her adorer, not her friend.

[21] IACHIMO. As fair and as good — a kind of side-by-side comparison — would have been almost too fair and too good for any lady in Britain. If she stands above other women I've seen the way that diamond of yours outshines many I've beheld, I could believe she excels many. But I have not seen the most precious diamond there is — nor have you seen the most perfect lady.

[22] POSTHUMUS. I praised her at her own worth. So do I my stone.

[23] IACHIMO. What do you value it at?

[24] POSTHUMUS. More than all the world possesses.

[25] IACHIMO. Either your peerless mistress is dead, or she's outpriced by a trifle.

[26] POSTHUMUS. You're mistaken. The one may be sold or given, if there were wealth enough to buy it or merit enough to give it. The other is not a thing for sale — it is only the gift of the gods.

[27] IACHIMO. Which the gods have given to you?

[28] POSTHUMUS. Which by their grace I will keep.

[29] IACHIMO. You may possess her in name; but you know strange birds light upon neighboring ponds. Your ring may be stolen too. So of your two priceless possessions, one is fragile and the other unguarded. A cunning thief, or a courtier expert in such matters, would risk winning both of them.

[30] POSTHUMUS. Your Italy contains no courtier so accomplished as to overcome the honor of my mistress — if by holding or losing it you call her frail. I have no doubt you have plenty of thieves; nonetheless, I do not fear for my ring.

[31] PHILARIO. Let's leave it there, gentlemen.

[32] POSTHUMUS. Sir, with all my heart. This worthy signior, I thank him, treats me as no stranger — we're at ease at first meeting.

[33] IACHIMO. With five times as much conversation I'd make ground on your fair mistress — make her give way to the point of yielding — if I had admission and opportunity as my allies.

[34] POSTHUMUS. No, no.

[35] IACHIMO. I dare stake half my estate on it against your ring — which in my opinion outvalues even that. But I'm wagering against your confidence rather than her reputation. And to keep you from being offended: I'd dare it against any lady in the world.

[36] POSTHUMUS. You're greatly mistaken in too bold a belief, and I don't doubt you'll receive what you deserve for the attempt.

[37] IACHIMO. What's that?

[38] POSTHUMUS. A rejection — though your attempt, as you call it, deserves worse: a punishment too.

[39] PHILARIO. Gentlemen, enough of this. It came up too suddenly. Let it die as it was born, and please be better acquainted.

[40] IACHIMO. I wish I'd staked my estate and my neighbor's on the proof of what I've said!

[41] POSTHUMUS. What lady would you choose to test?

[42] IACHIMO. Yours — the one you think stands so safe in her constancy. I'll bet you ten thousand ducats against your ring that, if you give me a letter of introduction to the court where your lady is, with no advantage beyond the opportunity for a second meeting, I'll bring back from there that honor of hers you think so well guarded.

[43] POSTHUMUS. I'll bet gold against your gold. My ring I hold as dear as my finger — it is part of me.

[44] IACHIMO. You're prudent, and that's wise of you. Even if you bought women's flesh at a million a grain, you couldn't keep it from spoiling. But I see you have some religion left in you, since you fear.

[45] POSTHUMUS. That's just talk on your tongue — you mean something graver, I hope.

[46] IACHIMO. I am the master of what I say, and I'd answer for every word — I swear it.

[47] POSTHUMUS. Will you? Then I shall merely lend my diamond till you return. Let contracts be drawn between us. My mistress's goodness exceeds the vastness of your unworthy thinking. I take you up on this match: here's my ring.

[48] PHILARIO. I won't have it be a wager.

[49] IACHIMO. By the gods, it is one. If I don't bring you sufficient proof that I have enjoyed the dearest bodily part of your mistress, my ten thousand ducats are yours — and so is your diamond. If I fail, and leave her in such honor as you trust her with, then she, your jewel, and this — your jewel — and my gold are yours: provided I have your recommendation for my freer access.

[50] POSTHUMUS. I accept these conditions. Let us have articles drawn up. Only this far you shall answer: if you make your conquest of her, and give me clearly to understand you have prevailed, I am no further your enemy — she's not worth our quarreling over. If she remains unseduced, and you do not prove otherwise, then for your bad opinion and the assault you've made on her chastity, you shall answer me with your sword.

[51] IACHIMO. Your hand — a contract! We'll have these things set down by lawful counsel, and straight off for Britain, lest the bargain catch cold and starve. I'll fetch my gold and have our two wagers recorded.

[52] POSTHUMUS. Agreed.

[53] [Exeunt POSTHUMUS and IACHIMO.]

[54] FRENCHMAN. Will this hold, do you think?

[55] PHILARIO. Signior Iachimo won't back away from it. Pray let us follow them.

[56] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 6: Act 1, Scene 6 — Britain. Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter QUEEN, Ladies, and CORNELIUS.]

[1] QUEEN. While the dew is still on the ground, gather those flowers. Hurry — who has the list of them?

[2] LADY. I do, madam.

[3] QUEEN. Get on with it.

[4] [Exeunt Ladies.]

[5] Now, Master Doctor — have you brought those drugs?

[6] CORNELIUS. If it please your Highness, yes. Here they are, madam.

[7] [Presenting a box.]

[8] But I beg your Grace, without offense — my conscience requires me to ask — why have you commanded these most poisonous compounds from me, which cause a lingering death but, though slow, are still deadly?

[9] QUEEN. I wonder, Doctor, that you ask me such a question. Haven't I been your pupil for a long time? Haven't you taught me how to make perfumes, distill, preserve — so well that our great King himself often begs me for my preparations? Having come this far (unless you think me devilish), isn't it fitting that I extend my knowledge to other studies? I'll test the strength of these compounds of yours on such creatures as we count not worth the hanging — but no human — to learn their power, and to find antidotes to their effects, and by that gather their various virtues and uses.

[10] CORNELIUS. Your Highness will only harden your heart by this practice. Besides, seeing these effects will be both unpleasant and infectious.

[11] QUEEN. Oh, calm yourself.

[12] [Enter PISANIO.]

[13] [Aside.] Here comes a flattering rascal — on him

[14] I'll work first. He's loyal to his master, an enemy to my son. — How now, Pisanio! — Doctor, your service for this time is finished. Go your own way.

[15] CORNELIUS.

[16] [Aside.] I suspect you, madam —

[17] but you shall do no harm.

[18] QUEEN.

[19] [To PISANIO.] Listen — a word.

[20] CORNELIUS.

[21] [Aside.] I do not like her. She thinks she has

[22] strange lingering poisons. I know her temper, and I will not trust someone of her malice with a drug of such damned nature. What she has will stupefy and dull the senses for a while — which she will probably test first on cats and dogs, then later on higher creatures. But there is no danger in the appearance of death it produces — beyond locking up the spirits for a time, only to wake fresher, revived. She's fooled by a most false effect — and I am the truer for being false with her.

[23] QUEEN. No further service, Doctor, until I send for you.

[24] CORNELIUS. I humbly take my leave.

[25] [Exit.]

[26] QUEEN. She's still weeping, you say? Don't you think in time she'll calm down, and let good counsel enter where folly now lives? Get to work. When you bring me word she loves my son, I'll tell you in that instant you're as great as your master — greater, for his fortunes all lie silent, and his name is at its last gasp. He cannot return, nor stay where he is. To change his situation is only to exchange one misery for another, and every day that comes wastes a day's work in him. What can you expect from depending on a thing that's already leaning over — that cannot be rebuilt, and has no friends, not even one to prop him up?

[27] [The QUEEN drops the box. PISANIO picks it up.]

[28] You pick up — without knowing what it is. But take it for your service. It is a thing I made, which has redeemed the King from death five times. I know nothing more restorative. No, please — take it. It's an earnest of more good I intend toward you. Tell your mistress how matters stand with her — do it as if from yourself. Think what a chance you're trading up to. Remember — you still have your mistress, and now my son in addition, who will look after you. I'll persuade the King to give you any preferment you desire. And then I myself, above all, who set you on this path, am bound to load your merit richly. Call my women. Think on my words.

[29] [Exit PISANIO.]

[30] A sly and constant knave, not to be shaken — the agent for his master, and the one who keeps reminding her to hold fast to her lord. I have given him something which, if he takes it, will leave her without any messengers for her sweetheart. And after that, unless she changes her mood, she's guaranteed to taste of it too.

[31] [Enter PISANIO and Ladies.]

[32] So, so — well done, well done. The violets, cowslips, and primroses — bring them to my closet. Farewell, Pisanio. Think on my words.

[33] [Exeunt QUEEN and Ladies.]

[34] PISANIO. And I shall. But if I ever prove untrue to my good lord, I'll choke myself — that's all I'll ever do for you.

[35] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 7: Act 1, Scene 7 — Britain. The palace ===
[0] [Enter IMOGEN alone.]

[1] IMOGEN. A cruel father and a false stepmother — a foolish suitor pursuing a married lady whose husband is banished. Oh, that husband! The supreme crown of my grief! And these repeated vexations of it! Had I been kidnapped, like my two brothers, I'd be happy! But the most miserable thing is a desire that's grand. Blessed are those, however humble, who get their honest wishes — they're seasoned with comfort. Who can this be? Ugh!

[2] [Enter PISANIO and IACHIMO.]

[3] PISANIO. Madam, a noble gentleman of Rome comes from my lord with letters.

[4] IACHIMO. You change color, madam? The worthy Leonatus is safe, and greets your Highness fondly.

[5] [Presents a letter.]

[6] IMOGEN. Thanks, good sir. You're most welcome.

[7] IACHIMO.

[8] [Aside.] Everything about her on the outside is wonderfully rich!

[9] If her mind is furnished as rarely, she alone is the Arabian phoenix, and I have lost the wager. Boldness, be my friend! Arm me, audacity, head to foot! Or, like the Parthian, I'll be fighting as I flee — rather, I'll just flee outright.

[10] IMOGEN.

[11] [Reads.] He is one of the noblest character, to whose kindnesses I am

[12] infinitely tied. Treat him accordingly, as you value your trust.

[13] LEONATUS.

[55] IACHIMO. My humble thanks. I had almost forgotten to ask your Grace for one small favor — yet of consequence too, for it concerns your lord. I and other noble friends are partners in the business.

[56] IMOGEN. Pray, what is it?

[57] IACHIMO. Some dozen Romans of us, and your lord (the best feather of our wing), have pooled funds to buy a present for the Emperor. I, as agent for the rest, have done it in France. [60] IMOGEN. Oh no, no.

[61] IACHIMO. Yes, I beg you — or I'll break my word by lengthening my return. From Gaul I crossed the seas purposely and on a promise to see your Grace.

[62] IMOGEN. I thank you for your pains. But not leaving tomorrow!

[63] IACHIMO. Oh, I must, madam. Therefore I beg you, if you'd like to greet your lord by letter, do it tonight. I've stayed beyond my allotted time, which matters for the delivery of our present.

[64] IMOGEN. I will write. Send your trunk to me — it shall be safely kept and truly returned to you. You are very welcome.

[36] POSTHUMUS. Jove! Let me see it once more. Is that the one I left with her?

[37] IACHIMO. Sir — and I thank her — yes, that one. She stripped it from her arm. I see her still: her lovely gesture outvalued her gift, and yet enriched it too. She gave it to me, and said she had once valued it.

[38] POSTHUMUS. Perhaps she took it off to send it to me.

[39] IACHIMO. She writes that to you, does she?

[40] POSTHUMUS. Oh, no, no, no! It's true. Here — take this too —

[41] [Gives the ring.]

[42] It is a basilisk to my eye — kills me to look at it. Let there be no honor where there is beauty; no truth where there is appearance; no love where there is another man. The vows of women have no more binding force where they're made than they do to their virtues — which is nothing. Oh, beyond measure false!

[43] PHILARIO. Have patience, sir, and take your ring again. It's not yet won. It's possible she lost it — or who knows if one of her women, having been bribed, has stolen it from her?

[44] POSTHUMUS. Very true — and that's how I hope he came by it. Give me back my ring. Show me some bodily token of hers, more conclusive than this — for this was stolen.

[45] IACHIMO. By Jupiter, I had it from her arm!

[46] POSTHUMUS. Listen — he swears. By Jupiter he swears. It's true. No, keep the ring — it's true. I'm sure she would not lose it. Her attendants are all sworn and honorable. — They induced to steal it! And by a stranger! No, he has enjoyed her. The proof of her unfaithfulness is this: she has bought the name of whore at this dear price. There — take your wages. And all the fiends of hell divide themselves between you!

[47] PHILARIO. Sir, be patient. This is not strong enough to convince anyone well-disposed toward her.

[48] POSTHUMUS. Don't talk of it — she has been mounted by him.

[49] IACHIMO. If you seek further satisfaction, under her breast (worth pressing one's lips to) lies a mole, justly proud of that most delicate dwelling. By my life, I kissed it — and it gave me immediate hunger to feed again, though I was full. Do you remember this mark on her?

[50] POSTHUMUS. Yes — and it confirms another stain, as big as hell could hold, even if there were nothing more than this.

[51] IACHIMO. Will you hear more?

[52] POSTHUMUS. Spare your arithmetic — never count the turns. Once is a million!

[53] IACHIMO. I'll swear —

[54] POSTHUMUS. No swearing. If you'll swear you haven't done it, you lie — and I'll kill you if you deny that you've made me a cuckold.

[55] IACHIMO. I'll deny nothing.

[56] POSTHUMUS. Oh, that I had her here to tear her limb from limb! I'll go there and do it — at court, before her father. I'll do something —

[57] [Exit.]

[58] PHILARIO. He's quite beyond the rule of patience! You have won. Let's follow him and turn aside the present anger he has against himself.

[59] IACHIMO. With all my heart.

[60] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 12: Act 2, Scene 5 — Rome. Another room in Philario’s house ===
[0] [Enter POSTHUMUS.]

[1] POSTHUMUS. Is there no way for men to come into being but that women must be half the work? We are all bastards. And that most venerable man whom I called my father was — I know not where — when I was stamped. Some counterfeiter, with his tools, made me a forgery. Yet my mother seemed the Diana of her time, just as my wife seemed the nonpareil of this. O vengeance, vengeance! She restrained me of my lawful pleasure, and often begged me for forbearance. She did it with a modesty so rosy that the sweet sight of it might well have warmed old Saturn — so that I thought her as chaste as sun-untouched snow. O all the devils! This yellow Iachimo in an hour, was it not? Or less? On the first try? Perhaps he didn't even speak, but, like a full-acorned boar — a German one — cried 'O!' and mounted; found no opposition but what he expected she should oppose and guard against. Could I find out the woman's part in me! For there is no motion that tends to vice in man but I affirm it is the woman's part. Be it lying — note it — the woman's; flattering, hers; deceiving, hers; lust and rank thoughts, hers, hers; revenges, hers; ambitions, covetings, changing prides, disdain, nice longings, slanders, mutability — all faults that man can name, no, that hell knows — why, hers, in part or all; but rather all. For even in vice they are not constant, but are constantly changing one vice only a minute old for another not half so old as that. I'll write against them, detest them, curse them. Yet it's greater skill, in true hatred, to pray that they get their will: the devils themselves cannot plague them better.

[2] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 13: Act 3, Scene 1 — Britain. A hall in Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter in state CYMBELINE, QUEEN, CLOTEN, and Lords at one door, and at another CAIUS LUCIUS and Attendants.]

[1] CYMBELINE. Now say — what does Augustus Caesar want of us?

[2] LUCIUS. When Julius Caesar (whose memory still lives in men's eyes, and will forever be a theme for ears and tongues) was here in Britain, and conquered it, Cassibelan, your uncle — famed in Caesar's praises no less than in the feats that deserved them — for himself and his successors granted Rome a tribute, three thousand pounds yearly, which by you of late has been left unpaid.

[3] QUEEN. And, to put an end to wonder, shall always be so.

[4] CLOTEN. There will be many Caesars before another Julius. Britain is a world unto itself, and we'll pay nothing for the privilege of wearing our own noses.

[5] QUEEN. That opportunity which they once had to take from us, we now have the chance to take back. Remember, sir my liege — the kings your ancestors, together with the natural bravery of your island, which stands like Neptune's park, ribbed and paled in with unscalable rocks and roaring waters, with sands that won't bear your enemies' boats but suck them up to the topmast. A kind of conquest Caesar made here, but he did not here boast 'I came, I saw, I conquered.' With shame — the first that ever touched him — he was carried from off our coast, twice beaten; and his shipping (poor ignorant baubles!) on our terrible seas, moved like eggshells upon their swells, cracked as easily against our rocks. For joy of this, the famous Cassibelan, who was once on the point — O fickle fortune! — of mastering Caesar's sword, made Lud's Town bright with rejoicing fires, and Britons strut with courage.

[6] CLOTEN. Come, no more tribute is to be paid. Our kingdom is stronger than it was at that time, and, as I said, there are no more such Caesars. Others of them may have crooked noses, but to have such straight arms — none.

[7] CYMBELINE. Son, let your mother finish.

[8] CLOTEN. We have plenty among us yet who can grip as hard as Cassibelan. I'm not saying I'm one — but I have a hand. Why tribute? Why should we pay tribute? If Caesar can hide the sun from us with a blanket, or put the moon in his pocket, we'll pay him tribute for light — otherwise, sir, no more tribute, please.

[9] CYMBELINE. You must know that until the injurious Romans extorted this tribute from us, we were free. Caesar's ambition, which swelled so much that it almost stretched the sides of the world, against all justice here put the yoke on us — which to shake off becomes a warlike people, which we reckon ourselves to be.

[10] CLOTEN. We do.

[11] CYMBELINE. Tell Caesar then: our ancestor was that Mulmutius who ordained our laws, whose use Caesar's sword has too much mangled — whose restoration and freedom shall, by the power we hold, be our good work, though Rome be angered for it. Mulmutius made our laws — he who was the first of Britain to set his brow within a golden crown and call himself a king.

[12] LUCIUS. I'm sorry, Cymbeline, that I must pronounce Augustus Caesar (Caesar, who has more kings for servants than you have household officers) your enemy. Receive this from me, then: war and confusion in Caesar's name I pronounce against you. Look for fury that cannot be resisted. Thus I defy you, and I thank you for myself.

[13] CYMBELINE. You are welcome, Caius. Your Caesar knighted me — I spent much of my youth under him; I gathered honor from him — which, since he seeks it from me again, I am obliged to defend to the utmost. I am well aware that the Pannonians and Dalmatians have now taken up arms for their freedom — a precedent which, if not noticed, would show the Britons cold. Caesar will not find us so.

[14] LUCIUS. Let proof speak.

[15] CLOTEN. His majesty bids you welcome. Pass a day or two with us, or longer. If you seek us afterwards on other terms, you'll find us in our saltwater girdle. If you beat us out of it, it's yours; if you fall in the attempt, our crows shall fare the better for you — and there's an end.

[16] LUCIUS. So, sir.

[17] CYMBELINE. I know your master's pleasure, and he knows mine. All that remains is — welcome.

[18] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 14: Act 3, Scene 2 — Britain. Another room in Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter PISANIO reading a letter.]

[1] PISANIO. What? Adultery? Why don't you write what monsters accuse her? Leonatus! O master, what strange infection has fallen into your ear! What false Italian (as poisonous in tongue as in hand) has prevailed upon your too-ready hearing? Disloyal? No. She's punished for her truth, and endures, more goddess-like than wife-like, such assaults as would conquer ordinary virtue. O my master, your mind toward her is now as low as your fortunes once were. What? That I should murder her? On the love, and truth, and vows I have made to obey your command? I — kill her? Spill her blood? If this counts as good service, may I never be considered useful. How do I look, that I should seem to lack humanity enough to do such a deed?

[2] [Reads.]

[3] 'Do it. The letter I have sent her, by her own command, will give you the opportunity.' O damned paper, black as the ink upon you! Senseless thing, are you an accomplice in this deed, and yet look so innocent on the outside? Look, here she comes.

[4] [Enter IMOGEN.]

[5] I am ignorant of what I've been commanded.

[6] IMOGEN. How now, Pisanio?

[7] PISANIO. Madam, here is a letter from my lord.

[8] IMOGEN. Who? Your lord? That is my lord, Leonatus? O, that astronomer would be learned indeed who knew the stars as well as I know his handwriting; he could lay the future open. You good gods, let what is contained here taste of love, of my lord's health, of his contentment — yet not of contentment in our being apart; let that grieve him! Some griefs are medicinal; that is one of them, for it doctors love. Let everything else taste of his contentment. Good wax, by your leave. Blessed be you bees who make these seals of secrets! Lovers and men in dangerous debts don't pray alike; though you cast debtors into prison, you also clasp young Cupid's love-notes. Good news, gods!

[9] [Reads.]

[10] 'Justice and your father's wrath, should he take me in his territory, could not be so cruel to me as you, O dearest of creatures, would renew me even with your eyes alone. Know that I am in Cambria, at Milford Haven. Follow whatever your own love advises you out of this. So he wishes you all happiness who remains loyal to his vow, and you increasing in love.

[11] LEONATUS POSTHUMUS.'

[12] O for a horse with wings! Do you hear, Pisanio? He is at Milford Haven. Read, and tell me how far it is. If someone on minor business can plod there in a week, why may I not glide there in a day? Then, true Pisanio, who longs as I do to see your lord — who longs (O let me ease that!) but not like me, yet longs, only in a fainter way. O, not like me, for mine is beyond beyond. Speak, and speak quickly (love's counselor should fill the channels of hearing to the point of smothering sense) — how far is it to this same blessed Milford? And along the way, tell me how Wales was made so happy as to inherit such a haven. But first of all, how we may steal away from here; and how to excuse the gap of time between our leaving and our return. But first, how to get away. Why should excuses be born before they're conceived? We'll talk of that later. Please speak, how many score of miles can we cover well between each hour?

[13] PISANIO. One score between sunrise and sunset, madam, is enough for you — and even too much.

[14] IMOGEN. Why, a man riding to his execution could never go so slowly. I have heard of riding wagers where horses have been nimbler than the sands running through an hourglass. But this is foolishness. Go bid my woman to feign sickness; say she'll go home to her father; and prepare for me at once a riding suit, no costlier than would suit a freeholder's wife.

[15] PISANIO. Madam, you'd best consider.

[16] IMOGEN. I see only what's ahead, man. Not here, not here, nor what comes after — those have a fog in them I cannot see through. Away, I beg you; do as I bid you. There's no more to say. The only path open is the way to Milford.

[17] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 15: Act 3, Scene 3 — Wales. A mountainous country with a cave ===
[0] [Enter from the cave BELARIUS, GUIDERIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[1] BELARIUS. A fine day not to keep house with such a roof as low as ours! Stoop, boys; this gate teaches you how to adore the heavens, and bows you down to morning's holy office. The gates of monarchs are arched so high that giants may stride through and keep their impious turbans on without saying good morning to the sun. Hail, you fair heaven! We live in the rock, yet we do not use you so harshly as prouder livers do.

[2] GUIDERIUS. Hail, heaven!

[3] ARVIRAGUS. Hail, heaven!

[4] BELARIUS. Now for our mountain sport. Up to yonder hill — your legs are young; I'll tread these flatlands. Consider, when from above you see me looking like a crow, that it is position which diminishes and magnifies; and you may then think over the tales I have told you of courts, of princes, of the tricks in war. Service is not service merely by being done, but by being so regarded. Looking at things this way draws profit from everything we see, and often to our comfort we shall find the lowly beetle in a safer position than the full-winged eagle. O, this life is nobler than waiting to be rebuked, richer than doing nothing for a fine robe, prouder than rustling in unpaid-for silk. Such gain bows to the man who outfits him, yet keeps his account open. No life like ours!

[5] GUIDERIUS. You speak from your own experience. We, poor unfledged ones, have never winged from the sight of the nest, nor know what the air from home is like. Perhaps this life is best, if a quiet life is best; sweeter to you who have known a harsher one; well suited to your stiff old age. But to us it is a cell of ignorance, traveling while still in bed, a prison for a debtor who dares not step over a boundary line.

[6] ARVIRAGUS. What shall we have to talk about when we are as old as you? When we shall hear the rain and wind beat through dark December, how, in this pinched cave of ours, shall we pass the freezing hours by talking? We have seen nothing; we are like beasts: subtle as the fox in hunting, warlike as the wolf for what we eat. Our courage is to chase what flees us; we make our cage a choir, as the imprisoned bird does, and sing of our captivity freely.

[7] BELARIUS. How you talk! If you only knew the city's usuries, and felt them with full knowledge; the arts of the court, as hard to leave as to keep, whose top once climbed means certain falling, or is so slippery that the fear is as bad as the fall; the toil of war, a pain that only seems to seek out danger in the name of fame and honor, which dies in the search and as often gets a slanderous epitaph as a true record of fair deeds; nay, many times deserves ill by doing well, and what's worse, must bow to the criticism. O, boys, this story the world may read in me; my body is marked with Roman swords, and my reputation was once foremost among the best of note. Cymbeline loved me; and whenever a soldier was the topic, my name was not far off. Then I was like a tree whose boughs bent with fruit. But in one night, a storm — or robbery, call it what you will — shook down my ripe fruit, nay, even my leaves, and left me bare to the weather.

[8] GUIDERIUS. How uncertain favor is!

[9] BELARIUS. My fault being nothing, as I have often told you, but that two villains, whose false oaths prevailed against my perfect honor, swore to Cymbeline I was in league with the Romans. So followed my banishment, and these twenty years this rock and these lands have been my world, where I have lived in honest freedom, and paid more pious debts to heaven than in all the earlier part of my life. But up to the mountains! This is not hunters' language. He who first strikes the deer shall be the lord of the feast; the other two shall serve him; and we shall fear no poison, which attends greater pomp. I'll meet you in the valleys.

[10] [Exeunt GUIDERIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[11] How hard it is to hide the sparks of nature! These boys little know they are sons of the King, nor does Cymbeline dream they are alive. They think they are mine; and though raised this lowly in the cave where they bow, their thoughts reach the roofs of palaces, and nature urges them in simple and low things to display princely qualities far beyond the manner of others. This Polydore, the heir of Cymbeline and Britain, whom the King his father called Guiderius — Jove! When on my three-legged stool I sit and tell the warlike deeds I have done, his spirits fly out into my story; he says 'Thus my enemy fell, and thus I set my foot upon his neck'; even then princely blood flows into his cheek, he sweats, strains his young muscles, and puts himself in a posture that acts out my words. The younger brother, Cadwal — once Arviragus — in a similar way brings my speech to life, and shows even more of his own imagination. Hark, the game is roused! O Cymbeline, heaven and my conscience know you unjustly banished me! And so, when they were three and two years old, I stole these babies, thinking to deprive you of succession as you deprived me of my lands. Euriphile, you were their nurse; they took you for their mother, and every day pay honor to her grave. Myself — Belarius, who am called Morgan — they take for their natural father. The game is up.

[12] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 16: Act 3, Scene 4 — Wales, near Milford Haven ===
[0] [Enter PISANIO and IMOGEN.]

[1] IMOGEN. You told me, when we got off the horses, that the place was near at hand. My mother never longed to see me at birth as much as I have now longed for this. Pisanio! Man! Where is Posthumus? What is on your mind that makes you stare so? Why does that sigh break from inside you? Anyone painted with such a look would be interpreted as perplexed beyond self-explanation. Put yourself into a manner of less fear, before this wildness overcomes my calmer senses. What's the matter? Why do you hand me that paper with such an unkind look? If it is summer news, smile to it first; if wintry, you need only keep that face still. My husband's handwriting? That drug-cursed Italy has outsmarted him, and he is at some hard point. Speak, man; your tongue may soften some extremity which, to read, would be deadly to me.

[2] PISANIO. Please read it, and you'll find me, wretched man, the most disdained thing in fortune.

[3] IMOGEN.

[4] [Reads.] 'Your mistress, Pisanio, has played the strumpet in my bed,

[5] the proofs of which lie bleeding inside me. I speak not from weak suspicions, but from evidence as strong as my grief and as certain as I expect my revenge. That part, Pisanio, you must act for me, if your faith is not tainted with the breaking of hers. Let your own hands take away her life; I shall give you the opportunity at Milford Haven; she has my letter for that purpose. If you fear to strike, and to assure me it is done, you are the pimp to her dishonor, and equally disloyal to me.'

[6] PISANIO. What need shall I have to draw my sword? The paper has cut her throat already. No, this is slander, whose edge is sharper than the sword, whose tongue out-poisons all the serpents of the Nile, whose breath rides on the hurrying winds and slanders all corners of the world. Kings, queens, and states, maids, matrons, nay, even the secrets of the grave — this viperous slander enters them all. How fare you, madam?

[7] IMOGEN. False to his bed? What does it mean to be false? To lie awake there at night, thinking of him? To weep from hour to hour? If sleep overcomes my nature, to break it with a fearful dream of him, and to cry myself awake? That is being false to his bed, is it?

[8] PISANIO. Alas, good lady!

[9] IMOGEN. I, false! Your conscience bear witness! Iachimo, you accused him of unchastity; you then looked like a villain — now, it seems, your face is good enough. Some Italian jay, whose mother was her own paint-pot, has betrayed him. Poor I am stale, a garment out of fashion, and because I am richer than to hang upon the wall, I must be ripped up. To pieces with me! O, men's vows are women's traitors! By your revolt, O husband, all good appearance shall be thought put on for villainy — not born where it grows, but worn as bait for ladies.

[10] PISANIO. Good madam, hear me.

[11] IMOGEN. True honest men, when heard, were in his time thought false like false Aeneas; and Sinon's weeping cast scandal on many a holy tear, took pity from the truest wretchedness. So you, Posthumus, will spoil all proper men: handsome and gallant will be thought false and perjured because of your great failing. Come, fellow, be honest; do your master's bidding; when you see him, witness a little to my obedience. Look! I draw the sword myself; take it, and strike the innocent mansion of my love, my heart. Don't fear; it is empty of all things but grief; your master is not there, who was indeed its riches. Do his bidding; strike. You may be brave in a better cause, but right now you seem a coward.

[12] PISANIO. Away, vile instrument! You shall not damn my hand.

[13] IMOGEN. Why, I must die; and if I do not by your hand, you are no servant of your master's. There is a prohibition against self-slaughter so divine that it intimidates my weak hand. Come, here's my heart: something's in front of it. Soft, soft! We'll put up no defense, obedient as the scabbard. What's here? The writings of loyal Leonatus all turned into heresy? Away, away, corrupters of my faith, you shall no longer be a shield to my heart. Thus poor fools may believe false teachers; though those who are betrayed feel the treason sharply, yet the traitor stands in a worse case of woe. And you, Posthumus, who stirred up my disobedience against the King my father, and made me put into contempt the suits of princely men, shall hereafter find it is no act of common occurrence but a rare achievement; and I grieve myself to think, when you shall be dulled by her you now feed on, how your memory will then be tormented by me. Please, get on with it. The lamb begs the butcher. Where's your knife? You are too slow to do your master's bidding, when I desire it too.

[14] PISANIO. O gracious lady, since I received command to do this business, I have not slept a wink.

[15] IMOGEN. Do it, and then to bed.

[16] PISANIO. I will wake my eyeballs first.

[17] IMOGEN. Then why did you undertake it? Why have you abused so many miles with a pretense? This place? My action, and your own? Our horses' labor? The time inviting you? The troubled court because of my absence — to which I never intend to return. Why have you come so far only to unbend your bow when you have taken your stand, the chosen deer right before you?

[18] PISANIO. Only to win time to lose so bad an employment, in which I have considered a course of action. Good lady, hear me with patience.

[19] IMOGEN. Talk your tongue weary, speak. I have heard I am a whore, and my ear, falsely struck with that, can take no greater wound, nor be probed deeper. But speak.

[20] PISANIO. Then, madam, I thought you would not go back again.

[21] IMOGEN. Most likely so, when you brought me here to kill me.

[22] PISANIO. Not so, neither; but if I were as wise as I am honest, my purpose would work out well. It cannot be but that my master has been deceived. Some villain — and exceptional in his art — has done you both this cursed injury.

[23] IMOGEN. Some Roman courtesan!

[24] PISANIO. No, on my life! I'll only give notice that you are dead, and send him some bloody sign of it, because that's what I'm commanded to do. You shall be missed at court, and that will confirm it well.

[25] IMOGEN. Why, good fellow, what shall I do meanwhile? Where shall I stay? How shall I live? Or what comfort can I have in my life, when I am dead to my husband?

[26] PISANIO. If you would go back to court —

[27] IMOGEN. No court, no father, nor any more dealing with that harsh, noble, simple nothing — that Cloten — whose love-suit has been to me as fearful as a siege.

[28] PISANIO. If not at court, then you must not stay in Britain.

[29] IMOGEN. Where then? Does Britain have all the sun that shines? Day, night — are they not also outside Britain? In the world's volume our Britain seems part of it, but not in it; a swan's nest in a great pool. Please think — there are people who live outside Britain.

[30] PISANIO. I am most glad you think of another place. The ambassador, Lucius the Roman, comes to Milford Haven tomorrow. Now, if you could wear a mind dark as your fortune is, and merely disguise that which, if it appeared as itself now, would be put in self-danger — you would tread a path pretty and full of view; yea, perhaps near the residence of Posthumus; so near, at least, that though his actions were not visible, yet report would render him hourly to your ear as truly as he moves.

[31] IMOGEN. O! For such a means, though it threatened my modesty though not my life, I would risk it.

[32] PISANIO. Well then, here's the point: You must forget to be a woman; change command into obedience; fear and fastidiousness (the handmaids of all women, or, more truly, the very essence of woman) into a roguish courage; ready in jokes, quick-answered, saucy, and as quarrelsome as the weasel. Nay, you must forget that rarest treasure of your cheek, exposing it (but, O, the harder heart! Alas, no remedy) to the greedy touch of the common-kissing sun, and forget your elaborate and dainty adornments by which you made great Juno angry.

[33] IMOGEN. Nay, be brief; I see your point, and am almost a man already.

[34] PISANIO. First, just make yourself like one. Foreseeing this, I have already ready (it is in my cloak-bag) doublet, hat, hose, all that matches them. If you would, in their wearing — and with what imitation you can borrow from a youth of such a season — present yourself before noble Lucius, request his service, tell him what you can do well; which will make him know it, if his head has an ear for music. Doubtless with joy he will embrace you; for he is honorable, and, doubly so, most holy. As for your means abroad: you have me, who am rich; and I shall never fail to provide both beginning and continuing support.

[35] IMOGEN. You are all the comfort the gods will feed me with. Please go! There is more to consider; but we will adjust to all that good time will give us. I am soldier to this attempt, and will undertake it with a prince's courage. Away, I beg you.

[36] PISANIO. Well, madam, we must take a short farewell, lest, being missed, I be suspected of helping you away from court. My noble mistress, here is a box; I had it from the Queen. What is in it is precious. If you are sick at sea or queasy on land, a dram of this will drive away the distemper. To some shaded place, and dress yourself for your manhood. May the gods direct you to the best!

[37] IMOGEN. Amen. I thank you.

[38] [Exeunt severally.]


=== Chapter 17: Act 3, Scene 5 — Britain. Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter CYMBELINE, QUEEN, CLOTEN, LUCIUS and Lords.]

[1] CYMBELINE. This far, and so farewell.

[2] LUCIUS. Thanks, royal sir. My emperor has written; I must go from here, and am truly sorry I must report you my master's enemy.

[3] CYMBELINE. Our subjects, sir, will not endure his yoke; and for us to show less sovereignty than they do would inevitably appear unkingly.

[4] LUCIUS. So, sir. I request of you an escort overland to Milford Haven. Madam, all joy fall upon your Grace, and on you!

[5] CYMBELINE. My lords, you are appointed for that duty; omit no point of due honor. So farewell, noble Lucius.

[6] LUCIUS. Your hand, my lord.

[7] CLOTEN. Receive it as a friend; but from this time on, I wear it as your enemy.

[8] LUCIUS. Sir, the outcome is yet to name the winner. Fare you well.

[9] CYMBELINE. Do not leave the worthy Lucius, my good lords, until he has crossed the Severn. Happiness!

[10] [Exeunt LUCIUS and Lords.]

[11] QUEEN. He goes from here frowning; but it does us honor that we have given him cause.

[12] CLOTEN. All the better; your valiant Britons get their wishes in it.

[13] CYMBELINE. Lucius has written already to the Emperor how it goes here. It is therefore fitting that our chariots and horsemen be in readiness without delay. The forces he already has in Gallia will soon be brought to a head, from where he begins his war against Britain.

[14] QUEEN. This is no sleepy business, but must be attended to quickly and strongly.

[15] CYMBELINE. Our expectation that it would be like this has made us prepared. But, my gentle queen, where is our daughter? She has not appeared before the Roman, nor offered to us the day's duty. She looks to us like a thing more made of malice than of duty; we have noted it. Call her before us, for we have been too lenient.

[16] [Exit an Attendant.]

[17] QUEEN. Royal sir, since the exile of Posthumus, her life has been most withdrawn; the cure for which, my lord, only time can do. I beg your Majesty, refrain from sharp speeches to her; she is a lady so sensitive to rebukes that words are blows, and blows would be death to her.

[18] [Enter Attendant.]

[19] CYMBELINE. Where is she, sir? How can her contempt be answered?

[20] ATTENDANT. Please you, sir, her chambers are all locked, and there is no answer that will be given to all the loud noise we make.

[21] QUEEN. My lord, when last I went to visit her, she begged me to excuse her keeping herself shut up; saying that, constrained by her infirmity, she must leave that duty unpaid to you which daily she was bound to offer. This she wished me to make known; but our great court made me to blame for forgetting.

[22] CYMBELINE. Her doors locked? Not seen lately? Grant, heavens, that what I fear may prove false!

[23] [Exit.]

[24] QUEEN. Son, I say, follow the King.

[25] CLOTEN. That man of hers, Pisanio, her old servant — I have not seen him these two days.

[26] QUEEN. Go, look for him.

[27] [Exit CLOTEN.]

[28] Pisanio, you who stand so for Posthumus! He has a drug of mine. I pray his absence comes from swallowing it; for he believes it is something most precious. But as for her — where is she gone? Perhaps despair has seized her; or, winged by the fervor of her love, she has flown to her desired Posthumus. Gone she is to death or to dishonor, and my plan can make good use of either. With her down, I have the placing of the British crown.

[29] [Enter CLOTEN.]

[30] How now, my son?

[31] CLOTEN. It is certain she has fled. Go in and cheer the King. He rages; none dare come near him.

[32] QUEEN. All the better. May this night prevent him from seeing the coming day!

[33] [Exit.]

[34] CLOTEN. I love and hate her; for she is fair and royal, and has all the courtly qualities more exquisitely than any lady, ladies, woman. From each one she has the best, and she, compounded of all, surpasses them all. I love her therefore; but her disdaining me and throwing favors on the low Posthumus so slanders her judgment that what is otherwise rare in her is choked; and in that point I will conclude to hate her — nay, indeed, to be revenged upon her. For when fools shall —

[35] [Enter PISANIO.]

[36] Who is here? What, are you packing, sirrah? Come here. Ah, you precious pimp! Villain, where is your lady? In a word, or else you go straight to the fiends.

[37] PISANIO. O good my lord!

[38] CLOTEN. Where is your lady? or, by Jupiter — I will not ask again. Secretive villain, I'll have this secret from your heart, or rip your heart out to find it. Is she with Posthumus? From whose so many weights of baseness not a dram of worth can be drawn.

[39] PISANIO. Alas, my lord, how can she be with him? When was she missed? He is in Rome.

[40] CLOTEN. Where is she, sir? Come nearer. No more delays! Satisfy me fully what has become of her.

[41] PISANIO. O my all-worthy lord!

[42] CLOTEN. All-worthy villain! Reveal where your mistress is at once, at the next word. No more 'worthy lord'! Speak, or your silence on the instant is your condemnation and your death.

[43] PISANIO. Then, sir, this paper is the history of my knowledge concerning her flight.

[44] [Presenting a letter.]

[45] CLOTEN. Let me see it. I will pursue her even to Augustus' throne.

[46] PISANIO.

[47] [Aside.] This or perish.

[48] She is far enough away; and what he learns from this may prove his travel, not her danger.

[49] CLOTEN. Humh!

[50] PISANIO.

[51] [Aside.] I'll write to my lord that she is dead. O Imogen,

[52] may you wander safe and return again safe!

[53] CLOTEN. Sirrah, is this letter true?

[54] PISANIO. Sir, as I believe.

[55] CLOTEN. It is Posthumus' handwriting; I know it. Sirrah, if you would not be a villain, but do me true service — undergo those tasks in which I should have reason to use you with serious effort, that is, whatever villainy I bid you do, perform it directly and truly — I would think you an honest man; you should lack neither my means for your relief nor my voice for your promotion.

[56] PISANIO. Well, my good lord.

[57] CLOTEN. Will you serve me? For since you have patiently and constantly stuck to the bare fortune of that beggar Posthumus, you cannot, in the course of gratitude, fail to be a diligent follower of mine. Will you serve me?

[58] PISANIO. Sir, I will.

[59] CLOTEN. Give me your hand; here's my purse. Do you have any of your former master's garments in your possession?

[60] PISANIO. I have, my lord, at my lodging, the same suit he wore when he took leave of my lady and mistress.

[61] CLOTEN. The first service you do me, fetch that suit here. Let it be your first service; go.

[62] PISANIO. I shall, my lord.

[63] [Exit.]

[64] CLOTEN. Meet you at Milford Haven! I forgot to ask him one thing; I'll remember it presently. Even there, you villain Posthumus, will I kill you. I wish those garments were here. She said once — the bitterness of which I now belch from my heart — that she held the very garment of Posthumus in more respect than my noble and natural person, together with all the adornment of my qualities. With that suit upon my back I will ravish her; first kill him, and in her eyes. There she shall see my valor, which will then be a torment to her contempt. With him on the ground, my speech of insult ended over his dead body, and when my lust has dined — which, as I say, to vex her I will execute in the clothes she so praised — to the court I'll knock her back, kick her home again. She has despised me with delight, and I'll be merry in my revenge.

[65] [Enter PISANIO with the clothes.]

[66] Are those the garments?

[67] PISANIO. Aye, my noble lord.

[68] CLOTEN. How long is it since she went to Milford Haven?

[69] PISANIO. She can scarcely be there yet.

[70] CLOTEN. Bring this apparel to my chamber; that is the second thing I have commanded you. The third is that you will be a voluntary mute to my plan. Be only dutiful and true, and promotion shall offer itself to you. My revenge is now at Milford — would I had wings to follow it! Come, and be true.

[71] [Exit.]

[72] PISANIO. You bid me to my loss; for to be true to you would be to prove false, which I will never be, to him who is most true. To Milford go, and find not her whom you pursue. Flow, flow, you heavenly blessings, upon her! This fool's speed be crossed with slowness! Labor be his reward!

[73] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 18: Act 3, Scene 6 — Wales. Before the cave of Belarius ===
[0] [Enter IMOGEN alone, in boy's clothes.]

[1] IMOGEN. I see a man's life is a tedious one. I have tired myself out, and for two nights together have made the ground my bed. I should be sick if my resolution did not help me. Milford, when from the mountain-top Pisanio showed you to me, you were within sight. O Jove! I think foundations fly from the wretched — by which I mean places where they should be relieved. Two beggars told me I could not miss my way. Will poor folks lie, who have afflictions on them, knowing it is a punishment or trial? Yes; no wonder, when rich ones scarce tell the truth. To lapse in plenty is more wicked than to lie out of need; and falsehood is worse in kings than beggars. My dear lord! You are one of the false ones. Now I think on you, my hunger is gone; but even before, I was on the point of fainting for food. But what is this? Here is a path to it; it's some savage hold. I had best not call; I dare not call. Yet famine, before it utterly overthrows nature, makes it valiant. Plenty and peace breed cowards; hardness ever is the mother of hardiness. Ho! who's here? If anything civilized, speak; if savage, take or lend. Ho! No answer? Then I'll enter. Best draw my sword; and if my enemy fears the sword as much as I do, he'll scarcely look at it. Such a foe, good heavens!

[2] [Exit into the cave.]


=== Chapter 19: Act 3, Scene 7 — The same ===
[0] [Enter BELARIUS, GUIDERIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[1] BELARIUS. You, Polydore, have proved the best woodsman and are master of the feast. Cadwal and I will play the cook and servant; that's our agreement. The sweat of work would dry up and die but for the end it works toward. Come, our stomachs will make the homely savory; weariness can snore upon the flint, while restless sloth finds the down pillow hard. Now, peace be here, poor house, that keep yourself!

[2] GUIDERIUS. I am thoroughly weary.

[3] ARVIRAGUS. I am weak with toil, yet strong in appetite.

[4] GUIDERIUS. There is cold meat in the cave; we'll browse on that while what we have killed is cooked.

[5] BELARIUS.

[6] [Looking into the cave.] Wait, don't come in.

[7] If it didn't eat our food, I should think we had a fairy here.

[8] GUIDERIUS. What's the matter, sir?

[9] BELARIUS. By Jupiter, an angel! or, if not, an earthly paragon! Behold divinity no older than a boy!

[10] [Enter IMOGEN.]

[11] IMOGEN. Good masters, do not harm me. Before I entered here I called, and meant to have begged or bought what I have taken. By my truth, I have stolen nothing; nor would I have, even if I had found gold strewn on the floor. Here's money for my meat. I would have left it on the table as soon as I had finished my meal, and departed with prayers for the provider.

[12] GUIDERIUS. Money, youth?

[13] ARVIRAGUS. All gold and silver should rather turn to dirt, as it is reckoned no better but by those who worship dirty gods.

[14] IMOGEN. I see you are angry. Know that, if you kill me for my fault, I should have died had I not committed it.

[15] BELARIUS. Where are you bound?

[16] IMOGEN. To Milford Haven.

[17] BELARIUS. What is your name?

[18] IMOGEN. Fidele, sir. I have a kinsman who is bound for Italy; he embarked at Milford. Going to him, almost spent with hunger, I have fallen into this offense.

[19] BELARIUS. Please, fair youth, do not think us churls, nor measure our good minds by this rude place we live in. Well met! It is almost night; you shall have better fare before you depart, and thanks for staying to eat it. Boys, bid him welcome.

[20] GUIDERIUS. Were you a woman, youth, I should woo hard to be your husband. In honesty I bid for you as if I would buy.

[21] ARVIRAGUS. I'll make it my comfort that he is a man. I'll love him as my brother; and such a welcome as I'd give to a brother after long absence, such is yours. Most welcome! Be cheerful, for you have fallen among friends.

[22] IMOGEN. Among friends, if brothers. [Aside.] Would it had been so that they had been my father's sons! Then had my worth been less, and so a more equal balance for you, Posthumus.

[23] BELARIUS. He suffers from some distress.

[24] GUIDERIUS. Would I could free him from it!

[25] ARVIRAGUS. Or I, whatever it be, whatever pain it cost, whatever danger! Gods!

[26] BELARIUS.

[27] [Whispering.] Listen, boys.

[28] IMOGEN.

[29] [Aside.] Great men,

[30] who had a court no bigger than this cave, who waited on themselves, and had the virtue which their own conscience approved in them, casting aside that nothing-gift of differing multitudes — could not outshine these two. Pardon me, gods! I'd change my sex to be a companion with them, since Leonatus is false.

[31] BELARIUS. So it shall be. Boys, we'll go dress our game. Fair youth, come in. Conversation is heavy on an empty stomach; when we have supped, we'll politely ask you for your story, as far as you'll speak of it.

[32] GUIDERIUS. Please come near.

[33] ARVIRAGUS. The night to the owl and morning to the lark are less welcome.

[34] IMOGEN. Thanks, sir.

[35] ARVIRAGUS. I beg you, come near.

[36] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 20: Act 3, Scene 8 — Rome. A public place ===
[0] [Enter two Roman Senators and Tribunes.]

[1] FIRST SENATOR. This is the substance of the Emperor's writ: Since the common men are now in action against the Pannonians and Dalmatians, and the legions now in Gallia are too weak to undertake our wars against the rebellious Britons, we are to incite the gentry to this business. He creates Lucius proconsul; and to you, the tribunes, for this immediate levy, he commands his absolute commission. Long live Caesar!

[2] TRIBUNE. Is Lucius general of the forces?

[3] SECOND SENATOR. Aye.

[4] TRIBUNE. Remaining now in Gallia?

[5] FIRST SENATOR. With those legions I have spoken of, to which your levy must be a supplement. The words of your commission will tie you to the numbers and the time of their dispatch.

[6] TRIBUNE. We will discharge our duty.

[7] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 21: Act 4, Scene 1 — Wales. Near the cave of Belarius ===
[0] [Enter CLOTEN alone.]

[1] CLOTEN. I am near the place where they should meet, if Pisanio has mapped it truly. How well his garments fit me! Why should his mistress, who was made by the one who made the tailor, not fit me too? The more so — with reverence for the word — for it is said a woman's fitness comes by fits. In that, I must play the workman. I dare say it to myself, for it is not vain-glory for a man and his looking-glass to confer in his own chamber. I mean, the lines of my body are drawn just as well as his; no less young, more strong, not beneath him in fortunes, beyond him in advantage of the time, above him in birth, equally versed in general accomplishments, and more remarkable in single combats. Yet this undiscerning thing loves him in spite of me. What mortality is! Posthumus, your head — which is now growing upon your shoulders — shall within this hour be off; your mistress enforced; your garments cut to pieces before her face; and all this done, kick her home to her father, who may, perhaps, be a little angry at my rough usage; but my mother, having power over his testiness, shall turn it all to my praise. My horse is tied up safely. Out, sword, and to a sore purpose! Fortune, put them into my hand. This is the very description of their meeting-place; and the fellow dares not deceive me.

[2] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 22: Act 4, Scene 2 — Wales. Before the cave of Belarius ===
[0] [Enter from the cave BELARIUS, GUIDERIUS, ARVIRAGUS and IMOGEN.]

[1] BELARIUS.

[2] [To IMOGEN.] You are not well. Remain here in the cave;

[3] we'll come to you after hunting.

[4] ARVIRAGUS.

[5] [To IMOGEN.] Brother, stay here.

[6] Aren't we brothers?

[7] IMOGEN. So man and man should be; but clay and clay differs in dignity, though the dust is alike. I am very sick.

[8] GUIDERIUS. You go hunting; I'll stay with him.

[9] IMOGEN. I am not so sick — yet not well; but not so cosseted a softie as to seem to die before being sick. So, please, leave me; stick to your daily course. Breaking custom is breaking everything. I am ill, but your being by me cannot mend me; society is no comfort to one who's not sociable. I am not very sick, since I can still reason about it. Please trust me here. I'll rob no one but myself; and let me die, stealing so meagerly.

[10] GUIDERIUS. I love you; I have said so. As much in quantity, in weight as much as I love my father.

[11] BELARIUS. What? How? How?

[12] ARVIRAGUS. If it is sin to say so, sir, I yoke myself with my good brother's fault. I do not know why I love this youth, and I have heard you say love's reason has no reason. The coffin at the door, and if asked who shall die, I'd say 'My father, not this youth.'

[13] BELARIUS.

[14] [Aside.] O noble nature!

[15] O worthiness of nature! Breed of greatness! Cowards father cowards and base things father base. Nature has meal and bran, contempt and grace. I am not their father; yet whoever this should be, the very fact that he is loved before me is a miracle. — It's the ninth hour of morning.

[16] ARVIRAGUS. Brother, farewell.

[17] IMOGEN. I wish you good sport.

[18] ARVIRAGUS. Good health to you. [To BELARIUS.] So please you, sir.

[19] IMOGEN.

[20] [Aside.] These are kind creatures. Gods, what lies I

[21] have heard! Our courtiers say all is savage outside court. Experience, O, you disprove report! The proud seas breed monsters; for the dish, poor little tribute rivers give us the sweetest fish. I am sick still; heart-sick. Pisanio, I'll now taste your drug.

[22] [Swallows some.]

[23] GUIDERIUS. I could not move him. He said he was well-born but unfortunate; wrongly afflicted, but still honest.

[24] ARVIRAGUS. He answered me the same way; yet said hereafter I might know more.

[25] BELARIUS. To the field, to the field! We'll leave you for now. Go in and rest.

[26] ARVIRAGUS. We will not be long away.

[27] BELARIUS. Please don't be sick, for you must be our housekeeper.

[28] IMOGEN. Well or ill, I am bound to you.

[29] BELARIUS. And shall be always.

[30] [Exit IMOGEN into the cave.]

[31] This youth, however troubled, appears to have had good ancestors.

[32] ARVIRAGUS. How angel-like he sings!

[33] GUIDERIUS. But his neat cookery! He cut our roots into shapes, and seasoned our broths as if Juno were sick and he her dietitian.

[34] ARVIRAGUS. He nobly yokes a smile with a sigh, as if the sigh were because it was not such a smile; the smile mocking the sigh that it would want to fly from so divine a temple to mix with winds that sailors curse.

[35] GUIDERIUS. I do notice that grief and patience, rooted in him both, mingle their roots together.

[36] ARVIRAGUS. Grow, patience! And let the stinking elder, grief, untwist his dying root from the growing vine!

[37] BELARIUS. It is broad morning. Come, away! Who's there?

[38] [Enter CLOTEN.]

[39] CLOTEN. I cannot find those runaways; that villain has mocked me. I am faint.

[40] BELARIUS. Those runaways? Doesn't he mean us? I partly know him; it is Cloten, the son of the Queen. I fear some ambush. I have not seen him for many years, and yet I know it is he. We are held to be outlaws. Away from here!

[41] GUIDERIUS. He is only one; you and my brother go search what companies are nearby. Please go; let me alone with him.

[42] [Exeunt BELARIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[43] CLOTEN. Soft! What are you who flee me like this? Some villainous mountaineers? I have heard of such. What slave are you?

[44] GUIDERIUS. A thing more slavish I never did than answering a slave without giving him a blow.

[45] CLOTEN. You are a robber, a law-breaker, a villain. Yield yourself, thief.

[46] GUIDERIUS. To whom? To you? What are you? Don't I have an arm as big as yours, a heart as big? Your words, I grant, are bigger, for I don't wear my dagger in my mouth. Say what you are; why I should yield to you.

[47] CLOTEN. You base villain, don't you know me by my clothes?

[48] GUIDERIUS. No, nor your tailor, rascal, who is your grandfather; he made those clothes, which, as it seems, make you.

[49] CLOTEN. You precious knave, my tailor did not make them.

[50] GUIDERIUS. Then begone, and thank the man who gave them to you. You are some fool; I am loath to beat you.

[51] CLOTEN. You insulting thief, just hear my name, and tremble.

[52] GUIDERIUS. What's your name?

[53] CLOTEN. Cloten, you villain.

[54] GUIDERIUS. Cloten, you double villain — be that your name, I cannot tremble at it. Were it Toad, or Adder, or Spider, it would move me sooner.

[55] CLOTEN. To increase your fear — nay, to your sheer confusion — you shall know I am son to the Queen.

[56] GUIDERIUS. I am sorry for that; you don't seem as worthy as your birth.

[57] CLOTEN. Are you not afraid?

[58] GUIDERIUS. Those whom I revere, those I fear — the wise; at fools I laugh, I do not fear them.

[59] CLOTEN. Die the death. When I have slain you with my own hand, I'll follow those who just now fled from here, and on the gates of Lud's Town set your heads. Yield, rustic mountaineer.

[60] [Exeunt, fighting.]

[61] [Enter BELARIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[62] BELARIUS. No company about?

[63] ARVIRAGUS. None in the world; you must have mistaken him, surely.

[64] BELARIUS. I cannot tell; long is it since I saw him, but time has not blurred those lines of his face which he then wore; the snatches in his voice, the bursts of his speech, were as his. I am certain it was very Cloten.

[65] ARVIRAGUS. In this place we left them. I wish my brother makes good progress against him, since you say he is so fierce.

[66] BELARIUS. Being scarcely full-grown into a man, he had no apprehension nor roaring terrors; for lack of judgment is often the ceasing of fear.

[67] [Enter GUIDERIUS with CLOTEN's head.]

[68] But, see, your brother.

[69] GUIDERIUS. This Cloten was a fool, an empty purse; there was no money in it. Not Hercules could have knocked out his brains, for he had none; yet, had I not done this, the fool would have borne my head as I do his.

[70] BELARIUS. What have you done?

[71] GUIDERIUS. I know perfectly what: cut off one Cloten's head, son to the Queen, by his own report; who called me traitor, mountaineer, and swore with his own single hand he would take us in, displace our heads from where, thanks to the gods, they grow, and set them on Lud's Town.

[72] BELARIUS. We are all undone.

[73] GUIDERIUS. Why, worthy father, what do we have to lose but what he swore to take — our lives? The law does not protect us; then why should we be tender, and let an arrogant piece of flesh threaten us, play judge and executioner all by himself, just because we fear the law? What company did you discover abroad?

[74] BELARIUS. No single soul can we set eye on, but in all reason he must have some attendants. Though his mood was nothing but change — yes, and that from one bad thing to worse — not frenzy, not absolute madness could have raved so far as to bring him here alone. Although perhaps it may be heard at court that such as we cave here, hunt here, are outlaws, and in time might make some stronger force; which, hearing of, as it is like him, he might have broken out and sworn he'd fetch us in; yet it is not probable for him to come alone, either he undertaking it so or they suffering it so. Then on good grounds we may fear, if we do fear, that this body has a tail more dangerous than the head.

[75] ARVIRAGUS. Let what is ordained come as the gods foretell. However it goes, my brother has done well.

[76] BELARIUS. I had no mind to hunt this day; the boy Fidele's sickness made my way out feel long.

[77] GUIDERIUS. With his own sword, which he waved against my throat, I have taken his head from him. I'll throw it into the creek behind our rock, and let it go to the sea, and tell the fishes he is the Queen's son, Cloten. That's all I care about.

[78] [Exit.]

[79] BELARIUS. I fear it will be revenged. Would, Polydore, that you had not done it! Though valor becomes you well enough.

[80] ARVIRAGUS. Would I had done it, so the revenge alone pursued me! Polydore, I love you as a brother, but I envy you very much that you have robbed me of this deed. I wish revenges, that any possible strength could meet, would seek us through, and put us to our answer.

[81] BELARIUS. Well, it is done. We'll hunt no more today, nor seek for danger where there is no profit. Please go to our rock. You and Fidele play the cooks; I'll stay till hasty Polydore returns, and bring him to dinner presently.

[82] ARVIRAGUS. Poor sick Fidele! I'll willingly go to him; to gain back his color I'd let a parish of such Clotens' blood, and praise myself for charity.

[83] [Exit.]

[84] BELARIUS. O you goddess, you divine Nature, you display yourself in these two princely boys! They are as gentle as breezes blowing below the violet, not stirring its sweet head; and yet as rough, when their royal blood is roused, as the rudest wind that takes the mountain pine by the top and makes it stoop to the valley. It is a wonder that an invisible instinct should frame them to royalty unlearned, to honor untaught, to civility not seen in others, to valor that grows wild in them, but yields a crop as if it had been sown. Yet still it's strange what Cloten's being here to us means, or what his death will bring us.

[85] [Enter GUIDERIUS.]

[86] GUIDERIUS. Where's my brother? I have sent Cloten's blockhead down the stream, on embassy to his mother; his body is hostage for his return.

[87] [Solemn music.]

[88] BELARIUS. My ingenious instrument! Listen, Polydore, it sounds. But what reason has Cadwal now to set it playing? Listen!

[89] GUIDERIUS. Is he at home?

[90] BELARIUS. He went out just now.

[91] GUIDERIUS. What does he mean? Since the death of my dearest mother it has not spoken before. All solemn things should answer solemn occasions. What's the matter? Triumphs for nothing and lamenting trifles is jollity for apes and grief for boys. Is Cadwal mad?

[92] [Enter ARVIRAGUS with IMOGEN as dead, bearing her in his arms.]

[93] BELARIUS. Look, here he comes, and brings in his arms the dire occasion for what we blame him for!

[94] ARVIRAGUS. The bird is dead that we made so much of. I had rather have skipped from sixteen years of age to sixty, and turned my leaping time into a crutch, than have seen this.

[95] GUIDERIUS. O sweetest, fairest lily! My brother does not wear you half so well as when you grew yourself.

[96] BELARIUS. O melancholy! Who ever yet could sound your bottom? Find the mud to show what coast your sluggish vessel might most easily harbor in? You blessed thing! Jove knows what man you might have made; but I — you died, a most rare boy, of melancholy. How did you find him?

[97] ARVIRAGUS. Stiff, as you see; smiling thus, as if some fly had tickled his slumber, not as if death's dart had been laughed at; his right cheek resting on a cushion.

[98] GUIDERIUS. Where?

[99] ARVIRAGUS. On the floor; his arms folded like this. I thought he slept, and put my hobnailed shoes off my feet, whose roughness made my steps too loud.

[100] GUIDERIUS. Why, he only sleeps. If he is gone, he'll make his grave a bed; with female fairies will his tomb be haunted, and worms will not come to you.

[101] ARVIRAGUS. With fairest flowers, while summer lasts and I live here, Fidele, I'll sweeten your sad grave. You shall not lack the flower that's like your face, pale primrose; nor the azured harebell, like your veins; no, nor the leaf of eglantine, which, not to slander, did not surpass the sweetness of your breath. The robin would, with charitable bill (O bill, sorely shaming those rich-left heirs who let their fathers lie without a monument!) bring you all this; yes, and furry moss besides, when no flowers can be had, to winter-blanket your corpse —

[102] GUIDERIUS. Please have done, and do not play in girlish words with what is so serious. Let us bury him, and not prolong with admiration what is now a debt due. To the grave.

[103] ARVIRAGUS. Say, where shall we lay him?

[104] GUIDERIUS. By good Euriphile, our mother.

[105] ARVIRAGUS. Be it so; and let us, Polydore, though now our voices have got the manly crack, sing him to the ground, as we once did our mother; use the same notes and words, except that Euriphile must be Fidele.

[106] GUIDERIUS. Cadwal, I cannot sing. I'll weep, and speak the words with you; for notes of sorrow out of tune are worse than priests and shrines that lie.

[107] ARVIRAGUS. We'll speak it, then.

[108] BELARIUS. Great griefs, I see, lessen the smaller ones, for Cloten is quite forgotten. He was a queen's son, boys; and though he came our enemy, remember he was paid for that. Though mean and mighty, rotting together, have one same dust, yet reverence — that angel of the world — does make distinction of place between high and low. Our foe was princely; and though you took his life, since he was our foe, yet bury him as a prince.

[109] GUIDERIUS. Please fetch him here. Thersites' body is as good as Ajax', when neither are alive.

[110] ARVIRAGUS. If you'll go fetch him, we'll say our song in the meantime. Brother, begin.

[111] [Exit BELARIUS.]

[112] GUIDERIUS. Nay, Cadwal, we must lay his head to the East; my father has a reason for that.

[113] ARVIRAGUS. It is true.

[114] GUIDERIUS. Come on, then, and shift him.

[115] ARVIRAGUS. So. Begin.

[116] SONG

[117] GUIDERIUS. Fear no more the heat of the sun, nor the furious winter's rages; your worldly task is done, home you've gone, and taken your wages. Golden lads and girls all must, as chimney-sweepers, come to dust.

[118] ARVIRAGUS. Fear no more the frown of the great; you are past the tyrant's stroke. Care no more to clothe and eat; to you the reed is as the oak. The scepter, learning, medicine, must all follow this and come to dust.

[119] GUIDERIUS. Fear no more the lightning flash.

[120] ARVIRAGUS. Nor the all-dreaded thunder-stone.

[121] GUIDERIUS. Fear not slander, or rash censure;

[122] ARVIRAGUS. You have finished joy and moan.

[123] BOTH. All lovers young, all lovers must consign to you and come to dust.

[124] GUIDERIUS. No exorcist harm you!

[125] ARVIRAGUS. Nor any witchcraft charm you!

[126] GUIDERIUS. Ghost unlaid stay away from you!

[127] ARVIRAGUS. Nothing ill come near you!

[128] BOTH. Quiet consummation have, and renowned be your grave!

[129] [Enter BELARIUS with the body of CLOTEN.]

[130] GUIDERIUS. We have finished our funeral rites. Come, lay him down.

[131] BELARIUS. Here are a few flowers; but around midnight, more. The herbs that bear cold night-dew on them are the fittest strewings for graves. Upon their faces. You were as flowers, now withered. Even so shall these little herbs we strew upon you. Come on, away. Apart, upon our knees. The ground that gave them first has them again. Their pleasures here are past, so is their pain.

[132] [Exeunt all but IMOGEN.]

[133] IMOGEN.

[134] [Awaking.] Yes, sir, to Milford Haven. Which is the way?

[135] I thank you. By yonder bush? Please, how far from here? By God's pity! Can it be six miles yet? I have gone all night. By faith, I'll lie down and sleep. But, soft! No bedfellow. O gods and goddesses!

[136] [Seeing the body.]

[137] These flowers are like the pleasures of the world; this bloody man, the care that comes with them. I hope I dream; for so I thought I was a cave-keeper, and cook to honest creatures. But it is not so; it was but a bolt of nothing, shot at nothing, which the brain makes out of fumes. Our very eyes are sometimes, like our judgments, blind. By faith, I tremble still with fear; but if there is yet left in heaven as small a drop of pity as a wren's eye, feared gods, give me a part of it! The dream is here still. Even when I wake it is outside me, as within me; not imagined, felt. A headless man? The garments of Posthumus? I know the shape of his leg; this is his hand, his Mercurial foot, his Martial thigh, the brawn of Hercules; but his Jove-like face — Murder in heaven! What! It is gone. Pisanio, all the curses mad Hecuba gave the Greeks, and mine on top, be hurled at you! You, in league with that lawless devil, Cloten, have cut off my lord here. To write and to read be henceforth treacherous! Damned Pisanio has with his forged letters (damned Pisanio) struck the main-mast from this most brave vessel of the world. O Posthumus! Alas, where is your head? Where's that? Alas, where's that? Pisanio might have killed you at the heart, and left this head on. How could this be? Pisanio? It is he and Cloten; malice and greed in them have laid this woe here. O, it is clear, clear! The drug he gave me, which he said was precious and cordial for me — have I not found it murderous to the senses? That confirms it. This is Pisanio's deed, and Cloten's. O! Give color to my pale cheek with your blood, so that we, the more horrible, may seem to those who chance to find us. O, my lord, my lord!

[138] [Falls fainting on the body.]

[139] [Enter LUCIUS, Captains and a Soothsayer.]

[140] CAPTAIN. To them, the legions garrisoned in Gallia have, after your wish, crossed the sea, awaiting you here at Milford Haven; with your ships, they are in readiness.

[141] LUCIUS. But what news from Rome?

[142] CAPTAIN. The Senate has stirred up the borderers and gentlemen of Italy, most willing spirits, who promise noble service; and they come under the leadership of bold Iachimo, Sienna's brother.

[143] LUCIUS. When do you expect them?

[144] CAPTAIN. With the next favorable wind.

[145] LUCIUS. This forwardness makes our hopes fair. Order our present numbers to be mustered; bid the captains see to it. Now, sir, what have you dreamed lately of this war's purpose?

[146] SOOTHSAYER. Last night the very gods showed me a vision (I fasted and prayed for their counsel) thus: I saw Jove's bird, the Roman eagle, winged from the spongy south to this part of the west, and there vanished in the sunbeams; which portends, unless my sins corrupt my divination, success to the Roman army.

[147] LUCIUS. Dream often so, and never false. Soft, ho! What trunk is here without his top? The ruin tells that once it was a worthy building. What? A page? Either dead or sleeping upon him? But dead, rather; for nature does abhor making her bed with the dead, or sleeping upon a corpse. Let's see the boy's face.

[148] CAPTAIN. He's alive, my lord.

[149] LUCIUS. He'll then instruct us about this body. Young one, inform us of your fortunes; for it seems they crave to be asked. Who is this you make your bloody pillow? Or who was he who, contrary to what noble nature did, has altered that good picture? What is your interest in this sad wreck? How did it come about? Who is it? What are you?

[150] IMOGEN. I am nothing; or if not, it would be better to be nothing. This was my master, a very brave Briton and a good one, who lies here slain by mountaineers. Alas! There are no more such masters. I may wander from east to west, cry out for service, try many, all good, serve truly, and never find another such master.

[151] LUCIUS. Alas, good youth! You move me no less by your complaining than your master by his bleeding. Tell his name, good friend.

[152] IMOGEN. Richard du Champ. [Aside.] If I lie, and do no harm by it, though the gods hear, I hope they'll pardon it. — Did you say something, sir?

[153] LUCIUS. Your name?

[154] IMOGEN. Fidele, sir.

[155] LUCIUS. You prove yourself to be just that; your name well fits your faith, your faith your name. Will you take your chances with me? I will not say you will be so well mastered, but be sure, no less beloved. The Roman Emperor's letters, sent by a consul to me, should not sooner advance you than your own worth. Come with me.

[156] IMOGEN. I'll follow, sir. But first, if it please the gods, I'll hide my master from the flies, as deep as these poor pickaxes can dig; and when, with wild wood-leaves and weeds, I have strewn his grave, and said over it a hundred prayers — such as I can — twice through, I'll weep and sigh; and leaving his service so, follow you, if it please you to take me.

[157] LUCIUS. Aye, good youth; and I'd rather father you than master you. My friends, the boy has taught us manly duties; let us find the prettiest daisied plot we can, and with our pikes and partisans make him a grave. Come, take up his arms. Boy, he is preferred by you to us; and he shall be buried as soldiers can. Be cheerful; wipe your eyes. Some falls are means the happier to arise.

[158] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 23: Act 4, Scene 3 — Britain. Cymbeline’s palace ===
[0] [Enter CYMBELINE, Lords, PISANIO and Attendants.]

[1] CYMBELINE. Again! And bring me word how she is.

[2] [Exit an Attendant.]

[3] A fever from the absence of her son; a madness that her life is in danger from. Heavens, how deeply you at once touch me! Imogen, the great part of my comfort, gone; my queen on a desperate sickbed, and in a time when fearful wars point at me; her son gone, so needed for this present moment. It strikes me past the hope of comfort. But as for you, fellow, who must know about her departure and yet seem so ignorant, we'll force it out of you by a sharp torture.

[4] PISANIO. Sir, my life is yours; I humbly set it at your will; but as for my mistress, I know nothing — where she remains, why gone, nor when she intends to return. I beg your Highness, hold me to be your loyal servant.

[5] LORD. Good my liege, the day she went missing he was here. I dare bind that he is true and shall perform all parts of his duty loyally. As for Cloten, there is no lack of diligence in seeking him, and he will no doubt be found.

[6] CYMBELINE. The time is troubled.

[7] [To PISANIO.] We'll let you slip for a season; but our suspicion

[8] still hangs over you.

[9] LORD. So please your Majesty, the Roman legions, all drawn from Gallia, have landed on your coast, with a supplement of Roman gentlemen sent by the Senate.

[10] CYMBELINE. Now for the counsel of my son and queen! I am overwhelmed with this matter.

[11] LORD. Good my liege, your preparation can face no less than what you have heard of. Come more, for more you are ready. The only need is to put those forces in motion that long to move.

[12] CYMBELINE. I thank you. Let's withdraw, and meet the time as it seeks us. We do not fear what can come from Italy to vex us; but we grieve over events here. Away!

[13] [Exeunt all but PISANIO.]

[14] PISANIO. I have heard no letter from my master since I wrote him that Imogen was slain. It is strange. Nor do I hear from my mistress, who promised to yield me news often. Neither do I know what has happened to Cloten, but remain perplexed in everything. The heavens still must work. In what I am false, I am honest; not true, in order to be truly loyal. These present wars shall find I love my country, even to the King's notice, or I'll fall in them. All other doubts, by time let them be cleared: Fortune brings in some boats that are not steered.

[15] [Exit.]


=== Chapter 24: Act 4, Scene 4 — Wales. Before the cave of Belarius ===
[0] [Enter BELARIUS, GUIDERIUS and ARVIRAGUS.]

[1] GUIDERIUS. The noise is all around us.

[2] BELARIUS. Let us get away from it.

[3] ARVIRAGUS. What pleasure, sir, do we find in life, by locking it away from action and adventure?

[4] GUIDERIUS. Nay, what hope have we in hiding? In this way the Romans must either slay us as Britons, or accept us as barbarous and unnatural defectors during their use of us, and slay us afterward.

[5] BELARIUS. Sons, we'll go higher into the mountains; there we'll be safe. To the King's side there is no going. The newness of Cloten's death (we being unknown, not mustered among the troops) may drive us to confess where we have lived, and so extort from us what we have done, the answer to which would be death, drawn out with torture.

[6] GUIDERIUS. This is, sir, a doubt which in such a time does not become you, nor satisfy us.

[7] ARVIRAGUS. It is not likely that when they hear the Roman horses neigh, behold their quartered fires, have both their eyes and ears so importantly occupied as now, that they will waste their time on noticing us, to know where we are from.

[8] BELARIUS. O, I am known to many in the army. Many years, though Cloten was then but young, have not worn him from my memory, you see. And besides, the King has not deserved my service nor your love, who find in my exile the lack of upbringing, the certainty of this hard life; ever hopeless to have the courtesy your cradle promised, but to be still hot summer's sun-tanned ones and the shrinking slaves of winter.

[9] GUIDERIUS. Than be so, better to cease to be. Please, sir, to the army. I and my brother are not known; you, yourself, so out of mind, and to that so overgrown, cannot be questioned.

[10] ARVIRAGUS. By this sun that shines, I'll go there. What kind of thing is it that I have never seen a man die, scarcely ever looked on blood except that of cowardly hares, hot goats, and venison! Never bestrode a horse, except one that had a rider like myself, who never wore rowel nor iron on his heel! I am ashamed to look upon the holy sun, to have the benefit of his blessed beams, remaining so long a poor unknown.

[11] GUIDERIUS. By heavens, I'll go! If you will bless me, sir, and give me leave, I'll take the better care; but if you will not, the danger therefore due fall on me by the hands of Romans!

[12] ARVIRAGUS. So say I. Amen.

[13] BELARIUS. There's no reason that I, since you set so slight a valuation on your lives, should reserve my cracked one with more care. Come on, boys! If in your country's wars you chance to die, that is my bed too, lads, and there I'll lie. Lead, lead. [Aside.] The time seems long; their blood scorns delay till it flies out and shows them princes born.

[14] [Exeunt.]


=== Chapter 25: Act 5, Scene 1 — Britain. The Roman camp ===
[0] [Enter POSTHUMUS alone, with a bloody handkerchief.]

[1] POSTHUMUS. Yes, bloody cloth, I'll keep you; for I wished you to be colored like this. You married ones, if each of you should take this course, how many would have to murder wives much better than themselves for going only a little astray! O Pisanio! Every good servant does not perform every command; he is bound only to do just ones. Gods! If you had taken vengeance on my faults, I never would have lived to put on this; so you would have saved the noble Imogen to repent, and struck me, a wretch more deserving your vengeance. But alas, you snatch some away for little faults — that's love, to keep them from falling further. You permit some to follow ills with worse ills, each later one worse, and make them dread it, to the doers' profit. But Imogen is your own. Do your best will, and make me blessed to obey. I am brought here among the Italian gentry, and to fight against my lady's kingdom. It is enough that, Britain, I have killed your mistress; peace! I'll give you no wound. Therefore, good heavens, hear patiently my plan. I'll disrobe myself of these Italian garments, and dress as a British peasant. So I'll fight against the side I came with; so I'll die for you, O Imogen, even for whom my life is every breath a death. And thus unknown, neither pitied nor hated, to the face of peril I'll dedicate myself. Let me make men know there is more valor in me than my clothes show. Gods, put the strength of the Leonati in me! To shame the world's fashion, I will begin the fashion less without and more within.

[2] [Exit.]

=== Chapter 27: Act 5, Scene 3 — Another part of the field ===
[0] [Enter POSTHUMUS and a Briton Lord.]

[1] LORD. Did you come from where they made the stand?

[2] POSTHUMUS. I did: though you, it seems, come from those who fled.

[3] LORD. I did.

[4] POSTHUMUS. No blame to you, sir, for all was lost except that the heavens fought. The King himself stripped of his wings, the army broken, and nothing but the backs of Britons seen, all fleeing through a narrow lane; the enemy, full of heart, lolling their tongues with slaughter, having more work than tools to do it, struck down some mortally, some lightly touched, some falling merely through fear, so that the narrow pass was dammed with dead men hurt from behind, and with cowards who lived only to die with prolonged shame.

[5] LORD. Where was this lane?

[6] POSTHUMUS. Close by the battle, ditched, and walled with turf, which gave advantage to an old soldier, an honest one, I warrant, who deserved as long an upbringing as his white beard came to, in doing this for his country. Across the lane he, with two striplings (lads more likely to run the country game than to commit such slaughter; with faces fit for masks, or rather fairer than those that are encased for preservation or shame) made good the passage, cried to those who fled 'Our Britain's stags die fleeing, not our men. To darkness flee souls that fly backward! Stand; or we are Romans and will give you that, like beasts, which you shun beastly, and may save only by looking back with a frown. Stand, stand!' These three — three thousand confident, in act as many — for three performers are the file when all the rest do nothing — with this word 'Stand, stand!' accommodated by the place, more charming with their own nobleness, which could have turned a distaff to a lance, gilded pale faces, part shame, part spirit renewed; so that some who had turned coward only by example (O, a sin in war damned in the first beginners) began to look the way they did and to grin like lions upon the pikes of the hunters. Then began a stop in the chaser, a retreat; soon a rout, confusion thick. At once they flee, chickens, the way which as eagles they swooped down; slaves, the strides they took as victors; and now our cowards, like fragments in hard voyages, became the life of the need. Having found the back-door open of the unguarded hearts, heavens, how they wound! Some slain before, some dying, some seeing their friends overborne in the former wave. Ten chased by one are now each one the slaughterman of twenty. Those who would have died rather than resist are grown the deadly terrors of the field.

[7] LORD. This was a strange chance: a narrow lane, an old man, and two boys.

[8] POSTHUMUS. Nay, do not wonder at it; you are made rather to wonder at the things you hear than to do any. Will you rhyme upon it, and vent it as a mockery? Here is one:

[9] 'Two boys, an old man (twice a boy), a lane, preserved the Britons, was the Romans' bane.'

[10] LORD. Nay, be not angry, sir.

[11] POSTHUMUS. Alas, to what end? Whoever dares not stand his foe, I'll be his friend; for if he'll do as he is made to do, I know he'll quickly fly my friendship too. You have put me into rhyme.

[12] LORD. Farewell; you are angry.

[13] [Exit.]

[14] POSTHUMUS. Still going? This is a lord! O noble misery, to be on the field and ask 'What news?' of me! Today how many would have given their honors to have saved their carcasses! Took to their heels to do it, and yet died too! I, in my own woe charmed, could not find death where I heard him groan, nor feel him where he struck. Being an ugly monster, it is strange he hides himself in fresh cups, soft beds, sweet words; or has more agents than we who draw his knives in the war. Well, I will find him; for being now a favorer of the Briton, no more a Briton, I have resumed again the part I came in. Fight I will no more, but yield myself to the merest peasant who shall once touch my shoulder. Great is the slaughter made here by the Roman; great must be the answer Britons take. For me, my ransom is death; on either side I come to spend my breath, which neither here I'll keep nor bear again, but end it by some means for Imogen.

[15] [Enter two British Captains and soldiers.]

[16] FIRST CAPTAIN. Great Jupiter be praised! Lucius is taken. It is thought the old man and his sons were angels.

[17] SECOND CAPTAIN. There was a fourth man, in a poor garment, who gave the front with them.

[18] FIRST CAPTAIN. So it is reported; but none of them can be found. Stand! Who's there?

[19] POSTHUMUS. A Roman, who would not now be drooping here if supports had answered him.

[20] SECOND CAPTAIN. Lay hands on him; a dog! Not a leg of Rome shall return to tell what crows have pecked them here. He brags of his service, as if he were of note. Bring him to the King.

[21] [Enter CYMBELINE, BELARIUS, GUIDERIUS, ARVIRAGUS, PISANIO and Roman captives. The Captains present POSTHUMUS to CYMBELINE, who delivers him over to a jailer.]

[22] [Exeunt omnes.]


=== Chapter 28: Act 5, Scene 4 — Britain. A prison ===
[0] [Enter POSTHUMUS and two Gaolers.]

[1] FIRST GAOLER. You shall not now be stolen, you have locks upon you; so graze as you find pasture.

[2] SECOND GAOLER. Aye, or a stomach.

[3] [Exeunt Gaolers.]

[4] POSTHUMUS. Most welcome, bondage! For you are a way, I think, to liberty. Yet am I better off than one who is sick with the gout, since he had rather groan so perpetually than be cured by the sure physician death, who is the key to unbar these locks. My conscience, you are fettered more than my shanks and wrists; you good gods, give me the penitent instrument to pick that bolt, then, free forever! Is it enough I am sorry? So children appease earthly fathers; gods are more full of mercy. Must I repent, I cannot do it better than in chains, desired more than constrained. To satisfy, if of my freedom it is the main part, take no stricter render from me than my all. I know you are more clement than vile men, who from their broken debtors take a third, a sixth, a tenth, letting them thrive again on what's reduced from them; that is not my desire. For Imogen's dear life take mine; and though it is not so dear, yet it is a life; you coined it. Between man and man they do not weigh every stamp; though light, they take pieces for the figure's sake; you should rather take mine, being yours. And so, great powers, if you will take this audit, take this life, and cancel these cold bonds. O Imogen! I'll speak to you in silence.

[5] [Sleeps.]

[6] [Solemn music. Enter, as in an apparition, SICILIUS LEONATUS, father to POSTHUMUS, an old man attired like a warrior; leading in his hand an ancient matron, his wife and Mother to POSTHUMUS, with music before them. Then, after other music, follows the two young Leonati, brothers to POSTHUMUS, with wounds, as they died in the wars. They circle POSTHUMUS round as he lies sleeping.]

[7] SICILIUS. No more, you thunder-master, show your spite on mortal flies. Quarrel with Mars, scold with Juno, that scolds and revenges your adulteries. Has my poor boy done aught but well, whose face I never saw? I died while in the womb he stayed, awaiting nature's law; whose father then, as men report you are an orphans' father, you should have been, and shielded him from this earth-vexing smart.

[8] MOTHER. Lucina lent me not her aid, but took me in my throes, so that from me was Posthumus ripped, came crying among his foes, a thing of pity.

[9] SICILIUS. Great Nature, like his ancestry, molded the stuff so fair that he deserved the praise of the world as great Sicilius' heir.

[10] FIRST BROTHER. When once he was mature for man, in Britain where was he that could stand up his parallel, or fruitful object be in the eye of Imogen, who best could deem his dignity?

[11] MOTHER. Why was he mocked with marriage, to be exiled and thrown from the Leonati seat and cast from her, his dearest one, sweet Imogen?

[12] SICILIUS. Why did you suffer Iachimo, slight thing of Italy, to taint his nobler heart and brain with needless jealousy, and to become the dupe and scorn of the other's villainy?

[13] SECOND BROTHER. For this from quieter seats we came, our parents and us two, that, striking in our country's cause, fell bravely and were slain, our fealty and Tenantius' right with honor to maintain.

[14] FIRST BROTHER. Like courage Posthumus has performed for Cymbeline. Then, Jupiter, you king of gods, why have you thus postponed the graces due his merits, having all turned to sorrows?

[15] SICILIUS. Open your crystal window; look out; no longer exercise upon a valiant race your harsh and potent injuries.

[16] MOTHER. Since, Jupiter, our son is good, take off his miseries.

[17] SICILIUS. Peep through your marble mansion. Help! Or we poor ghosts will cry to the shining synod of the rest against your deity.

[18] BROTHERS. Help, Jupiter! Or we appeal, and from your justice fly.

[19] [JUPITER descends in thunder and lightning, sitting upon an eagle. He throws a thunderbolt. The Ghosts fall on their knees.]

[20] JUPITER. No more, you petty spirits of region low, offend our hearing; hush! How dare you ghosts accuse the Thunderer whose bolt, you know, sky-planted, batters all rebelling coasts? Poor shadows of Elysium, hence, and rest upon your never-withering banks of flowers. Be not with mortal accidents oppressed: no care of yours it is; you know it is ours. Whom best I love I cross; to make my gift, the more delayed, the more delighted. Be content; your low-laid son our godhead will uplift; his comforts thrive, his trials are well spent. Our Jovial star reigned at his birth, and in our temple was he married. Rise, and fade! He shall be lord of Lady Imogen, and made much happier by his affliction. Lay this tablet upon his breast, wherein our pleasure his full fortune confines; and so, away; no further with your din express impatience, lest you stir up mine. Mount, eagle, to my crystalline palace.

[21] [Ascends.]

[22] SICILIUS. He came in thunder; his celestial breath was sulphurous to smell; the holy eagle stooped as if to take us. His ascension is more sweet than our blessed fields. His royal bird preens the immortal wing, and cloys his beak, as when his god is pleased.

[23] ALL. Thanks, Jupiter!

[24] SICILIUS. The marble pavement closes, he has entered his radiant roof. Away! And, to be blessed, let us with care perform his great behest.

[25] [Ghosts vanish.]

[26] POSTHUMUS.

[27] [Waking.] Sleep, you have been a grandsire and begot

[28] a father to me; and you have created a mother and two brothers. But, O scorn, gone! They went hence as soon as they were born. And so I am awake. Poor wretches, that depend on greatness' favor, dream as I have done; wake and find nothing. But, alas, I swerve; many do not dream of finding, neither deserve, and yet are steeped in favors; so am I, who have this golden chance, and know not why. What fairies haunt this ground? A book? O rare one! Be not, as is our newfangled world, a garment nobler than what it covers. Let your effects follow to be most unlike our courtiers, as good as promise.

[29] [Reads.] 'When a lion's whelp shall, to himself unknown, without

[30] seeking find, and be embraced by a piece of tender air; and when from a stately cedar shall be lopped branches which, being dead many years, shall after revive, be jointed to the old stock, and freshly grow; then shall Posthumus end his miseries, Britain be fortunate and flourish in peace and plenty.'

[31] It is still a dream, or else such stuff as madmen speak and do not think; either both or nothing, or senseless speaking, or a speaking such as sense cannot untie. Be what it is, the action of my life is like it, which I'll keep, if only for sympathy.

[32] [Enter Gaoler.]

[33] GAOLER. Come, sir, are you ready for death?

[34] POSTHUMUS. Over-roasted rather; ready long ago.

[35] GAOLER. Hanging is the word, sir; if you are ready for that, you are well cooked.

[36] POSTHUMUS. So, if I prove a good repast for the spectators, the dish pays the bill.

[37] GAOLER. A heavy reckoning for you, sir. But the comfort is, you shall be called to no more payments, fear no more tavern bills, which are often the sadness of parting, as well as the producing of mirth. You come in faint for want of meat, depart reeling with too much drink; sorry that you have paid too much, and sorry that you are paid too much; purse and brain both empty; the brain the heavier for being too light, the purse too light, being drawn of its heaviness. O, of this contradiction you shall now be quit. O, the charity of a penny cord! It sums up thousands in a moment. You have no true debtor and creditor but it; of what is past, is, and to come, it gives discharge. Your neck, sir, is pen, book, and counters; so the receipt follows.

[38] POSTHUMUS. I am merrier to die than you are to live.

[39] GAOLER. Indeed, sir, he who sleeps feels not the toothache. But a man who were to sleep your sleep, with a hangman to help him to bed, I think he would change places with his officer; for look you, sir, you know not which way you shall go.

[40] POSTHUMUS. Yes indeed I do, fellow.

[41] GAOLER. Your death has eyes in his head, then; I have not seen him so pictured. You must either be directed by some who claim to know, or take upon yourself that which I am sure you do not know, or jump the after-inquiry on your own peril. And how you shall fare in your journey's end, I think you'll never return to tell anyone.

[42] POSTHUMUS. I tell you, fellow, no one lacks eyes to direct him the way I am going, except those who close them and will not use them.

[43] GAOLER. What an infinite mockery this is, that a man should have the best use of eyes to see the way of blindness! I am sure hanging is the way of winking.

[44] [Enter a Messenger.]

[45] MESSENGER. Knock off his manacles; bring your prisoner to the King.

[46] POSTHUMUS. You bring good news: I am called to be made free.

[47] GAOLER. I'll be hanged then.

[48] POSTHUMUS. You shall be then freer than a jailer; no bolts for the dead.

[49] [Exeunt POSTHUMUS and Messenger.]

[50] GAOLER. Unless a man would marry a gallows and beget young gibbets, I never saw one so eager. Yet, on my conscience, there are more truly knavish ones who desire to live, for all he be a Roman; and there are some of them too who die against their wills; so should I, if I were one. I wish we were all of one mind, and that one mind good. O, there would be desolation of jailers and gallowses! I speak against my present profit, but my wish has a higher good in it.

[51] [Exit.]

[8] [Enter CORNELIUS and Ladies.]

[9] There's business in these faces. Why do you greet our victory so sadly? You look like Romans, and not like the court of Britain.

[10] CORNELIUS. Hail, great King! To sour your happiness I must report that the Queen is dead.

[11] CYMBELINE. Whom worse than a physician would this report become? But I consider that by medicine life may be prolonged, yet death will seize the doctor too. How did she end?

[12] CORNELIUS. With horror, dying madly, like her life; which, being cruel to the world, ended most cruel to herself. What she confessed I will report, if you please; these women of hers can correct me if I err, who with wet cheeks were present when she finished.

[13] CYMBELINE. Please, speak.

[14] CORNELIUS. First, she confessed she never loved you; she only loved the greatness she got from you, not you; married your royalty, was wife to your position; abhorred your person.

[15] CYMBELINE. She alone knew this; and if she had not spoken it while dying, I would not believe her lips in saying it. Proceed.

[16] CORNELIUS. Your daughter, whom she made a show of loving with such integrity, she did confess was as a scorpion to her sight; whose life, but that her flight prevented it, she had meant to take by poison.

[17] CYMBELINE. O most delicate fiend! Who can read a woman? Is there more?

[18] CORNELIUS. More, sir, and worse. She did confess she had for you a deadly mineral, which, being taken, should feed on life by the minute, and lingering, by inches waste you. In which time she purposed, by watching, weeping, attending, kissing, to overcome you with her show; and in time, when she had fitted you to her craft, to work her son into the adoption of the crown; but failing of her end through his strange absence, she grew shamelessly desperate, opened, in spite of heaven and men, her purposes, repented that the evils she hatched were not accomplished; so, despairing, died.

[19] CYMBELINE. Did you all hear this, her women?

[20] LADIES. We did, if it please your Highness.

[21] CYMBELINE. My eyes were not at fault, for she was beautiful; my ears, which heard her flattery; nor my heart that thought her like her seeming. It would have been vicious to have mistrusted her; yet, O my daughter! That it was folly in me, you may say, and prove it in your feeling. Heaven mend all!

[22] [Enter LUCIUS, IACHIMO, the Soothsayer and other Roman prisoners, guarded; POSTHUMUS behind, and IMOGEN.]

[23] You come not, Caius, now for tribute; that the Britons have wiped out, though with the loss of many a bold one, whose kinsmen have requested that their good souls may be appeased with slaughter of you their captives, which we ourself have granted; so think of your fate.

[24] LUCIUS. Consider, sir, the chance of war. The day was yours by accident; had it gone with us, we should not, when the blood was cool, have threatened our prisoners with the sword. But since the gods will have it thus, that nothing but our lives may be called ransom, let it come. It suffices that a Roman with a Roman's heart can suffer. Augustus lives to think on it; and so much for my particular care. This one thing only I will entreat: my boy, a Briton born, let him be ransomed. Never master had a page so kind, so dutiful, diligent, so tender over his needs, true, so apt, so nurse-like; let his virtue join with my request, which I'll dare to say your Highness cannot deny; he has done no Briton harm, though he has served a Roman. Save him, sir, and spare no other blood.

[25] CYMBELINE. I have surely seen him; his face is familiar to me. Boy, you have looked yourself into my grace, and are mine own. I know not why or wherefore I say 'Live, boy.' Never thank your master. Live; and ask of Cymbeline whatever boon you will, fitting my bounty and your state, I'll give it; yes, even though you should demand a prisoner, the noblest taken.

[26] IMOGEN. I humbly thank your Highness.

[27] LUCIUS. I do not bid you beg for my life, good lad, and yet I know you will.

[28] IMOGEN. No, no! Alas, there is other work in hand. I see a thing bitter to me as death; your life, good master, must shuffle for itself.

[29] LUCIUS. The boy disdains me, leaves me, scorns me. Briefly die the joys of those who place them on the truth of girls and boys. Why stands he so perplexed?

[30] CYMBELINE. What would you have, boy? I love you more and more; think more and more what's best to ask. Do you know him you are looking at? Speak — will you have him live? Is he your kin? Your friend?

[31] IMOGEN. He is a Roman, no more kin to me than I am to your Highness; who, being born your vassal, am something nearer.

[32] CYMBELINE. Why do you eye him so?

[33] IMOGEN. I'll tell you, sir, in private, if you please to give me hearing.

[34] CYMBELINE. Aye, with all my heart, and lend my best attention. What's your name?

[36] CYMBELINE. You're my good youth, my page; I'll be your master. Walk with me; speak freely.

Once upon a time — unhappy was the clock that struck the hour — I was in Rome, accursed was the mansion where: it was at a feast — O, would our food had been poisoned (or at least what I lifted to my head) — the good Posthumus (what should I say? He was too good to be where ill men were, and was the best of all among the rarest of good ones) sitting sadly hearing us praise our loves of Italy for beauty that made barren the swelled boasting of the man who could best speak; for features, putting to shame the shrine of Venus or upright Minerva, postures beyond brief nature; for character, a shop of all the qualities for which man loves woman; besides that hook of marriage, fairness which strikes the eye.

[60] CYMBELINE. I stand on fire. Come to the matter.

[61] IACHIMO. All too soon I shall, unless you wish to grieve quickly. This Posthumus, most like a noble lord in love and one who had a royal lover, took his cue; and (not disparaging whom we praised, in that he was as calm as virtue) he began his mistress' picture; which, by his tongue being painted, and then a mind put into it, either our brags were cracked open as merely of kitchen sluts, or his description proved us speechless dolts.

[62] CYMBELINE. Nay, nay, to the purpose.

[63] IACHIMO. Your daughter's chastity (there it begins) he spoke of her as if Diana had hot dreams and she alone were cold; whereat I, wretch, made a scruple of his praise, and wagered with him pieces of gold against this ring which he then wore upon his honored finger, to win in suit the place of her bed, and win this ring by hers and my adultery. He, true knight, no less confident of her honor than I did truly find her, stakes this ring; and would so, had it been a carbuncle of Phoebus' wheel; and might so safely, had it been all the worth of his chariot. Away to Britain I post in this design. Well may you, sir, remember me at court, where I was taught by your chaste daughter the wide difference between amorous and villainous. Being thus quenched of hope, not of longing, my Italian brain began in your duller Britain to operate most vilely; for my advantage, excellent; and, to be brief, my plot so prevailed that I returned with simulated proof enough to make the noble Leonatus mad, by wounding his belief in her renown with tokens thus and thus; averring marks of chamber-hangings, pictures, this her bracelet (O cunning, how I got it!) — nay, some secret marks on her person, so that he could not but think her bond of chastity quite cracked, I having taken the forfeit. Whereupon, methinks I see him now —

[130] CYMBELINE. Did you ever meet?

[131] ARVIRAGUS. Aye, my good lord.

[132] GUIDERIUS. And at first meeting loved, continued so until we thought he died.

[133] CORNELIUS. By the Queen's dram she swallowed.

[134] CYMBELINE. O rare instinct! When shall I hear it all through? This fierce abridgment has circumstantial branches to it, which distinction should be rich in. Where? How did you live? And when did you come to serve our Roman captive? How did you part from your brothers? How did you first meet them? Why did you flee from the court? And whither? These, and your three motives to the battle, with I know not how much more, should be asked, and all the other side-dependencies, from chance to chance; but neither the time nor place will serve our long interrogations. See, Posthumus anchors upon Imogen; and she, like harmless lightning, throws her eye on him, her brothers, me, her master, hitting each object with a joy; the exchange of looks is severally in all. Let's quit this ground, and smoke the temple with our sacrifices.

[135] [To BELARIUS.] You are my brother; so we'll hold you ever.

[136] IMOGEN. You are my father too, and did relieve me to see this gracious season.

[137] CYMBELINE. All overjoyed except these in bonds. Let them be joyful too, for they shall taste our comfort.

[138] IMOGEN. My good master, I will yet do you service.

[140] CYMBELINE. The forlorn soldier, who fought so nobly, he would have well graced this place and graced the thankings of a king.

[141] POSTHUMUS. I am, sir, the soldier who accompanied these three in poor garments; it was fitting for the purpose I then followed. That I was he, speak, Iachimo. I had you down, and might have made you finish.

[142] IACHIMO.

[143] [Kneeling.] I am down again;

[144] but now my heavy conscience bends my knee, as then your force did. Take that life, I beseech you, which I so often owe; but your ring first, and here the bracelet of the truest princess that ever swore her faith.

[145] POSTHUMUS. Kneel not to me. The power that I have on you is to spare you; the malice towards you, to forgive you. Live, and deal with others better.

[146] CYMBELINE. Nobly judged! We'll learn our generosity from a son-in-law; pardon is the word to all.

[147] ARVIRAGUS. You helped us, sir, as you did mean indeed to be our brother; joyful are we that you are.

When I awoke, I found this label on my chest; whose contents are so removed from sense in their obscurity that I can make no meaning of it. Let him show his skill in the interpretation.

[149] LUCIUS. Philarmonus!

[150] SOOTHSAYER. Here, my good lord.

[151] LUCIUS. Read, and declare the meaning.

[152] SOOTHSAYER.

[153] [Reads.] 'When a lion's whelp shall, to himself unknown, without

[154] seeking find, and be embraced by a piece of tender air; and when from a stately cedar shall be lopped branches which, being dead many years, shall after revive, be jointed to the old stock, and freshly grow; then shall Posthumus end his miseries, Britain be fortunate and flourish in peace and plenty.' You, Leonatus, are the lion's whelp; the fit and apt construction of your name, being Leo-natus, imports so much.

[155] [To CYMBELINE.] The piece of tender air, your virtuous daughter,

[156] which we call 'mollis aer,' and 'mollis aer' we term 'mulier'; which 'mulier' I divine is this most constant wife, who even now, answering the letter of the oracle, unknown to you, unsought, was enclosed by this most tender air.

[157] CYMBELINE. This has some fitting.

[158] SOOTHSAYER. The lofty cedar, royal Cymbeline, represents you; and your lopped branches point out your two sons, who, stolen by Belarius, for many years thought dead, are now revived, joined to the majestic cedar, whose offspring promise Britain peace and plenty.

[159] CYMBELINE. Well, my peace we will begin. And, Caius Lucius, although the victor, we submit to Caesar and to the Roman empire, promising to pay our customary tribute, from which we were dissuaded by our wicked queen, whom heavens in justice, both on her and hers, have laid most heavy hand.

[160] SOOTHSAYER. The fingers of the powers above do tune the harmony of this peace. The vision which I made known to Lucius before the stroke of this scarce-cold battle is at this instant fully accomplished; for the Roman eagle, from south to west on wing soaring aloft, lessened herself and in the beams of the sun so vanished; which foreshowed our princely eagle, the imperial Caesar, should again unite his favor with the radiant Cymbeline, which shines here in the west.

[161] CYMBELINE. Let us praise the gods; and let our crooked smokes climb to their nostrils from our blessed altars. Let us publish this peace to all our subjects. Let us set forward; let a Roman and a British ensign wave friendly together. So through Lud's Town march; and in the temple of great Jupiter our peace we'll ratify; seal it with feasts. Set on there! Never was a war did cease, before bloody hands were washed, with such a peace.

[162] [Exeunt.]

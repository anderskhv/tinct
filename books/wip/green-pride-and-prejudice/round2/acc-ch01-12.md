## Findings in brief (17 in total, 1 blocking)

**Blocking**
- [3.3]/[3.4]: the paragraph break falls in the middle of a sentence ("suggesting that he had" / "gone to London…"). The two paragraphs need to be rejoined.

**Non-blocking**
- [3.4]: "his five sisters and a cousin" looks like a contradiction of "his two sisters" in the same paragraph. The six-person report should be marked clearly as rumour.
- [2.8]: Mr. Bennet's cough quip repeats itself ("no sense of timing… times them badly").
- [2.14]: The reasoning in "if _we_ don't make the effort, someone else will" is muddled.
- [4.11]: "doubted whether he might not spend" is an archaic double negative that invites a misreading.
- [5.0]: A dangling modifier ("By nature harmless… his presentation… had made him courteous").
- [6.21]: "power my song" is a jarring, mechanical verb.
- [7.28]: In "if you have them today", it is unclear who "has" the horses.
- [8.15]: "Cheapside" needs a light gloss (the merchants' district), or the sneer is lost.
- [8.58]: "an express" needs glossing as "an urgent messenger".
- [9.0]: "two elegant ladies who attended his sisters" reads as guests rather than ladies' maids.
- [9.1]: The sentence is overloaded, and "followed her" has an ambiguous referent.
- [9.37]: "their giving one" has no antecedent. It should name the officers.
- [10.10]: "fix your pen" should be "mend your quill".
- [11.6]: Nicholls and "white soup" go unexplained.
- [11.11]: "She was desperate to know" is meant to be Miss Bingley, but Elizabeth is the nearest antecedent.
- [12.1]: "enough was said… to work on Jane, so…" is tangled.

## Passages that read especially well

- [1.0]–[1.33]: The opening dialogue keeps its rhythm and irony intact. "They are old friends of mine" [1.28] lands perfectly.
- [6.1]–[6.10]: Charlotte's case for marriage as a matter of chance is clear and modern without losing its edge.
- [7.0]: The in-line gloss of "entailed" is a model light-touch clarification.
- [8.42]–[8.53]: The "accomplished woman" exchange is crisp, and every speaker is easy to track.
- [10.24]–[10.41]: The persuasion debate follows a hard line of argument and stays easy to follow.
- [11.14]–[11.31]: The Darcy/Elizabeth sparring ("My good opinion, once lost, is lost forever") is clean and keeps its voice.

| 9–12 | Substantially accessible, with referent clean-ups at [9.1], [9.37] and [11.11], plus [9.0], [10.10], [11.6] and [12.1] |

**Readability summary.** This is a strong, readable light-touch edition. It keeps the irony, the period formality and the distinct voices of Mrs. Bennet, Mr. Bennet, Miss Bingley and Darcy, and a general reader today can follow it without effort. Most of the text needs no work. Only one problem stops comprehension: the mid-sentence paragraph break at [3.3]/[3.4], which is probably a segmentation or alignment error rather than a prose problem. It will sound especially broken in audio. The other issues are small:
- a handful of period terms whose point depends on context (Cheapside, an express, mending a quill, white soup);
- three or four unclear pronouns;
- one archaic double negative;
- a couple of redundant or clumsy phrasings.

None of these calls for a broader pass.

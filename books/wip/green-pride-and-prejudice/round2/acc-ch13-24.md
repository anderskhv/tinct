## Findings in brief

There are 17 findings. 2 are blocking. Full details are in `acc-ch13-24.json`.

**Blocking (structural paragraph splits)**
- **[14.12]/[14.13]:** a sentence is split across two paragraphs ("he was glad to invite him" / "to read aloud to the ladies"). Merge the two paragraphs, and remove the doubled "was glad".
- **[22.3]/[22.4]:** a sentence is split mid-clause ("had ever" / "inspired before"). Merge the two paragraphs.

**Non-blocking**
- **Speaker:** [13.17] has no speech tag. It reads as Mr. Bennet continuing, but the speaker is Mrs. Bennet.
- **Vocabulary:**
  - [13.14] "Saturday week"
  - [15.10] "lottery tickets", which is a card game and comes up again in 16.7 and 16.59
  - [16.25] "in his gift"
  - [22.2] "disinterested" and "establishment"
- **Ambiguous or inverted sense:**
  - [16.2] the double negative in "unreasonable admiration"
  - [16.15] Wickham's "cannot pretend to be sorry … not be valued beyond"
  - [18.49] "deserved much less attention", which readers can take in the opposite sense
- **Unclear referents:**
  - [16.56] "his sister" and "he" can be read as Bingley but mean Darcy
  - [18.66] "fixed on her"
  - [22.12] a dangling "though by no means as clever as herself"
  - [23.15] "she … she … she"
- **Overloaded sentences:** [15.10] Mrs. Philips's long dash-interrupted sentence; [24.2] the closing "whether … whichever … though" sentence.
- **Garbled:** [24.23] "or at least, it is slight" ("it" has no antecedent).

I found nothing anachronistic, slangy or in therapy-speak.

**Readability summary.** These chapters read smoothly as modern English. They keep Austen's irony, her social distinctions and each character's voice, and the period formality (Collins's set speeches, Mr. Bennet's dry asides) is never an obstacle. Most dialogue is clearly attributed and easy to follow aloud.

The problems are few and local:

- Two paragraph breaks fall mid-sentence. These are the only blocking issues and are easy to fix by merging.
- One line has no speaker tag.
- A handful of period terms matter to the plot: "in his gift", the "lottery tickets" card game, and "disinterested establishment".
- A few pronoun chains or double negatives force a re-read.

Once these small edits are made, the chapters should be fully accessible to a general adult reader or listener.

## 3. Considered and left alone

- **32.1**: "He had been told all the ladies were at home" slightly over-specifies "he had understood". I left it: the meaning barely changes.
- **32.27**: "All outdoor sports were over" stands in for "field sports", which means hunting and shooting. It is vague but not wrong. "Charlotte's hopeful mind" for "Charlotte's wishes" is acceptable.
- **32.28**: "her own limited knowledge" adds "limited", but that is implied. "Truly engaged" for "animated" is acceptable.
- **32.30**: "the more pleasant man" for "beyond comparison, the pleasantest man" works in context, since the comparison is with Darcy. "Power to grant church appointments" is a good gloss of "patronage in the church", and Charlotte's mercenary irony survives.
- **33.20**: "easygoing" for "tractable" loses a little of the echo of "difficult to manage", but the meaning holds.
- **33.34 and 33.36**: small losses of degree ("rather disposed"; "very sadly"). The jest in 33.36 is still clear.
- **34.4**: "the dignity he was sacrificing" for "the consequence he was wounding" is an acceptable reading.
- **34.11**: "cruel" for "ungenerous" is slightly stronger. I left it.
- **34.16**: "what lie can you use to deceive others" for "under what misrepresentation can you here impose upon others" is acceptable.
- **35.1 and 35.2**: "proud composure" and "very small hand" are fine. "Close hand" means cramped writing.
- **35.3**: "had my honor not required it" for "my character" is borderline, but I left it.
- **35.4 ("best patron")**: this stands in for "his best friend", meaning Darcy's father. It is interpretive but correct.
- **35.4 (Mrs. Younge)**: "the woman who ran her household" for "the lady who presided over it" loses "lady" slightly. "With her help and encouragement" for "connivance and aid" is acceptable.
- **36.0**: the contractions in narration ("didn't", "it's easy to imagine") are an edition-wide style question, not a fidelity defect.
- **36.3**: "misconduct" for "profligacy" is weaker, but I left it.
- **36.5**: the dropped italics on _now_ are covered by "before" later in the sentence.
- **36.8**: "useless or pointless suspicion" for "useless or blameless distrust". The source reading here is textually doubtful (other editions have "blameable"), so I left it.
- **36.9**: "Charlotte's opinion had always been the same" is a fair rendering.
- **36.13**: "relieved" for "rejoiced" is acceptable.
- **37.0**: "sad scene" and "parting bow" lose some of Mr. Collins's pomposity, but the irony survives.
- **37.9**: "riding on top" for "barouche-box" is acceptable.
- **37.15**: "varied wildly" is a slight intensification of "at times widely different". I left it.
- **38.1**: "grateful for the honor" for "condescension" is acceptable in context.
- **38.2**: "the grateful one" already carries the contrast of _her_ feeling "the obliged".
- **38.5**: the dropped "at least" in "I flatter myself" is trivial.
- **38.15**: dropping "the kindness of her aunt" is trivial.

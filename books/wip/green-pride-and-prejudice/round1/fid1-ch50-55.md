## 2. Proposals (24 total: 23 blocking, 1 non-blocking)

- **50.8**: "They will never be admitted into any house in this neighborhood" says the opposite of "Into _one_ house… they shall never have admittance", which bars them only from Longbourn.
- **50.9**: "stubbornness" → "resentment", the motive Mrs. Bennet attributes to him. Also "the weeks" → "the two weeks", restoring the source's fortnight.
- **50.18**: In the candidate, Colonel Forster settles Wickham's debts. In the source he only assures the creditors that they will be paid.
- **51.3**: "not nearly as warm" → "not quite so warm", which restores Austen's understatement.
- **51.35**: "walking away" → "running away".
- **52.3 (Mrs. Gardiner's letter), five fixes:**
  - "If you genuinely don't understand me" → "If you choose not to understand me". The source teases that Elizabeth is pretending not to understand.
  - Restores "it was his own fault" and "loving or trusting" in Darcy's stated motive.
  - "a problem he had helped to create" → "a wrong he had brought about himself". Darcy takes the whole blame, not part of it.
  - Restores the condition that Lydia return to her family "as soon as they could be persuaded to take her back".
  - Fixes the garbled "I won't tell you … except that I saw". The source says she would not mention Lydia's behavior if Jane's letter had not already shown it.
- **52.3**: "reserved/reserve" → "sly/slyness". This restores Mrs. Gardiner's joke that Darcy is hiding his feelings.
- **52.14**: "though I could take it in" → "otherwise I could take it in" (source: "or else").
- **52.35** (non-blocking, obstruction): the double negative "there was not no truth in that" → "it wasn't entirely without foundation".
- **53.26**: Restores "because of it". The neighbors will see Bingley first because Mr. Bennet refuses to call.
- **53.39**: "in her mother's house" → "in her mother's presence".
- **54.10**: "calculating mother" → "prudent mother". This keeps the irony implicit instead of turning it into a narrator's verdict.
- **54.18**: "a renewed proposal" → "a renewal of his love".
- **55.19**: The candidate reverses "unless Mr. Darcy returned within the stated time" into "later than planned". Also restores the hedge in "Seriously, however, she felt fairly sure" (source: "tolerably persuaded").
- **55.21**: "too clever for her own good" → "too clever for her": the mother outwitted Elizabeth, the scheme did not backfire. Also restores "as if in earnest conversation", and "like the others" → "like Jane" (source: "the other").
- **55.46**: Restores Jane's qualifier "in many ways" (source: "in many respects").

## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone

- **50.3**: "Lydia's expenses had always nearly reached that amount" is an acceptable reading of "very little within that sum".
- **50.5**: "ended up on the streets" for "come upon the town" is a fair euphemism. The irony of the spiteful old ladies' "well-meaning wishes" survives.
- **50.11**: "an alliance with the man" drops "relationship of the nearest kind", but "alliance" plus the context carries the meaning. "Downfall" for "frailty" is fine.
- **50.12**: "possessive of his good opinion" for "jealous of his esteem" is acceptable.
- **50.18**: "commission" for "ensigncy" is acceptable (an ensign is the lowest commissioned rank).
- **50.22**: "so reasonably" drops "so mildly". This is minor and leaves the sense intact.
- **51.21**: "desperate financial circumstances" is licensed by the debts established in 52.3.
- **51.22**: "county" for "country" is harmless.
- **52.3**:
  - "dismissed for some reason he didn't specify" loses "disapprobation", but the dismissal already implies it.
  - "lack of proper openness" for "want of proper consideration" is a slight drift, but tolerable in context ("reserve").
  - Dropping "probable" before "credit" is minor.
  - "P." expanded to "Pemberley" and "phaeton" rendered as "low carriage" are acceptable clarifications.
- **52.6**: Dropping "frequently meet" is minor. "Her aunt and uncle" correctly resolves "she and her uncle".
- **53.13 / 53.14**: The candidate keeps Mrs. Bennet's rambling voice. "Three pairs of ducks" is fine.
- **53.41, 53.42, 53.53**: "Pained and embarrassed", "blushing" and "disastrous" are small shifts of degree. None changes the meaning.
- **53.43**: "she said to herself" is a reasonable reading.
- **53.55**: "supposedly" is licensed by the source's ironic "were to offer no compensation".
- **54.25**: "desperate need for whist players" for "rapacity" loses a little comedy, but it is not a meaning error.
- **54.27**: The joke that she likes the nieces because they are "not at all pretty" is preserved.
- **55.10**: Rendering Mary's "instrument" as "piano" is consistent with the novel.
- **55.28**: "anxious meddling" for "anxious circumspection" is sharper than the source, but it sits inside Elizabeth's own sarcastic exclamation, so I left it.
- **55.37**: "so agreeable" for "so complying" is borderline. The next clause ("nothing will ever be decided") carries the meaning.
- **55.42**: The irony in "barbarous neighbour … could not be enough detested" is preserved.
- **55.51**: "against Darcy" correctly resolves "against him".

## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone

- **39.0** "dressing a salad and cucumber" -> "preparing a salad": a trivial loss. The punctuality aside is the only fix proposed here.
- **39.16** "pleasant men" -> "good-looking men". This fits Lydia's voice, and the rest of the long speech is faithful.
- **39.21** "carsick" is slightly anachronistic for a coach but clear. Not worth a change.
- **39.22** "generality of female minds" -> "majority of young women". Mary's pedantry survives in "Far be it from me", "infinitely prefer".
- **40.1** "sisterly partiality" -> "sisterly loyalty". This slightly loses the note of bias, but it is acceptable.
- **40.24** "said she" -> "she said to herself". This is a reasonable reading of a soliloquy.
- **40.25** "feelings of her friends" -> "her family's feelings". This is correct for Austen's usage of "friends".
- **41.17** "often involved" -> "constantly be dragged". A slight intensification that I left alone. "Assurance" -> "arrogance" is acceptable.
- **41.19** "object of prey" -> "worth anyone's pursuit". The fortune-hunter sense is kept.
- **42.4** "made public" -> "read aloud". This is a sensible reading of the family context.
- **42.5** "cruel and malicious" loses "malicious", and "dared to hope" is a small addition. Neither is worth a change.
- **42.8** "with impunity" -> "without incident". Acceptable.
- **42.9** "steady sense" -> "steady temperament". A minor loss.
- **42.13** "She must own" -> "She had to claim". This reads correctly as her pretext.
- **42.15** "could not acquiesce" -> "wouldn't rest". Close enough.
- **43.35** "will be just like him" -> "is just like him". Mrs. Reynolds's idiomatic "will be" means "is". "affable" -> "kind" is acceptable.
- **43.36** "family prejudice" -> "natural family loyalty". Mr. Gardiner's view is still framed as his own ("what he saw as").
- **43.54** "nobler fall of ground" is dropped. This is minor scenic detail.
- **43.56** "they were told" -> "the gardener told them". The gardener is the obvious agent. "mischievously construed" -> "awkwardly interpreted" is weaker but not wrong. I left it.
- **43.65** "awkward tête-à-tête" repeats "awkward", which is harmless.
- **44.0** keeps Austen's own slip "the day after she arrived at Pemberley" (it should be Darcy's arrival). This is faithful, and no silent correction is needed.
- **44.10** keeps "Miss Bennet" for Elizabeth (she is the eldest present). This is correct.
- **44.15** "where their two selves only were concerned" -> "when the two of them were alone". A slight narrowing. The rest of this long paragraph is faithful.
- Ch 1 para 0 is outside this batch.

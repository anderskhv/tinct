## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone

**Word choices that are acceptable modernizations or clarifications:**
- 45.1: "saloon" to "drawing room"
- 45.3: "noticed only by a courtesy" to "curtsy" (period sense of "courtesy")
- 45.5: "her post" to "her duties as hostess"
- 46.2: "express" to "express messenger"
- 47.36: "my brother" to "brother-in-law"
- 48.29: "Miss Bennet" to "Jane"
- 48.30: "powdering gown" to "dressing gown"
- 48.34: "review" to "military review"
- 49.0: "shrubbery" to "garden"
- 49.52: "calico" to "cotton"

**Italic emphasis:** it is dropped throughout (45.8 "_your_ family", 47.65, 49.30, and others). This appears to be an edition convention, so I did not flag it paragraph by paragraph.

**Chapter 45:**
- 45.3: the dropped "awkward as such pauses must always be". It is a minor generalization.
- 45.4: "just as glad to be spared the effort". The sense is kept.
- 45.9: "probably added to his concern" slightly firms up "it is probable that it might add something". Acceptable.
- 45.10: "the very trick". Acceptable.
- 45.16: "'Her a beauty!'" for "_She_ a beauty!". Idiomatic, and the insult still lands.

**Chapter 46:**
- 46.4: "in the most tactful way possible" for "most creditable to his heart". It sits next to "With the kindest concern", which carries the sense.
- 46.20: "the belief that he was giving her up" for "self-conquest". An acceptable reading.
- 46.25: "formerly been glad to see ending". The source is hypothetical ("would formerly have rejoiced"), but the rendering is close enough.
- 46.28: "unable to function". Slightly strong for "incapable of exertion", but borderline.

**Chapter 47:**
- 47.7: "no brothers to defend her honor" and "military consequences". Slight glosses that keep the sense.
- 47.11: "insinuating" to "charming". Borderline, left alone.
- 47.73: the lost hedge "might not be impossible". Minor.

**Chapter 48:**
- 48.3: the irony of "everyone suddenly realized they had always mistrusted..." is preserved well.
- 48.12: Collins's "condescendingly" to "graciously". His admiring sense is kept.

**Chapter 49:**
- 49.26: "I would rather not" for "I dislike it very much". It echoes Jane's line and is acceptable.
- 49.40: "make her ashamed" for "make her miserable". A fair reading of the remorse sense.
- 49.48: "I always said he would manage everything". A small shift in Mrs. Bennet's words, left alone.
- 49.54: "felt it deeply" for "felt it so". A slight intensification, not flagged.

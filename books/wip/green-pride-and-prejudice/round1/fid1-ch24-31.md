# Fidelity review, round 1: Pride and Prejudice modern-en, chapters 24-31

## 1. Coverage
I read every paragraph of chapters 24-31 one at a time, source against candidate. That is 209 paragraphs: ch24 0-30 (31), ch25 0-19 (20), ch26 0-29 (30), ch27 0-22 (23), ch28 0-19 (20), ch29 0-41 (42), ch30 0-12 (13) and ch31 0-29 (30). I used the side-by-side file and read the neighbouring paragraphs for context. I then re-read each chapter's candidate as a whole for cross-boundary consistency. I checked the "condescension" wording against ch14 in candidate.json. 30.6/30.7 is a split sentence in the source, and the candidate splits it the same way.

## 2. Proposals (9, all blocking)
- 24.14 "men make sure that it should" -> "that they do". Attribution: the source's "they" means the women, not admiration.
- 24.22 "social standing" -> "pride". This restores Elizabeth's pointed thematic word.
- 26.29 "just like everyone else" -> "just as plain ones do". This restores the handsome/plain joke.
- 27.22 "first impressions" -> "first outpourings". "Effusions" are the accounts travellers pour out, not their private perceptions.
- 28.6 "all friendliness and graciousness" -> "...and gracious condescension". This restores Collins's catchphrase, keeping it consistent with ch14.
- 29.9 "rose graciously" -> "with great condescension, rose". The narrator is ironically borrowing Collins's word. This is also consistent with 29.0.
- 29.14 "about whom she knew the least" -> "whose connections she knew least about". This restores the social "connections".
- 30.2 "other parish appointments to be gained" -> "other church livings in the family's gift to be handed out". "Family livings" means the de Bourgh family controls these posts, which is the patronage irony.
- 31.11 "the performer's face" -> "the lovely performer's face". "Fair" is a signal of Darcy's admiration.

## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone
- 24.2, 24.25: the free indirect style and the pronoun references ("her" = Mrs. Bennet) are clear enough.
- 24.23 "By imagining such a scenario" (source: "By supposing such an affection"): vaguer than the source, but the meaning survives in context.
- 24.29 "recent events" drops "perverse". Minor. The ironic close ("always disliked Mr. Darcy before they had known anything") is intact.
- 25.7/25.8 "violently in love" -> "deeply in love" / "how deep": this loses some of the link to "incivility", but "deeply in love" is today's equally hackneyed phrase and the joke still works.
- 25.19 "Fitzwilliam Darcy" (dropped "Mr." and "formerly"): trivial.
- 26.6 dropped "immediate" (want of fortune): minor, and the sense holds.
- 26.20 "lost in the mail": a small concretization of "by some accident". Acceptable.
- 26.25 "natural and understandable" (source: "natural and amiable"): a small shift. Jane's charitable voice survives.
- 26.28 "financial security" (source: "independence"): acceptable. The irony about Elizabeth being "less clear-sighted" than with Charlotte is kept.
- 27.7 "money-motivated" against "mercenary" in 27.8: the echo is weakened, but the meaning is intact.
- 28.3 "fireplace screen" for "fender": slightly inaccurate furniture, not meaning-bearing.
- 29.1 adds italics to _dine_: harmless emphasis that the tea/dine contrast already implies.
- 29.40 "every hand he won" for "every fish" (counters): acceptable simplification.
- 30.3 "held no official position of authority" for "not in the commission of the peace": acceptable gloss. "magistrate" irony kept.
- 30.5 "Miss Lucas and Elizabeth": the source's referent is kept as is.
- 31.12 "in such state": slightly old-fashioned but readable in context.
- 31.14 "unflattering impression" for ironic "pretty notion": it makes the irony explicit, but that is acceptable because the tone of the speech remains playful.
- The voices hold throughout: Mrs. Bennet's grievances (25.3), Lady Catherine's interrogation (29.15-39, 31.5-9, 31.27), Collins's pomp (28.6, 29.1, 29.5), Sir William's court talk (29.2) and Mr. Bennet's irony (24.26-28).

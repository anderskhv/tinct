## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone

- **56.0.** Kept: "shrubbery" → "garden", "announced" for "formed", and the dropped detail that the servant rode ahead. These are small and harmless.
- **56.16.** Kept "wilderness" (meaning a planted wooded area) in Lady Catherine's speech. Explaining it would disturb her voice, and the copse in 56.19 makes it clear.
- **56.20.** Kept "she said to herself". The two walk in silence, so the added "to herself" is justified.
- **56.32.** Kept "blunt" for "frankness". It is still an ironic jab at the frankness Lady Catherine claims for herself.
- **56.42.** Kept "understanding" for "tacit engagement", which is an adequate equivalent.
- **56.60.** Kept "grounds of Pemberley" for "shades". The 61.12 callback ("pollution which its woods had received") still works.
- **56.67.** Kept "laughingstock" for "the contempt of the world".
- **56.74.** Kept "extraordinarily kind" for "prodigiously civil". It is borderline, but the effusive register survives.
- **57.2.** Kept "quietly broken", a small addition that does not change the meaning.
- **57.13.** Kept "helpful, gossiping Lucases" (the irony is kept), "I won't bore you" and "It's priceless!"
- **57.17.** Kept "graciousness" for "condescension" (Collins means it in the positive sense), "young addition to the family" for "olive-branch" and "prim" for "missish". All three are clear and faithful.
- **58.7.** "More than common awkwardness" was simplified to "all the awkwardness". The loss is minor.
- **58.15.** Kept "entirely without feeling" for "devoid of every proper feeling". It is close enough.
- **59.33.** Kept "perfectly good" for "perfectly amiable".
- **59.38.** Kept "I would have had to repay him" for "must and would have paid". The emphasis is weakened, but the meaning stands.
- **59.44.** Left "special license" unglossed. A gloss would clog Mrs. Bennet's effusion, and the context of a grand wedding carries it.
- **60.13.** Kept "outrageous" for "unjustifiable". It is slightly stronger, but still acceptable.
- **60.27.** Kept "county" for Austen's "country", a legitimate reading.
- **61.0.** The ironic opening and the "got rid of" line are well preserved.
- **61.3.** "The further disadvantage of Lydia's society" was simplified. The loss is minor.
- **61.11.** Kept "Elizabeth's example" for "instructions", which is minor.

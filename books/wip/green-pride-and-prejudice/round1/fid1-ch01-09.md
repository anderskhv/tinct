## 2. Proposals (17 total: 15 blocking, 2 obstruction-only)

- **3.3 (omission):** "could not accept their kind invitation" → "the honor of their invitation, and so on". This restores Austen's mocking "etc."
- **3.5 (irony):** "being considered the most forbidding, disagreeable man present" → "having a most forbidding, disagreeable face". His *countenance* turns ugly once his manners offend, and "a most" is not a superlative.
- **3.13 (omission / set-up and pay-off):** "passable" → "tolerable". Charlotte quotes "_tolerable_" back in 5.8.
- **4.4 (qualification):** "many a more foolish man" → "many a stupider person".
- **4.10 (qualification):** "designed to please anyone in particular" → "calculated to please people in general". The candidate had reversed the meaning.
- **4.10 (irony):** "They therefore felt entitled" → "They were therefore, in every respect, entitled". This keeps the narrator's mock-logic.
- **4.11 (qualification):** "freedom to enjoy a gentleman's life" → "sporting rights of a manor" (source: "liberty of a manor").
- **6.1 (qualification):** "courage" → "heart enough" (source: "heart enough to be really in love").
- **6.8 (obstruction, non-blocking):** adds "the card game" before Vingt-un.
- **6.9 (qualification):** "their share of misery" → "irritation" (source: "vexation"). This keeps Charlotte's understatement.
- **6.15 (tone):** "take the offensive first" → "begin by being impertinent myself". "Impertinence" is a key word for Elizabeth later in the novel.
- **6.35 (qualification):** "good company" → "superior society". Sir William is talking about rank.
- **7.0 (obstruction, non-blocking):** a short gloss on "entailed" ("legally bound to pass ... to").
- **8.0 (irony):** restores "the enjoyment of all her original dislike".
- **8.42 (qualification):** removes the added hedge "seem to be" → "are". Bingley asserts it outright, and Caroline's retort depends on that.
- **9.28 (omission):** "the baking" → "the mince pies".
- **9.35 (tone):** "well-developed" → "well-grown". The modern word adds a sexualized implication.

## 3. Considered and deliberately left alone

- **1.0:** "in need of a wife" is the lead's repair and it is fine.
- **1.9:** "Michaelmas" → "the end of September" is an accurate, unobtrusive clarification.
- **1.23:** "establishment" → "match". This loses some of the economic sense, but in Mrs. Bennet's mouth it reads correctly.
- **1.28:** "with consideration" → "with great feeling" keeps Mr. Bennet's irony.
- **1.33:** "fancied herself nervous" → "convinced herself she was ill". This loses the word "nerves", but the nerves running joke is already carried in 1.27-1.28 and 2.7. "News" → "gossip" is acceptable.
- **2.6:** "deigned not" → "did not bother". **2.22:** "acquaintance" → "opportunity". Both are minor and not meaning-changing enough to act on.
- **3.6:** "Everyone agreed: he was the proudest…" correctly keeps this as communal opinion (free indirect speech). Good.
- **3.13:** "give consequence to" → "pay attention to" is close enough.
- **3.15:** "his wife's views" → "fantasies" is slightly pejorative, but defensible as Mr. Bennet's slant.
- **3.16:** the "_Boulanger_" (the name of a dance) → "the final dance". This is an acceptable simplification.
- **4.12:** "by no means unwilling" → "perfectly willing". The litotes is softened, but the meaning survives.
- **5.19:** "prided herself" for "piqued herself" is good.
- **6.0:** modern "condescension" (= superciliousness) is correct here. Watch that it doesn't collide with Collins's period usage later.
- **6.17 / 6.20:** "teasing" → "pressuring" / "put on the spot" loses the verbal echo, but both lines still work.
- **6.24:** "indignation" → "disapproval" and **6.48** "desired" → "demanded" are small shifts in degree that I judged not defects.
- **7.2:** the joke of paying duty to "the aunt and a milliner's shop" survives as "their aunt and a hat shop".
- **7.12:** "Clarke's library" → "bookshop" is technically a lending library. It is minor, so I left it.
- **7.38:** the added italic on _Jane_ is harmless.
- **8.36-37:** "realistic possibilities" makes the joke explicit, but only lightly.
- **8.44-45:** "net purses" → "knit" is acceptable.
- **9.18:** "dismissive mention" is licensed by "offended by his manner".
- **9.24:** "twenty-four different families" is fine.
- **9.35:** dropping "to this sudden attack" is minor.
- **9.37:** Lydia's backshifted indirect speech was converted to direct present. That is fine.

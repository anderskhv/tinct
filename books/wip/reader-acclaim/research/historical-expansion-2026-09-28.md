# Historical readers: expansion of the Tinct classics research

Research date: 28 September 2026. This is an additive research pass, not an exhaustive historical search across all 99 titles. Original modern-influencer evidence remains unchanged.

Use distinct labels: praised/recommended; documented reading; influence/adaptation; ancient testimony; secondary evidence awaiting original verification. A historical connection is not an endorsement of Tinct or its edition. Quoted English translations require edition attribution.

## Caesar and Aristotle: boundaries

Julius Caesar: Suetonius, Julius 30, citing Cicero, says Caesar habitually quoted Euripides' Phoenician Women. This is outside the catalogue and cannot become a recommendation for Medea or The Bacchae. Suetonius 56 mentions Caesar's own youthful Oedipus tragedy; it is not evidence of endorsing Sophocles' Oedipus Rex. Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Julius%2A.html

Alexander was taught by Aristotle, but that does not establish reading or recommending the catalogue's Politics, Nicomachean Ethics or Poetics. The strongest title-specific match is the Iliad through Plutarch. Plutarch also records books by Euripides, Sophocles and Aeschylus being sent to him, without identifying the individual plays.

- MLK on The Republic: “I feel that it brings together more of the insights of history than any other book.” (1965 interview; source above).
- Darwin on Paradise Lost: “Formerly Milton’s Paradise Lost had been my chief favourite” (autobiography; source above).
- Keller on the Iliad: “It was the Iliad that made Greece my paradise.” (autobiography; source above).
- Rilke on Niels Lyhne: “the more one reads it the more everything seems to be contained within” (Poetry in Translation version of 23 April 1903 letter).
- Emerson on Montaigne: “I remember the delight and wonder in which I lived with it.” (essay; source above).
- Nietzsche on Montaigne: “The joy of living on this earth is increased by the existence of such a man.” (Schopenhauer as Educator; source above).
- Freud on The Brothers Karamazov: “the most magnificent novel ever written” (secondary quotation pending original-edition check).

## Why read old books?

- C. S. Lewis: “We all, therefore, need the books that will correct the characteristic mistakes of our own period.” Introduction to Athanasius, 1944; reproduced at https://thecslewis-studygroup.org/the-c-s-lewis-study-group/study-resources/bookshelf/c-s-lewis-on-the-reading-of-old-books/
- Arthur Schopenhauer: “There is nothing that so greatly recreates the mind as the works of the old classic writers.” On Reading and Books: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/11945/11945-h/11945-h.htm
- Henry David Thoreau: “For what are the classics but the noblest recorded thoughts of man?” Walden, Reading: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/205/205-h/205-h.htm

# Tech readers and Tinct’s 99 classics
Research date: 26 September 2026

This is a source-checked research pass for a feature that pairs classic books with short comments from technology founders, investors and adjacent thinkers. It covers the 99 Modern English titles in the local catalogue; the Bible is a separate catalogue route and is excluded.

The evidence register contains **91 records involving 30 people and 42 individual catalogue titles**, plus two collection-level selections. These are **not 91 recommendations**. Of them, **31 records across 22 books** are explicit recommendations, positive ratings or recommended-list selections supported by a primary source or the authorized Naval compilation. The rest include secondary-source endorsements, author-wide praise, reading plans, ownership, interpretation and leads.

The research combined two title-by-title discovery searches for all 99 books with person-specific searches, personal reading lists, essays, interviews, transcripts and original-source follow-ups. Search results are discovery aids, not evidence of endorsement. No-result entries mean no eligible evidence was located in this pass, not that no recommendation exists.

**Why read old books?**

These are the strongest short excerpts for the people requested. “Old” does not always mean public-domain: Collison’s argument, for example, also covers books only a decade old.

| Naval Ravikant | “Any book that survived for two thousand years has been filtered through many people.” | For enduring human problems, long survival is a useful filter. This is stronger evidence than treating every title on his planned reading list as a reviewed recommendation.

These should not receive verified-quote status until their original sources are recovered.

**Patrick Collison: what the deeper search adds**

Within the actual 99, the strongest results are *Democracy in America* and *Anna Karenina*. His 2024 list also establishes reading *Jane Eyre* and *Heart of Darkness*. His personal shelf contains *War and Peace*, *The Wealth of Nations*, Montaigne, *Republic*, *The Odyssey*, Augustine’s *Confessions* and *Ulysses*, but they have no affirmative color rating, and he says he has read only about half the shelf. Those are ownership records, not recommendations. [Bookshelf](https://patrickcollison.com/bookshelf) · [2024 reading report](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2024/12/patrick-collison-on-classic-novels.html).

There is more classic material outside the current catalogue: *Middlemarch* and *Bleak House* are among his explicit standouts; *The Count of Monte Cristo*, *Dracula* and Seneca’s *On the Shortness of Life* receive positive shelf ratings. These should be considered separately if the catalogue expands, not counted among the 99.

**Sam, Deutsch and Karpathy: useful limits**

Sam’s *Meditations* and *Republic* evidence is promising, but remains secondary in this pass. His *Republic* selection is preserved in a [contemporary Hacker News transcription](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=10423017) of a video answer. His own participation in that thread confirms a correction to a different title: the recommended *Art of War* was Charles Oman’s medieval history, **not Sun Tzu**. His *Anna Karenina* and *Pride and Prejudice* appearances are bookshelf evidence only.

Deutsch’s *Frankenstein* observation can work in a feature explicitly labelled “On this book”: “a sort of allegory of what can happen with a parent who doesn’t respect their creation.” It should not be labelled a reading recommendation. [Primary interview](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/david-deutsch/).

Karpathy remains a worthwhile person to follow for this theme, but the discovered *Dracula* example is outside the 99 and demonstrates a reading tool. I found no defensible classic recommendation to attach to a current catalogue card. His large indexed reading list includes intentions and mixed reviews; its size is not evidence of 349 endorsements. [Reader3](https://github.com/karpathy/reader3) · [Personal reviews](https://karpathy.ai/books.html).

**How to use this research**

Use “Recommended by,” “Rated highly by,” “Read by” and “Discussed by” as distinct labels. Author-wide praise needs an author label; collection selections need a collection label. The supplied quote is about the original work or the edition the person read, not an endorsement of Tinct or its Modern English text.

Start with primary-source cards for DHH/*Notes from Underground*, Naval/*Meditations* and *Wealth of Nations*, O’Reilly/*Pride and Prejudice*, Mullenweg/Thucydides, Collison/*Democracy in America*, Andreessen/Nietzsche and Holiday’s specific selections. Then upgrade the attractive secondary candidates—DHH/*Manual*, Tobi/*Meditations*, Collison/*Anna Karenina*, Sam and Musk—through original-post or audio checks.

The companion **catalogue-matrix.md** has one row for every one of the 99 titles, with evidence IDs. **evidence.json** preserves source links, dates where established, recommendation types, qualifications and outstanding checks. **search-log.json** and **search-log-targeted.json** are internal discovery records, not publication-ready quote sources.

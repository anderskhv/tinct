# Medea: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Medea · central

The woman from Colchis, wife of Jason and mother of their two sons.

## Jason · major

The Greek hero of the Argo’s quest, husband of Medea and father of their sons.

## Creon · major

King of Corinth and father of Jason’s new bride.

## Creon’s daughter · supporting

The princess of Corinth, Jason’s new bride. She is not named in this text.

## The nurse · supporting

Medea’s elderly servant and confidante.

## The children’s attendant · supporting

The elderly servant who looks after Medea and Jason’s sons.

## Medea and Jason’s sons · major

The two young sons of Medea and Jason. Their personal names are not given.

## The first child · supporting

One of Medea and Jason’s two sons, distinguished by his speaker label.

## The other child · supporting

The other of Medea and Jason’s two sons, distinguished by his speaker label.

## The women of Corinth · major

The local women who speak and sing as the chorus.

## The chorus leader · supporting

The spokeswoman for the women of Corinth.

## A woman of the chorus · supporting

An individual woman speaking from the chorus.

## Another woman of the chorus · supporting

A second woman speaking from the chorus.

## Aegeus · supporting

King of Athens and an old friend of Medea.

## The messenger · supporting

A servant from the royal household who brings news to Medea.

## Pelias · reference

The king of Iolcus who sent Jason in search of the Golden Fleece.

## The daughters of Pelias · reference

The daughters of King Pelias, also called the Peliads.

## Pittheus · reference

The king of Troezen, son of Pelops, whom Aegeus plans to consult.

## Pelops · reference

Pittheus’s father, a king of Greek legend.

## Pandion · reference

Aegeus’s father in the Athenian royal line.

## Erechtheus · reference

A legendary king and ancestor associated with Athens.

## Zeus · reference

The chief god of the Greek pantheon.

## Themis · reference

The goddess associated with divine order, justice and sacred obligations.

## Hecate · reference

The goddess of magic whom Medea invokes as her special protector.

## Aphrodite · reference

The goddess of love, here called the Cyprian.

## Orpheus · reference

The legendary Greek singer and musician.

## Apollo · reference

The god whose oracle Aegeus consults at Delphi, also called Phoebus.

## Maia · reference

The mother of Hermes in Greek mythology.

## Hermes · reference

The divine messenger and protector of travelers, son of Maia.

## The Muses · reference

The nine goddesses of poetry, music and the arts.

## Ino · reference

A woman of Greek legend whom the chorus recalls as a mother driven mad by Hera.

## Hera · reference

The queen of the gods and goddess of marriage.

## Skylla · reference

The sea monster of Greek legend, also called Scylla.

## Athena · reference

The goddess of wisdom and protector of Athens, here called Pallas.

## Pan · reference

The rustic god whose influence was associated with sudden terror and frenzy.

## Helios · reference

The sun god, Medea’s divine grandfather.

## The old handmaid · reference

The elderly woman among the princess’s attendants in the messenger’s account.

# The Odyssey character package

Both full English editions, all twenty-four books; 356 entries in the original
and 354 in the modern, 3253 and 3243 exact mentions. Content revision
2026-09-11.1. Authoring-agent review, not independent editorial approval.
Validated content, awaiting runtime integration.

Odysseus and Telemachus are Central. Penelope, Athena, Zeus, Poseidon, Laertes,
Euryclea, Eumaeus, Philoetius, Melanthius, Antinous, Eurymachus, Amphinomus,
Nestor, Menelaus, Helen, Alcinous, Arete, Nausicaa, Circe, Calypso, Polyphemus,
Agamemnon, Teiresias, Theoclymenus and Eurylochus are Major. The rest of the
acting cast is Supporting; genealogy, myth and the figures named only in a
simile or a citation are Reference.

## The Roman / Greek divergence

This is the largest edition divergence in the library. Butler's original uses
the Roman names throughout and the modern edition restores the Greek:

| Jove (185) | Zeus (188) | Hercules | Heracles |

Plus about twenty transliteration pairs (Alcmena/Alcmene, Lampetie/Lampetia,
Oicleus/Oecleus, Idothea/Eidothea, Anticlea/Anticleia, Cnossus/Knossos,
Oedipodes/Oedipus, Ilithuia/Eileithyia, Lapithae/Lapiths, Pelasgi/Pelasgians,
Ceteians/Ceteans, Pleiads/Pleiades, Acroneos/Acroneus, Laerceus/Laerces,
Ctesius/Ctesias, Leiocritus/Leocritus, Arceisius/Arcesius, Aetes/Aeetes,
Paeeon/Paeon, Tyndarus/Tyndareus, Gerene/Gerenia, Telepylos/Telepylus).

Every pair is carried as two aliases on one entity, so one card serves both
editions and no edition-specific code is needed for names. The editions were
compared paragraph by paragraph for every name before the namesake tables below
were written; apart from the substitutions recorded here, the two texts place
every name in the same chapter and paragraph.

The modern edition also replaces the original's Achaeans, Argives and Danaans
with Greeks, and Lacedaemon with Sparta. Achaeans, Argives, Danaans and Greeks
are one group entity.

## Editorial checks

**1. Namesakes.** Thirteen names belong to more than one person or thing. Each
is resolved by paragraph, from the text itself:

| Name | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Ajax | The three at 4:41 are the leader wrecked on the rocks of Gyrae; the other six are Telamon's son, who lost the contest for Achilles' armour |
| Polybus | Four men: Eurymachus's father (six places), the rich Egyptian of Thebes (4:10), the Phaeacian who made the ball (8:31), the suitor son of Polyctor (22:25, 22:28) |
| Amphion | Zeus and Antiope's son, who founded Thebes (11:19); Iasus's son, king of Orchomenus and Chloris's father (11:22) |
| Antiphates | The Laestrygonian king (book 10); Melampus's son and Oicleus's father (15:19) |
| Antiphus | Aegyptius's son, killed in the Cyclops's cave (2:1); the living elder of Ithaca who sits with Mentor and Halitherses (17:6). The text kills the first before the second appears |
| Castor | Leda and Tyndareus's son (11:23); the Cretan father Odysseus invents for himself, son of Hylax (14:11) |
| Anchialus | Mentes's father, king of the Taphians (1:13); a young Phaeacian competitor (8:7) |
| Pisenor | Ops's father and Euryclea's grandfather (1:31, 2:24); the herald of the Ithacan assembly (2:3) |
| Iasus | Father of Amphion of Orchomenus (11:22); father of Dmetor of Cyprus (17:42) |
| Argos | The place, sixteen times; Odysseus's old dog, twice (17:26, 17:29). Only the dog is cast |
| Cyclops | The singular always means Polyphemus; the plural is the people |
| Eurynome | Penelope's housekeeper — except at 7:0 in the modern edition only, where it is the Phaeacian nurse (see defects) |
| Halios / Halius | One son of Alcinous, spelled both ways in **both** editions (8:7 and 8:31) |

**Left unbound, deliberately.** The Polyctor named at 17:17 as one of the three
who built the town fountain is not bound. The suitors' father Polyctor is bound
at 18:27 and 22:25. The text does not say whether they are the same man, and
this package does not decide it in either direction.

**2. Person or not.** Places, rivers, mountains, islands, ships, stars and the
adjectival forms of place names are excluded: Argos, Ithaca, Troy, Pylos,
Sparta, Crete, Olympus, Scheria, Aeaea, Ogygia, Thrinacia, the Argo, the
Pleiades, Boötes, the river Aegyptus (which is the Nile, not the Ithacan
Aegyptius). Enipeus and Alpheus are rivers spoken of as persons and are
included as personifications, on the text's own terms. Lampus and Phaethon are
Dawn's two horses, and are marked as animals; so is Argos the dog.

**3. Scriptural and mythological references.** The genealogies in book 11 (Tyro,
Antiope, Alcmena, Epicaste, Chloris, Leda, Iphimedeia) and the seer's line in
book 15 (Melampus through Theoclymenus) are covered figure by figure. Each card
says who the person is, not what the whole myth contains. Erinyes/Furies,
Graces, Naiads, the Gorgon and the Muse are entities; Gaia is named as Tityus's
mother in the original and replaced by "Earth" in the modern.

**4. Ambiguous references.** Odysseus's three assumed names are their own
entries, each stating plainly that it is a name he gives: **Noman** to the
Cyclops (Nobody in the modern), **Aethon** to Penelope, **Eperitus** to Laertes.
The lowercase "nobody" in Polyphemus's shout at 9:27 is the pun, not the name,
and is not bound. Mentes and Mentor are bound to the men whose names they are;
Athena takes both shapes, and each card says so, so the reader who taps either
name learns both facts without the package having to guess which of the two is
speaking in the borderline places (4:55, 24:29).

**5. Spot-read.** Twelve mentions were drawn at random from each edition and
read in context; all twenty-four bound the right person.

**6. Both editions independently.** Two entities are absent from the modern
edition and are recorded as `omittedEntities` for it: **Gaia** (7:25, "son of
Earth") and **the Sicels** (20:33, "to Sicily"). Nothing else is missing from
either side.

## Source defects, recorded not repaired

| Defect | Effect |
|---|---|
| `modern-en` names Nausicaa's old nurse **Eurynome** of Apeira at 7:0, where the original has **Eurymedusa**; Eurynome is also Penelope's housekeeper | Bound to the Phaeacian nurse at that one paragraph by an explicit rule in the builder. Without it the modern edition would show Penelope's housekeeper on Scheria |
| Both editions spell Alcinous's son **Halios** at 8:7 and **Halius** at 8:31 | Cosmetic; both spellings are aliases of one entity |
| `original-en` spells the suitor **Leiocritus** at 2:14 and **Leocritus** at 22:29; the modern normalises to Leocritus | Both are aliases of one entity |
| `modern-en` spells the nurse **Eurycleia** sixteen times and **Euryclea** seventeen times; the prophet **Tiresias** nine times and **Teiresias** seven | Both spellings are aliases; counts match the original's totals exactly |
| `modern-en` replaces Achaeans, Argives and Danaans with Greeks, and Lacedaemon with Sparta | Not a defect; one group entity, and Lacedaemon/Sparta is a place and not cast |

None of these blocks safe enablement. No edition file was changed. Any repair to
these source bytes invalidates the recorded fingerprints and requires a
restored-text review and a rebuild.

## Snapshots

Four later identity clarifications, each released at the end of the paragraph
that establishes it. None anticipates an outcome: Telemachus after 16:22 (his
father has revealed himself), Eumaeus after 15:33 (he was born a king's son on
Syra), Irus after 18:10, Theoclymenus after 20:30.

## Validation

`python3 books/characters/build_odyssey.py --check`, then
`python3 -m unittest discover -s books/characters -p 'test_*.py'`. Thirteen
focused tests for this book; full suite 447 tests. Compiler assembly in
`build_reviewed.py` is shared and deterministic; all identity prose and every
contextual rule is manually authored. No edition changes, no network generation.

## Integration

Compare both fingerprints against the shipping checkout, register both English

Athena under both name systems, the two Ajaxes at 4:41 versus 11:44, all four
men named Polybus, Argos the dog at 17:26 with no card on Argos the place,
Noman in the original against Nobody in the modern at 9:23–9:32, and the
Phaeacian nurse at 7:0 in the modern edition.

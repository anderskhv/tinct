# The Odyssey: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Odysseus · central

King of Ithaca, husband of Penelope and father of Telemachus. He sacked Troy and has been twenty years getting home. Butler’s original calls him by his Roman name, Ulysses.

## Telemachus · central

Odysseus’s son, a young man who has grown up without ever meeting his father.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 23: Odysseus’s son. He has now met his father, who revealed himself to him at the swineherd’s hut.

## Penelope · major

Odysseus’s wife, holding his household together against the suitors who want her to remarry.

## Laertes · major

Odysseus’s father, the old king of Ithaca, who has withdrawn to his farm in the country.

## Anticlea · supporting

Odysseus’s mother, daughter of Autolycus.

## Euryclea · major

The old nurse of Odysseus’s house, bought by Laertes; she nursed Odysseus and then Telemachus. The modern edition spells her name both Euryclea and Eurycleia.

## Eumaeus · major

Odysseus’s swineherd, who keeps the pig farm in the hills and is loyal to the absent master.

After source chapter 15, paragraph 34: Odysseus’s swineherd, loyal to the absent master. He was born a king’s son on the island of Syra, the child of Ctesius son of Ormenus.

## Philoetius · major

Odysseus’s stockman, in charge of the cattle on the mainland.

## Melanthius · major

Odysseus’s goatherd, son of Dolius, who has taken the suitors’ side.

## Eurynome · supporting

Penelope’s head waiting woman and housekeeper.

## Dolius · supporting

The old gardener given to Penelope by her father when she married; father of Melanthius and Melantho.

## Melantho · supporting

A maid of the house, daughter of Dolius, brought up by Penelope; she sides with the suitors.

## Medon · supporting

A servant and herald of Odysseus’s house who waits on the suitors at table.

## Phemius · supporting

The bard of Ithaca, son of Terpes, forced to sing for the suitors.

## The maid who keeps the bedroom door · supporting

Penelope’s maidservant, daughter of Actor, the only servant who knows the secret of the marriage bed.

## Argos · supporting

Odysseus’s hunting dog, bred by him before he left for Troy and now old and neglected.

## Ctimene · supporting

Odysseus’s younger sister, brought up alongside Eumaeus and married away to Same.

## Antinous · major

Son of Eupeithes and the most aggressive of the suitors courting Penelope.

## Eurymachus · major

Son of Polybus and, with Antinous, a leader among the suitors.

## Amphinomus · major

A suitor from Dulichium, son of Nisus; the most decent-spoken of them, and Penelope’s favourite among them.

## Agelaus · supporting

A suitor, son of Damastor.

## Ctesippus · supporting

A suitor from Same, a coarse-mannered rich man’s son.

## Leiodes · supporting

A suitor, son of Oenops, who acts as sacrificial priest to the others.

## Eurynomus · supporting

A suitor, one of the three surviving sons of the old Ithacan Aegyptius.

## Amphimedon · supporting

A suitor of Ithaca, son of Melaneus, who once hosted Agamemnon.

## Demoptolemus · supporting

A suitor who fights in the front rank at the end.

## Pisander · supporting

A suitor, son of Polyctor.

## Polybus · supporting

A suitor, son of Polyctor.

## Polybus · reference

Father of the suitor Eurymachus.

## Eurydamas · supporting

A suitor.

## Leocritus · supporting

A suitor, son of Evenor, who breaks up the Ithacan assembly. Butler’s original spells him Leiocritus at his first appearance.

## Euryades · supporting

A suitor killed in the hall.

## Elatus · supporting

A suitor killed in the hall.

## Mulius · supporting

A servant from Dulichium who waits on Amphinomus.

## Autonoe · supporting

One of Penelope’s waiting women.

## Hippodamia · supporting

One of Penelope’s waiting women.

## Irus · supporting

The town beggar of Ithaca, a big idle man whose real name is Arnaeus; the young men call him Irus because he runs their errands.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 11: The town beggar of Ithaca, really named Arnaeus, who challenged the stranger at the door and was beaten.

## Mentor · supporting

An old friend of Odysseus, left in charge of the household when he sailed for Troy. Athena repeatedly takes his shape and voice.

## Mentes · supporting

Chief of the Taphians and an old guest-friend of Odysseus’s house, son of Anchialus; Athena comes to Ithaca in his shape.

## Halitherses · supporting

The best reader of omens in Ithaca, son of Mastor, and an old friend of Odysseus’s house.

## Aegyptius · supporting

A very old Ithacan who speaks first at the assembly; father of Antiphus, Eurynomus and two sons who work his land.

## Antiphus · 

Son of the old Ithacan Aegyptius; he sailed with Odysseus and was killed by the Cyclops.

## Antiphus · 

An elder of Ithaca, one of the old friends of Odysseus’s house with whom Telemachus sits in the assembly.

## Eupeithes · supporting

Father of Antinous; Odysseus once saved him from an Ithacan mob.

## Noemon · supporting

An Ithacan, son of Phronius, who lends Telemachus a ship.

## Phronius · reference

Father of Noemon.

## Piraeus · supporting

Telemachus’s friend, son of Clytius, who keeps his guest and his gifts while the house is unsafe.

## Clytius · reference

Father of Piraeus.

## Theoclymenus · major

A seer of the house of Melampus, in exile from Argos for killing a man; Telemachus brings him home to Ithaca.

After source chapter 20, paragraph 31: The seer Telemachus brought home from Pylos, of the house of Melampus; he reads the darkness that falls over the suitors at their meal.

Penelope’s father.

Penelope’s sister, daughter of Icarius, married to Eumelus of Pherae; Athena sends a phantom in her likeness.

Iphthime’s husband, who lives at Pherae.

## Arceisius · reference

Odysseus’s grandfather, father of Laertes.

## Autolycus · supporting

Odysseus’s maternal grandfather, famous as the most accomplished thief and perjurer alive; he named the boy and gave him the boar-hunt scar.

## Amphithea · reference

Odysseus’s grandmother, wife of Autolycus.

## Icmalius · reference

The craftsman who made Penelope’s inlaid chair.

One of the three men said to have built the town fountain of Ithaca.

## Neritus · reference

One of the three men said to have built the town fountain of Ithaca.

## The Sicel woman · supporting

The old servant who looks after Laertes on his country farm. The modern edition calls her Sicilian.

## Athena · major

Daughter of Zeus, goddess of war and cunning, and Odysseus’s protector throughout. Butler’s original calls her Minerva.

## Zeus · major

King of the gods and father of Athena. Butler’s original calls him Jove.

## Poseidon · major

God of the sea and the earthquake, and the one god set against Odysseus, because Odysseus blinded his son Polyphemus. Butler’s original calls him Neptune.

## Hermes · supporting

The messenger god, guide of travellers and of the dead. Butler’s original calls him Mercury.

## Apollo · supporting

God of archery, prophecy and sudden death.

## Artemis · supporting

Goddess of the hunt and Apollo’s sister. Butler’s original calls her Diana.

## Aphrodite · supporting

Goddess of love and beauty. Butler’s original calls her Venus.

## Ares · supporting

God of war. Butler’s original calls him Mars.

## Hephaestus · supporting

The smith of the gods, lame and famously skilful, husband of Aphrodite. Butler’s original calls him Vulcan.

## Hera · reference

Zeus’s wife, queen of the gods. Butler’s original calls her Juno.

## Demeter · reference

Goddess of the harvest. Butler’s original calls her Ceres.

## Persephone · supporting

Queen of the dead, who rules in Hades. Butler’s original calls her Proserpine.

## Hades · supporting

God of the underworld; the name also stands for his house, where the dead go.

## Helios · supporting

The sun god, whose cattle graze on Thrinacia. Butler’s original calls him Hyperion.

## Cronus · reference

Zeus’s father. Butler’s original calls him Saturn.

## Leto · reference

Mother of Apollo and Artemis by Zeus.

## Dawn · supporting

The goddess of daybreak, who rises each morning from beside Tithonus. Butler’s original also names her Aurora.

## Oceanus · reference

The great river encircling the earth, spoken of as a god and as the father of nymphs.

## Amphitrite · reference

Sea goddess; the waves are called hers.

## Proteus · supporting

The old man of the sea, a shape-shifting immortal who tells the truth only under compulsion.

## Idothea · supporting

Daughter of Proteus, who takes pity on Menelaus in Egypt and teaches him how to trap her father.

## Ino · supporting

A mortal woman, daughter of Cadmus, since raised to godhood as Leucothea; she gives Odysseus her veil in the storm.

## Calypso · major

The goddess on Ogygia who kept Odysseus seven years and wanted to marry him.

## Circe · major

The goddess and enchantress of Aeaea, daughter of the sun and sister of Aeetes.

## Themis · reference

Goddess invoked as the beginning and end of councils.

## Hebe · reference

Daughter of Zeus and Hera, married to Heracles among the gods.

## Ilithuia · reference

Goddess of childbirth, who has a cave at Amnisus in Crete.

## Paeeon · reference

The healer of the gods; the Egyptian physicians are called his race.

## Bacchus · reference

God of wine, who gave the golden urn that holds Achilles’ bones and who denounced Ariadne.

## Atlas · reference

Calypso’s father, who knows the depths of the sea and holds the pillars of heaven apart.

## Thoosa · reference

A sea nymph, daughter of Phorcys and mother of Polyphemus by Poseidon.

## Phorcys · reference

A sea god, father of Thoosa; a harbour of Ithaca bears his name.

## Crataiis · reference

Scylla’s mother, whom Odysseus is told to call on as he passes.

## Halosydne · reference

A sea goddess; the seals are called her flock.

## Perse · reference

## Neaera · reference

A nymph, mother by Helios of Phaethusa and Lampetie.

A goddess, daughter of Helios, who herds the sun’s cattle on Thrinacia with her sister.

A goddess, daughter of Helios, who herds the sun’s cattle on Thrinacia and reports their slaughter to her father.

Hermes’s mother.

The Centaur whom wine inflamed at Peirithous’s house.

## Maera · reference

One of the women’s ghosts Odysseus names.

## Clymene · reference

One of the women’s ghosts Odysseus names.

## Eriphyle · reference

A ghost Odysseus names, the woman who sold her own husband for gold.

## Tityus · reference

A giant son of Gaia, punished in Hades for assaulting Leto.

## Tantalus · reference

A shade punished in Hades with water and fruit that withdraw whenever he reaches.

## Sisyphus · reference

A shade punished in Hades by rolling a stone that always runs back down.

## Heracles · reference

The greatest of the strong men, whose phantom Odysseus meets in Hades while he himself feasts among the gods. Butler’s original calls him Hercules.

## The Gorgon · reference

The monstrous head Odysseus fears Persephone will send up out of Hades.

## Rhadamanthus · reference

The fair-haired ruler of the Elysian plain.

## Orion · reference

The hunter loved by Dawn and killed by Artemis in Ortygia; he hunts still among the dead.

## Iasion · reference

The man Demeter loved and Zeus struck down.

## Tithonus · reference

Dawn’s consort, from whose side she rises each morning.

## Cadmus · reference

Father of Ino, who became the goddess Leucothea.

## Erechtheus · reference

The king of Athens to whose house Athena goes.

## Lampus · reference

One of the two horses that draw Dawn’s chariot.

## Phaethon · reference

One of the two horses that draw Dawn’s chariot.

## Actor · reference

Father of the maidservant who keeps Penelope’s bedroom door.

## Melampus · reference

The seer and rich man of Pylos, driven out by Neleus, from whom Theoclymenus is descended.

## Phylacus · reference

The king in whose house Melampus was held prisoner for a year.

## Antiphates · 

A son of Melampus, father of Oicleus.

## Mantius · reference

A son of Melampus, father of Polypheides and Cleitus.

## Oicleus · reference

Son of Antiphates and father of the seer Amphiaraus.

## Amphiaraus · reference

A seer dearly loved by Zeus and Apollo, killed at Thebes through a woman’s bribe.

## Alcmaeon · reference

A son of Amphiaraus.

## Amphilochus · reference

A son of Amphiaraus.

## Polypheides · reference

Son of Mantius and the greatest seer alive after Amphiaraus; Theoclymenus’s father.

## Cleitus · reference

Son of Mantius, carried off by Dawn for his beauty.

## Castor · 

The rich and honoured Cretan whom Odysseus, in his false story, claims as his father.

## Hylax · reference

Named in Odysseus’s false Cretan story as his supposed grandfather.

## Aethon · supporting

The name Odysseus gives Penelope when he tells her he is a Cretan and Idomeneus’s younger brother.

## Deucalion · reference

Named in Odysseus’s false story as Minos’s son and the father of Idomeneus.

## Eperitus · supporting

The name Odysseus gives Laertes before telling him the truth.

## Apheidas · reference

A king named in Odysseus’s last false story as his supposed father.

## Polypemon · reference

Named in Odysseus’s last false story as his supposed grandfather.

## Pheidon · reference

King of the Thesprotians, who in Odysseus’s tale entertained him and had news of the real Odysseus.

## Acastus · reference

King of Dulichium, to whom the Thesprotian crew were told to take the beggar.

## Thoas · reference

Son of Andraemon, who in Odysseus’s campfire story ran to the ships and left his cloak behind.

## Andraemon · reference

Father of Thoas.

## Orsilochus · reference

Idomeneus’s son, whom the beggar claims in his story to have killed in Crete.

## Dmetor · reference

A great man of Cyprus, son of Iasus, to whom the beggar says he was given.

## Iasus · 

Father of Dmetor of Cyprus.

## Arybas · reference

A rich man of Sidon, father of the nurse who carried Eumaeus away.

## Ctesius · reference

Eumaeus’s father, son of Ormenus, king of one of the two communities of Syra.

## Ormenus · reference

Eumaeus’s grandfather.

## Echetus · reference

A king on the mainland held up as a threat, who mutilates everyone brought to him.

## Iphitus · reference

Son of Eurytus, who gave Odysseus the great bow in Messene and was later killed by Heracles.

## Eurytus · reference

The archer of Oechalia who would shoot against the gods; Iphitus’s father.

## Anchialus · 

Father of Mentes, king of the Taphians.

## Alcimus · reference

Named as Mentor’s father.

## Alpheus · reference

A river spoken of as an ancestor, grandfather of Diocles.

## The Greeks · supporting

The army that fought at Troy. The original calls them Achaeans, Argives and Danaans where the modern edition says Greeks.

## The Trojans · reference

The people of Troy, against whom the Greeks fought for nine years.

## The Phaeacians · supporting

The seafaring people of Scheria, near kin to the gods, who ferry strangers home.

## The Cyclopes · supporting

A lawless people who neither plant nor plough and have no assemblies or laws. The singular Cyclops in this book always means Polyphemus.

## The Laestrygonians · supporting

The giant people of Telepylus who wreck Odysseus’s fleet.

## The Cicons · supporting

The people of Ismarus, the first Odysseus raided after Troy. The modern edition calls them Cicones.

## The Thesprotians · reference

The mainland people whose king Pheidon figures in the beggar’s story.

## The Phoenicians · reference

Seafaring traders who appear in both the beggar’s tales and Eumaeus’s.

## The Sidonians · reference

The people of Sidon.

## The Egyptians · reference

The people of Egypt, skilled in medicine.

## The Ethiopians · reference

The people at the world’s end, split into two halves, where Poseidon goes to be feasted.

## The Taphians · reference

A seafaring and pirating people; Mentes is their chief.

## The Myrmidons · reference

Achilles’ people, ruled after him by Neoptolemus and by old Peleus.

## The Cephallenians · reference

The people over whom Odysseus and Laertes ruled.

## The Epeans · reference

The people who rule in Elis.

## The Cretans · reference

The people of Crete, among whom the beggar claims his family stood high.

## The Pylians · reference

Nestor’s people.

## The Lapithae · reference

The people among whom the Centaur Eurytion grew violent with wine. The modern edition calls them the Lapiths.

## The Cimmerians · reference

The people who live wrapped in mist and darkness at the edge of Oceanus.

## The Sintians · reference

The people of Lemnos, whose speech Hephaestus calls barbarous.

## The Solymi · reference

A people whose mountains Poseidon passes on his way back from Ethiopia.

## The Erembians · reference

One of the peoples Menelaus visited in his wanderings.

## The Messenians · reference

The people who carried off three hundred Ithacan sheep.

## The Eteocretans · reference

One of the peoples of Crete.

## The Dorians · reference

One of the peoples of Crete, of threefold race.

## The Pelasgi · reference

One of the peoples of Crete. The modern edition calls them the Pelasgians.

## The Ceteians · reference

The people who fell around Eurypylus at Troy. The modern edition calls them the Ceteans.

## The Cydonians · reference

A people of Crete, living around the river Iardanus.

## The Sicels · reference

A people to whom the suitors propose selling the beggar and the seer.

## The Cauconians · reference

A people to whom Athena, as Mentor, says she must go to collect a debt.

# Julius Caesar recognition cards

All eighteen scenes, 997 paragraphs per English edition, 73 entries per edition; 1,780 original and 1,790 modern exact mentions. Source files unchanged. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval; runtime registration pending.

Brutus and Caesar are Central; Cassius, Antony, Octavius, Portia and Casca are Major. Other participants are Supporting. Historical, mythical and reported figures are Reference. All cards are brief recognition aids; no scene-by-scene recap snapshots or future assassination, suicide, battle outcome or accession biographies.

- Ordinary family identities are available at first mention: Brutus and Portia are married; Calphurnia is Caesar’s wife; Octavius is Caesar’s adopted son and heir. The card does not announce Octavius’s later historical career as emperor. Characters do not become Reference merely because they are historical people.
- Decius Brutus is distinct from Marcus Brutus. The last Brutus in Cassius’s speech at 2:44 is the earlier Lucius Junius Brutus; the other mentions address the play’s protagonist. The source’s Decius spelling is retained.
- Cinna in the conspiracy scenes is distinct from Cinna the poet in scene 10. The later camp Poet is another role, first entering at 13:49. Numbered citizen entries represent recurring dramatic labels, without inventing a biography or proving that the same four individuals attend both crowd scenes.
- Publius the senator, Publius Cimber the exile, and Publius identified as Antony’s nephew are separate. The source’s nephew name is retained without substituting a different historical reconstruction. Lepidus’s brother and Antony’s sister are also retained as distinct references.
- Portia’s father Cato and young Cato are separate. Marcus Cato in the young man’s declaration names his father. At 17:6 the first Cato is the son and the last is the father. Flavius the opening tribune is separate from the officer in the final battle scenes. Lucius Pella is not Brutus’s servant Lucius.
- Octavius Caesar uses a distinct identity. At 14:25, Caesar’s wounds refer to Julius; another Caesar means Octavius. Brutus’s next address, Caesar at 14:26, is also Octavius. Earlier Julius/Octavius combinations are resolved by the full name.
- Lucilius’s assumed Brutus identity at 17:10, 17:12 and 17:15 is bound to Lucilius, whose speaker label already makes his identity known to the reader. Antony’s subsequent discussion of the actual Brutus remains bound to Brutus.
- Caesar’s ghost is a separate apparition card first available at 13:150. Early talk of Caesar’s spirit or Antony’s prediction does not expose this later appearance. Household servants of Caesar, Antony and Octavius are separate; camp soldiers are distinct from Antony’s captors. The latter do not inherit friendly camp identities through reused speaker numbers.
- The men and women in Casca’s omen report are described as reported figures. Aeneas, Anchises, Ate, Plutus, Epicurus, the Fates and the gods remain references. Erebus and Olympus are place allusions here, and Colossus a statue comparison, not extra speaking characters.

Coordinates use one-based scenes and zero-based paragraphs. Exact local source text governs bindings. No exhaustive pronoun or anonymous-extra coverage is claimed. All 73 authored identities occur in both English editions.

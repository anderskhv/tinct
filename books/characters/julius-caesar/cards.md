# Julius Caesar: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Marcus Brutus · central

The Roman senator, Portia’s husband and the play’s central figure.

## Julius Caesar · central

The Roman leader whose growing power divides Rome, and Calphurnia’s husband.

## Caius Cassius · major

The Roman senator who urges Brutus to resist Caesar’s power.

## Mark Antony · major

Caesar’s close ally, the Roman commander also called Antonius.

## Octavius Caesar · major

Caesar’s adopted son and heir, the young Roman also called Octavius.

## Portia · major

Brutus’s wife and Cato’s daughter.

## Casca · major

The Roman who reports Caesar’s reception by the crowd to Brutus and Cassius.

## Calphurnia · supporting

Caesar’s wife.

## Decius Brutus · supporting

The Roman associate of Cassius, distinct from Marcus Brutus.

## Metellus Cimber · supporting

The Roman whose brother Publius Cimber is in exile.

## Trebonius · supporting

One of the Romans in Cassius’s circle.

## Cicero · supporting

The Roman senator and celebrated orator.

## Caius Ligarius · supporting

The ailing Roman whom Brutus summons to his house.

## Artemidorus · supporting

The man carrying a written warning for Caesar.

## Lepidus · supporting

The Roman political leader associated with Antony and Octavius.

## Publius · supporting

The elderly senator accompanying Caesar to the Capitol.

## Popilius Lena · supporting

The senator who speaks to Cassius on the way to the Capitol.

## Marullus · supporting

One of the tribunes challenging the celebrations in Caesar’s honor.

## Flavius, the tribune · supporting

The tribune who confronts the workers celebrating Caesar.

## Lucius · supporting

Brutus’s young servant, who also plays music for him.

## Lucilius · supporting

An officer serving Brutus.

## Titinius · supporting

The Roman associated with Cassius and Brutus, also named in Cassius’s recollection of Caesar.

## Messala · supporting

The officer who brings military and political news to Brutus.

## Pindarus · supporting

Cassius’s servant, whom Cassius took captive in Parthia.

## Young Cato · supporting

The younger Marcus Cato, Portia’s brother, fighting with Brutus.

## Flavius, the officer · supporting

An officer in Brutus’s army, distinct from the opening tribune.

## Labeo · supporting

The officer whom Brutus orders to arrange his forces.

## Clitus · supporting

One of Brutus’s attendants on the battlefield.

## Dardanius · supporting

An attendant accompanying Brutus in the final scene.

## Strato · supporting

One of Brutus’s attendants.

## Volumnius · supporting

Brutus’s former schoolmate and battlefield companion.

## Varro · supporting

One of Brutus’s servants, stationed in his tent.

## Claudius · supporting

The servant who shares the watch in Brutus’s tent with Varro.

## Statilius · supporting

The man sent ahead to signal to Brutus with a torch.

## Cinna · supporting

The Roman meeting with Cassius and his associates.

## Cinna the poet · supporting

The poet who shares a name with Cinna in Cassius’s circle.

## The Soothsayer · supporting

The diviner warning Caesar about the Ides of March.

## The carpenter · supporting

The worker whom the tribunes question in the opening street scene.

## The cobbler · supporting

The shoemaker who answers the tribunes with puns.

## Caesar’s servant · supporting

The household servant sent to consult the priests.

## Antony’s servant · supporting

The messenger sent to speak for Antony before he approaches Brutus.

## Octavius’s servant · supporting

The messenger reporting Octavius’s movements to Antony.

## The poet in the camp · supporting

The poet who interrupts Brutus and Cassius in their tent.

## The battlefield messenger · supporting

The messenger reporting the enemy’s advance to Antony and Octavius.

## The first camp soldier · supporting

One of the soldiers calling the halt outside Brutus’s tent.

## The second camp soldier · supporting

Another soldier passing the order to halt in the camp.

## The third camp soldier · supporting

The third soldier repeating the command to halt.

## Antony’s first soldier · supporting

The soldier confronting Lucilius on the battlefield.

## Antony’s second soldier · supporting

The soldier calling for Antony during Lucilius’s capture.

## The first citizen · supporting

One of the numbered voices of the Roman crowd in the Forum and street scenes.

## The second citizen · supporting

One of the numbered voices of the Roman crowd in the Forum and street scenes.

## The third citizen · supporting

One of the numbered voices of the Roman crowd in the Forum and street scenes.

## The fourth citizen · supporting

One of the numbered voices of the Roman crowd in the Forum and street scenes.

## The Roman citizens · supporting

The crowd of ordinary Romans addressed by the political leaders.

## Caesar’s ghost · supporting

The apparition of Caesar that appears to Brutus.

## Pompey · reference

The Roman commander and political rival of Caesar recalled by the speakers.

## Lucius Junius Brutus · reference

The earlier Brutus associated with expelling Rome’s kings.

## Tarquin · reference

The last king of Rome, recalled in Brutus’s account of his ancestors.

## Marcus Cato · reference

Portia’s father and the father of young Cato, the Roman opponent of Caesar.

## Publius Cimber · reference

Metellus Cimber’s exiled brother.

## Publius, Antony’s nephew · reference

The son of Antony’s sister, named in the discussion of proscription.

## Lepidus’s brother · reference

The brother mentioned in the leaders’ discussion of proscription.

## Antony’s sister · reference

Publius’s mother, named through her relationship to Antony.

## Lucius Pella · reference

The Roman whom Brutus has condemned for taking bribes at Sardis.

## Epicurus · reference

The Greek philosopher whose teachings Cassius discusses.

## Aeneas · reference

The Trojan hero who carries his father from burning Troy.

## Anchises · reference

Aeneas’s father, carried to safety in Cassius’s comparison.

## Ate · reference

The goddess of ruin invoked in Antony’s prediction of vengeance.

## Plutus · reference

The classical god of wealth, invoked by Cassius.

## The Fates · reference

The divine powers associated with the course and end of human lives.

## The gods · reference

The divine powers invoked in the Romans’ prayers and oaths.

## The slave in Casca’s report · reference

The man whose burning but unharmed hand Casca describes as an omen.

## The women in Casca’s report · reference

The frightened women said by Casca to have seen men walking in flames.

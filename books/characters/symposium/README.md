# Symposium recognition cards

All eight local sections and 217 paragraphs in each English edition. 93 original / 91 modern entries, with 510 / 506 exact mentions. Modern omits the translator references to Aristotle and Pope. Source editions unchanged; authoring-agent review, not independent editorial approval. Runtime registration remains pending.

Socrates is Central. Aristodemus, Agathon, Phaedrus, Pausanias, Eryximachus, Aristophanes, Diotima and Alcibiades are Major. Apollodorus and his unnamed listener are Supporting framing participants. Other actual household and gathering roles are Supporting; cited people, mythical figures and personifications are Reference. Every entry has one concise recognition snapshot rather than a sequence of speech summaries.

- The local source starts partway through the outer frame. Its unnamed companion is not assigned the name Glaucon from another passage or edition. The only local Glaucon is Charmides’ father at 7:107. Apollodorus retells Aristodemus’s eyewitness account.
- Agathon’s occupation and hosting role, Pausanias’s relationship to Agathon, and Eryximachus’s medical profession are ordinary initial identities. Diotima is identified through Socrates’ account of her teaching, without asserting her historical existence or adding a priestly office.
- Alcibiades first enters at 7:69. The original translator’s earlier 1 Alcibiades and Gorgias references at 7:45 are dialogue titles and do not create premature encounters.
- Eros is the figure of Love debated by the speakers. His card does not settle their competing accounts of age or parentage. Aphrodite’s identity likewise does not impose one genealogy on the different aspects discussed. Urania and Polyhymnia at 4:3 are the Muses. Poros, Metis and Penia retain the translated names Plenty, Discretion and Poverty.
- God resolves by occurrence: Eros at 2:7 and 5:6, Zeus at 5:5, Ares in God of War, and general divinity in the remaining selected passages. Earth binds Gaia only in the personified creation references, not every physical-earth occurrence.
- Olympus is the musician, Euthydemus the son of Diocles, and Glaucon Charmides’ father. Gorginian language refers to the rhetorician Gorgias; the Gorgonian comparison invokes the mythical Gorgon. Silenus and Marsyas remain comparison figures rather than aliases for Socrates.
- The welcoming and reporting servants and the two flute-girl appearances have separately scoped role cards. This does not claim the text proves separate individuals across all appearances. Late revelers are distinct from the initial household gathering. Generic comparisons with flute-players do not bind these attendants.
- Translator citations are included when they name people; titles alone are excluded. Aristotle and Pope are original-only. Sthenoaoea and Hyppolytus are play titles; Melanippe is explicitly invoked as a speaking character. The edition’s wording is not silently repaired.

Coordinates use zero-based paragraph indices. Local source text governs exact bindings; no claim is made to resolve every pronoun, generic philosophical example or anonymous collective.

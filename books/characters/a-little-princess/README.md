# A Little Princess recognition cards

All nineteen chapters and 1,701 paragraphs in both local English editions. 86 entries per edition. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. Source editions unchanged; runtime registration pending.

Sara is Central. Captain Crewe, Miss Minchin, Ermengarde, Becky, Carrisford and Ram Dass are Major. Other school and household participants, the neighboring family, the bakery characters, pets and dolls are Supporting. People chiefly recalled or discussed and literary/historical figures are Reference. The review copy shows every category.

## Recognition and disclosure

- Sara's stable reminder is simply “The story’s main character, Captain Crewe’s daughter.” Miss Minchin is the headmistress, Mariette the French maid, Dufarge the French teacher. No running chapter recaps or duplicate subtitles.
- Ermengarde becomes Sara's school friend after 3:67; Becky her friend after 6:58 and her personal maid after 19:23. Lottie's adoptive-mother relationship is available after 4:55.
- The Indian gentleman is an Englishman returned from India. His ordinary name and neighboring-house identity are available at his first explicit mention. His business connection with Captain Crewe is concealed until 12:26, when the reader learns it. It does not wait until Sara's discovery in Chapter 17. Guardianship appears after 18:55. “Uncle Tom” is a familiar form of address, not a biological relationship.
- The possible Paris school lead is initially only “The girl at the Paris school,” a pupil Carmichael is investigating. Her card does not disclose the search's connection with Sara before the source does, or reveal her different identity in advance. Emily Carew is named after 17:33. Exact mentions of Emily Carew never resolve to Emily the doll.
- Mrs. Brown's bakery occupation is available at the first encounter. Anne starts as the hungry girl outside that shop; her name and new home/work appear after 19:40. Her card does not predict what follows Sara's gift of buns.

All gate coordinates use zero-based paragraph indices.

## Alias and minor-role review

- Sara's imagined Montmorency family is the Carmichael family. Janet/Veronica Eustacia, Nora/Rosalind Gladys and Donald/Guy Clarence are paired identities, not duplicate characters. Five other children retain their explicitly imagined names; their unprinted real names are not invented. Both parents and the grandmother are separate entries.
- Miss Amelia and Maria Minchin remain distinct, including Becky's “Meliar” pronunciation. Mr. St. John and Miss St. John are distinct. Barrow and Skipworth are separate solicitors.
- The young secretary in Chapter 14 is distinct from Carrisford. Ram Dass addresses the secretary as Sahib in that scene and refers to Carrisford by the same title within his explanation. Bindings distinguish the addressee from the absent employer. The doctor, nurse, carriage footman and arrival attendants retain separate roles.
- Emily and the Last Doll are separate objects. Melchisedec, Mrs. Melchisedec, their young, the sparrows, monkey, pony and Boris are retained. Boris is a Russian boarhound, not a pony. Earlier comparisons with a spoiled pet monkey do not resolve as the next-door pet.
- Sara's mother and ayah, Lottie's parents, Ermengarde's relatives, Amelia's aunt, the Russian adoptive family and the Paris-school officer remain distinct. The kind aunt is not asserted to be Aunt Eliza or one of the two criticizing aunts: the text does not establish that identification.
- Historical and literary references include the poets, English monarchs, Marie Antoinette/Widow Capet, Robespierre, Lamballe, the Count of Monte Cristo, Sleeping Beauty, Buddha and Sara's underwater story figures. Generic Czar, geography, grammar-example relatives and hypothetical princesses do not produce asserted historical identities. The unnamed lamprey-history answer is not invented as a printed name. Magic is Sara's way of describing events, not an added supernatural character.
- All 86 authored entries bind in both editions. No claim is made to resolve every pronoun, generic title, anonymous crowd or imagined figure. Original Carrrisford is preserved as a source alias; modern spelling is corrected in its inherited source.

The local editions govern every binding. Comparative primary text: [Project Gutenberg's A Little Princess](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/146/146-h/146-h.htm).

# Candide recognition cards

Thirty chapters, 709 paragraphs in each local English edition. 210 entries per edition; 1,364 original / 1,346 modern exact mentions. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. Source editions unchanged and runtime registration pending.

Candide is Central. Cunegonde, Pangloss, Cacambo, Martin, the old woman and Cunegonde's brother are Major. Direct participants such as the royal diners, Admiral Byng, Clairon, the dervish and the Turkish farmer are Supporting. Authors, biblical figures, family references and figures merely discussed are Reference. Historical status alone does not make a narrative participant a Reference.

## Recognition and disclosure

- Candide is the young Westphalian raised in the baron's household. His proposed parentage remains a household suspicion, not a fact asserted by the cards.
- Pangloss is the optimistic tutor; Cacambo the resourceful valet; Martin the pessimistic scholar. These remain concise reminders, with no chapter-by-chapter travel or misfortune summaries.
- At her first appearance the old woman is an elderly servant helping Candide in Lisbon. Calling her Cunegonde's servant then would reveal Cunegonde's survival. That connection appears only after 7:11, and her papal parentage after 11:0. Urban X is explicitly an invented pope, not a historical office-holder.
- Cunegonde's brother is recognized as the baron's son from 1:2. His Major category and Jesuit occupation wait until the recognition at 14:28. The unidentified Commandant's title before that point is deliberately not bound to the known brother. Generic descriptions of returned beggars, veiled women and galley slaves likewise do not acquire advance identity bindings.
- The false Cunegonde's name usages in 22:74–79 do not resolve as the real woman. Her separate card becomes available at cheat/swindler in 22:86, when the deception is identified. The maid's earlier description does not expose the deception.
- Cunegonde becomes wife in the reminder after 30:1, where marriage is established, not merely intended in 30:0. Paquette's later occupation is reflected after 24:13; Giroflee becomes a former Theatine friar after 30:5. These are brief identity changes rather than outcome recaps.

All indices above are zero-based paragraph indices within the printed chapter.

## Distinctions and references

- Father and son share Baron, but are scoped separately. The older baron is still the referent in Paquette's retrospective at 24:13. The baroness, the baron's sister, his son and Candide's putative father have independent bindings.
- Paquette's confessor, the friar at Badajos, Giroflee and the Paris parson are separate. So are the army surgeon, the French surgeon at Azof, Paquette's doctor and the Portuguese surgeon/barber who treats Pangloss. Their wives, judges and patients are not merged.
- The elder of El Dorado and the Turkish farmer are distinct; so are the various captains, governors, prisoners, scholars, servants and family references.
- The fourth and fifth royal diners are Augustus III and Stanisław Leszczyński, both labelled King of Poland in the source. The imagined dinner is fiction, not evidence that those rulers historically met there. The remaining diners retain their recognizable names and offices.
- Miss Monime alludes to Adrienne Lecouvreur, not a new woman named Monime. Clairon is the actress; Queen Elizabeth is the represented queen. Fréron is an allusive comparison with the unnamed theater critic, not an automatic identity merge.
- Perseus in the final catalogue is the Macedonian king. The emperor Henry IV is distinct from the grouped French Henrys. Saint James of Compostela is distinct from James the Anabaptist. The Messiah, Sin and Death in the library discussion belong to the Paradise Lost allusion.
- The historical assassins suggested only by dates in 22:90 are not invented as selectable names; local editions omit the explanatory footnote text. Similarly, philosophical-school adjectives do not create entries for their founders. Geographic churches, islands, wine names and institutional labels are not indiscriminately matched as people.
- Original/modern variants include Grey Friar/Franciscan friar, Iman/imam, Robek/Robeck, Leibnitz/Leibniz, Baasa/Baasha and AEneas/Aeneas. All 210 authored entries have reviewed bindings in both editions. No claim is made to resolve every pronoun, generic title or collective army reference.

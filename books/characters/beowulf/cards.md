# Beowulf: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Beowulf · central

The poem’s hero, a Geatish warrior, son of Ecgtheow and nephew of King Hygelac.

After source chapter 31, paragraph 9: The poem’s hero, now king of the Geats; Ecgtheow’s son and Hygelac’s nephew.

## Beowulf, son of Scyld · reference

The early Danish ruler, son of Scyld and father of Healfdene; a different Beowulf from the poem’s hero.

## Hrothgar · major

The Danish king who built Heorot, husband of Wealhtheow.

## Grendel · major

The monster who attacks Hrothgar’s hall, Heorot.

## Grendel’s mother · major

The unnamed mother of Grendel, who lives in the waters near the moors.

## The dragon · major

The fire-breathing dragon guarding an ancient hoard in Geatland.

## Wiglaf · major

The young warrior in Beowulf’s service, son of Weohstan and a kinsman of Beowulf.

## Hygelac · major

The king of the Geats, Beowulf’s uncle and lord; spelled Higelac in this translation.

## Wealhtheow · supporting

Hrothgar’s queen and the mother of Hrethric, Hrothmund and Freaware.

## Unferth · supporting

Hrothgar’s courtier who challenges Beowulf’s reputation, son of Ecglaf.

## Wulfgar · supporting

Hrothgar’s court officer who receives Beowulf’s party, a prince of the Wendels.

## Hygd · supporting

Hygelac’s queen, daughter of Hæreth and mother of Heardred.

## Hrothulf · supporting

Hrothgar’s nephew, raised in his household and seated beside him at the feast.

## Hrethric · supporting

One of the sons of Hrothgar and Wealhtheow.

## Hrothmund · supporting

One of the sons of Hrothgar and Wealhtheow.

## Freaware · supporting

Hrothgar and Wealhtheow’s daughter, betrothed to Ingeld of the Heathobards.

## Hondscio · supporting

One of Beowulf’s Geatish companions at Heorot.

## Æschere · supporting

Hrothgar’s trusted adviser and companion in battle, elder brother of Yrmenlaf.

## Scyld Scefing · reference

The founding ruler of the Danish Scylding line in the poem’s opening.

## Healfdene · reference

The Danish king who is the father of Hrothgar, Heorogar, Halga and Elan.

## Heorogar · reference

Hrothgar’s elder brother and predecessor as Danish king, also called Heregar.

## Halga · reference

Hrothgar’s brother, one of Healfdene’s sons.

## Elan · reference

Hrothgar’s sister, identified in this translation as Ongentheow’s wife.

## Ongentheow · reference

The Swedish king, father of Onela and Ohthere, in the Geats’ history of their wars.

## Ecgtheow · reference

Beowulf’s father, who married King Hrethel’s daughter.

## Hrethel · reference

The former king of the Geats, Beowulf’s maternal grandfather and Hygelac’s father.

## Heatholaf · reference

The Wilfing warrior in Hrothgar’s account of Ecgtheow’s feud.

## Ecglaf · reference

Unferth’s father.

## Breca · reference

Beowulf’s youthful swimming rival, son of Beanstan and a lord of the Brondings.

## Beanstan · reference

Breca’s father.

## Wayland · reference

The legendary smith credited with making Beowulf’s armor.

## Sigmund · reference

The legendary hero praised in the court poet’s dragon-fighting tale.

## Fitela · reference

Sigmund’s nephew and companion in the tale recalled by the poet.

## Wæls · reference

Sigmund’s father, whose name appears in his family designation.

## Heremod · reference

The earlier Danish king used as a warning about a ruler’s pride and cruelty.

## Ecgwela · reference

An early Danish ruler whose people are recalled in Hrothgar’s warning.

## Finn · reference

The Frisian king in the hall-singer’s story, husband of Hildeburg.

## Hnæf · reference

The Danish leader in the Finn episode, Hildeburg’s brother.

## Hildeburg · reference

Finn’s Danish wife, sister of Hnæf and daughter of Hoce.

## Hoce · reference

The father of Hildeburg and Hnæf.

## Hengest · reference

The Danish warrior who leads Hnæf’s surviving men in the Finn episode.

## Folcwalda · reference

Finn’s father.

## Hun · reference

The Frisian in this translation who gives a sword to Hengest.

## Guthlaf · reference

One of Hnæf’s Danish warriors in the Finn episode.

## Oslaf · reference

One of Hnæf’s Danish warriors in the Finn episode.

## Hama · reference

The legendary hero who carries off the Brosingmen’s necklace in the poet’s comparison.

## Eormenric · reference

The king whose hostility Hama escapes in the necklace allusion.

## Swerting · reference

Hygelac’s ancestor, called his grandfather in this translation.

## Yrmenlaf · reference

Æschere’s younger brother.

## Hæreth · reference

Hygd’s father.

## Thrytho · reference

The queen in the poet’s contrast with Hygd, wife of Offa.

## Heming · reference

A kinsman named in the account of Offa and Eomær.

## Offa · reference

The legendary king of the Angles, husband of Thrytho and father of Eomær.

## Eomær · reference

The son of Offa and Thrytho, grandson of Garmund.

## Garmund · reference

Offa’s father and Eomær’s grandfather.

## Froda · reference

The Heathobard ruler who is Ingeld’s father.

## Ingeld · reference

The Heathobard prince betrothed to Hrothgar’s daughter Freaware.

## Hereward · reference

Heorogar’s son, named in the history of the armor Beowulf brings home.

## Heardred · reference

The son of Hygelac and Hygd, who succeeds his father as king of the Geats.

## Hereric · reference

Heardred’s uncle.

## Ohthere · reference

The Swedish prince, son of Ongentheow and father of Eanmund and Eadgils.

## Onela · reference

The Swedish king, brother of Ohthere and uncle of Eanmund and Eadgils.

## Eadgils · reference

The Swedish prince, son of Ohthere and brother of Eanmund.

## Eanmund · reference

The Swedish prince, son of Ohthere and brother of Eadgils.

## Herebald · reference

King Hrethel’s eldest son, brother of Hæthcyn and Hygelac.

## Hæthcyn · reference

Hrethel’s son and Hygelac’s brother, an earlier king of the Geats.

## Eofor · reference

The Geatish warrior in the war with Ongentheow, brother of Wulf.

## Dæghrefn · reference

The warrior of the Hugs in Beowulf’s account of his earlier battles.

## Weohstan · reference

Wiglaf’s father, also called Wihstan in this translation.

## Ælfhere · reference

The kinsman of Wiglaf named at his introduction.

## Wulf · reference

The Geatish warrior in the war with Ongentheow, brother of Eofor and son of Wonred.

## Wonred · reference

The father of Wulf and Eofor.

## The Merovingian ruler · reference

The Frankish ruler called Merewing in the account of Hygelac’s raid.

## The Danish coastguard · supporting

Hrothgar’s coastal watchman, who questions Beowulf’s arriving party.

## The Geatish guide · supporting

The seafaring man who shows Beowulf’s party the way before departure.

## The poet on the ride · supporting

The Danish poet who praises Beowulf and recalls Sigmund during the ride from the mere.

## Hrothgar’s hall-singer · supporting

The court performer who tells the Finn and Hildeburg story at the feast.

## The singer in Heorot · supporting

The hall-singer whose song of creation is heard at the opening of the poem.

## Beowulf’s mother · reference

Hrethel’s daughter, wife of Ecgtheow and mother of Beowulf.

## Hildeburg’s son · reference

The son of Hildeburg and Finn in the hall-singer’s tale.

## Sigmund’s dragon · reference

The treasure-guarding dragon in the poet’s story of Sigmund.

## Unferth’s brothers · reference

The brothers whose deaths Beowulf raises in his argument with Unferth.

## The old Heathobard warrior · reference

The veteran whom Beowulf imagines stirring resentment at Ingeld’s wedding feast.

## The young Heathobard warrior · reference

The young man whom the imagined veteran urges to avenge his father.

## The young warrior’s father · reference

The fallen Heathobard whose sword appears in Beowulf’s imagined wedding dispute.

## Freaware’s Danish attendant · reference

The Danish warrior wearing inherited spoils in Beowulf’s imagined wedding dispute.

## The last survivor · reference

The unnamed man who deposits his vanished people’s treasures in the barrow.

## The fugitive servant · supporting

The servant who takes a cup from the dragon’s hoard while seeking refuge.

## The servant’s master · reference

The lord from whom the fugitive servant seeks forgiveness with the stolen cup.

## The hanged son · reference

The young man on the gallows in the comparison used for Hrethel’s grief.

## The grieving father · reference

The old father in the gallows comparison, distinct from Hrethel himself.

## The Geatish messenger · supporting

The warrior who reports news of the dragon fight to the waiting Geats.

## Ongentheow’s queen · reference

The Swedish king’s wife and mother of Onela and Ohthere in the war account.

## Hygelac’s daughter · reference

The king’s unnamed daughter, given in marriage to Eofor.

## The mourning woman · supporting

The woman lamenting at Beowulf’s funeral, called a widow in this translation.

## Hrunting · supporting

Unferth’s sword, lent to Beowulf for the journey into the mere.

## Nægling · supporting

Beowulf’s sword in the fight with the dragon.

## Láfing · reference

The sword given to Hengest by Hun in this translation’s Finn episode.

## The Brosingmen’s necklace · reference

The legendary necklace carried off by Hama, used as a comparison for Beowulf’s gift.

## God · reference

The divine creator invoked by the poem’s narrator and speakers.

## Cain · reference

The biblical son of Adam and Eve, brother of Abel, whose line the poem associates with monsters.

## Abel · reference

Cain’s brother in the biblical story recalled by the narrator.

## Fate · reference

Destiny personified by the poet, called Weird or Wyrd as well as Fate.

## The Danes · supporting

Hrothgar’s people, also called Scyldings and by several Danish epithets.

## The Geats · supporting

Beowulf’s people, ruled by Hygelac when he sets out; also called Weders.

## The Scylfings · reference

The Swedish royal family and its people in the accounts of war with the Geats.

## The Wægmundings · reference

The family line shared by Beowulf and Wiglaf.

## The Wendels · reference

The people of whom Wulfgar is called a prince.

## The Wilfings · reference

Heatholaf’s people, involved in Ecgtheow’s feud.

## The Brondings · reference

Breca’s people.

## The Heathoremes · reference

The people whose coast Breca reaches in Unferth’s account of the swimming contest.

## The Finns · reference

The people whose land Beowulf says he reaches after the swimming ordeal.

## The Frisians · reference

The people of Finn in the lay and of the region raided by Hygelac.

## The Jutes · reference

The people called Jutemen in the Finn episode.

## The Franks · reference

The people encountered in the account of Hygelac’s fatal raid.

## The Hugs · reference

A name used here for the Franks, Dæghrefn’s people.

## The Hetwars · reference

The continental people fighting the Geats during Hygelac’s raid.

## The Heathobards · reference

Ingeld’s people, whose feud with the Danes is addressed through his betrothal.

## The Gepids · reference

A Germanic people named among possible sources of warriors.

## The giants · reference

The ancient beings whom the narrator places among the enemies of God.

## The elves · reference

The supernatural beings listed in the narrator’s account of Cain’s descendants.

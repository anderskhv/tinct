# Beowulf recognition cards

All 43 sections and 375 paragraphs of both local Hall-based English editions. 121 entries per edition. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. Source editions unchanged; runtime integration pending.

The hero Beowulf is Central. Hrothgar, Hygelac, Grendel, Grendel's mother, the dragon and Wiglaf are Major. Other direct court and journey participants are Supporting. Genealogies, historical recollections and the figures of embedded lays are Reference. Named weapons are objects with Supporting or Reference roles; peoples and family lines are groups. The full reading copy displays every category.

## Recognition boundaries

- The first Beowulf, at 1:2 and 2:0, is Scyld's son and Healfdene's father. He is a Reference, distinct from the Geatish hero. The hero first binds to liegeman/retainer at 4:1, while the name Higelac in that expression still belongs to Hygelac.
- The hero is identified immediately as Ecgtheow's son and Hygelac's nephew. His reminder becomes king of the Geats after 31:8, where his rule is explicit. It does not anticipate the crown from early princely epithets. This is the sole later snapshot; battles and deaths do not generate running recaps.
- Hrothgar is the Danish king and Wealhtheow's husband; Hygd is Hygelac's queen. Æschere's counselor identity is available at the unnamed attack passage in 20:4; Hondscio is a Geatish companion at 12:5, well before he is named in the retrospective account. Wulfgar's court office is given at his first question, 6:2.
- Sigmund's dragon in Section 14 is distinct from the hoard-guarding dragon first encountered in Section 31. Generic dragon-skin and other monster imagery do not become sightings of that later dragon. Grendel's mother is a separate identity from Grendel.
- The three song-performing roles are recorded independently; the text does not require the opening singer, riding poet and Finn-lay singer to be the same individual. The anonymous messenger, coastal watchman, fugitive servant and ancient treasure depositor are likewise distinct.

All gate coordinates use zero-based paragraph indices.

## Source variants and limits

The source is the Hall translation, including its older readings, not a modern scholarly reconstruction. Comparative primary edition and historical glossary: [J. Lesslie Hall's Beowulf](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16328/16328-h/16328-h.htm). The following distinctions are intentional:

- Higelac/Hygelac; Heorogar/Heregar; Hrethel/Hrethla/Hrethrel; Wealhtheow/Wealhtheo; Weohstan/Wihstan; Ongentheow/Ongentheo. Accented and ligature names remain exact, including Æschere, Hnæf, Hæthcyn, Eomær and Nægling.
- Elan is described as Hrothgar's sister and as Ongentheow's consort **in this translation**. That reflects an uncertain historical textual reading. The later unnamed Swedish queen is not automatically merged with Elan or given an invented biography.
- The source calls Swerting Hygelac's grandfather; the card attributes that relationship to this translation. Hun is retained as the Frisian sword-giver as printed. No claim is made to settle alternative readings of the Finn episode.
- Section 34:0 describes Beowulf as an enemy to Eadgils, unlike the usual modern account of his support for Eadgils. The card supplies the secure family identity without adding an allegiance that conflicts with the local text. Review the inherited translation separately if edition correction is contemplated.
- The late Scyldings at 41:8 is a disputed reading and is excluded from the automatic Danish group binding. It is not silently corrected to Geats or Swedes.
- The funeral woman is called a widow in the source, but the cards do not declare her Beowulf's wife. The father and hanged son in the grief simile are not merged with Hrethel and Herebald. The imagined Heathobard wedding participants remain explicitly hypothetical in their descriptions.
- Lord of the Scyldings, Lord of the Weders and Wielder of Weders are human titles; they do not resolve to God. The All-Father here belongs to the poem's Christian divine vocabulary; it is not an added Odin entry. Fate/Weird/Wyrd are a personification; Fate-sent and Fate-cursed adjectives are not selectable occurrences.
- All 121 authored entries have reviewed bindings in both editions. Coverage does not claim every pronoun, anonymous warrior, kenning or inflected tribal adjective. The source's omitted editorial footnotes and marginal headings are not fabricated as selectable prose.

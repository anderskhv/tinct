# Oedipus Rex: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Oedipus · central

King of Thebes and husband of Jocasta.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 146: The king of Thebes, now revealed as the son of Laius and of his own wife, Jocasta.

## Jocasta · major

Queen of Thebes, wife of Oedipus and sister of Creon. Formerly married to Laius.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 146: Oedipus’s wife and mother, formerly married to Laius; Creon’s sister.

## Creon · major

Jocasta’s brother and an influential member of the Theban royal household.

## Teiresias · major

The blind prophet consulted by Thebes.

## Laius · major

The former king of Thebes and Jocasta’s previous husband.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 146: The former king of Thebes, husband of Jocasta and father of Oedipus.

## Polybus · reference

King of Corinth and Oedipus’s father.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 47: The king of Corinth who raised Oedipus as his son, though they were not related by blood.

## Merope · reference

Queen of Corinth, wife of Polybus and mother of Oedipus.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 146: Polybus’s wife, who raised Oedipus in Corinth as her son.

## The priest of Zeus · supporting

The elderly priest speaking for the people gathered outside Oedipus’s palace.

## The Theban elders · major

The elders of Thebes who speak and sing as the chorus.

## The Corinthian messenger · supporting

The messenger bringing news from Corinth.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 55: The messenger from Corinth, a former shepherd who brought the infant Oedipus to Polybus.

## Laius’s herdsman · supporting

The old shepherd who served King Laius.

## The palace messenger · supporting

The servant reporting events inside the Theban palace.

## Antigone · supporting

A daughter of Oedipus and Jocasta, and Ismene’s sister.

## Ismene · supporting

A daughter of Oedipus and Jocasta, and Antigone’s sister.

## Menoeceus · reference

The father of Creon and Jocasta.

## Labdacus · reference

Laius’s father, an ancestor of the Theban royal line.

## Polydorus · reference

Cadmus’s son and Labdacus’s father in the Theban royal ancestry.

## Cadmus · reference

The legendary founder of Thebes.

## Agenor · reference

Cadmus’s father in the royal ancestry recited by Oedipus.

## The Sphinx · reference

The riddling monster that once threatened Thebes.

## Zeus · reference

The chief god of the Greek pantheon.

## Athena · reference

The Greek goddess of wisdom and protector of cities, also called Pallas.

## Apollo · reference

The god of prophecy whose oracle at Delphi guides the inquiry. Also called Phoebus and Loxias.

## Artemis · reference

The Greek goddess of the hunt, Apollo’s sister.

## Ares · reference

The Greek god of war, invoked here as a destructive power afflicting the city.

## Amphitrite · reference

The sea goddess, whose domain is invoked as a distant place of banishment.

## Pluto · reference

The ruler of the underworld, also known as Hades.

## Bacchus · reference

Dionysus, the god of wine and ecstatic worship, closely associated with Thebes.

## Pan · reference

The rustic god of shepherds and wild mountain places.

## Hermes · reference

The messenger god associated with Mount Cyllene.

## The Pythia · reference

The priestess who speaks Apollo’s oracle at Delphi.

## The Fates · reference

The divine powers governing destiny in Greek tradition.

## The Maenads · reference

The ecstatic female followers of Bacchus.

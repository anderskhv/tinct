# Wuthering Heights: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Mr. Lockwood · central

He rents Thrushcross Grange and calls on his landlord at Wuthering Heights. His account begins with his own impressions of the place.

## Mrs. Dean · central

Lockwood turns to the housekeeper for company and information about his neighbours.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 33: She grew up at Wuthering Heights alongside the Earnshaw children. Her history of the household includes events in which she took part, as well as things she witnessed.

## Heathcliff · central

He owns the house Lockwood rents. Lockwood admires his reserve on their first meeting, though Heathcliff offers him little welcome.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 38: Mr. Earnshaw brought him home from Liverpool as a child with no known family. Heathcliff is his whole name. Nelly recalls his friendship with Catherine and Hindley’s hostility; his ancestry remains unknown.

## Catherine Earnshaw · central

Lockwood finds this name scratched on the ledge of a bed enclosed with wooden panels. Its repetition draws his attention to the room’s earlier occupant.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 28: Her name appears in an old book and on the window ledge. Lockwood dreams of a child calling herself Catherine Linton at the window; his account does not establish a supernatural event.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 33: The elder Catherine is Hindley’s sister, a child in the Earnshaw household. She is distinct from the young Mrs. Heathcliff whom Lockwood meets.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 125: The elder Catherine marries Edgar Linton and becomes Mrs. Linton. Nelly describes her attachment to Heathcliff alongside her wish for the position Edgar offers.

## Mrs. Heathcliff · central

Lockwood addresses the young woman at the house as Mrs. Heathcliff. He has not yet understood her place in the household.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 48: She is Heathcliff’s daughter-in-law and his son’s widow. Lockwood’s guesses that she is the wife of Heathcliff or of the young man beside him are mistaken.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 16: Nelly identifies the young widow as Catherine Linton, her late master’s daughter and her former charge. She is distinct from the Catherine whose name Lockwood found in the bedroom.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 1: The younger Catherine is the daughter of Edgar Linton and Catherine Earnshaw. Nelly recounts her birth and her mother’s death as separate lives in the same household.

## Hindley Earnshaw · major

Catherine’s diary names Hindley as the person ruling the household in her father’s absence. She describes his treatment of Heathcliff as atrocious.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 33: Hindley is the elder Catherine’s brother and Hareton’s father. Nelly grew up alongside him and recalls the household under his father, Mr. Earnshaw.

## Hareton Earnshaw · major

He gives Lockwood his name angrily after the visitor has repeatedly guessed his position in the household incorrectly.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 33: Hareton is Hindley Earnshaw’s son. The young man Lockwood meets is a separate person from the name on the house’s carving dated 1500.

After source chapter 8, paragraph 14: Hareton is Hindley and Frances’s son. Nelly cares for him as a baby after Frances dies; he and the adult whom Lockwood meets are the same person.

## Edgar Linton · major

Heathcliff describes seeing Edgar in the Grange’s comfortable drawing room with his sister. Their quarrel over a little dog attracts the watching children’s contempt.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 125: Edgar, Isabella’s brother, marries Catherine Earnshaw. He belongs to the Linton family at Thrushcross Grange.

## Isabella Linton · major

She is one of the children Heathcliff watches through the drawing-room window at Thrushcross Grange. His hostile description comes from outside her household.

After source chapter 13, paragraph 7: Isabella has married Heathcliff. Her note to Edgar asks for remembrance and reconciliation, but receives no reply. Her married title, Mrs. Heathcliff, also belongs to a different woman in Lockwood’s opening visits.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 66: After leaving Wuthering Heights, Isabella settles near London and gives birth to a son named Linton. She describes the child as sickly in her correspondence with Edgar.

## Linton · major

Nelly recounts Isabella’s life away from the neighbourhood and the naming of her child.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 66: Linton is Isabella and Heathcliff’s son. Linton is his given name; he is distinct from his uncle Edgar Linton and from his father Heathcliff.

## Joseph · supporting

Heathcliff calls on Joseph to take Lockwood’s horse. Lockwood finds the elderly servant sour and unwelcoming.

## Frances · supporting

Catherine’s account places Frances beside the man commanding the children. She joins in his rough treatment of Heathcliff.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 10: Frances is Hindley’s wife. Catherine’s diary describes her pulling Heathcliff’s hair at Hindley’s request and then sitting on her husband’s knee.

After source chapter 8, paragraph 14: Frances is Hareton’s mother. Nelly recounts her illness and death after his birth; little is known of her family before she married Hindley.

## Mr. Earnshaw · supporting

Nelly recalls the older master of Wuthering Heights setting off on foot for Liverpool.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 33: He is Hindley and the elder Catherine’s father. Nelly remembers his promises of presents before a journey to Liverpool.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 38: He brings a homeless child back from Liverpool and raises him in the household as Heathcliff. His care for the child does not establish biological parentage.

## Mrs. Earnshaw · supporting

She waits with the children for Mr. Earnshaw to return from his journey to Liverpool.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 36: She is the mother of Hindley and the elder Catherine. Nelly recalls her anger when her husband brings the unknown child home. She is distinct from Hindley’s wife Frances.

## Mr. Linton · supporting

He belongs to the older generation at Thrushcross Grange. Heathcliff’s account places the children in the drawing room while the adults are absent.

After source chapter 6, paragraph 8: He is Edgar and Isabella’s father, a magistrate at Thrushcross Grange. In Heathcliff’s account he treats the unknown boy as a potential criminal. He is distinct from his son Edgar.

## Mrs. Linton · supporting

She belongs to the older generation at Thrushcross Grange, where Heathcliff and Catherine watch the children through a window.

After source chapter 6, paragraph 8: She is Edgar and Isabella’s mother, addressed by her husband as Mary. She is distinct from Catherine Earnshaw, who also bears the title Mrs. Linton later in the account.

## Mr. Kenneth · supporting

Kenneth is the medical practitioner consulted by the household. His opinions reach the reader through Nelly’s account.

## Zillah · supporting

She comes to Lockwood’s aid after his encounter with the dogs. She serves at Wuthering Heights, a different household from Mrs. Dean’s at the Grange.

## Mr. Shielders · reference

Mr. Linton names Shielders while criticising the children’s upbringing. He is the parish curate, distinct from the preacher in Lockwood’s dream.

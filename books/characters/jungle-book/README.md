# The Jungle Book recognition cards

All seven stories and their accompanying verse, 944 paragraphs and 104 entries in each English edition; 1,847 exact mentions each. Source bytes unchanged. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval; runtime registration pending.

Mowgli, Kotick, Rikki and Little Toomai are Central in their respective stories. The principal animal companions and antagonists are Major, including the military animals’ ensemble. Local participants are Supporting. Recalled figures and allusions are Reference. The Amir and Viceroy are Supporting participants in the review, while the Empress is a Reference in the described chain of command. The collection is not treated as one continuous cast surrounding Mowgli.

- Ordinary identities lead: Mowgli is the human raised by wolves, Kotick the white seal, Rikki the mongoose in Teddy’s household, and Little Toomai the elephant driver’s son. Mowgli’s first unnamed man’s cub at 1:27 is identified. Mother Wolf/Raksha, Shere Khan/Lungri and the other ordinary names are not held back as mysteries.
- Mowgli and Nathoo remain separate. Messua recognizes a resemblance to her lost son; the package does not turn that belief into proven identity through shared metadata. Purun Dass remains the moneylender in Buldeo’s story; the tiger is not identified as his reincarnation. Bagheera’s account of his mother is retained separately.
- Akela’s reminder changes to former pack leader after 1:112, when the source explicitly shows the leadership open. All other Mowgli-story reminders remain short, without repeated adoption, rescue, killing or village summaries. Mowgli’s description uses human rather than an age that becomes false at the end.
- Little Toomai, Big Toomai, Black Toomai and the earlier Toomai of the Elephants are four generations. The title at 6:7 names the ancestor. The boy’s name changes only after the honor is awarded at 6:81. Kala Nag, his mother Radha Pyari, Pudmini, Hathi and Two Tails are separate elephants, not interchangeable elephant aliases.
- Teddy’s father, Teddy’s mother Alice, and Rikki’s mongoose parents are separate. The unnamed boy at 5:4, mother at 5:5 and big man at 5:6 receive their useful family identities immediately. Darzee’s wife is a separate tailorbird with her own card. Nag and Nagaina are distinct cobras; Chua is the rat and Chuchundra the animal called a muskrat by the story. These are fictional character descriptions, not a zoological correction of the edition.
- Sea Lion’s Neck is excluded as a place; the individual sea lion first appears at 4:45. Kotick’s companion, the challenging seal, the old seal of Masafuera, the albatross, the gull and the hunters are differentiated. Sea Cow represents the sea cows collectively rather than an invented uniquely named individual. The narrator is the human storytelling voice, not asserted to be a literal biographical identification of Kipling.
- Billy is the experienced mule; the young recruit, Australian troop horse, baggage camel, pair of gun bullocks and Two Tails have separate cards. Dick Cunliffe is the rider. The human officer and visiting chief at the end are also separate. The force’s ranks are not copied into the animals’ identities.
- Named reference coverage includes the lullaby’s divine family and grasshopper, the earlier Toomais, Theodore, Sunol, Carbine, Alexander, Hercules, Cupid, the Frog Footman and the opening Tweedle names. The Frog Footman is a literary comparison, not Teddy’s mother Alice. Marshal Niel roses, place names, and the tune Bonnie Dundee are not miscast as present people.

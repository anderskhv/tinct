# A Midsummer Night’s Dream: reviewed character package

Both registered English editions, all nine reading units. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval or live verification. Read cards.md for the complete copy and visible categories; source-review.md records first bindings.

Four young lovers are Central. Bottom, Oberon, Titania and Puck are Major. Court figures, craftsmen, fairies and performed roles are Supporting. Cited mythical figures are Reference. Actors remain separate from their dramatic roles. No duplicate subtitles or chapter-by-chapter plot recap.

Bottom’s changed appearance is gated after 5:40 and restoration after 7:29. Demetrius’s declared love for Helena is gated after 7:54. Paragraphs are zero-based. Ordinary occupations and family relationships are immediate. No allegations about Oberon’s or Titania’s past lovers are presented as independently established history.

Helen is Helena in the lovers’ dialogue, Helen of Troy at 9:2, and the players’ mistake for Hero at 9:53. Robin Starveling is not Robin Goodfellow. The unnamed fairy in unit 3 is not assumed to be either numbered singer in unit 4. ALL belongs to craftsmen in unit 2 and fairies in unit 5. Moonshine, Wall and Lion labels do not turn ordinary moonlight, walls or animals elsewhere into characters.

Original inferred references: Carthage queen = Dido, false Trojan = Aeneas, Thracian singer = Orpheus. The players’ Limander/Helen and Shafalus/Procrus forms are explained by the editorial notes in [this public-domain annotated edition](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/A_midsummer_night%27s_dream_%28IA_midsummernightsd04shak%29.pdf). Bottom’s invented plant parents are explicitly framed as a joke. Jack/Jill are a generic proverbial couple, not distinct narrative people. The vestal is left unidentified rather than asserting an Elizabeth I interpretation as fact. Proposed parent roles without names are not merged with the actual parents in the outer play.

Modern edition omits the proper references Hiems, Neptune, Philomel, Aurora, Jove and Hecate; descriptive replacements are not mechanically treated as names. Source text remains unchanged.

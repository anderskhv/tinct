# A Midsummer Night’s Dream: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Hermia · central

Egeus’s daughter, in love with Lysander.

## Lysander · central

The young Athenian whom Hermia loves.

## Helena · central

Hermia’s friend, in love with Demetrius.

## Demetrius · central

The suitor Egeus wants Hermia to marry.

After source chapter 7, paragraph 55: The young Athenian who now declares his love for Helena.

## Theseus · supporting

Duke of Athens, engaged to Hippolyta.

## Hippolyta · supporting

Queen of the Amazons, engaged to Theseus.

## Egeus · supporting

Hermia’s father.

## Philostrate · supporting

Theseus’s master of revels, responsible for entertainments.

## Nick Bottom · major

The weaver in Peter Quince’s amateur acting company.

After source chapter 5, paragraph 41: The weaver and amateur actor, now wearing an ass’s head through Puck’s magic.

After source chapter 7, paragraph 30: The weaver and amateur actor, restored to his human appearance.

## Peter Quince · supporting

The carpenter who organizes the amateur players.

## Francis Flute · supporting

The bellows-mender in Quince’s acting company.

## Tom Snout · supporting

The tinker in Quince’s acting company.

## Snug · supporting

The joiner in Quince’s acting company.

## Robin Starveling · supporting

The tailor in Quince’s acting company.

## Oberon · major

King of the fairies and husband of Titania.

## Titania · major

Queen of the fairies and wife of Oberon.

## Puck · major

Oberon’s mischievous fairy servant, also called Robin Goodfellow.

## The fairy · supporting

A fairy serving Titania who meets Puck in the wood.

## The first singing fairy · supporting

One of the fairies singing Titania to sleep.

## The second fairy · supporting

Another fairy attending Titania as she sleeps.

## Peaseblossom · supporting

One of Titania’s attendant fairies.

## Cobweb · supporting

One of Titania’s attendant fairies.

## Moth · supporting

One of Titania’s attendant fairies.

## Mustardseed · supporting

One of Titania’s attendant fairies.

## Titania’s fairies · supporting

The fairy attendants who sing and serve Titania.

## The amateur players · supporting

Quince’s company of Athenian craftsmen.

## The Indian boy · supporting

The young child in Titania’s care, whom Oberon wants as his attendant.

## The boy’s mother · reference

The mortal woman from India who was Titania’s friend and follower.

## The Indian king · reference

The unnamed king from whom Puck says the boy was taken.

## Pyramus · supporting

The male lover in the amateur players’ story of Pyramus and Thisbe.

## Thisbe · supporting

The female lover in the amateur players’ story of Pyramus and Thisbe.

## Wall · supporting

The wall personified as a speaking part in the amateur play.

## Lion · supporting

The lion portrayed in the amateur play.

## Moonshine · supporting

Moonlight personified as a part in the amateur play.

## Prologue · supporting

The speaker who introduces the amateur play.

## Nedar · reference

Helena’s father.

## Cupid · reference

The god of love, represented with a bow and arrows.

## Venus · reference

The goddess of love.

## Diana · reference

The goddess of the hunt and chastity, associated with the moon.

## Dido · reference

The queen of Carthage in the story of Aeneas.

## Aeneas · reference

The Trojan hero loved by Dido.

## Hercules · reference

The legendary hero famed for his strength; Bottom calls him Ercles.

## Apollo · reference

The god associated with music and the sun; Bottom calls him Phibbus.

## The Fates · reference

The mythological powers who govern mortal destiny.

## Corin · reference

The shepherd identity Titania says Oberon has assumed.

## Phillida · reference

The woman Titania says Oberon courted as Corin.

## Perigenia · reference

A woman associated with Theseus in Greek legend.

## Aegles · reference

One of the women Oberon names among Theseus’s former loves.

## Ariadne · reference

The Cretan princess associated with Theseus.

## Antiopa · reference

An Amazon associated with Theseus in Greek legend.

## Hiems · reference

Winter imagined as an aged person.

## Neptune · reference

The Roman god of the sea.

## Daphne · reference

The nymph pursued by Apollo in Greek mythology.

## Philomel · reference

A poetic name for the nightingale, recalling the myth of Philomela.

## Ninus · reference

The legendary Assyrian king whose tomb is the lovers’ meeting place in the amateur play.

## Mistress Squash · reference

The mother Bottom jokingly gives Peaseblossom, playing on the fairy’s name.

## Master Peascod · reference

The father Bottom jokingly gives Peaseblossom; Peascod means a pea pod.

## Aurora · reference

The Roman goddess of dawn.

## Cadmus · reference

The legendary founder of Thebes, recalled as a hunting companion.

## Saint Valentine · reference

The saint whose feast day was associated with birds pairing and lovers.

## The Centaurs · reference

The half-human, half-horse creatures of Greek myth.

## The Bacchanals · reference

The frenzied followers of the wine god, mentioned in a proposed entertainment.

## Orpheus · reference

The legendary musician meant by the Thracian singer in the proposed entertainment.

## The Muses · reference

The nine goddesses of poetry, music and the arts.

## Learning · reference

Learning personified in one of the proposed entertainments.

## Jove · reference

Another name for Jupiter, king of the Roman gods.

## Leander · reference

The mythic lover of Hero, called Limander in the amateur play.

## Hero · reference

The woman loved by Leander in Greek legend, mistakenly called Helen by Thisbe.

## Cephalus · reference

The husband of Procris in Greek legend, called Shafalus by the players.

## Procris · reference

The wife of Cephalus in Greek legend, called Procrus by the players.

## Hecate · reference

The goddess associated with night and magic.

## Helen of Troy · reference

The legendary beauty invoked by Theseus.

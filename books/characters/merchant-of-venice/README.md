# The Merchant of Venice recognition cards

Complete twenty-scene scope, 779 paragraphs and 90 entries in each English edition; 1,197 original and 1,202 modern exact mentions. Source bytes unchanged. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. Runtime registration pending.

Antonio, Portia and Shylock are Central. Bassanio, Nerissa, Gratiano, Jessica, Lorenzo and Launcelet are Major. Other local participants are Supporting; discussed suitors, family references and historical, biblical and classical allusions are Reference. Ordinary identity leads: Antonio is the merchant, Portia the heiress, Shylock Jessica’s father, and Nerissa Portia’s waiting-woman. Shylock’s description does not adopt the other characters’ accusations or insults.

- Portia and Bassanio’s marriage becomes available after 16:1; Nerissa and Gratiano’s after 16:12; Jessica and Lorenzo’s after 17:6. Proposals, vows and plans for ceremonies do not count as completed marriages. Launcelet’s new employment is available after Bassanio accepts him at 5:50. These are short identity updates, not chapter recaps.
- The earlier unknown court messenger at 18:22 is not identified as Nerissa before the explicit stage entrance at 18:26. The lawyer recommended at 18:36 and named Balthazar at 18:40 is not identified as Portia before her explicit entrance at 18:42. Earlier appearance entries then reveal their identities; Portia’s and Nerissa’s own cards acquire the disguise facts at the same gates. The reader need not wait until the characters discover the truth in scene 20.
- Balthazar, Portia’s household servant in scene 16, is distinct from the courtroom alias. Doctor Bellario is a separate person, already identified as Portia’s cousin in the source. Later references to the doctor and clerk resolve to Portia and Nerissa where the audience already knows the disguise. The invented legal scholar’s supposed education is attributed to Bellario’s letter, not presented as a real biography.
- At 1:34 the first Portia is the heiress and the second is the historical Portia, Cato’s daughter and Brutus’s wife. The three Venetian names Solanio, Salarino and Salerio remain separate. The local source introduces Salerio in the court’s stage direction but labels the messenger announcement at 18:22 SALARINO in both editions. That inherited label discrepancy is recorded for release review; this package preserves the explicit named tokens rather than silently merging identities or changing source bytes.
- Launcelet Gobbo and Old Gobbo are separate. The son’s self-address as Gobbo at 5:1 is not assigned to his father. Margery and Dobbin remain respectively mother and horse. The German suitor is the Duke of Saxony’s nephew, not the duke himself. Morocco is separate from the Moorish woman in Lorenzo’s later report.
- Named minor references remain, including Leah, Chus, the Marquis of Montferrat, the Persian figures in Morocco’s boast, and the suitors discussed at Belmont. Leah’s identity follows the scholarly reading as Shylock’s wife; no narrative of her death or Jessica’s upbringing is invented. The court’s demand that Shylock convert does not erase his Jewish family identity or warrant an invented completed conversion ceremony.
- The biblical figures, the Roman Portia’s family, Heraclitus, Pythagoras and the mythical lovers remain distinct from the participants. Rebecca and Hesione are identified at the source’s explicit relational descriptions. Daniel is the biblical comparison, not an additional judge. The modern Nazarene, Sibyl and messenger variants are preserved. Lord in a gentleman’s title is not a reference to God. Andrew the ship, Erebus the place and Colchos the place are not extra people.

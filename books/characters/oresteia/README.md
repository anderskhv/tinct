# Oresteia recognition cards

All three plays: 26 local sections and 771 paragraphs in each English edition. 103 entries per edition; 979 original / 986 modern exact mentions. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. No edition changes or runtime registration.

Agamemnon, Clytemnestra and Orestes are Central across the trilogy. Cassandra, Aegisthus, Electra, the three choruses, Apollo and Athena are Major. Speaking helpers, the individual elders, the nurse, servants and jurors are Supporting. Family history and mythological allusions are Reference.

Ordinary identity appears immediately, including Orestes's parents, Clytemnestra's children, Cassandra's status as Trojan princess and captive, and Kilissa's occupation as Orestes's nurse. Orestes's Central category is withheld until his first direct appearance (after 11:0), Apollo's Major category until 19:5, Athena's until 22:0, and the Furies' until Orestes explicitly reports their presence at 17:100. These are category gates, not withheld names or deliberately vague bodies. All paragraph indices here are zero-based.

Every body is one recognition reminder. No later chapter recap, murder outcome, acquittal or final settlement is attached. Clytemnestra's ghost resolves to her existing identity, not a second invented person.

## Binding review

- CHORUS means the Argive elders in sections 1–10, the enslaved libation bearers in 11–18, and the Furies in 19–26. Eleven separately labelled elders in 10:9–19 are individually selectable; no unsupported names or personalities are invented. The chorus leader and first Fury have their own speaking-role entries. The closing CHANT is the Athenian escort, not the Furies.
- SLAVE at 17:2 is the doorkeeper. The servant raising the alarm at 17:50/53 is kept separate. Kilissa is the nurse throughout; the purple-robed attendants and Athenian jurors are distinct groups.
- Scylla at 9:45 is the sea monster; Scylla at 16:2 is the king's daughter in the Minos story. Their identities are not merged.
- Original Atrides in 3:16 means Agamemnon; 4:5/7 means Menelaus. The modern text spells these references out. Paris/Alexander and Apollo/Loxias/Phoebus share identities. Phoebe is a separate Titan goddess.
- Inherited OSESTES, CLYTEMNESTSA and AEgisthus spellings are recognized exactly. Neither source is corrected by the card package.
- The Alcmena name and her son reference remain separately selectable. Likewise Pheres and the mortal in his household (Admetus). The latter identification is cross-checked against the [Smyth translation's note on Eumenides 723](https://www.theoi.com/Text/AeschylusEumenides.html).
- Atè / Ate are the same personification. Original Zephyr corresponds to modern West Wind. Earth, Night, Justice and the Sun are bound only in reviewed divine contexts; ordinary night, soil and legal words are not broadly matched.
- Gorgons and Harpies are mythical comparisons, not alternative identities for the actual Furies. Triton, Scamander, Hades and the Pleiades in geographical/astronomical contexts do not become cast entries.
- Pleisthenes receives only the ancestral identification warranted by this text; no disputed extra genealogy is added. Alluded children and fathers remain short, explicit relationship references rather than speculative biographies.

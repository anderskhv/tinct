# The Oresteia: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Agamemnon · central

The king of Argos, commander of the Greek expedition to Troy, and husband of Clytemnestra.

## Clytemnestra · central

Agamemnon’s wife and queen of Argos, mother of Orestes, Electra and Iphigenia.

## Orestes · central

The son of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, and brother of Electra.

## Cassandra · major

The Trojan princess and prophetess brought back as Agamemnon’s captive.

## Aegisthus · major

Clytemnestra’s lover and the son of Thyestes, from the rival branch of Agamemnon’s family.

## Electra · major

The daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, and sister of Orestes.

## Apollo · major

The god of prophecy, worshipped at Delphi and also called Loxias or Phoebus.

## Athena · major

The patron goddess of Athens, daughter of Zeus, also called Pallas.

## The Furies · major

The ancient avenging goddesses who punish violations of family bonds.

## The Argive elders · major

The elders of Argos who form the chorus in Agamemnon.

## The libation bearers · major

The enslaved women of the palace who accompany Electra and form the second play’s chorus.

## The watchman · supporting

The palace watchman waiting for the beacon that will signal news from Troy.

## The herald · supporting

The twelve Athenian citizens assembled by Athena to hear the case.

The women and children who form the ceremonial procession at the end of the trilogy.

## The palace maidens · supporting

Clytemnestra’s attendants carrying the purple robes for Agamemnon’s reception.

## Atreus · reference

The father of Agamemnon and Menelaus, and brother of Thyestes.

## Thyestes · reference

Atreus’s brother and rival, and father of Aegisthus.

## Thyestes’s children · reference

The children of Thyestes recalled in the account of the family’s older crimes.

## Menelaus · reference

Agamemnon’s brother and Helen’s husband, a leader of the Greek expedition.

## Helen · reference

Menelaus’s wife, whose departure with Paris lies behind the Trojan War.

## Paris · reference

The Trojan prince who takes Helen from Menelaus, also called Alexander.

## Priam · reference

The king of Troy and father of Paris and Cassandra.

## Tyndareus · reference

Clytemnestra’s father.

## Leda · reference

Clytemnestra’s mother.

## Calchas · reference

The seer who interprets signs for the Greek army at Troy.

## Iphigenia · reference

The daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra recalled in the account of the expedition’s departure.

## Odysseus · reference

The Greek commander praised by Agamemnon for his loyalty during the war.

## Geryon · reference

The three-bodied mythical figure used in Clytemnestra’s comparison.

## Strophius · reference

The ruler in Phocis who is caring for Orestes, and father of Pylades.

## Asclepius · reference

The divine healer famed for restoring the dead to life.

## Heracles · reference

The hero, son of Alcmena, cited as someone who endured servitude.

## Alcmena · reference

The mother of Heracles.

## Itys · reference

The child mourned in the mythical nightingale’s song.

## Scylla, the sea monster · reference

The monster feared by sailors, used in Cassandra’s comparison.

## Scylla, the king’s daughter · reference

The daughter who betrays her father for Minos in the chorus’s story.

## Chryseis · reference

The Trojan captive associated with Agamemnon, invoked as a type of his wartime lovers.

## Tantalus · reference

An ancestor of the royal house of Atreus.

## Pleisthenes · reference

An ancestral name used for the royal family of Agamemnon.

## Pelops · reference

The father of Atreus and Thyestes, an ancestor of the royal house.

## Orpheus · reference

The legendary musician whose song charms its listeners.

## Althea · reference

The mother in the story of a son’s life bound to a burning brand.

## Althea’s son · reference

The son whose life is bound to a piece of wood in the chorus’s story.

## Minos · reference

The Cretan king for whom Scylla betrays her father.

## Scylla’s father · reference

The king whose magical lock of hair is cut by his daughter Scylla.

## Perseus · reference

The mythical hero invoked as a model of resolve.

## Delphos · reference

The legendary king who welcomes Apollo to Delphi.

## Pentheus · reference

The king associated with opposition to Dionysus, recalled by the priestess.

## Phineus · reference

The king whose food is seized by the Harpies in the mythical comparison.

## Ixion · reference

The mythical figure whose purification is recalled as a precedent.

## Theseus · reference

The legendary Athenian king associated with the conflict against the Amazons.

## Aegeus · reference

Theseus’s father.

## Pheres · reference

The king whose household is recalled in the story of Apollo and the Fates.

## Erechtheus · reference

An ancient king and hero of Athens.

## Cranaos · reference

A legendary early king whose descendants stand for the Athenians.

## Zeus · reference

The chief Olympian god, invoked as guardian of justice and hospitality.

## Pan · reference

The god of shepherds and wild places.

## Artemis · reference

The goddess of the hunt, invoked in the account of the Greek fleet.

## Hephaestus · reference

The god of fire, named in the description of the beacon signal.

## Hermes · reference

The messenger god, son of Zeus and Maia, who also guides souls.

## Ares · reference

The god of war.

## Persephone · reference

The queen of the underworld.

## Maia · reference

Hermes’s mother.

## Themis · reference

## Phoebe · reference

The Titan goddess who passes the Delphic oracle to Apollo.

The river god to whom Orestes dedicates a lock of hair.

# The Taming of the Shrew: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Katherina Minola · central

Baptista’s elder daughter and Bianca’s sister; one of the play’s two central characters.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 1: Baptista’s elder daughter and Bianca’s sister.

After source chapter 5, paragraph 62: Petruchio’s wife, Baptista’s elder daughter and Bianca’s sister.

## Petruchio · central

A gentleman from Verona seeking a wealthy wife; one of the play’s two central characters.

After source chapter 5, paragraph 62: Katherina’s husband, a gentleman from Verona.

## Bianca Minola · major

Baptista’s younger daughter and Katherina’s sister, courted by several men.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 76: Lucentio’s wife, Baptista’s younger daughter and Katherina’s sister.

## Lucentio · major

Vincentio’s son, a young gentleman who comes to Padua to study.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 38: Vincentio’s son, courting Bianca while disguised as the tutor Cambio.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 76: Bianca’s husband and Vincentio’s son; formerly disguised as Cambio.

## Tranio · major

Lucentio’s resourceful servant and companion.

After source chapter 1, paragraph 63: Lucentio’s servant, posing as his master.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 81: Lucentio’s servant, who impersonated him during the courtship.

## Baptista Minola · major

The wealthy Paduan father of Katherina and Bianca.

## Gremio · major

An elderly, wealthy suitor of Bianca.

## Hortensio · major

A suitor of Bianca and a friend of Petruchio.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 28: Bianca’s suitor, disguised as the music teacher Licio.

After source chapter 7, paragraph 18: Petruchio’s friend, formerly Bianca’s suitor and the disguised tutor Licio.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 2: The widow’s husband and Petruchio’s friend; formerly disguised as Licio.

## Aristotle · reference

The Greek philosopher whom Tranio mentions when discussing Lucentio’s studies.

## Ovid · reference

The Roman poet of love and mythology, contrasted with Aristotle in Tranio’s advice.

## Socrates · reference

The Greek philosopher mentioned through his marriage to Xanthippe.

## Xanthippe · reference

Socrates’s wife, traditionally portrayed as sharp-tempered.

## Lucretia · reference

The Roman woman traditionally celebrated for chastity, invoked in Petruchio’s comparison.

## Minerva · reference

The Roman goddess of wisdom and the arts, invoked in praise of Bianca.

## Anna · reference

Dido’s sister and confidante in Virgil’s Aeneid.

## Dido · reference

The queen of Carthage in Virgil’s Aeneid; Anna’s sister.

## Agenor · reference

The mythological king who is Europa’s father.

## Europa · reference

Agenor’s daughter, whom Jupiter carries to Crete in Greek myth.

## Jupiter · reference

The Roman king of the gods, called Jove.

## Florentius · reference

The knight in the medieval tale who promises to marry an apparently ugly old woman.

## Florentius’s bride · reference

The apparently ugly woman whom Florentius agrees to marry in the medieval tale.

## The Sibyl · reference

The ancient prophetess invoked as an example of extreme age.

## Leda · reference

The mythological mother of Helen of Troy.

## Helen of Troy · reference

Leda’s daughter, famed for her beauty and many suitors.

## Paris · reference

The Trojan prince associated with Helen of Troy.

## Hercules · reference

The mythological hero famed for his twelve labors; also called Alcides.

## Diana · reference

The Roman goddess of the hunt and chastity, called Dian in the original edition.

## Griselda · reference

The patient wife of medieval storytelling, called Grissel in Petruchio’s comparison.

## Priam · reference

The king of Troy named in the Latin lesson.

## Ajax · reference

The Greek warrior at Troy whom Lucentio identifies by the epithet Æacides.

## Aeacus · reference

Ajax’s grandfather in Greek myth, the ancestor referred to in the Latin lesson.

## Jack in the song · reference

The boy addressed in the song fragment quoted by Grumio.

## God · reference

The Christian deity invoked in the characters’ prayers and oaths.

## Saint Anne · reference

The saint traditionally identified as the Virgin Mary’s mother, invoked by Sly.

## Saint George · reference

The saint invoked in Petruchio’s oath.

## Saint James · reference

The saint invoked as Saint Jamy in Biondello’s oath.

## The Bentivoglio family · reference

The family from which Lucentio says his father Vincentio descends.

## The Duke of Venice · reference

The Venetian ruler named in Tranio’s invented story about a quarrel with Mantua.

## The Duke of Mantua · reference

The Mantuan ruler named in Tranio’s invented story about a quarrel with Venice.

## The Duke of Padua · reference

The ruler in whose name Vincentio demands an arrest.

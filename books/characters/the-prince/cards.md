# The Prince: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Lorenzo de’ Medici · reference

Lorenzo di Piero de’ Medici, the young Florentine ruler to whom Machiavelli dedicates the book.

## Niccolò Machiavelli · reference

The Florentine diplomat and author of this advice on political power.

## Francesco Sforza · reference

The military commander who became Duke of Milan; Lodovico’s father.

## Ferdinand of Aragon · reference

Ferdinand II of Aragon, the Spanish king repeatedly used as an example of political strategy.

## Pope Julius II · reference

Giuliano della Rovere, the pope whose military and political campaigns Machiavelli examines.

## Louis XII · reference

The French king whose attempts to hold territory in Italy Machiavelli analyzes.

## Lodovico Sforza · reference

The Duke of Milan known as Lodovico Moro; Francesco Sforza’s son and Beatrice d’Este’s husband.

## Beatrice d’Este · reference

Lodovico Sforza’s wife, the duchess of Milan.

## Antiochus III · reference

The Seleucid king whose intervention in Greece brought him into conflict with Rome.

## Philip V of Macedon · reference

The Macedonian king who fought Rome and was defeated by Flamininus.

## Philip II of Macedon · reference

Alexander the Great’s father, the Macedonian king cited on military power.

## Charles VIII · reference

The French king whose invasion of Italy preceded the campaigns of Louis XII.

## Pope Alexander VI · reference

Rodrigo Borgia, the pope and father of Cesare Borgia.

## Jeanne of France · reference

Louis XII’s first wife and Louis XI’s daughter.

## Louis XI · reference

The French king who was Charles VII’s son and Jeanne’s father.

## Anne of Brittany · reference

The duchess of Brittany who married Charles VIII and later Louis XII.

## Georges d’Amboise · reference

The French cardinal and archbishop of Rouen, an adviser to Louis XII.

## Cesare Borgia · reference

Pope Alexander VI’s son, the military ruler also called Duke Valentino.

## Alexander the Great · reference

The Macedonian conqueror and son of Philip II.

## Darius III · reference

The Persian king whose empire Alexander the Great conquered.

## Darius I · reference

The Persian king cited for installing dependent rulers in Greek territories.

## Pyrrhus · reference

The king of Epirus cited in the discussion of holding conquered territory.

## Cyrus the Great · reference

The founder of the Persian Empire, used as a model of a ruler who establishes a new state.

## Girolamo Savonarola · reference

The Florentine friar and religious reformer cited as an unarmed prophet.

## Hiero II · reference

The ruler of Syracuse used as an example of building power through one’s own forces.

## Bianca Maria Visconti · reference

Francesco Sforza’s wife and Filippo Maria Visconti’s daughter.

## Filippo Maria Visconti · reference

The Duke of Milan whose daughter Bianca Maria married Francesco Sforza.

## Vitellozzo Vitelli · reference

The military commander, brother of Pagolo Vitelli and associate of Oliverotto da Fermo.

## Paolo Orsini · reference

The Orsini commander who mediates the reconciliation with Cesare Borgia; called Signor Pagolo.

## Ramiro d’Orco · reference

Cesare Borgia’s governor in the Romagna, also called Ramiro de Lorqua.

## Raffaello Riario · reference

The cardinal of San Giorgio, named among those injured by Cesare Borgia.

## Ascanio Sforza · reference

The cardinal of the Milanese Sforza family named among Cesare Borgia’s opponents.

## Giovanni Colonna · reference

The Colonna cardinal named among those with grounds to resent Cesare Borgia.

## Agathocles · reference

The ruler of Syracuse used in the discussion of acquiring power through violence.

## Hamilcar · reference

The Carthaginian commander in Sicily with whom Agathocles reaches an understanding.

## Oliverotto da Fermo · reference

The military commander and nephew of Giovanni Fogliani, discussed as a ruler who seizes power violently.

## Giovanni Fogliani · reference

Oliverotto da Fermo’s maternal uncle, who brings him up.

## Paolo Vitelli · reference

The Florentine military commander called Pagolo Vitelli; Vitellozzo’s brother.

## L. Arthur Burd · reference

The editor and commentator on The Prince cited in the translation’s notes.

## Nabis · reference

The ruler of Sparta used in the argument about securing popular support.

The Florentine political leader cited as an example of misplaced reliance on popular support.

## Titus Quinctius Flamininus · reference

The Roman commander who fought Philip V and Nabis.

The pope cited in the account of papal authority and Rome’s rival baronial families.

Giovanni de’ Medici, the Medici pope reigning when Machiavelli writes.

The English writer whose history of Henry VII is quoted in an editorial note.

The English king who is the subject of Bacon’s history cited in a note.

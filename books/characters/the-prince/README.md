# The Prince recognition package

Dedication, all 26 chapters and surviving editorial notes in both English editions: 145 entries and 504 exact mentions each. All are Reference: this is political argument illustrated through historical people, not a novel with a central cast. Categories appear next to every entry in cards.md. Cards identify rulers, family relationships, commanders, writers and religious/literary figures concisely; they do not paraphrase every argument.

Namesakes: Alexander VI / Alexander the Great / Severus Alexander; Philip II / Philip V; Darius III / Darius I; Louis XII / Louis XI; Charles VIII / Charles VII / Charles the Bold; Annibale I / Annibale II; Muzio / Francesco / Sforza family; Paolo Orsini / Paolo Vitelli; Colonna cardinal / family; the two Biancas. Caesar at 20:17 is a title granted to Albinus, not Julius Caesar. The deliberately unnamed ruler at 19:12 stays unbound; the next editorial note names Ferdinand. The generic King of France in the comparison of constitutions is not forced onto a particular king. Generic pope, king of Naples, ruler, hypothetical prince, military population and abstract Fortune labels are not assigned arbitrary individual identities.

Both edition sources were reviewed, including the spelling changes Beatrice d’Este / d'Este, Forli / Forlì, Nicolo / Niccolò, Bernabo / Bernabò and Domnia / Donnina. The 1484/1510 Duke of Ferrara example spans two Este dukes: it receives a dynasty card rather than falsely assigning both events to one man. The later Louis XII alliance list refers to Ercole I. Sante Bentivoglio is identified at the Florentine relative raised as a blacksmith's son. The first father-Sforza in 13:7 is Muzio; the later Sforza followed by Francesco is the son. 13:15 refers to Muzio.

Inherited source issues require release review before enabling: 13:9 calls Joanna II the widow of Ladislaus (her brother); 27:4 calls the future Clement VII Giuliano rather than Giulio. 13:12 also gives Colleoni's death year as 1457; no dates are repeated in his card. Sources remain unchanged in this content lane. A source repair invalidates hashes and requires rebuilding bindings. The note's reference to an appended Borgia narrative is inherited; that appendix is not present in these edition files, so no invented coverage of it is claimed.

[Treccani's Joanna II entry](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/tag/giovanna-i-di-napoli/) identifies Ladislaus as her brother; [its Clement VII entry](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/papa-clemente-vii_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/) identifies Giulio.

# King Lear: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## King Lear · central

The aging king of Britain and father of Goneril, Regan and Cordelia; the play’s central character.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 1: The aging king and father of Goneril, Regan and Cordelia.

## The Earl of Kent · major

A nobleman in Lear’s service.

After source chapter 1, paragraph 59: Lear’s loyal nobleman, banished for defending Cordelia.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 1: The banished earl serving Lear in disguise.

After source chapter 26, paragraph 129: The Earl of Kent, who served Lear in disguise as Caius.

## The Earl of Gloucester · major

A nobleman at Lear’s court, father of Edgar and Edmund.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 62: Edgar and Edmund’s father, now blinded.

## Edmund · major

Gloucester’s younger son, born outside marriage, and Edgar’s half-brother.

After source chapter 14, paragraph 8: Gloucester’s younger son, now given his father’s earldom by Cornwall.

## Edgar · major

Gloucester’s elder son and heir, Edmund’s half-brother.

After source chapter 8, paragraph 2: Gloucester’s elder son, hiding as the beggar Poor Tom.

After source chapter 22, paragraph 1: Gloucester’s elder son, now dressed as a peasant after his disguise as Poor Tom.

After source chapter 26, paragraph 76: Gloucester’s elder son, who has revealed himself after fighting Edmund.

## Goneril · major

Lear’s eldest daughter and the Duke of Albany’s wife.

## Regan · major

Lear’s second daughter and the Duke of Cornwall’s wife.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 24: Lear’s second daughter and Cornwall’s widow.

## Cordelia · major

Lear’s youngest daughter, sister to Goneril and Regan.

After source chapter 1, paragraph 83: Lear’s youngest daughter, chosen as wife by the King of France.

After source chapter 19, paragraph 1: Lear’s youngest daughter and the Queen of France.

## The Duke of Cornwall · major

## The King of France · supporting

# The Aeneid: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Aeneas · central

Son of Venus and Anchises, leader of the Trojan survivors and the man the poem follows from burning Troy to Italy.

## Turnus · central

King of the Rutulians, betrothed to Lavinia before the Trojans came, and Aeneas’s enemy through the second half of the poem.

## Dido · major

Queen and founder of Carthage, a widow who fled Tyre after her brother murdered her husband. The poem also calls her Eliza.

## Ascanius · major

Aeneas’s young son, also called Iulus, from whom Rome’s Julian line is traced.

## Anchises · major

Aeneas’s father, carried out of Troy on his son’s shoulders.

## Venus · major

Goddess of love and Aeneas’s mother, who works constantly on his behalf.

## Juno · major

Queen of the gods, who hates the Trojans and drives the whole action against them. The poem also calls her Saturnia.

## Jove · major

King of the gods, who holds to the fate promised the Trojans. The poem also calls him Jupiter and the Thunderer.

## Latinus · major

King of Latium, Lavinia’s father, who would give his daughter to Aeneas.

## Lavinia · major

Latinus’s only daughter and heiress, promised first to Turnus and then to Aeneas.

## Amata · major

Latinus’s queen, who wants Turnus for a son-in-law and is driven mad by the Fury.

## Evander · major

The old Arcadian king at Pallanteum on the future site of Rome, who allies with Aeneas.

## Pallas · major

Evander’s young son, sent to war under Aeneas’s protection.

## Pallas · major

Minerva under her Greek by-name; the goddess who helped ruin Troy and whose temple and image recur through the poem.

## Pallas · reference

Evander’s great-grandsire, after whom the town of Pallanteum is named.

## Mezentius · major

The exiled Etruscan tyrant, a contemner of the gods, who fights for Turnus.

## Lausus · major

Mezentius’s son, the handsomest of the Italian youth, who dies defending his father.

## Camilla · major

The Volscian warrior queen, raised to the hunt and the spear, who leads her troops for Turnus.

## Achates · major

Aeneas’s closest companion, called faithful throughout.

## The Sibyl · major

The prophetess of Cumae, Apollo’s and Diana’s priestess, who guides Aeneas through the underworld. Her name is Deiphobe.

## Priam · major

The old king of Troy, killed at his own altar on the night the city fell.

## Hector · major

Priam’s son and Troy’s great defender, already dead when the poem opens; his ghost warns Aeneas to flee.

## Creusa · major

Aeneas’s first wife, lost in the sack of Troy; her ghost sends him on to Italy.

## Anna · major

Dido’s sister and confidante.

## Nisus · major

A Trojan, son of Hyrtacus, famous for his devotion to Euryalus; the two go out together on the night raid.

## Euryalus · major

A Trojan boy of great beauty, Nisus’s inseparable friend.

## Acestes · major

King of the Sicilian Trojans, born of Trojan parentage, who twice receives Aeneas’s fleet.

## Mnestheus · major

A Trojan captain, founder of the Memmian line, who commands in Aeneas’s absence.

## Serestus · supporting

A Trojan captain who walks the camp with Mnestheus. Dryden spells him Serestus once and Seresthus elsewhere.

## Cloanthus · supporting

A Trojan captain, founder of the Cluentian line, who wins the boat race.

## Ilioneus · supporting

The eldest and gravest of the Trojan captains, who speaks for them to Dido and to Latinus.

## Sergesthus · supporting

A Trojan captain, founder of the Sergian line, whose galley is wrecked on the rocks in the boat race.

## Messapus · major

A Latin captain, Neptune’s son, whom no steel can wound.

## Drances · major

A Latin elder, a powerful speaker and Turnus’s bitter opponent in council.

## Juturna · major

Turnus’s sister, a nymph made immortal by Jove, who keeps her brother from his death as long as she can.

## Tarchon · supporting

The Etruscan chief who brings his people to Aeneas.

## Helenus · major

Priam’s son, a seer, now reigning in Epirus with Andromache; he tells Aeneas his route.

## Andromache · supporting

Hector’s widow, taken by Pyrrhus and afterwards married to Helenus.

## Palinurus · major

Aeneas’s helmsman, lost overboard off the Italian coast.

## Misenus · supporting

Aeneas’s trumpeter, once Hector’s, who challenges the sea gods and drowns for it.

## Sinon · major

The Greek who lets himself be captured and talks Troy into taking in the horse.

## Laocoon · major

Neptune’s priest at Troy, who warns against the horse and is killed with his sons by serpents. The modern edition spells him Laocoön.

## Pyrrhus · supporting

Achilles’ son, who kills Priam at the altar; also called Neoptolemus.

## Ulysses · supporting

The Greek whose cunning the Trojans blame for their ruin; the poem also calls him Ithacus.

## Achilles · supporting

The greatest of the Greeks at Troy, dead before the poem begins; also called Pelides.

## Diomede · supporting

A Greek captain at Troy, now settled in Italy, who refuses to join the war against Aeneas. The poem spells him Diomede and Diomedes.

## Aeolus · supporting

King of the winds, who looses the storm at Juno’s request.

## Neptune · major

God of the sea, who calms the storm and later demands one life for the fleet’s safe passage.

## Mercury · supporting

The messenger god, sent to move Aeneas out of Carthage. The poem also calls him Cyllenius and, in the modern edition, Hermes.

## Iris · supporting

Juno’s messenger, who comes down on the rainbow.

## Alecto · major

The Fury Juno raises out of hell to break the peace.

The smith of the gods, who forges Aeneas’s shield at Venus’s asking. Called the Lemnian god and Mulciber.

## Apollo · major

God of prophecy and archery, whose oracles direct the Trojans; called Phoebus and the Delian god.

Priam’s young son, dragged behind his chariot in the frescoes.

## Sarpedon · reference

The Lycian who fell at Troy, named as father of the brothers who hold the wall.

## Glaucus · reference

One of Antenor’s sons, seen among the shades of the Trojan dead.

## Medon · reference

One of Antenor’s sons, seen among the shades of the Trojan dead.

## Antenor · reference

The Trojan who escaped and founded Padua, cited as a precedent by Venus.

## Harpalyce · reference

The Thracian huntress Venus is compared to.

## Iopas · supporting

The long-haired bard who sings at Dido’s feast.

## Cupid · supporting

Venus’s son, sent to Carthage in Ascanius’s shape to fire Dido with love.

## The Amazons · reference

The warrior women of the frescoes and of Camilla’s comparison.

## The Cyclops · supporting

The one-eyed giants of Sicily; Polyphemus is their most famous. Dryden spells the singular Polypheme.

## Polyphemus · supporting

The blinded Cyclops whom the Trojans see groping down to the sea. Dryden calls him Polypheme.

## Scylla · supporting

The monster whose waist ends in dogs, waiting on the right of the strait.

## Charybdis · supporting

The whirlpool on the left of the strait, which swallows and spews the sea three times a day.

## The Chimaera · reference

The fire-breathing monster, seen at the gate of hell and blazoned on Turnus’s crest.

## The Centaurs · reference

The double-shaped creatures who besiege the gate of hell.

## Briareus · reference

The hundred-handed monster at the gate of hell.

## The Gorgons · reference

Monsters at the gate of hell.

## The Hydra · reference

The many-headed monster at the gate of hell, and in Lerna’s lake.

## Typhoeus · reference

A monster named among the terrors Hercules did not fear.

## The Trojans · major

Aeneas’s people, the survivors of Troy; also called Dardans, Phrygians and Teucrians.

## The Latins · major

Latinus’s people, whose land the Trojans are fated to hold.

## The Rutulians · major

Turnus’s people, of Ardea.

## The Tyrians · supporting

Dido’s people, the settlers of Carthage.

## The Greeks · supporting

The army that took Troy; the poem also calls them Grecians, Argives and Danaans.

## The Arcadians · supporting

Evander’s people at Pallanteum.

## The Tuscans · supporting

The Etruscan people who rise against Mezentius and join Aeneas.

## The Volscians · supporting

Camilla’s people.

## The Italians · supporting

The peoples of Italy taken together; also called Ausonians and Oenotrians.

## The Auruncans · reference

An Italian people from the hills who join Turnus.

## The Equicolae · reference

A rough Italian people under Ufens; the modern edition spells them Equicoli.

## The Faliscans · reference

An Italian people called to the war.

## The Sicanians · reference

An old people of Sicily and Italy.

## The Numidians · reference

An African people on Carthage’s frontier.

## The Gauls · reference

The people whose night attack on the Capitol the geese betrayed, shown on Aeneas’s shield.

## The Scythians · reference

A far northern people, named among those Rome will face.

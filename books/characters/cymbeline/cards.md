# Cymbeline: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Imogen · central

Cymbeline’s daughter, who has married Posthumus against her father’s will.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 39: Cymbeline’s daughter, leaving Britain in a boy’s clothes at Pisanio’s advice.

After source chapter 19, paragraph 19: Cymbeline’s daughter, travelling as a boy under the name Fidele.

After source chapter 29, paragraph 91: Cymbeline’s daughter, known again at her father’s court.

## Posthumus Leonatus · central

A gentleman raised at Cymbeline’s court and married to Imogen; his surname is Leonatus.

After source chapter 25, paragraph 2: Imogen’s husband, come back to Britain in the Roman army but meaning to fight for Britain as a peasant.

After source chapter 27, paragraph 20: Imogen’s husband, giving himself out as a Roman so as to be taken prisoner.

## Cymbeline · major

The King of Britain, Imogen’s father.

## The Queen · major

Cymbeline’s second wife and Cloten’s mother; the play gives her no name.

## Cloten · major

The Queen’s son by an earlier husband, and Cymbeline’s stepson.

## Iachimo · major

An Italian gentleman at Philario’s house in Rome, brother to the Duke of Siena.

## Pisanio · major

Posthumus’s servant, left behind in Britain in Imogen’s household.

## Belarius · major

An old man living in a cave in the Welsh mountains, who goes by the name Morgan.

After source chapter 15, paragraph 12: The banished lord Belarius, who stole Cymbeline’s two sons in revenge and has raised them in Wales under the name Morgan.

## Guiderius · major

A young man raised in the Welsh cave, called Polydore by the old man who brought him up.

After source chapter 15, paragraph 12: Cymbeline’s elder son, stolen in infancy and raised in Wales as Polydore.

## Arviragus · major

The younger of the two young men raised in the Welsh cave, called Cadwal.

After source chapter 15, paragraph 12: Cymbeline’s younger son, stolen in infancy and raised in Wales as Cadwal.

## Caius Lucius · major

The Roman general sent as ambassador to Britain to demand the tribute.

## Philario · supporting

Posthumus’s host in Rome, a friend of his father’s.

## Cornelius · supporting

A physician at Cymbeline’s court.

## The Soothsayer · supporting

The Roman soothsayer who reads the omens for Lucius; Lucius calls him Philarmonus.

## Sicilius Leonatus · supporting

Posthumus’s father, who fought beside Cassibelan and died before his son was born; he appears in the prison as an apparition.

## Jupiter · major

The king of the gods, also called Jove and the Thunderer, who descends in the prison.

## The First Lord · supporting

A lord attending Cloten, who flatters him.

## The Second Lord · supporting

A lord attending Cloten, who mocks him in asides.

## Helen · supporting

The lady who attends Imogen; Imogen calls her by name at her bedside.

## The Queen’s lady · supporting

One of the ladies gathering flowers for the Queen.

## The lord at court · supporting

A lord attending Cymbeline as the Roman war begins.

## The lord in the field · supporting

The British lord who runs from the battle and meets Posthumus.

## The Gaoler · supporting

The gaoler who keeps Posthumus in the British prison and talks with him about dying.

## The Second Gaoler · supporting

The second gaoler in the British prison, who calls Posthumus out.

## The Second Senator · supporting

A Roman senator who assents to the commission.

## The attendant · supporting

An attendant at Cymbeline’s court.

## The Roman captain · supporting

The captain who takes the disguised Imogen into Lucius’s service.

## The First Captain · supporting

A British captain in the field after the battle.

## The Second Captain · supporting

A British captain in the field after the battle.

## The Tribune · supporting

A Roman tribune ordered to levy troops for Britain.

## The First Senator · supporting

A Roman senator who reads out the Senate’s commission.

## The messenger at court · supporting

The messenger who announces the Roman ambassadors.

## The messenger at the prison · supporting

The messenger who calls Posthumus before the King.

## The First Gentleman · supporting

A gentleman of Cymbeline’s court who explains the marriage to a newcomer.

## The Second Gentleman · supporting

The gentleman to whom the court’s story is explained.

## The Frenchman · supporting

A French gentleman at Philario’s house who has met Posthumus before in Orleans.

## The Dutchman · supporting

A Dutch gentleman among the company at Philario’s house.

## The Spaniard · supporting

A Spanish gentleman among the company at Philario’s house.

## Posthumus’s mother · supporting

Posthumus’s mother, who died in giving birth to him; she appears in the prison as an apparition.

## The First Brother · supporting

The elder of Posthumus’s two brothers, killed in the wars; he appears in the prison as an apparition.

## The Second Brother · supporting

The younger of Posthumus’s two brothers, killed in the wars; he appears in the prison as an apparition.

## The lords · supporting

The lords of Cymbeline’s court.

## The ladies · supporting

The ladies attending the Queen.

## The attendants · supporting

The attendants at Cymbeline’s court.

## The musicians · supporting

The musicians Cloten brings to play beneath Imogen’s window.

## The Roman senators · supporting

The senators who commission the levy for Britain.

## The Roman tribunes · supporting

The tribunes given the Senate’s commission.

## The gaolers · supporting

The gaolers who bring Posthumus in irons.

## The captains · supporting

The captains of the two armies.

## The Leonati · supporting

Posthumus’s family, the Leonati of Britain.

## Augustus Caesar · reference

The Roman emperor who demands the tribute from Britain.

## Julius Caesar · reference

The Roman general who conquered Britain in Cassibelan’s time.

## Cassibelan · reference

The British king who resisted Julius Caesar; Cymbeline’s uncle.

## Tenantius · reference

The British king from whom Sicilius had his titles; Cymbeline’s father.

## Mulmutius · reference

The ancestor who ordained Britain’s laws and first wore the golden crown.

## Lud · reference

The British king whose town the play calls Lud’s Town.

## Euriphile · reference

The nurse who stole Cymbeline’s sons and whom the boys take for their mother.

## Dorothy · reference

One of Imogen’s women, named once when Imogen sends word to her.

## The Duke of Siena · reference

Iachimo’s brother, named only as the leader whose men serve under Lucius.

## Richard du Champ · reference

A name Imogen invents on the spot for the master she claims to have lost; no such man exists in the play.

## Cleopatra · reference

The Egyptian queen worked into the tapestry in Imogen’s bedchamber, meeting Antony on the Cydnus.

## Tarquin · reference

The Roman who crept upon the sleeping Lucrece, whom Iachimo names as he steps from the trunk.

## Sinon · reference

The Greek whose false weeping opened Troy to the wooden horse.

## Aeneas · reference

The Trojan whose faithlessness to Dido made honest men doubted.

## Hecuba · reference

The queen of Troy, whose curses on the Greeks Imogen calls down on Pisanio.

## Thersites · reference

The most contemptible of the Greeks at Troy, whose dead body Guiderius says is as good as Ajax’s.

## Ajax · reference

The great Greek warrior at Troy, set against Thersites in Guiderius’s comparison.

## Hercules · reference

The strongest of the Greek heroes, who could not knock brains out of a head that had none.

## Philomel · reference

The woman of the Ovid story Imogen has been reading, turned into a nightingale.

## Tereus · reference

The king of the same story, who violated Philomel.

## Diana · reference

The chaste goddess and huntress, whose bathing is carved on Imogen’s chimneypiece.

## Juno · reference

The queen of the gods.

## Venus · reference

The goddess of love, also called Cytherea.

## Minerva · reference

The goddess of wisdom, set beside Venus in Iachimo’s praise.

## Lucina · reference

The goddess of childbirth, who failed Posthumus’s mother.

## Mars · reference

The god of war.

## Saturn · reference

The cold planet-god, set against the fire of Iachimo’s account.

## Neptune · reference

The god of the sea, whose park Britain is called.

## Phoebus · reference

The sun god, whose horses drink at the springs in the song.

## Cupid · reference

The god of love.

## Titan · reference

The sun, under his other name.

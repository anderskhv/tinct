# Heart of Darkness: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Charlie Marlow · central

The seaman whose journey into Central Africa forms the main story.

## Mr. Kurtz · major

The Company’s leading ivory agent, based at the Inner Station.

## The frame narrator · supporting

One of the men aboard the Nellie, listening to and recounting Marlow’s story.

## The Director of Companies · supporting

The captain and host of the gathering aboard the Nellie.

## The lawyer · supporting

One of Marlow’s companions aboard the Nellie.

## The Nellie’s accountant guest · supporting

The accountant listening to Marlow aboard the Nellie.

## Marlow’s aunt · supporting

Marlow’s aunt, who uses her connections to help him obtain the river command.

## Fresleven · supporting

The Danish steamboat captain whom Marlow replaces.

## The village chief · supporting

The village leader involved in Fresleven’s dispute over hens.

## The chief’s son · supporting

The son of the village chief in the account of Fresleven’s quarrel.

## The older knitting woman · supporting

The older woman at the Company’s European office, watching the visitors.

## The younger knitting woman · supporting

The woman at the Company’s European office who guides visitors into the waiting room.

## The secretary · supporting

The secretary who handles Marlow’s visit and paperwork at the Company’s European office.

## The Company’s head · supporting

The senior Company official who receives Marlow at the European headquarters.

## The young clerk · supporting

The Company employee who escorts Marlow to his medical examination.

## The Company doctor · supporting

The doctor who examines recruits before they leave for Africa.

## The Swedish captain · supporting

The captain of the small coastal steamer that carries Marlow to the Company station.

## The Swedish traveler · reference

The man mentioned by the Swedish captain in his account of the journey inland.

## The chained laborers · supporting

The African workers forced to carry loads while chained together near the station.

## The guard · supporting

The armed African guard accompanying the chained laborers.

## The young worker in the grove · supporting

The severely weakened young African worker whom Marlow approaches under the trees.

## The Company’s chief accountant · supporting

The Company bookkeeper at the first station Marlow visits.

## The accountant’s laundry worker · reference

The African woman who maintains the accountant’s linen.

## The sick agent · supporting

The ill Company agent placed in the accountant’s office.

## The road official · supporting

The European official Marlow meets on the overland route, claiming responsibility for the road.

## Marlow’s traveling companion · supporting

The European man accompanying Marlow on the overland journey to the Central Station.

## The Central Station manager · major

The Company official in charge of the Central Station, overseeing the upriver expedition.

## The manager’s young servant · supporting

The young African servant employed by the Central Station manager.

## The man blamed for the fire · supporting

The African man accused of causing the fire at the Central Station.

## The brickmaker · supporting

The Central Station agent assigned to make bricks, closely aligned with the manager.

## The boilermaker · supporting

The mechanics’ foreman who works with Marlow on repairing the steamboat.

## The manager’s uncle · supporting

The leader of the Eldorado Exploring Expedition and uncle of the Central Station manager.

## The Eldorado Exploring Expedition · supporting

The expedition led by the manager’s uncle in search of wealth in the interior.

## The pilgrims · supporting

Marlow’s ironic name for the European Company agents.

## The African crew · supporting

The African men hired to work aboard Marlow’s steamboat, whom he calls cannibals.

## The crew’s headman · supporting

The leader of the African crew working aboard Marlow’s steamboat.

## The fireman · supporting

The African crewman tending the steamboat’s boiler.

## The helmsman · supporting

The African crewman who steers Marlow’s steamboat.

## The poleman · supporting

The crewman measuring the depth of the river from the steamboat’s bow.

## Towson or Towser · reference

The author of the seamanship manual Marlow finds; Marlow is unsure of the surname.

## The Russian trader · major

The young independent trader in patched clothes, an admirer of Kurtz.

## The Russian trader’s father · reference

The arch-priest from the Tambov region whose son is the young trader.

## Van Shuyten · reference

The Dutch trader who supplied the young Russian for his journey.

## Kurtz’s Intended · supporting

Kurtz’s fiancée, living in Europe.

## Kurtz’s mother · reference

Kurtz’s mother, described as half-English.

## Kurtz’s father · reference

Kurtz’s father, described as half-French.

## Kurtz’s African companion · supporting

The African woman closely associated with Kurtz at the Inner Station.

## The Company’s representative · supporting

The official who visits Marlow in Europe to claim Kurtz’s papers for the Company.

## Kurtz’s cousin · supporting

The man who identifies himself as Kurtz’s cousin and describes his musical gifts.

## The journalist · supporting

The journalist who knew Kurtz and discusses his public speaking with Marlow.

## Sir Francis Drake · reference

The English seafarer associated with the Golden Hind.

## Sir John Franklin · reference

The British naval explorer associated with the Erebus and Terror.

## Elizabeth I · reference

The English queen who visited Drake’s Golden Hind.

## The Buddha · reference

The Buddhist spiritual teacher invoked in comparisons with Marlow’s seated pose.

## Plato · reference

The ancient Greek philosopher named in the young clerk’s remark.

## Mars · reference

The Roman god of war, invoked in Marlow’s ironic imagery.

## Mephistopheles · reference

The devil figure of the Faust story, used in Marlow’s comparison of the brickmaker.

## Jove · reference

Another name for Jupiter, the Roman chief god, invoked in oaths and imagery.

## God · reference

The divine being invoked in religious expressions and oaths.

# Hamlet: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Prince Hamlet · central

The prince of Denmark. Son of Gertrude and the recently deceased King Hamlet; nephew of the new king, Claudius.

## Claudius · major

The new king of Denmark. Hamlet’s uncle, now married to Hamlet’s mother, Gertrude.

## Gertrude · major

The queen of Denmark and Hamlet’s mother. Widow of the old king and now Claudius’s wife.

## Ophelia · major

Polonius’s daughter and Laertes’s sister. Hamlet has been courting her.

## Polonius · major

A senior adviser at the Danish court. Father of Ophelia and Laertes.

## Horatio · major

Hamlet’s friend and a scholar from Wittenberg.

## Laertes · major

Polonius’s son and Ophelia’s brother.

## The Ghost · major

The mysterious apparition seen by the watchmen at Elsinore.

After source chapter 1, paragraph 37: The apparition seen by the watchmen at Elsinore, resembling Denmark’s dead king.

After source chapter 5, paragraph 11: The apparition that identifies itself to Hamlet as his father’s spirit.

## King Hamlet · supporting

Denmark’s recently deceased king. Prince Hamlet’s father and Gertrude’s first husband.

## Rosencrantz · supporting

A friend of Hamlet from his youth, usually seen with Guildenstern.

## Guildenstern · supporting

A friend of Hamlet from his youth, usually seen with Rosencrantz.

## Young Fortinbras · supporting

A Norwegian prince. His father lost lands to Hamlet’s father in single combat.

## The elder Fortinbras · reference

The Norwegian ruler defeated by King Hamlet. Father of the young Fortinbras.

## The King of Norway · reference

Young Fortinbras’s uncle, the elderly ruler of Norway.

## Barnardo · supporting

A soldier keeping watch at Elsinore, alongside Marcellus.

## Francisco · supporting

The soldier whose watch Barnardo takes over at the opening.

## Marcellus · supporting

A soldier at Elsinore, keeping watch with Barnardo.

## Voltemand · supporting

A Danish courtier, paired with Cornelius as an envoy.

## Cornelius · supporting

A Danish courtier, paired with Voltemand as an envoy.

## Reynaldo · supporting

Polonius’s servant.

## The First Player · supporting

An actor in the visiting company that Hamlet welcomes to Elsinore.

## The Player King · supporting

The ruler in the play performed at court, also called Gonzago. A different figure from Claudius and Hamlet’s father.

## The Player Queen · supporting

The ruler’s wife in the play performed at court, also called Baptista. A different figure from Gertrude.

## Lucianus · supporting

A character in the play performed at court, introduced as the king’s nephew.

## The First Gravedigger · supporting

The cemetery worker who jokes and debates while digging. Also labeled First Clown.

## The Second Gravedigger · supporting

The First Gravedigger’s companion in the cemetery. Also labeled Second Clown.

## Osric · supporting

An elaborately polite courtier at Elsinore.

## Yorick · reference

The late king’s jester, whom Hamlet knew as a child.

## Julius Caesar · reference

The Roman ruler invoked in Horatio’s comparison with ominous events in Denmark.

## Neptune · reference

The Roman god of the sea.

## Jesus Christ · reference

Jesus Christ, the central figure of Christianity, also called the Saviour.

## The Norwegian captain · supporting

An officer in Fortinbras’s army, whom Hamlet questions about the campaign.

## The court gentleman · supporting

An unnamed courtier who brings news to the Danish royal household.

## The court lord · supporting

An unnamed courtier who carries a message between the king and Hamlet.

## The sailor · supporting

One of the sailors who bring Horatio a letter from Hamlet.

## The messenger · supporting

An unnamed messenger carrying letters to the king.

## Horatio’s attendant · supporting

The servant who announces visitors to Horatio.

## The priest · supporting

The clergyman conducting the burial service.

## The English ambassador · supporting

The spokesman for the ambassadors arriving from England.

## The Prologue · supporting

The speaker who introduces the performance at court.

## Claudio · reference

A man who passes on the letters delivered to the king. The play gives little other information about him.

## Lamord · reference

A gentleman from Normandy, praised as a horseman and acquainted with Laertes.

## Yaughan · reference

The person to whom the gravedigger sends his companion to fetch a drink. His identity is not explained.

## Hyperion · reference

A sun god in Greek mythology, used in Hamlet’s comparison of his father and uncle.

## Niobe · reference

A grieving mother from Greek mythology, remembered for her tears.

## Hercules · reference

The hero of classical mythology famed for his strength.

## The Nemean lion · reference

The formidable lion from the labors of Hercules.

## Saint Patrick · reference

The patron saint of Ireland, invoked in Hamlet’s oath.

## Roscius · reference

A celebrated actor of ancient Rome.

## Seneca · reference

The Roman writer of tragedies whom Polonius names while discussing theater.

## Plautus · reference

A Roman writer of comedies, paired with Seneca in Polonius’s account of the actors.

## Jephthah · reference

A biblical judge of Israel, the father in the ballad Hamlet quotes to Polonius.

## Aeneas · reference

A Trojan hero who tells Dido about the fall of Troy in the speech Hamlet recalls.

## Dido · reference

The queen of Carthage, listening to Aeneas’s account of Troy.

## Priam · reference

The elderly king of Troy in the speech recited by the visiting actor.

## Pyrrhus · reference

A Greek warrior attacking Troy in the speech Hamlet asks the actor to recite.

## Hecuba · reference

The queen of Troy and Priam’s wife, portrayed in the actor’s speech.

## The Cyclopes · reference

The one-eyed giants of classical mythology, pictured here forging armor.

## Mars · reference

The Roman god of war.

## Fortune · reference

Luck imagined as a goddess who turns a wheel, raising people up and casting them down.

## Termagant · reference

A blustering figure from older popular drama, invoked as an example of overacting.

## Herod · reference

The biblical king as portrayed in older religious drama, notorious onstage for ranting.

## Vulcan · reference

The Roman god of fire and metalworking, imagined at his forge.

## Brutus · reference

The Roman conspirator associated with the killing of Julius Caesar.

## Phoebus · reference

A classical name for the sun god, imagined driving a chariot across the sky.

## Tellus · reference

The Roman goddess who personifies the earth.

## Hymen · reference

The classical god of marriage.

## Hecate · reference

A goddess associated with magic and witchcraft, invoked in the performed play.

## Damon · reference

A name Hamlet borrows from verse when addressing his friend Horatio.

## Nero · reference

The Roman emperor notorious for cruelty, including having his mother killed.

## Jove · reference

Another name for Jupiter, king of the Roman gods.

## Mercury · reference

The messenger of the Roman gods.

## Saint Valentine · reference

The saint whose feast day appears in Ophelia’s song about lovers.

## Robin · reference

The beloved named in a song Ophelia sings; not a separately introduced courtier.

## Adam · reference

The first man in the biblical creation story, invoked in the gravediggers’ joke about digging.

## Cain · reference

The biblical son of Adam who killed his brother Abel.

## Alexander the Great · reference

The ancient Macedonian king and conqueror mentioned in Hamlet’s reflections on mortality.

## The sailors · supporting

The sailors who carry Hamlet’s letter to Horatio.

## The English ambassadors · supporting

The envoys arriving from England at the Danish court.

## The players · supporting

The visiting company of actors welcomed by Hamlet.

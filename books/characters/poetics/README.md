# Poetics: recognition references

All 26 sections in both English editions, 105 entries each. Poets, painters, performers, critics, cited story figures and explicit grammatical example names are retained. Every category is Reference in this treatise; prominence in a different book is not imported. Cards identify a figure without retelling the source tragedies’ outcomes.

Repeated-name and attribution review: Crates is the comic writer; Dionysius is the painter; Pindarus is the performer in the acting discussion; Hippias is explicitly of Thasos. Glaucon is identified only as the critic cited here, not conjecturally equated with Plato’s brother. Eucleides is the critic called the elder, not silently changed into the geometer Euclid. Polyidus is the sophist discussing dramatic construction. Icadius is an explicitly alternative account of Odysseus’s father-in-law, not a spelling correction erasing the distinction from Icarius.

The two Cleons in “Cleon son of Cleon” have distinct father/son entries. Theodorus and Hermo-caico-xanthus are linguistic example names, not asserted historical people. Story figures named by eponymous titles are identified as those figures; other work titles, technical terms and places are not person cards. Hades in “scenes laid in Hades” is a place, not a god binding. Group titles Phorcides and Phthiotides identify the sisters/women, not invented personal names. The Cyclopes phrase denotes the compared portrayals of a Cyclops.

Cross-checked the difficult artistic examples against [Butcher’s text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/poetics.html) and [Bywater’s annotated edition](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/205911/files/b20762576_C098825507.pdf), including Scylla as a dithyrambic example. The source contains inherited transliterated Greek and angle-bracket editorial marks; those bytes are unchanged.

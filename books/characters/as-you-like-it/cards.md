# As You Like It: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Rosalind · central

Duke Senior’s daughter and Celia’s cousin; the play’s central heroine.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 48: Duke Senior’s daughter, disguised as the young man Ganymede.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: Orlando’s bride and Duke Senior’s daughter; formerly disguised as Ganymede.

## Orlando · central

Sir Rowland de Boys’s youngest son and Oliver’s brother.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: Rosalind’s bridegroom, Sir Rowland de Boys’s youngest son.

## Celia · major

Duke Frederick’s daughter and Rosalind’s close friend and cousin.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 49: Rosalind’s cousin, traveling in disguise as Aliena.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: Oliver’s bride and Rosalind’s cousin; formerly disguised as Aliena.

## Duke Senior · major

Rosalind’s father, driven into exile by his younger brother Frederick.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 53: Rosalind’s father, restored to his dukedom after exile.

## Duke Frederick · major

Celia’s father and Duke Senior’s younger brother, who has taken over the dukedom.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 53: Celia’s father and Duke Senior’s brother, who has renounced his rule for a religious life.

## Touchstone · major

The court fool who is close to Celia and Rosalind.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: The court fool and Audrey’s bridegroom.

## Le Beau · supporting

A courtier in Duke Frederick’s household.

## Charles · supporting

Duke Frederick’s wrestler.

## Sir Rowland de Boys · supporting

The late father of Oliver, Jaques de Boys and Orlando.

## Jaques · major

The melancholy courtier living in exile with Duke Senior.

## Jaques de Boys · supporting

Sir Rowland’s second son, brother to Oliver and Orlando.

## Amiens · supporting

A courtier and singer among Duke Senior’s companions in exile.

## Hisperia · supporting

An attendant of Celia at Duke Frederick’s court.

## Adam · supporting

The elderly servant of the de Boys family, devoted to Orlando.

## Oliver · major

Sir Rowland de Boys’s eldest son and Orlando’s elder brother.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: Celia’s bridegroom and Orlando’s elder brother.

## Corin · supporting

An elderly shepherd in the Forest of Arden.

## Silvius · major

The young shepherd in love with Phebe.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 51: The shepherd paired with Phebe at the wedding.

## Phebe · major

The shepherdess loved by Silvius.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 51: The shepherdess who agrees to marry Silvius.

## Jane Smile · supporting

The countrywoman whom Touchstone recalls as an old love.

## Audrey · supporting

The countrywoman courted by Touchstone.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 48: Touchstone’s bride, a countrywoman of Arden.

## Sir Oliver Martext · supporting

The village vicar whom Touchstone asks to marry him to Audrey.

## William · supporting

The young countryman who is in love with Audrey.

## The first exiled lord · supporting

One of Duke Senior’s companions in the Forest of Arden.

## The second exiled lord · supporting

One of Duke Senior’s companions in the Forest of Arden.

## The first court lord · supporting

A lord attending Duke Frederick at his court.

## The second court lord · supporting

A lord attending Duke Frederick at his court.

## The lord returning from the hunt · supporting

One of the forest lords whom Jaques meets after the deer hunt.

## The first page · supporting

One of the Duke’s pages who sings for Touchstone and Audrey.

## The second page · supporting

One of the Duke’s pages who sings for Touchstone and Audrey.

## Corin’s employer · supporting

The owner of the cottage, pasture and flock that are offered for sale.

## The old religious man · supporting

The religious man whom Frederick meets at the edge of the forest.

## The uncle in Ganymede’s story · supporting

The religious uncle whom Rosalind, as Ganymede, credits with teaching her about love.

## The magician in Rosalind’s story · supporting

The magician whom Rosalind claims to have known since childhood.

## The lover in Ganymede’s story · supporting

The man whom Rosalind, as Ganymede, claims to have cured of love.

## Hymen · supporting

The figure of the god of marriage who presides over the couples’ union.

## Ovid · reference

The Roman poet whose exile is invoked by Touchstone.

## Pythagoras · reference

The Greek philosopher associated with the transmigration of souls, invoked in Rosalind’s joke.

## Cleopatra · reference

The queen of Egypt invoked as an example of majesty in the poem about Rosalind.

## Lucretia · reference

The Roman woman traditionally celebrated for chastity, invoked in the poem about Rosalind.

## Christopher Marlowe · reference

The poet of Hero and Leander, addressed as the dead shepherd when Phebe quotes him.

## Julius Caesar · reference

The Roman general whose boast about coming, seeing and conquering Rosalind quotes.

## Fortune · reference

Luck personified as a woman distributing gifts from her wheel.

## Nature · reference

Nature personified as the giver of physical qualities, contrasted with Fortune.

## The Fates · reference

The classical powers governing destiny, called the Destinies.

## Hercules · reference

The mythological hero of extraordinary strength, invoked at the wrestling match.

## Cupid · reference

The classical god of love.

## Juno · reference

The Roman goddess of marriage, associated with Jupiter.

## Jupiter · reference

The Roman king of the gods, also called Jove.

## Ganymede in mythology · reference

The Trojan youth who serves as Jupiter’s cupbearer.

## Adam in the Bible · reference

The first man in the biblical creation story, invoked in Duke Senior’s reflection on exile.

## Diana · reference

The classical goddess of hunting and chastity, also invoked as the moon.

## Helen of Troy · reference

The woman celebrated for beauty in Trojan legend, invoked in the poem about Rosalind.

## Atalanta · reference

The swift runner and huntress of Greek myth, invoked in comparisons with Rosalind and Orlando.

## Gargantua · reference

The giant in Rabelais’s stories, invoked for the size of his mouth.

## Judas Iscariot · reference

The biblical betrayer of Jesus, invoked in Celia’s jokes about hair and kisses.

## Troilus · reference

The Trojan lover invoked in Rosalind’s argument about dying for love.

## Leander · reference

The mythological lover who swims the Hellespont to visit Hero.

## Hero · reference

The priestess at Sestos loved by Leander in classical legend.

## Venus · reference

The Roman goddess of love.

## The phoenix · reference

The legendary bird evoked as an emblem of rarity in Rosalind’s teasing.

## God · reference

The Christian deity invoked in prayers and oaths.

## The wrestlers’ father · supporting

The elderly father of the three young men who wrestle Charles.

## The three brothers · supporting

The three brothers who take on Charles in the wrestling match.

## Oliver in the song · reference

The Oliver addressed in the song fragment Touchstone quotes to the vicar.

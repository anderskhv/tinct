# Othello: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Othello · central

The general serving Venice, Desdemona’s husband and the play’s central figure.

## Desdemona · central

Brabantio’s daughter and Othello’s wife.

## Iago · central

Othello’s ensign and Emilia’s husband.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 177: Emilia’s husband, promoted by Othello from ensign to lieutenant.

## Emilia · major

Iago’s wife and Desdemona’s attendant.

## Michael Cassio · major

The Florentine officer chosen as Othello’s lieutenant.

After source chapter 6, paragraph 94: The Florentine officer dismissed from Othello’s lieutenancy.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 138: The Florentine officer appointed to succeed Othello in governing Cyprus.

## Roderigo · major

The Venetian gentleman pursuing Desdemona with Iago’s help.

## Brabantio · major

Desdemona’s father, a Venetian senator.

## Bianca · supporting

Cassio’s lover in Cyprus.

## Montano · supporting

The governor of Cyprus whom Othello is sent to replace.

## Lodovico · supporting

Desdemona’s kinsman, the envoy who comes from Venice.

## Gratiano · supporting

Brabantio’s brother and Desdemona’s uncle.

## The Duke of Venice · supporting

The ruler of Venice, presiding over the council.

## The first senator · supporting

One of the Venetian senators discussing the military threat.

## The second senator · supporting

Another senator comparing reports about the enemy fleet.

## The sailor · supporting

The sailor bringing Angelo’s report to the Venetian council.

## The council messenger · supporting

The messenger bringing Montano’s report to the Venetian council.

## The Cyprus messenger · supporting

The messenger reporting the crowd’s sighting of a sail off Cyprus.

## The first gentleman · supporting

One of Montano’s companions watching the sea from Cyprus.

## The second gentleman · supporting

The gentleman helping identify the ships arriving at Cyprus.

## The third gentleman · supporting

The gentleman bringing news of the fleet to Montano in Cyprus.

## Othello’s herald · supporting

The official who publicly reads Othello’s proclamation.

## The Clown · supporting

Othello’s household servant, who jokes with the musicians and carries messages.

## The first musician · supporting

The musician answering the Clown outside Othello’s residence.

## The musicians · supporting

The players Cassio brings to perform outside Othello’s residence.

## The wine-serving servant · supporting

The attendant bringing wine to the officers’ gathering.

## The Venetian senators · supporting

The council members overseeing Venice’s public and military affairs.

## The officers · supporting

The military and civic officers attending the leading characters.

## The gondolier · reference

The boatman mentioned in Roderigo’s account of Desdemona’s departure.

## Signior Angelo · reference

The official whose naval report the sailor brings to Venice.

## Marcus Luccicos · reference

The man the duke asks for during the council’s discussion of Cyprus.

## Iago’s patrons · reference

The three influential Venetians whom Iago says supported his request for promotion.

## Othello’s mother · reference

The mother recalled in Othello’s account of the handkerchief.

## Othello’s father · reference

The father recalled in Othello’s account of the handkerchief.

## The Egyptian woman · reference

The woman who, in Othello’s account, gave his mother the handkerchief and described its powers.

## The sibyl · reference

The prophetess said by Othello to have sewn the handkerchief.

## Desdemona’s mother · reference

Brabantio’s wife, recalled in Desdemona’s story about her maid.

## Barbary · reference

Desdemona’s mother’s maid, remembered for her willow song.

## Barbary’s lover · reference

The man recalled in Desdemona’s account of Barbary.

## The Anthropophagi · reference

The man-eating people in Othello’s traveler’s tales.

## The men with heads beneath their shoulders · reference

The marvelous people described in Othello’s traveler’s tales.

## The Ottoman forces · reference

The forces threatening Venice’s possessions in the eastern Mediterranean.

## The man in Aleppo · reference

The Turkish man in Othello’s account of an incident in Aleppo.

## The Venetian in Aleppo · reference

The Venetian whom Othello recalls seeing attacked in Aleppo.

## The Judean in the comparison · reference

The figure in Othello’s comparison about throwing away a precious pearl; the precise identity is disputed.

## Janus · reference

The Roman god associated with beginnings and doorways, traditionally shown with two faces.

## Cupid · reference

The Roman god of love.

## Jove · reference

Jupiter, the Roman king of the gods.

## King Stephen · reference

The English king in Iago’s drinking song.

## Julius Caesar · reference

The Roman commander used as a comparison for military ability.

## The Hydra · reference

The many-headed monster of Greek myth.

## Diana · reference

The goddess associated with chastity, invoked here as Dian.

## Saint Peter · reference

The apostle traditionally associated with the keys of heaven.

## Prometheus · reference

The mythical bringer of fire, invoked in Othello’s image of life-giving heat.

## God · reference

The deity invoked in the play’s prayers and exclamations.

## The devil · reference

The supernatural figure invoked in curses and accusations.

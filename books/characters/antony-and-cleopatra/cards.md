# Antony and Cleopatra: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Mark Antony · central

One of the three rulers of the Roman world, kept in Egypt by Cleopatra.

After source chapter 8, paragraph 4: One of the three rulers of the Roman world, now married to Octavius Caesar’s sister Octavia.

## Cleopatra · central

The Queen of Egypt, and Antony’s lover.

After source chapter 42, paragraph 12: The Queen of Egypt, a prisoner in her own monument.

## Octavius Caesar · central

The youngest of the three rulers of the Roman world, and Antony’s partner in it.

## Domitius Enobarbus · major

Antony’s closest officer and the plainest speaker in his following.

After source chapter 30, paragraph 8: Antony’s old officer, now in Octavius Caesar’s camp.

## Charmian · major

One of Cleopatra’s attendants, and the closest to her.

## Sextus Pompeius · major

The son of Pompey the Great, in arms at sea against the three rulers.

## Lepidus · major

The third of the three rulers of the Roman world, and the weakest of them.

## Menas · major

A pirate captain in Sextus Pompeius’s service.

## Eros · major

Antony’s servant and armour-bearer.

## Agrippa · major

One of Octavius Caesar’s chief officers.

## Dolabella · major

One of Octavius Caesar’s officers, sent to attend Cleopatra.

## Iras · supporting

One of Cleopatra’s attendants.

## Alexas · supporting

An attendant on Cleopatra, who carries her messages to Antony.

## Maecenas · supporting

One of Octavius Caesar’s counsellors.

## Octavia · supporting

Octavius Caesar’s sister, married to Antony to bind the two men together.

After source chapter 8, paragraph 4: Octavius Caesar’s sister, now married to Antony.

## Scarus · supporting

An officer in Antony’s army, wounded fighting for him.

## Thidias · supporting

Octavius Caesar’s messenger to Cleopatra, whipped for his pains.

## Canidius · supporting

Antony’s lieutenant-general on land.

## Proculeius · supporting

An officer of Octavius Caesar’s, whom Antony tells Cleopatra to trust.

## Mardian · supporting

A eunuch in Cleopatra’s household.

## Diomedes · supporting

An attendant on Cleopatra, sent after Antony with her message.

## Dercetus · supporting

A soldier of Antony’s who takes his sword to Octavius Caesar.

## Ventidius · supporting

An officer of Antony’s, victorious against the Parthians.

## Silius · supporting

An officer serving under Ventidius in Syria.

## Seleucus · supporting

Cleopatra’s treasurer, who betrays what she has kept back.

## Gallus · supporting

An officer of Octavius Caesar’s, sent with Proculeius to the monument.

## Taurus · supporting

Octavius Caesar’s lieutenant-general on land at Actium.

A pirate captain with Menas in Sextus Pompeius’s service.

## Varrius · supporting

A follower of Sextus Pompeius who brings him news of Antony.

## Philo · supporting

A follower of Antony’s, who opens the play deploring his change.

## Demetrius · supporting

A follower of Antony’s, to whom Philo speaks.

## Julius Caesar · reference

The Roman conqueror, Octavius Caesar’s adoptive father, and Cleopatra’s lover before Antony.

## Pompey the Great · reference

Sextus Pompeius’s father, defeated by Julius Caesar at Pharsalia.

## Gnaeus Pompey · reference

Pompey the Great’s elder son, named by Antony among Cleopatra’s earlier lovers.

## Fulvia · reference

Antony’s first wife, who made war on Octavius Caesar and dies early in the play.

## Lucius · reference

Antony’s brother, who joined Fulvia’s war against Octavius Caesar.

## Caesarion · reference

Cleopatra’s son, whom Egypt calls Julius Caesar’s.

## Alexander · reference

Antony and Cleopatra’s son, given Media, Parthia and Armenia at the enthronement.

## Ptolemy · reference

Cleopatra’s brother and husband, dead before the play begins; she is called his queen.

## Ptolemy · reference

Antony and Cleopatra’s son, given Syria, Cilicia and Phoenicia at the enthronement.

## Marcus Crassus · reference

The Roman general the Parthians killed, whom Ventidius avenges.

## Marcus Octavius · reference

One of Antony’s captains at Actium; not Octavius Caesar.

## Marcus Justeius · reference

One of Antony’s captains at Actium.

## Publicola · reference

One of Antony’s captains at Actium.

## Caelius · reference

One of Antony’s captains at Actium.

## Pacorus · reference

King Orodes’s son, killed by Ventidius in Parthia.

The Parthian king, Pacorus’s father.

## Labienus · reference

The Roman who led a Parthian army into Asia against Antony.

## Sossius · reference

An officer of Antony’s in Syria, cited by Ventidius as a warning.

## Photinus · reference

The titan who holds up the world; Cleopatra calls Antony the demi-Atlas of the earth.

## Narcissus · reference

The youth of the myth, in Cleopatra’s abuse of the messenger.

## Gorgon · reference

The monster whose look turned men to stone, in Cleopatra’s figure for Antony.

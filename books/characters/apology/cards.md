# Apology: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Socrates · central

The Athenian philosopher defending himself at his trial.

## Meletus · major

One of Socrates’ three accusers, whom he questions directly in his defense.

## Anytus · major

One of Socrates’ three accusers, associated with the politicians and craftsmen.

## Lycon · supporting

One of Socrates’ three accusers, associated with the public speakers.

## Aristophanes · reference

The Athenian comic playwright whose Clouds caricatures Socrates.

## Gorgias · reference

A traveling teacher of rhetoric from Leontini, charging fees for instruction.

## Prodicus · reference

A traveling teacher from Ceos, charging fees for instruction.

## Hippias · reference

A traveling teacher from Elis, charging fees for instruction.

## Callias · reference

A wealthy Athenian who pays for education from the Sophists. Son of Hipponicus.

## Hipponicus · reference

Callias’ father.

## Evenus · reference

A teacher from Paros whose fees Socrates discusses with Callias.

## Chaerephon · supporting

Socrates’ longtime friend, who consulted the oracle at Delphi.

## The Delphic priestess · reference

The priestess who speaks the oracle’s response at Delphi.

## Chaerephon’s brother · supporting

The unnamed brother of Chaerephon, present in court.

## Anaxagoras · reference

The philosopher from Clazomenae known for his explanations of the natural world.

## Euripides · reference

The Athenian tragic playwright, mentioned in an editorial note about Anaxagoras.

## Achilles · reference

The Greek warrior at Troy, son of the sea goddess Thetis.

## Thetis · reference

The sea goddess who is Achilles’ mother.

## Hector · reference

The Trojan warrior opposed to Achilles in the Iliad.

## Patroclus · reference

Achilles’ close companion in the Iliad.

## Zeus · reference

The chief god of the Greek pantheon.

## Leon · reference

The man from Salamis whom the Thirty ordered Socrates to arrest.

## The Thirty · reference

The oligarchic rulers who briefly governed Athens after the democracy was overthrown.

## The Prytanes · reference

The presiding members of the Athenian council, among whom Socrates served.

## The Eleven · reference

The Athenian magistrates responsible for prisoners.

## Crito · supporting

Socrates’ friend and fellow citizen, father of Critobulus.

## Critobulus · supporting

Crito’s son, present at Socrates’ trial.

## Lysanias · reference

Aeschines’ father, from the district of Sphettus.

## Aeschines · reference

An associate of Socrates and son of Lysanias.

## Antiphon · reference

Epigenes’ father, from Cephisus. This is the parent named among Socrates’ supporters.

## Epigenes · reference

An associate of Socrates and son of Antiphon.

## Nicostratus · reference

Theodotus’ brother, named among the relatives supporting Socrates.

## Theosdotides · reference

The father of Nicostratus and Theodotus.

## Theodotus · reference

An associate of Socrates and brother of Nicostratus.

## Paralus · reference

Demodocus’ son and Theages’ brother.

## Demodocus · reference

The father of Paralus and Theages.

## Theages · reference

An associate of Socrates, brother of Paralus and son of Demodocus.

## Adeimantus · reference

Plato’s brother and son of Ariston.

## Ariston · reference

The father of Adeimantus and Plato.

## Plato · supporting

Socrates’ associate, present at the trial; also the author of this account.

## Aeantodorus · reference

Apollodorus’ brother, named among Socrates’ supporters.

## Apollodorus · supporting

Socrates’ friend and brother of Aeantodorus, present at the trial.

## Minos · reference

The legendary king of Crete, named as a judge of the dead.

## Rhadamanthus · reference

A legendary figure renowned for justice, named as a judge of the dead.

## Aeacus · reference

A legendary Greek king, named as a judge of the dead.

## Triptolemus · reference

The legendary figure associated with teaching agriculture, here named among the judges of the dead.

## Orpheus · reference

The legendary Greek singer and poet.

## Musaeus · reference

A legendary Greek poet associated with sacred song.

## Hesiod · reference

The early Greek poet known for Works and Days and the Theogony.

## Homer · reference

The Greek poet traditionally credited with the Iliad and Odyssey.

## Palamedes · reference

A Greek hero of the Trojan War, cited as a victim of unjust judgment.

## Ajax · reference

The Greek warrior at Troy, son of Telamon.

## Telamon · reference

Ajax’s father in Greek legend.

## Odysseus · reference

The Greek king of Ithaca and hero of the Odyssey.

## Sisyphus · reference

The cunning king of Greek legend, whom Socrates imagines questioning.

## Agamemnon · reference

The commander of the Greek expedition against Troy.

## Socrates’ children · reference

Socrates’ three sons, whose upbringing he mentions in his defense. They are not named here.

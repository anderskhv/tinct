# The Epic of Gilgamesh: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Gilgamesh · central

The king of Uruk and the epic’s principal hero, son of Ninsun and Lugalbanda.

## Enkidu · central

The wild man created by Aruru to match Gilgamesh in strength.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 16: Gilgamesh’s closest friend and companion, originally a wild man raised among animals.

## Shamash · major

The sun god, protector of Gilgamesh and Enkidu.

## Humbaba · major

The fearsome guardian appointed by Enlil to protect the Cedar Forest.

## Ishtar · major

The goddess of love and war, daughter of Anu and Antum.

## Utnapishtim · major

Gilgamesh’s distant ancestor, a man granted everlasting life.

## Ninsun · supporting

Gilgamesh’s mother, a wise cow-goddess who interprets his dreams.

## Lugalbanda · supporting

Gilgamesh’s divine father, invoked as his protector.

## Shamhat · supporting

The sex worker from Uruk sent to introduce Enkidu to human society.

## Siduri · supporting

The tavern-keeper who lives beside the sea. Also spelled Sirudi here.

## Urshanabi · supporting

Utnapishtim’s ferryman, who crosses the Waters of Death. Also spelled Urshinabi here.

## Anu · supporting

The sky god and father of Ishtar.

## Enlil · supporting

A leading god who exercises authority over humanity and the other gods. Also called Bel here.

## Ea / Enki · supporting

The god of wisdom and fresh water, also called Enki and Nudimmud.

## Aruru · supporting

The creator goddess who fashions human beings.

## Adad · supporting

The god of storms and thunder.

## Sin · supporting

The moon god, also invoked as Namra-sit.

## Ereshkigal · supporting

The queen of the underworld, also called Irkalla in this edition.

## Nergal · supporting

A god associated with death, warfare and the underworld.

## Ninurta · supporting

A warrior god who takes part in the gods’ council and the Flood story.

## Antum · supporting

Ishtar’s mother and Anu’s consort.

## The Bull of Heaven · supporting

The divine bull under Anu’s control, requested by Ishtar.

## The Anunnaki · supporting

A collective name for the great gods who determine human fate.

## The trapper · supporting

The hunter who encounters Enkidu at the animals’ watering place.

## The trapper’s father · supporting

The trapper’s father, whom he consults about Enkidu.

## The elders of Uruk · supporting

The city’s senior counselors, who advise Gilgamesh.

## The scorpion-man · supporting

One of the guardians of the mountain of Mashu and the sun’s passage.

## The scorpion-man’s wife · supporting

The scorpion-man’s wife and fellow guardian at Mount Mashu.

## The scorpion guardians · supporting

The beings who guard the entrance to Mount Mashu.

## The Stone Men · supporting

Urshanabi’s stone boat crew, immune to the Waters of Death.

## Utnapishtim’s wife · supporting

Utnapishtim’s wife, who lives with him at his distant dwelling.

## The barber · supporting

The barber who grooms Enkidu as he enters human society.

## The wedding guest · supporting

The traveler who explains the wedding customs of Uruk to Enkidu.

## The figure in Enkidu’s dream · supporting

The terrifying humanlike figure with animal claws in Enkidu’s dream.

## The underworld scribe · supporting

The scribe who reads a tablet before Ereshkigal.

## The carpenter · reference

The carpenter whose shop Gilgamesh recalls in the story of his ball and mallet.

## The carpenter’s wife · reference

The carpenter’s wife, whom Gilgamesh regards as being like a mother.

## The carpenter’s daughter · reference

The carpenter’s daughter, whom Gilgamesh regards as being like a sister.

## The serpent · supporting

The snake at the watering place on Gilgamesh’s return journey.

## Aya · reference

The goddess who is Shamash’s consort.

## Nisaba · reference

The grain goddess, invoked in the description of Enkidu’s hair.

## The Igigi · reference

A collective name for a group of gods.

## Irnina · reference

A divine figure associated with victory and the underworld, invoked in Ninsun’s prayer.

## Irnini · reference

The divine name attached to the Cedar Forest’s sanctuary in this translation; printed Imini in the original edition.

## Ningishzida · reference

An underworld god invoked in Ninsun’s prayer.

## Dumuzi · reference

Ishtar’s youthful husband, a shepherd god.

## The Thunderbird · reference

The mighty supernatural bird invoked in the heroes’ dreams and comparisons.

## The allallu-bird · reference

The brightly colored bird named among Ishtar’s former lovers.

## Silili · reference

The mother of the horse named among Ishtar’s former lovers.

## Ishullanu · reference

Anu’s gardener, named among the men desired by Ishtar.

## The lion · reference

The powerful lion named among Ishtar’s former lovers.

## The horse · reference

The warhorse named among Ishtar’s former lovers, offspring of Silili.

## The shepherd · reference

The herdsman named among Ishtar’s former lovers.

## Etana · reference

A legendary king encountered among the inhabitants of the underworld.

## Shakkan · reference

The god of wild animals and herds, named in the underworld vision.

## Namtar · reference

A divine agent of death and disease associated with the underworld.

## Hushbisha · reference

A divine attendant in the court of the underworld.

## Quassu-tabat · reference

A divine attendant of Ereshkigal, honored in the funeral offerings.

## Ninshuluhha · reference

A deity associated with cleaning the underworld’s house.

## Bibbu · reference

A deity serving in the underworld’s court, honored in the funeral offerings.

## Dumuzi-abzu · reference

A separate underworld deity from Ishtar’s husband Dumuzi, named in the funeral offerings.

## Ubar-Tutu · reference

Utnapishtim’s father, also spelled Ubara-Tutu here.

## Mammitum · reference

The goddess who determines human fate with the great gods.

## Ennugi · reference

A god named among the leaders of the divine council.

## Puzur-Amurri · reference

The boatman entrusted with Utnapishtim’s ship in the Flood story.

## Shullat · reference

One of the heralds who accompany the storm god Adad.

## Hanish · reference

One of the heralds who accompany the storm god Adad.

## Ninazu · reference

An underworld god, a son of Ereshkigal in this tradition.

## Asakku · reference

A demon associated with sickness and death.

## The seven Sages · reference

The legendary sages credited with laying the foundations of Uruk’s wall.

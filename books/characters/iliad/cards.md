# The Iliad: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Achilles · central

Son of Peleus and Thetis, the best fighter among the Greeks; the poem opens with his quarrel with Agamemnon.

## Hector · central

Priam’s eldest son and the commander of Troy’s defence.

## Agamemnon · major

King of Mycenae and commander-in-chief of the Greek army, Menelaus’s brother.

## Patroclus · major

Son of Menoetius and Achilles’ closest companion, raised in Peleus’s house.

## Priam · major

The old king of Troy, father of Hector and of fifty sons.

## Menelaus · major

King of Sparta and Agamemnon’s brother; the war is fought over his wife.

## Helen · major

Menelaus’s wife, now living in Troy with Paris; the woman the war is fought over.

## Paris · major

Priam’s son, who carried Helen away from Sparta. The poem calls him Alexandrus as often as Paris.

## Odysseus · major

King of Ithaca, the shrewdest of the Greek captains. Butler’s original calls him Ulysses.

## Diomed · major

Son of Tydeus and, after Achilles, the most dangerous fighter on the Greek side. The modern edition also spells him Diomedes.

## Nestor · major

The old king of Pylos, knight of Gerene, who has outlived two generations and advises everyone.

## Ajax · major

Son of Telamon, the biggest man in the Greek army and its most reliable defender.

## Ajax son of Oileus · major

The Locrian captain, a small man and the fastest runner in the army; called Ajax the less to distinguish him from Telamon’s son.

## The two Ajaxes · supporting

Ajax son of Telamon and Ajax son of Oileus, who fight side by side and are often named as a pair.

## Aeneas · major

Son of Anchises and Aphrodite, leader of the Dardanians and the best Trojan fighter after Hector.

## Sarpedon · major

Son of Zeus and captain of the Lycians, the strongest of Troy’s allies.

## Glaucus · supporting

Son of Hippolochus and Sarpedon’s second in command among the Lycians.

## Idomeneus · major

King of Crete, son of Deucalion, and one of the older Greek captains.

## Meriones · major

Idomeneus’s second in command among the Cretans, son of Molus.

## Antilochus · major

Nestor’s son, the fastest runner among the younger Greek captains.

## Teucer · major

Ajax’s half-brother by the same father, and the best archer in the Greek army.

## Polydamas · supporting

Son of Panthous, born the same night as Hector; the one Trojan whose counsel Hector ought to take.

## Briseis · major

The woman awarded to Achilles from the sack of Lyrnessus, whom Agamemnon takes from him.

## Chryseis · supporting

Chryses’s daughter, taken as Agamemnon’s prize; her father’s prayer brings the plague.

## Chryses · supporting

Priest of Apollo, father of Chryseis, whose rejected ransom opens the poem.

## Calchas · supporting

Son of Thestor and the army’s chief augur, who guided the fleet to Troy.

## Andromache · major

Hector’s wife, daughter of Eetion of Thebe.

## Astyanax · supporting

Hector and Andromache’s baby son. His father named him Scamandrius; the people call him Astyanax.

## Hecuba · supporting

Priam’s wife and queen of Troy, mother of Hector and Paris.

## Automedon · supporting

Son of Diores and Achilles’ charioteer, who drives for Patroclus in his place.

## Phoenix · supporting

The old man who brought Achilles up in Peleus’s house and is sent as one of the three envoys to him.

## Thersites · supporting

The ugliest and most abusive man in the Greek army, beaten by Odysseus for railing at Agamemnon.

## Dolon · supporting

Son of the herald Eumedes, a rich Trojan who volunteers to spy on the Greek camp by night.

## Rhesus · supporting

The newly arrived Thracian king whose white horses Odysseus and Diomed carry off.

## Machaon · supporting

A son of Aesculapius and one of the army’s two surgeons.

## Podalirius · reference

A son of Aesculapius and, with his brother Machaon, a surgeon to the army.

## Thetis · major

A sea goddess, Achilles’ mother, married to the mortal Peleus.

## Peleus · supporting

Achilles’ father, an old king among the Myrmidons.

## Menoetius · reference

Patroclus’s father, son of Actor, who put his son in Peleus’s care.

## Atreus · reference

Father of Agamemnon and Menelaus.

## Tydeus · reference

Diomed’s father, one of the Seven against Thebes, and a byword for reckless courage.

## Telamon · reference

Father of Ajax and Teucer.

## Oileus · reference

Father of Ajax the less and of the bastard Medon.

## Laertes · reference

Odysseus’s father.

## Anchises · reference

Aeneas’s father, whom Aphrodite loved on the slopes of Ida.

## Hippolochus · reference

Glaucus’s father, son of Bellerophon.

## Antenor · supporting

An elder of Troy and its wisest counsellor, who argues for giving Helen back.

## Theano · supporting

Daughter of Cisseus, wife of Antenor and priestess of Athena in Troy.

## Deiphobus · supporting

A son of Priam and one of Troy’s leading fighters.

## Helenus · supporting

A son of Priam and the best augur in Troy.

## Cebriones · supporting

A bastard son of Priam who drives Hector’s chariot.

## Polydorus · supporting

Priam’s youngest and dearest son, whom his father forbade to fight.

## Lycaon · supporting

A son of Priam by Laothoe, sold into slavery by Achilles and taken again at the river.

## Pandarus · supporting

Son of Lycaon of Zelea, the archer who breaks the truce by shooting Menelaus.

## Lycaon · reference

Pandarus’s father, of Zelea under Ida.

## Asius · supporting

Son of Hyrtacus, a Trojan ally who refuses to leave his chariot at the wall.

## Idaeus · supporting

Priam’s herald, who drives for him and carries his messages.

## Eurybates · supporting

A herald of the Greek army; the man of Ithaca who attends Odysseus.

## Agenor · supporting

A son of Antenor and one of the steadiest Trojan fighters.

## Asteropaeus · supporting

Son of Pelegon and grandson of the river Axius, captain of the Paeonians and ambidextrous with the spear.

## Euphorbus · supporting

Son of Panthous, the Trojan who wounds Patroclus first.

## Eurypylus · supporting

Son of Euaemon, a Greek captain who is wounded in the thigh and nursed by Patroclus.

## Menestheus · supporting

Son of Peteos and captain of the Athenians.

## Meges · supporting

Son of Phyleus and captain of the men of Dulichium.

## Thrasymedes · supporting

A son of Nestor and a captain of the night watch.

## Sthenelus · supporting

Son of Capaneus and Diomed’s charioteer and constant companion.

## Protesilaus · supporting

The first Greek killed at Troy, leaping ashore ahead of the rest; his men now follow Podarces.

## Podarces · supporting

Son of Iphiclus, who leads Protesilaus’s men after his brother’s death.

## Philoctetes · reference

The skilful archer left behind sick on Lemnos, whose men came without him.

## Zeus · major

King of the gods, who holds the balance of the war. Butler’s original calls him Jove throughout, with a single slip into Zeus at 16:51.

## Hera · major

Zeus’s wife and sister, implacably against Troy. Butler’s original calls her Juno.

## Athena · major

Zeus’s daughter, goddess of war and craft, and the Greeks’ fiercest partisan. Butler’s original calls her Minerva.

## Apollo · major

God of archery and plague, and Troy’s chief defender among the gods; also called Phoebus.

## Poseidon · major

God of the sea and the earthquake, who works for the Greeks against his brother’s orders. Butler’s original calls him Neptune.

## Ares · major

God of war, who fights on the Trojan side until Diomed wounds him. Butler’s original calls him Mars.

## Aphrodite · supporting

Goddess of love, Aeneas’s mother, and Paris’s protector. Butler’s original calls her Venus.

## Hephaestus · supporting

The lame smith of the gods, who makes Achilles’ new armour. Butler’s original calls him Vulcan.

## Hermes · supporting

The messenger god, who guides Priam through the Greek camp. Butler’s original calls him Mercury.

## Artemis · supporting

Goddess of the hunt and Apollo’s sister. Butler’s original calls her Diana.

## Iris · supporting

The gods’ messenger, who carries their orders down to the plain.

## Leto · reference

Mother of Apollo and Artemis; the original also names her Latona.

## Hades · supporting

God of the world below; the name also stands for his house.

## Persephone · reference

Queen of the dead. Butler’s original calls her Proserpine.

## Demeter · reference

Goddess of the harvest. Butler’s original calls her Ceres.

## Cronus · reference

Zeus’s father, imprisoned below with the Titans. Butler’s original calls him Saturn.

## Rhea · reference

Mother of Zeus, Poseidon and Hades. The original spells her Rhaea.

## Dione · reference

Aphrodite’s mother, who comforts her on Olympus after Diomed wounds her.

## Hebe · reference

Daughter of Zeus and Hera, who pours the nectar and harnesses Hera’s chariot.

## Themis · reference

A goddess of Olympus who greets Hera on her return and summons the gods to council.

## Charis · reference

Hephaestus’s wife, who receives Thetis in the smith’s house.

## Dawn · supporting

The goddess of daybreak, who rises from beside Tithonus.

## Oceanus · reference

The stream encircling the earth, spoken of as the father of the gods.

## Tethys · reference

Oceanus’s wife, named with him as the source of the gods.

## Sleep · supporting

Death’s own brother, whom Hera bribes to close Zeus’s eyes.

## Scamander · major

The river of the Trojan plain, who rises against Achilles; men call him Scamander and the gods Xanthus.

## Ilithuia · reference

Goddess of the pains of childbirth. The original also uses the plural Ilithuiae.

## Paeeon · reference

The healer of the gods, who cures Ares and Hades of their wounds.

## Bacchus · reference

God of wine, driven into the sea by Lycurgus when he was young.

## The Erinyes · reference

The avenging spirits a wronged parent calls down; the original also uses the singular Erinys.

## The Muses · supporting

## Nereus · reference

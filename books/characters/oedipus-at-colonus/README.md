# Oedipus at Colonus recognition cards

All 11 local sections, 566 paragraphs per English edition. Fifty-four entries in each. Authoring-agent review, not independent editorial approval. No source changes or runtime registration.

Oedipus is Central. Antigone, Ismene, Theseus, Creon, Polyneices and the chorus are Major. The resident stranger, messenger, attendants and sacred grove's goddesses are Supporting. Genealogical names, the Argive champions and divine references are Reference.

Ordinary relationships are immediate: Antigone is Oedipus's daughter and guide; Theseus is king of Athens; Creon is Jocasta's brother. The family history is already part of this play's premise, not a concealed identity to rediscover from Oedipus Rex. Reminders remain short throughout; no curse outcome, abduction outcome, death report, secret burial place or final instruction becomes a running recap.

## Source distinctions

- Eteocles (original Etocles), Oedipus's son, is separate from Eteoclus, the Argive champion. Amphiaraus is spelled Amphiaraiis in the original. These exact variants are bound without modifying source files.
- Polyneices's wife is recognized at the child/daughter word in the marriage statement. Adrastus retains a separate name span; the later child of Atalanta is not misassigned to the wife. Talaos is described according to the genealogy this source supplies for Hippomedon.
- Jocasta is unnamed in these local editions but identified through Oedipus's explicit mother references. The generic unborn child's mother's womb is not assigned to her.
- The local stranger and Theseus's messenger remain distinct. Creon's attendants and the Athenian party also remain separate groups.
- Colonus names both a district and its patron hero. Only 1:27's explicit patron is bound to the hero. Hades as a destination is not treated as a person; Aidoneus names the god.
- The Queen and Maid in the Eleusinian passage are Demeter and Persephone. Persephassa and the infernal Queen are Persephone. The underworld watchdog is Cerberus. The giver of eternal sleep is recorded as the personified power of Death, without asserting a disputed genealogy. Earth and Darkness/Night are limited to explicit divine contexts. Tartarus's geographical and genealogical usages are not given blanket personal aliases.
- The source's text-corruption notices, embedded footnote numerals and repeated entrance directions remain unchanged. No missing dialogue is invented.

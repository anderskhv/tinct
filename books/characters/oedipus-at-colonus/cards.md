# Oedipus at Colonus: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Oedipus · central

The blind former king of Thebes, now an exile traveling with his daughter Antigone.

## Antigone · major

Oedipus’s daughter and guide during his exile.

## Ismene · major

Oedipus’s daughter and Antigone’s sister.

## Theseus · major

The king of Athens, son of Aegeus.

## Creon · major

The Theban statesman, Jocasta’s brother and uncle of Oedipus’s children.

## Polyneices · major

Oedipus’s elder son and brother of Eteocles, Antigone and Ismene.

## The elders of Colonus · major

The local elders who speak for the community at Colonus.

## The local stranger · supporting

The resident of Colonus who first explains the sacred ground to Oedipus.

## The messenger · supporting

An attendant of Theseus who reports what happens away from the stage.

## The Eumenides · supporting

The avenging goddesses whose sacred grove is at Colonus, respectfully called the Gracious Ones.

## Creon’s men · supporting

The attendants who accompany Creon from Thebes.

## Theseus’s men · supporting

The Athenian attendants and fighters serving Theseus.

## Jocasta · reference

Oedipus’s mother and wife, the mother of his children and sister of Creon.

## Laius · reference

Oedipus’s father, an earlier king of Thebes.

## Labdacus · reference

Laius’s father and Oedipus’s grandfather.

## Eteocles · reference

Oedipus’s younger son, Polyneices’s rival for the throne of Thebes. Printed Etocles in the original.

## Adrastus · reference

The king of Argos and father-in-law of Polyneices.

## Polyneices’s wife · reference

The daughter of Adrastus whom Polyneices marries in Argos.

## Amphiaraus · reference

The warrior and seer among the champions marching with Polyneices. Printed Amphiaraiis in the original.

## Tydeus · reference

The Aetolian champion, son of Oeneus, allied with Polyneices.

## Oeneus · reference

Tydeus’s father.

## Eteoclus · reference

The Argive champion allied with Polyneices; distinct from Oedipus’s son Eteocles.

## Hippomedon · reference

One of the Argive champions marching with Polyneices.

## Talaos · reference

The father of Hippomedon in the genealogy given here.

## Capaneus · reference

The champion who boasts of burning Thebes.

## Parthenopaeus · reference

The Arcadian champion, son of Atalanta, allied with Polyneices.

## Atalanta · reference

The legendary huntress and mother of Parthenopaeus.

## Aegeus · reference

Theseus’s father and former king of Athens.

## Pelops · reference

The legendary hero whose name is associated with the Peloponnese.

## Peirithous · reference

The hero and companion of Theseus, recalled through their pact.

## Colonus · reference

The local hero and patron from whom the district takes its name.

## Poseidon · reference

The god of the sea and horses, worshipped at Colonus.

## Prometheus · reference

The fire-bearing Titan honored near the sacred grove.

## Apollo · reference

The god of prophecy, also called Phoebus.

## Athena · reference

The patron goddess of Athens, also called Pallas and Athene.

## Zeus · reference

The chief Olympian god, associated with thunder and the protection of suppliants.

## Dionysus · reference

The god of wine and ecstatic worship, celebrated here as the Bacchic god.

## The nymphs · reference

The nature spirits who cared for the young Dionysus.

## The Muses · reference

The goddesses of song and the arts.

## Aphrodite · reference

The goddess of love, called the Cyprian here.

## Kronos · reference

The Titan who is father of Poseidon and Zeus.

## The Nereids · reference

The sea nymphs whose dancing feet appear in the chorus’s image of swift oars.

## Ares · reference

The god of war, associated with the Athenian hill called the Areopagus.

## Rhea · reference

The mother of Poseidon and other Olympian gods.

## Artemis · reference

The hunting goddess and sister of Apollo.

## Demeter · reference

The mother goddess worshipped with her daughter Persephone in the Eleusinian mysteries.

## Persephone · reference

The queen of the underworld and daughter of Demeter, also called Persephassa.

## The Eumolpidae · reference

The priestly family associated with the secret rites at Eleusis.

## Hermes · reference

The god who guides the souls of the dead.

## Hades · reference

The ruler of the underworld, addressed as Aidoneus.

## Cerberus · reference

The watchdog guarding the entrance to the underworld.

## Death · reference

The divine power of death addressed as the giver of eternal sleep.

## Earth · reference

The primordial divine Earth, named in the genealogy of the sacred powers.

## Night · reference

The primordial power of darkness associated with the avenging goddesses.

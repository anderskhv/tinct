# Twelfth Night: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Viola · central

The play’s main character, Sebastian’s twin sister, shipwrecked on the coast of Illyria.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 2: The main character, Sebastian’s twin sister, serving Orsino under the name Cesario.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 154: The main character, Sebastian’s twin sister and Orsino’s intended bride; Cesario is her assumed name.

## Sebastian · major

Viola’s twin brother.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 67: Viola’s twin brother and Olivia’s husband.

## Orsino · major

The Duke of Illyria, who is courting Olivia.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 154: The Duke of Illyria, now intending to marry Viola.

## Olivia · major

The wealthy countess whom Orsino is courting, and Sir Toby’s niece.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 67: The wealthy countess, Sir Toby’s niece and Sebastian’s wife.

## Sir Toby Belch · major

Olivia’s uncle, a resident in her household and Sir Andrew’s drinking companion.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 148: Olivia’s uncle, now married to Maria.

## Sir Andrew Aguecheek · major

Sir Toby’s companion, the knight seeking Olivia’s hand.

## Maria · major

Olivia’s waiting-woman, who keeps watch over the household’s unruly guests.

After source chapter 18, paragraph 148: Olivia’s waiting-woman and Sir Toby’s wife.

## Malvolio · major

Olivia’s steward, responsible for managing her household.

## Feste · major

Olivia’s jester, labeled Clown in the dialogue.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 13: Olivia’s jester, also appearing under the clerical name Sir Topas.

## Antonio · major

The sea captain who helps Sebastian.

## Valentine · supporting

Orsino’s attendant, sent to Olivia on his behalf.

## Curio · supporting

One of Orsino’s attendants.

## Fabian · supporting

A member of Olivia’s household and Sir Toby’s companion.

## Viola’s captain · supporting

The captain who helps Viola after the shipwreck.

## The Priest · supporting

The clergyman Olivia brings to solemnize her vows.

## The first officer · supporting

One of the Illyrian officers who arrest Antonio.

## The second officer · supporting

The other officer taking Antonio into custody.

## Olivia’s servant · supporting

The attendant announcing Orsino’s messenger at Olivia’s house.

## Olivia’s handmaid · supporting

The unnamed attendant who gives Olivia’s reply to Valentine.

## The shipwrecked sailors · supporting

The sailors with Viola and the captain on the coast.

## The musicians · supporting

The musicians attending Orsino’s household.

## Sebastian of Messaline · reference

The father of the twins Viola and Sebastian.

## Olivia’s father · reference

The deceased count, father of Olivia and her brother.

## Olivia’s brother · reference

The brother whose death Olivia is mourning.

## Titus · reference

Orsino’s nephew, mentioned in the account of the naval fighting.

## Dick the surgeon · reference

The surgeon Sir Toby asks for after being injured.

## Mistress Mall · reference

The woman whose curtained portrait Sir Toby uses as a comparison.

## Quinapalus · reference

The invented authority Feste cites in his joke about wisdom and folly.

## Pigrogromitus · reference

The invented figure in Feste’s nonsense, recalled by Sir Andrew.

## The Vapians · reference

The imaginary people in Feste’s nonsense about crossing the equinoctial.

## The Myrmidons · reference

Achilles’s warriors in Greek legend, incongruously invoked by Feste.

## Peg-a-Ramsey · reference

The figure in a popular song whose name Toby applies to Malvolio.

## The man in Babylon · reference

The man mentioned in Sir Toby’s song fragment.

## The lady of the Strachy · reference

The woman whose marriage to a servant Malvolio cites as a precedent.

## The wardrobe servant · reference

The man said to have married the lady of the Strachy.

## Sowter · reference

A stock hound-name used in Fabian’s metaphor about Malvolio following a scent.

## The Sophy · reference

The Persian ruler referred to by his title in the characters’ comparisons and boasts.

## Capilet · reference

Sir Andrew’s gray horse.

## The hermit of Prague · reference

The supposed hermit in Feste’s invented anecdote.

## King Gorboduc · reference

The legendary British king named in Feste’s anecdote.

## Gorboduc’s niece · reference

The supposed niece addressed by the hermit in Feste’s anecdote.

## Robin · reference

The man addressed in Feste’s song.

## The Egyptian thief · reference

Thyamis, the bandit in Heliodorus’s romance, invoked in Orsino’s comparison about jealousy.

## The Brownists · reference

The religious separatists invoked in Sir Andrew’s comparison.

## Pythagoras · reference

The Greek philosopher associated here with the migration of souls between living creatures.

## Arion · reference

The legendary musician rescued at sea by a dolphin.

## Diana · reference

The goddess invoked in Orsino’s description of Cesario’s appearance.

## Mercury · reference

The Roman messenger god, invoked by Feste.

## Jove · reference

Jupiter, the king of the Roman gods.

## Penthesilea · reference

The Amazon queen whose name Sir Toby uses to address Maria.

## Lucrece · reference

The Roman Lucretia, associated with chastity and invoked through Olivia’s seal.

## Pandarus · reference

The go-between in the story of Troilus and Cressida.

## Cressida · reference

Troilus’s beloved, invoked in Feste’s joke about begging.

## Troilus · reference

The Trojan lover of Cressida.

## Vulcan · reference

The Roman smith-god, used as a comparison for a smoke-blackened face.

## Eve · reference

The first woman in the biblical creation story.

## Saint Anne · reference

The saint traditionally identified as the Virgin Mary’s mother.

## Jezebel · reference

The biblical queen whose name Sir Andrew uses as an insult.

## Noah · reference

The biblical survivor of the Flood.

## Legion · reference

The collective demonic name in the Gospel story of possession.

## Satan · reference

The devil, invoked in the characters’ jokes and accusations.

## Beelzebub · reference

The demonic figure Feste invokes, named here as Belzebub.

## God · reference

The deity invoked in the characters’ prayers and exclamations.

## The Vice · reference

The comic tempter of older morality plays, evoked in Feste’s song.

## The Egyptians · reference

The people evoked in Feste’s reference to the biblical plague of darkness.

# Utilitarianism: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Socrates · reference

The Athenian philosopher represented in Plato’s dialogues.

## Protagoras · reference

The Greek sophist who debates Socrates in Plato’s dialogue.

## Plato · reference

The Greek philosopher and author of the dialogue featuring Socrates and Protagoras.

## Jeremy Bentham · reference

The British philosopher and reformer associated with the greatest-happiness principle.

## Immanuel Kant · reference

The German philosopher whose account of moral duty Mill discusses.

## Epicurus · reference

The ancient Greek philosopher who made pleasure and freedom from pain central to a good life.

## Thomas Carlyle · reference

The Scottish writer and social critic quoted on the supposed right to happiness.

## Novalis · reference

The German Romantic poet and thinker cited in the discussion of happiness.

## Jesus of Nazareth · reference

The central figure of Christianity, invoked for the Golden Rule and love of others.

## John Galt · reference

The Scottish novelist who wrote Annals of the Parish.

## The Rev. J. Llewellyn Davis · reference

The clergyman who challenges Mill’s example about motives and rescuing a drowning person.

## Auguste Comte · reference

The French philosopher whose system of positive politics Mill discusses.

## Robert Owen · reference

The social reformer cited for his view that circumstances shape a person’s character.

## Alexander Bain · reference

The philosopher and psychologist whose work on the emotions and will Mill cites.

## Herbert Spencer · reference

The philosopher and author of Social Statics who corresponds with Mill about utility.

## God · reference

The divine being discussed in relation to morality, happiness and religious belief.

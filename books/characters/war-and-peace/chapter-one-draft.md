# War and Peace — Chapter 1 character-card sample

Editorial sample for Anders, 2026-09-09. Based on Book One (1805), Chapter 1 in both repository English editions (42 aligned paragraphs). This is a copy review, not a compiled or published character package.

## The people in the opening conversation

**Anna Pavlovna Scherer**  
The hostess of the gathering in St. Petersburg. A lady-in-waiting and favorite of the Dowager Empress.

**Prince Vasili Kuragin**  
An influential courtier and Anna Pavlovna’s first guest. Father of Hélène, Hippolyte, and Anatole.

## The families they discuss

**Hélène Kuragina**  
Prince Vasili’s daughter, admired for her beauty. Sister of Hippolyte and Anatole.

**Hippolyte Kuragin**  
Prince Vasili’s elder son; brother of Hélène and Anatole.

**Anatole Kuragin**  
Prince Vasili’s younger son. His father complains about his behavior and extravagant spending.

**Princess Marya Bolkonskaya**  
Old Prince Bolkonski’s daughter and Andrei’s sister. She lives with her father in the country.

**Old Prince Bolkonski**  
A wealthy, retired army officer. Father of Andrei and Marya.

**Prince Andrei Bolkonski**  
Old Prince Bolkonski’s son and Marya’s brother. An officer serving as Kutuzov’s aide, married to Lise.

**Lise Bolkonskaya**  
Andrei’s wife, also called Lise Meinen, her maiden name.

## Other people mentioned

**Vicomte de Mortemart**  
A French aristocrat living abroad, expected at Anna Pavlovna’s gathering.

**Abbé Morio**  
A clergyman and thinker invited to Anna Pavlovna’s gathering.

**Baron Funke**  
A candidate for a diplomatic post in Vienna, supported by the Dowager Empress.

## Historical names and references

**Napoleon Bonaparte**  
The French emperor, also called Buonaparte. The political conversation centers on opposition to him.

**Emperor Alexander**  
Russia’s reigning emperor, whom Anna Pavlovna praises so fervently.

**Empress Marya Fedorovna**  
The Dowager Empress and Anna Pavlovna’s patroness. “Dowager” means the widow of an emperor.

**Novosiltsev**  
A Russian envoy involved in the negotiations with England discussed here.

**Hardenberg**  
A Prussian statesman mentioned in the discussion of whether Prussia will oppose Napoleon. Spelled “Hardenburg” in the older English edition.

**Haugwitz**  
Another Prussian statesman mentioned alongside Hardenberg.

**Wintzingerode**  
An envoy mentioned in connection with seeking the King of Prussia’s support.

**The King of Prussia**  
The Prussian monarch whose support Russia is seeking. Later in this chapter, the same title is used as a nickname for old Prince Bolkonski.

**Kutuzov**  
A Russian general. Andrei Bolkonski serves as his aide.

**Lavater**  
A writer known for trying to read character from physical appearance. Vasili invokes him in a joke about his failings as a father.

**The Buonapartes**  
Napoleon Bonaparte’s family.

**The Montmorencys / The Rohans**  
French aristocratic families. Anna Pavlovna cites these connections to establish Mortemart’s social standing.

## Editorial application

These are reminders, not running summaries. Most cards need only the name and one or two short sentences; there is no need to repeat the same identity in a subtitle and body. Once the reader knows Anna Pavlovna, “The St. Petersburg hostess; a favorite of the Dowager Empress” can remain sufficient. Her latest remarks, gestures, or conversations do not require new snapshots.

The Anatole description above is appropriate at the end of Chapter 1. At his first mention, before the discussion of his spending, use simply: “Prince Vasili’s younger son; brother of Hélène and Hippolyte.” A compact distinguishing fact may be added when helpful, but a completed paragraph is not itself a reason to update a card.

Ordinary family identities may be clarified at first encounter even where the first name is supplied shortly afterward. Hélène appears here as Vasili’s unnamed daughter; Andrei first appears as Marya’s brother and “young Bolkonski.” Giving their names helps connect references. It does not release their later stories. Mary is the older translation’s form of Marya; edition-specific display names should follow the text. Anna Pavlovna and Annette Scherer refer to the same woman.

Do not put Pierre, Natasha, or other people absent from Chapter 1 into this chapter’s gallery. Do not label anyone a future spouse, romantic interest, or major character on the strength of later chapters. Do not turn Anna Pavlovna’s judgments about other people into objective character traits.

The literal King of Prussia and the nickname for old Prince Bolkonski require separate contextual resolution. “Prince,” “the Empress,” and unnamed family references also need reviewed bindings before this could be published. This draft does not claim to resolve those automatically. Incidental servants, the unnamed English ambassador, the Empress’s sister, and the deceased emperor are not given separate cards in this sample.

# Much Ado About Nothing recognition package

All seventeen scenes, both English editions: 60 entries each and 1,648 / 1,647 exact mentions. Hero, Claudio, Beatrice and Benedick are Central; Leonato, Don Pedro, Don John, Borachio, Dogberry and Friar Francis Major; other participants Supporting; religious, literary and remote-ruler examples Reference. All categories accompany copy in cards.md.

Relationships identify people from first encounter: Hero is Leonato's daughter and Beatrice's cousin; Benedick is the Paduan soldier behind Mountanto; Margaret and Ursula belong to Hero's household. Neither the allegations against Hero nor her supposed death becomes an established fact. Margaret's first card does not label her a knowing conspirator. Hero's secret survival releases after the agreed concealment plan at 12:74. The ending still anticipates formal weddings: final couple cards say intended husband/wife/bride, respecting 17:40 and 17:63 rather than treating Don Pedro's teasing married man as proof of a completed ceremony.

Don Pedro's assumed Claudio at 1:120, Claudio's assumed Benedick at 4:72/80, and Borachio's proposed Claudio at 5:14 are mapped to the actual man. At 5:14 and 9:63, Hero as the name applied to Margaret maps to Margaret; allegations about Hero herself and references to Hero's own garments remain Hero. Modern quotes around the assumed names and under versus by the name are handled explicitly. Leonato's proposed substitute niece is not fabricated as a new, genuine daughter of Antonio or linked to Hero before the unveiling. Antonio's actual son in 2:1 is retained separately.

George Seacoal answers under SECOND WATCH in these editions; FIRST WATCH is not automatically made Hugh Oatcake. Francis Seacoal is the summoned writer/sexton, not George or Friar Francis. The three messengers have separate roles. Deformed is the watch's imaginary thief, not another conspirator. Adam in 1:101 is the archer Adam Bell; later Adam is biblical. The first Europa at 17:25 means Europe and stays unbound; the second is the mythological woman. Saturn in born under Saturn is astrological, not a speaking god. Great Cham / Great Khan is a title; no particular historical khan is invented.

Primary/editorial checks: [Folger's full play](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/much-ado-about-nothing/read/) for cast, watch dialogue and pending weddings; the [earlier edited cast naming Francis Seacoal as sexton](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Much_ado_about_nothing-_a_comedy_%28IA_muchadoaboutnoth00shakrich%29.pdf); and [Middle English Texts Series on Adam Bell](https://metseditions.org/read/EeZlr66Hr830cE5bQhqeVXt6jMEXvB29). Binding uses the actual repository editions, not the line numbers or wording of these supplementary sources.

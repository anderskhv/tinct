# Much Ado About Nothing: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Beatrice · central

Leonato’s niece and Hero’s cousin, known for her sharp exchanges with Benedick.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 64: Benedick’s intended wife, Hero’s cousin and Leonato’s niece.

## Benedick · central

A gentleman and soldier from Padua, and Beatrice’s sparring partner.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 64: Beatrice’s intended husband, a gentleman and soldier from Padua.

## Hero · central

Leonato’s daughter and Beatrice’s cousin.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 124: Claudio’s intended bride, Leonato’s daughter and Beatrice’s cousin.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 75: Leonato’s daughter and Beatrice’s cousin, secretly alive while her death is announced.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 41: Claudio’s intended bride, reunited with him after her innocence is established.

## Count Claudio · central

The young Florentine soldier returning from war with Don Pedro.

After source chapter 4, paragraph 124: Hero’s intended husband, a young Florentine count and soldier.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 49: The Florentine count who has broken off his wedding to Hero.

After source chapter 17, paragraph 41: Hero’s intended husband, now reconciled with her.

## Leonato · major

The governor of Messina, Hero’s father and Beatrice’s uncle.

## Don Pedro · major

The Prince of Aragon and Don John’s brother, returning from war with Claudio and Benedick.

## Don John · major

Don Pedro’s illegitimate half-brother.

## Antonio · supporting

Leonato’s brother and uncle to Hero and Beatrice.

## Borachio · major

A follower of Don John.

## Conrade · supporting

A companion and follower of Don John.

## Margaret · supporting

Hero’s waiting gentlewoman, also called Meg.

## Ursula · supporting

An attendant and companion of Hero.

## Balthasar · supporting

Don Pedro’s attendant and a singer.

## Dogberry · major

The constable in charge of Messina’s watch.

## Verges · supporting

The elderly officer who assists Dogberry.

## George Seacoal · supporting

The watchman whom Dogberry chooses to lead the night patrol.

## Hugh Oatcake · supporting

One of the men proposed to lead the watch because he can read and write.

## Francis Seacoal, the sexton · supporting

The parish clerk whom Dogberry summons to record the examination of the prisoners.

## Friar Francis · major

The priest who attends Hero and Claudio’s wedding ceremony.

## Don Pedro’s messenger · supporting

The messenger who brings Leonato news of Don Pedro’s returning army.

## The wedding messenger · supporting

The messenger who calls Leonato to the wedding.

## The final messenger · supporting

The messenger who brings Don Pedro news of his brother.

## Claudio’s uncle · supporting

Claudio’s uncle in Messina, to whom news of his military success is sent.

## Hero’s mother · supporting

Leonato’s wife and Hero’s mother, mentioned in the opening exchange.

## Leonato’s fool · supporting

The household fool whom Beatrice recalls answering Benedick’s archery challenge.

## Antonio’s son · supporting

Leonato’s nephew, who is arranging music for the festivities.

## Antonio’s servant · supporting

The servant who overhears Don Pedro and Claudio in the orchard.

## Benedick’s boy · supporting

The young servant whom Benedick sends to fetch a book.

## The first watchman · supporting

One of the members of Messina’s night watch.

## The watch · supporting

Messina’s night watch, supervised by Dogberry and Verges.

## The lord at the tomb · supporting

A lord attending Don Pedro and Claudio at the tomb.

## Don Pedro’s father · supporting

The father of Don Pedro and Don John, mentioned in Beatrice’s banter.

## Adam Bell · reference

The legendary English archer invoked in Benedick’s joke about being shot at.

## Adam · reference

The first man in the biblical creation story, invoked in Beatrice and Benedick’s banter.

## Cupid · reference

The god of love, frequently invoked in the jokes about courtship.

## Vulcan · reference

The Roman smith god, contrasted with Cupid in Benedick’s joke.

## Saint Peter · reference

The apostle imagined by Beatrice as admitting her to heaven.

## Philemon · reference

The humble host who welcomes Jupiter in classical myth.

## Jupiter · reference

The Roman king of the gods, called Jove.

## Hercules · reference

The mythological hero famed for strength and his twelve labors.

## Ate · reference

The classical spirit of ruin and discord, invoked in Benedick’s complaint about Beatrice.

## Prester John · reference

The legendary Christian priest-king of a distant eastern kingdom.

## Hector · reference

The Trojan warrior invoked as a model of courage.

## Pharaoh · reference

The biblical Egyptian ruler evoked through a painting of his soldiers.

## Bel · reference

The Babylonian deity invoked through an image of his priests.

## Diana · reference

The Roman goddess associated with chastity, called Dian in the original edition.

## Venus · reference

The Roman goddess of love.

## Leander · reference

The legendary lover who swims across the Hellespont.

## Troilus · reference

The Trojan lover from the story of Troilus and Cressida.

## Phoebus · reference

Apollo as the sun god, invoked at daybreak.

## Hymen · reference

The classical god of marriage, invoked by Claudio.

## Europa · reference

The mythological woman carried away by Jupiter in the form of a bull.

## God · reference

The Christian deity invoked in the characters’ prayers and oaths.

## The Devil · reference

The infernal figure in Beatrice’s imagined account of the afterlife.

## The Great Cham · reference

The title of a powerful Asian ruler invoked in Benedick’s extravagant offer to travel to distant lands.

## The Pygmies · reference

The legendary people of small stature named in Benedick’s fanciful catalogue of distant journeys.

## The Duchess of Milan · reference

The duchess whose fashionable gown Margaret recalls seeing.

## The duke · reference

The unnamed ruler invoked as the authority behind Messina’s officers.

## Deformed · supporting

The imaginary thief whom the watch thinks Borachio has named, misunderstanding his complaint about fashion.

## Borachio and Conrade · supporting

Don John’s two followers, answering together during the examination.

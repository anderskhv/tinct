# Phaedo: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Socrates · central

The Athenian philosopher whose final conversation in prison Phaedo recounts.

## Phaedo · central

Socrates’ companion who narrates his final day to Echecrates.

## Echecrates · supporting

The man in Phlius asking Phaedo to recount Socrates’ final hours.

## Simmias · major

The Theban friend of Socrates who, with Cebes, questions him about the soul.

## Cebes · major

The Theban friend of Socrates who, with Simmias, examines his arguments about the soul.

## Crito · major

Socrates’ longtime friend, attending to his practical needs in prison; father of Critobulus.

## Apollodorus · supporting

The intensely emotional friend of Socrates present in the prison.

## Xanthippe · supporting

Socrates’ wife, visiting him in prison with their young child.

## Critobulus · supporting

Crito’s son, one of the friends present with Socrates.

## Hermogenes · supporting

One of Socrates’ Athenian companions present in the prison.

## Epigenes · supporting

One of Socrates’ Athenian companions present in the prison.

## Aeschines · supporting

The follower of Socrates present in the prison, distinct from the later Athenian orator.

## Antisthenes · supporting

The philosopher and follower of Socrates present in the prison.

## Ctesippus · supporting

The Athenian from Paeania listed among Socrates’ companions in prison.

## Menexenus · supporting

One of Socrates’ companions present in the prison.

## Phaedondes · supporting

The Theban visitor listed with Simmias and Cebes.

## Euclid of Megara · supporting

The philosopher visiting Socrates from Megara, distinct from the later mathematician.

## Terpison · supporting

Euclid’s fellow visitor from Megara, also known as Terpsion.

## Plato · reference

The philosopher who wrote this dialogue, mentioned among Socrates’ absent friends.

## Aristippus · reference

The follower of Socrates whom Echecrates asks about, reported to be in Aegina.

## Cleombrotus · reference

The acquaintance of Socrates reported to be in Aegina with Aristippus.

## Aesop · reference

The traditional Greek storyteller whose fables Socrates turns into verse.

## Evenus · reference

The poet and philosopher who asks about Socrates’ new verses.

## Philolaus · reference

The Pythagorean thinker whom Simmias and Cebes have heard teaching in Thebes.

## Anaxagoras · reference

The Greek philosopher whose account of mind and nature Socrates discusses.

## John Milton · reference

The English poet cited in a translator’s comparison with Comus.

## Homer · reference

The ancient Greek epic poet whose Odyssey is quoted.

## Aeschylus · reference

The Greek tragic poet whose play Telephus is cited.

## Glaucus · reference

The craftsman whose art is invoked as proverbial for extraordinary skill.

## Theseus · reference

The Athenian hero whose voyage to Crete is commemorated by the sacred ship.

## Apollo · reference

The Greek god honored by the mission to Delos and by Socrates’ hymn.

## Endymion · reference

The figure of Greek myth famous for his unending sleep.

## Penelope · reference

Odysseus’ wife in the Odyssey, recalled for weaving and unweaving her web.

## Heracles · reference

The Greek hero invoked in the comparison about needing help against two opponents.

## Iolaus · reference

Heracles’ companion and helper, invoked in the exchange between Socrates and Phaedo.

## Odysseus · reference

The hero of Homer’s Odyssey, quoted speaking sternly to his own heart.

## Harmonia · reference

The Theban goddess and wife of Cadmus; her name supplies a joke about Simmias’s harmony argument.

## Cadmus · reference

The legendary founder of Thebes and husband of Harmonia, used as a playful name for Cebes’s argument.

## Atlas · reference

The mythological bearer of the heavens, used in Socrates’ discussion of what supports the world.

## Asclepius · reference

The Greek god of healing to whom Socrates says a cock is owed.

## God · reference

The divine power invoked by Socrates and his companions in their discussion of the soul.

## The Eleven · supporting

The Athenian officials responsible for prisoners and executions.

## The prison doorkeeper · supporting

The prison official who admits Socrates’ friends on the final morning.

## The prison officer · supporting

The servant of the Eleven who comes to announce that it is time for Socrates to drink the poison.

## The poison attendant · supporting

The prison attendant who prepares and administers the poison.

## Crito’s servant · supporting

The servant whom Crito sends to fetch the poison attendant.

## Crito’s attendants · supporting

The members of Crito’s household who escort Xanthippe home.

## Socrates’ young child · supporting

The child whom Xanthippe holds during her prison visit.

## Socrates’ sons · supporting

The three sons of Socrates, two young and one older.

## The women of Socrates’ family · supporting

The female relatives who visit Socrates after his bath.

## The priest of Apollo · reference

The priest who crowns the sacred ship before its voyage to Delos.

## The fourteen young Athenians · reference

The young people whom Theseus brings safely back from Crete in the recalled tradition.

## The guiding spirit · reference

The personal guardian who leads a soul after death in Socrates’ account.

## The weaver in Cebes’s comparison · reference

The imagined weaver used to compare the soul’s endurance with the body’s.

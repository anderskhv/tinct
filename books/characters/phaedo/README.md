# Phaedo recognition cards

Nine local sections, 652 paragraphs in each English edition. 54 entries per edition, with 456 original / 466 modern exact mentions. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval. Sources unchanged; runtime registration pending.

Socrates and the narrating Phaedo are Central. Simmias, Cebes and Crito are Major. Echecrates, the present prison companions, family and officials are Supporting. Absent friends, teachers, poets and mythical figures chiefly discussed are Reference. Every body is a single stable recognition reminder; there are no successive argument or death-scene recaps.

- The opening explicitly establishes Socrates' death and the framing account of his final day. That basic orientation is available immediately. Echecrates is the listener in Phlius, not one of the prison visitors. Phaedo is both an eyewitness and the narrator.
- Xanthippe is Socrates' wife from the first encounter. Critobulus is Crito's son. Euclid is the philosopher from Megara, not the mathematician; Aeschines is Socrates' follower, not the later orator. Terpison is the source spelling of Terpsion. Plato is an absent friend and the dialogue's author, not a participant given invented speech.
- Anonymous prison roles are scoped: morning doorkeeper at 1:25, departing officer of the Eleven at 9:6, poison attendant at 1:55 and 9:7/10–11, and Crito's servant at 9:10. The local translation reuses jailer/guard wording, but does not establish that the announcing official and the returning poison administrator are one person. Cards label the roles without claiming a reconstructed staff list. Crito's household attendants are a separate group.
- The initial child in Xanthippe's arms and the later collective sons/female relatives are identified without adding unprinted personal names. The priest of Apollo and Theseus' fourteen companions remain available as references.
- Harmonia and Cadmus are mythological references used playfully for the arguments of Simmias and Cebes, not new dialogue participants or identities for those men. Heracles and Iolaus likewise remain the figures of an analogy. The geographic Pillars of Heracles are excluded from the hero's bindings.
- Glaucus is the craftsman of proverbial skill, not the sea god. Milton belongs to the translator's comparison with Comus, not Socrates' historical knowledge. Meno, Comus and Telephus are titles in these passages and do not automatically create characters. Hades, Oceanus and the underworld rivers are places in the account. God describes the divine power invoked in the dialogue without importing a Christian identification.
- Cebes' weaver and the guiding spirit in the afterlife account are explicitly analogical or religious figures. No claim is made to resolve all pronouns, imagined examples or generic gods.

All coordinates use zero-based paragraph indices. Comparative primary text: [Plato's Phaedo at the Harvard-affiliated Kosmos Society](https://kosmossociety.org/phaedo-plato/).

# The Merry Wives of Windsor: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Sir John Falstaff · central

A fat old knight lodging at the Garter, short of money.

After source chapter 19, paragraph 8: The old knight, escaped from Ford’s house in the fat woman of Brentford’s gown.

## Mistress Page · central

A Windsor wife, called Meg; Master Page’s wife and Anne’s mother.

## Mistress Ford · central

A Windsor wife, called Alice; Master Ford’s wife.

## Master Ford · major

A Windsor householder, and a jealous husband.

After source chapter 6, paragraph 61: A Windsor householder, visiting Falstaff under the name Brook to draw him out.

## Master Page · major

A Windsor householder, Anne’s and William’s father.

## Sir Hugh Evans · major

A Welsh parson and schoolmaster in Windsor.

## Mistress Quickly · major

Doctor Caius’s housekeeper, and go-between for everyone in the town.

## Justice Shallow · major

A country justice, come to Windsor with a grievance against Falstaff.

## Abraham Slender · major

Shallow’s foolish young cousin, and a suitor for Anne Page.

## Doctor Caius · major

A French physician of Windsor, and a suitor for Anne Page.

## The Host of the Garter · major

The landlord of the Garter Inn.

## Pistol · major

One of Falstaff’s followers.

## Peter Simple · major

Slender’s servant.

## Anne Page · major

Master Page’s daughter, sought by three suitors.

After source chapter 23, paragraph 81: Master Page’s daughter, married to Fenton at the church by Eton.

## Fenton · major

A young gentleman in love with Anne Page.

After source chapter 23, paragraph 81: The young gentleman who has married Anne Page.

## Bardolph · supporting

One of Falstaff’s followers, taken on as a drawer at the Garter.

## Nym · supporting

One of Falstaff’s followers.

## William Page · supporting

Master Page’s young son, taken through his Latin by Sir Hugh.

## John Rugby · supporting

Doctor Caius’s servant.

## Robin · supporting

Falstaff’s page, lent to Mistress Page.

## The First Servant · supporting

One of Ford’s servants, who carries the buck-basket.

## The Second Servant · supporting

One of Ford’s servants, who carries the buck-basket.

## The servant · supporting

A servant in Master Page’s house.

## The servants · supporting

Ford’s servants, who carry the buck-basket to Datchet Mead.

## The fairies · supporting

The children and neighbours dressed as fairies at Herne’s oak.

## Herne the Hunter · reference

The keeper of Windsor legend, said to walk the forest with great ragg’d horns.

## The fat woman of Brentford · reference

A wise woman of Brentford whom Ford has forbidden his house; Falstaff escapes in her gown.

## Mother Prat · reference

The name Mistress Ford gives the fat woman’s gown when Falstaff is wearing it.

## Master Thomas Page · reference

Anne Page’s father under his given name, as Sir Hugh states it.

## George · reference

Master Page under his given name, as his wife uses it.

## Frank · reference

Master Ford under his given name.

## Sackerson · reference

The bear Slender boasts of having led about.

## The witch of Brentford · reference

The woman Ford believes he is beating, being Falstaff in her gown.

## Jove · reference

The king of the gods, who took a bull’s shape for Europa and a swan’s for Leda.

## Europa · reference

The woman Jove carried off in the shape of a bull.

## Leda · reference

The woman Jove came to in the shape of a swan.

## Actaeon · reference

The hunter turned to a stag, a byword here for the horned cuckold.

## Alice Shortcake · reference

A Windsor woman Slender lent his Book of Riddles to.

## Ringwood · reference

One of Actaeon’s hounds, in Pistol’s warning to Ford.

## Hercules · reference

The strongest of the Greek heroes.

## Pandarus · reference

The go-between of the Troy story, in Pistol’s refusal.

## Hobgoblin · reference

The goblin of country belief, one of the parts in the fairy masque.

## Mephostophilus · reference

The devil of the Faustus play, in Pistol’s abuse of Simple.

## Lucifer · reference

The chief of the fallen angels.

## Barbason · reference

A demon whose name Ford will not answer to.

## Machiavel · reference

The byword for cunning statecraft the Host claims for himself.

## Cataian · reference

A Cathayan, meaning a sharper; Page’s word for a man he will not believe.

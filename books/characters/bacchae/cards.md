# The Bacchae: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Dionysus · central

The god of wine and ecstatic worship, son of Zeus and Semele, appearing in human form.

## Pentheus · central

The king of Thebes, son of Agave and Echion, and grandson of Cadmus.

## Cadmus · major

The founder and former king of Thebes, grandfather of Pentheus and Dionysus.

## Agave · major

Pentheus’s mother, daughter of Cadmus and sister of Semele.

## The chorus of Asian Bacchants · major

The women from Asia who accompany Dionysus and worship him.

## The chorus leader · supporting

The spokeswoman for the Asian women who follow Dionysus.

## The Theban Bacchants · major

The women of Thebes gathered on the mountain under Dionysus’s influence.

## Teiresias · supporting

The blind prophet of Thebes and old friend of Cadmus. Also spelled Tiresias.

## Autonoe · supporting

Agave’s sister and Actaeon’s mother, one of the daughters of Cadmus.

## Ino · supporting

Agave’s sister, one of the daughters of Cadmus.

## The soldier · supporting

The officer commanding Pentheus’s guards.

## Pentheus’s guards · supporting

The soldiers serving the king of Thebes.

## The herdsman messenger · supporting

The herdsman who reports on the women’s activities on the mountain.

## Pentheus’s attendant · supporting

The servant who accompanies Pentheus to the mountain and returns as a messenger.

## The townsman among the herdsmen · supporting

The town-experienced man who proposes capturing Agave in the herdsman’s account.

## Semele · reference

Dionysus’s mother, a daughter of Cadmus and sister of Agave.

## Echion · reference

Pentheus’s father and Agave’s husband, one of the earth-born warriors of Thebes.

## Harmonia · reference

Cadmus’s wife, a daughter of Ares.

## Agenor · reference

Cadmus’s father, associated with the family’s origins in Phoenicia.

## Actaeon · reference

The hunter, son of Autonoe and nephew of Agave, recalled as a warning.

## Actaeon’s father · reference

The father of the hunter Actaeon, recalled by Cadmus at the end of the play.

## Zeus · reference

The chief Olympian god and father of Dionysus.

## Hera · reference

Zeus’s wife, whose hostility to Semele is recalled in the play.

## Rhea · reference

The mother goddess associated here with the invention of the ritual drum.

## Cybele · reference

The Phrygian mother goddess whose mountain rites are celebrated by the chorus.

## Aphrodite · reference

The goddess of love, also called the Cyprian.

## Demeter · reference

The goddess of grain and the earth’s nourishment.

## Ares · reference

The god of war and father of Harmonia.

## Apollo · reference

The god of prophecy, also called Phoebus.

## Artemis · reference

The goddess of the hunt, invoked in the warning about Actaeon.

## Orpheus · reference

The legendary musician whose song charms trees and wild animals.

## Pan · reference

The god of shepherds and wild places.

## The nymphs · reference

The female nature spirits invoked in the mountain setting.

## The Muses · reference

The goddesses of song and the arts.

## The satyrs · reference

The wild companion spirits of Dionysus, associated with dancing and revelry.

## The Corybant · reference

One of the armed ritual dancers associated with the mother goddess.

## Achelous · reference

The river god named as Dirce’s father. Also spelled Acheloues.

## Dirce · reference

The sacred Theban water personified as a daughter of Achelous.

## Purity · reference

The divine power of holiness addressed by the chorus.

## Peace · reference

Peace personified as the nurturer of children, beloved by Dionysus.

## Justice · reference

Justice personified and invoked by the chorus.

## The spirit of earthquake · reference

The power of earthquake summoned by Dionysus.

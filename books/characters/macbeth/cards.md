# Macbeth: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Macbeth · central

A Scottish nobleman and soldier, known as the Thane of Glamis. Lady Macbeth’s husband.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 19: A Scottish nobleman holding the titles Thane of Glamis and Thane of Cawdor. Lady Macbeth’s husband.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 15: King of Scotland and Lady Macbeth’s husband.

## Lady Macbeth · major

Macbeth’s wife.

## Banquo · major

A Scottish commander and Macbeth’s fellow soldier. Father of Fleance.

## Fleance · supporting

Banquo’s young son.

## Macduff · major

A Scottish nobleman, the Thane of Fife. Lady Macduff’s husband.

## Duncan · major

King of Scotland and father of Malcolm and Donalbain.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 15: Scotland’s former king; father of Malcolm and Donalbain.

## Malcolm · major

Duncan’s elder son and Donalbain’s brother.

After source chapter 28, paragraph 27: Duncan’s elder son, now acclaimed king of Scotland. Donalbain’s brother.

## Donalbain · supporting

Duncan’s younger son and Malcolm’s brother.

## The three witches · major

The three supernatural women, also called the Weird Sisters.

## First Witch · supporting

One of the three witches, distinguished by her numbered speaker label rather than a personal name.

## Second Witch · supporting

One of the three witches, distinguished by her numbered speaker label rather than a personal name.

## Third Witch · supporting

One of the three witches, distinguished by her numbered speaker label rather than a personal name.

## Ross · supporting

A Scottish nobleman who carries news between the other characters.

## Angus · supporting

A Scottish nobleman, accompanying Ross when he brings news to Macbeth.

## Lennox · supporting

A Scottish nobleman at the royal court.

## Menteith · supporting

A Scottish nobleman among the commanders opposing Macbeth.

## Caithness · supporting

A Scottish nobleman among the commanders opposing Macbeth.

## Lady Macduff · supporting

Macduff’s wife and the mother of the boy in the scene at their home.

## Macduff’s son · supporting

The young son of Macduff and Lady Macduff.

## Siward · supporting

An English commander and the earl of Northumberland. Father of Young Siward.

## Young Siward · supporting

Siward’s son, a young soldier in the army opposing Macbeth.

## Seyton · supporting

An attendant serving Macbeth at Dunsinane.

## The porter · supporting

The gatekeeper at Macbeth’s castle.

## The wounded soldier · supporting

The wounded soldier who reports the battle to Duncan. Also addressed as Captain.

## The old man · supporting

The elderly man speaking with Ross outside Macbeth’s castle.

## The gentlewoman · supporting

An attendant to Lady Macbeth.

## The Scottish doctor · supporting

The physician attending Lady Macbeth at Dunsinane.

## The English doctor · supporting

The physician at the English king’s court. A different doctor from the one at Dunsinane.

## The Thane of Cawdor · supporting

A Scottish nobleman who holds the title Thane of Cawdor.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 19: The Scottish nobleman whose title of Thane of Cawdor is transferred to Macbeth.

## Macdonwald · reference

A rebel leader fighting against King Duncan.

## Sweno · reference

The Norwegian king whose army fights Duncan’s forces.

## Sinel · reference

Macbeth’s father, from whom he inherited the title Thane of Glamis.

## King Edward · reference

The English king, Edward the Confessor, at whose court Malcolm finds refuge.

## Graymalkin · reference

The First Witch’s familiar spirit, taking the form of a cat.

## Paddock · reference

The Second Witch’s familiar spirit, taking the form of a toad.

## Harpier · reference

The familiar spirit called by the Third Witch. The passage does not describe its form.

## Hecate · supporting

A goddess associated with witchcraft.

After source chapter 16, paragraph 3: The goddess who presides over the witches and their magic.

## Bellona · reference

The Roman goddess of war.

## Neptune · reference

The Roman god of the sea.

## Belzebub · reference

A name for the devil, invoked in the porter’s joke about keeping the gates of hell.

## The Gorgon · reference

A monster of Greek mythology whose face turns those who look at it to stone.

## Fortune · reference

Luck imagined as a female power favoring one side or another.

## Banquo’s ghost · supporting

The apparition of Banquo seen by Macbeth at the banquet.

## The armed head · reference

The first apparition called up by the witches: a head wearing armor.

## The bloody child · reference

The second apparition called up by the witches: a child covered in blood.

## The crowned child · reference

The third apparition called up by the witches: a crowned child carrying a tree.

## The eight kings · reference

The line of crowned figures shown to Macbeth by the witches.

## The vision of Banquo · reference

The figure of Banquo accompanying the line of kings in the witches’ vision.

# Henry V: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## King Henry V · central

The young King of England, who presses a claim to the crown of France.

After source chapter 14, paragraph 23: The King of England, who gives Pistol the name Harry le Roy while he walks the camp unknown.

After source chapter 21, paragraph 23: The King of England, victor on the field the heralds name Agincourt.

## The King of France · major

The French king, Katharine’s father; the play never gives his name.

## The Dauphin · major

The French king’s son and heir, who sends King Henry a mocking gift.

## The Constable of France · major

Charles Delabreth, High Constable of France and commander of its army.

## Fluellen · major

A Welsh captain in the English army, devoted to the ancient disciplines of war.

## Pistol · major

An ensign in the English army, formerly of Falstaff’s London company.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 12: An ensign in the English army, now married to the Hostess.

## The Duke of Exeter · major

King Henry’s uncle, who carries his demands to the French court.

## Princess Katharine · major

The French king’s daughter, whom King Henry claims as part of his terms.

After source chapter 23, paragraph 14: The French king’s daughter, named by King Henry as the first article of his terms.

## The Chorus · major

The speaker who opens each act and asks the audience to imagine what the stage cannot show.

## Montjoy · major

The French herald, who carries his king’s messages to King Henry.

## The Archbishop of Canterbury · major

The Archbishop, who lays out King Henry’s claim to France.

## The Duke of Orleans · major

Charles, Duke of Orleans, a French nobleman and the king’s nephew.

## Gower · major

An English captain and Fluellen’s friend.

## Michael Williams · major

A soldier in the English army who argues with the disguised King on the eve of battle.

## The Duke of Gloucester · supporting

King Henry’s brother, who directs the siege works at Harfleur.

## The Duke of Bedford · supporting

King Henry’s brother.

## The Duke of Clarence · supporting

King Henry’s brother.

## The Earl of Westmorland · supporting

An English nobleman of King Henry’s council.

## The Earl of Warwick · supporting

An English nobleman in King Henry’s train.

## The Earl of Salisbury · supporting

An English nobleman who takes his charge before Agincourt.

## The Earl of Huntingdon · supporting

An English nobleman at the French court for the peace terms.

## The Duke of York · supporting

Edward, Duke of York, who asks to lead the vanguard at Agincourt.

## The Bishop of Ely · supporting

The Bishop, who presses King Henry’s French claim alongside Canterbury.

## Lord Scroop · supporting

Henry, Lord Scroop of Masham, one of King Henry’s intimates.

## The Earl of Cambridge · supporting

Richard, Earl of Cambridge.

## Sir Thomas Grey · supporting

A knight of Northumberland.

## Sir Thomas Erpingham · supporting

An old English knight, whose cloak the King borrows the night before Agincourt.

## Captain Jamy · supporting

A Scottish captain in the English army.

## Captain Macmorris · supporting

An Irish captain in the English army, in charge of the mines at Harfleur.

## John Bates · supporting

A soldier in the English army.

## Alexander Court · supporting

A soldier in the English army; not the Macedonian king Fluellen argues about later.

## Bardolph · supporting

A lieutenant in the English army, formerly of Falstaff’s London company.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 33: A lieutenant of the English army, condemned for robbing a church.

## Nym · supporting

A corporal in the English army, formerly of Falstaff’s London company.

## The Hostess · supporting

Nell Quickly, who keeps a London tavern and has married Pistol.

After source chapter 3, paragraph 12: Nell Quickly, who keeps a London tavern and has married Pistol.

## The Boy · supporting

The boy who serves Pistol, Bardolph and Nym, and interprets for Pistol in France.

## Queen Isabel · supporting

The Queen of France, Katharine’s mother.

## Alice · supporting

An old gentlewoman attending Princess Katharine, who has been in England.

## The Duke of Burgundy · supporting

The French duke who brokers the meeting between the two kings.

## The Duke of Bourbon · supporting

John, Duke of Bourbon, a French nobleman.

## Lord Rambures · supporting

A French lord, master of the crossbows.

## Lord Grandpré · supporting

A French lord who describes the English army on the morning of Agincourt.

## The Duke of Berry · supporting

A French duke summoned to the defence.

## The Duke of Brittany · supporting

A French duke summoned to the defence.

## The Duke of Brabant · supporting

Anthony, Duke of Brabant, brother to the Duke of Burgundy.

## The Duke of Alençon · supporting

John, Duke of Alençon, from whose helmet King Henry took a glove.

## The Duke of Bar · supporting

Edward, Duke of Bar, a French nobleman.

## Jacques Chatillon · supporting

Admiral of France.

## Lord Boucicault · supporting

A French lord, taken prisoner at Agincourt.

## Sir Guichard Dauphin · supporting

Great Master of France; a nobleman of that surname, not the king’s son.

## Vaudemont · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Beaumont · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Roussi · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Fauconbridge · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Foix · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Lestrale · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Marle · supporting

A French earl named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Charolois · supporting

A French lord called to the field.

## The Governor of Harfleur · supporting

The governor who yields the town to King Henry.

## Monsieur Le Fer · supporting

A French gentleman-soldier taken prisoner by Pistol on the field.

## The English herald · supporting

King Henry’s herald, who brings him the count of the dead.

## The messenger at the French court · supporting

The messenger who announces the English embassy to the French king.

## The messenger in the French camp · supporting

The messenger who brings the French commanders word of the English position.

## The French ambassador · supporting

The ambassador who delivers the Dauphin’s answer and his gift to King Henry.

## The French ambassadors · supporting

The embassy sent from France to King Henry’s court.

## The English ambassadors · supporting

The embassy King Henry sends into France, headed by Exeter.

## The attendants · supporting

The attendants waiting on King Henry at his court.

## The lords · supporting

The English and French lords at the two courts.

## The ladies · supporting

The ladies attending the French queen and princess.

## The soldiers · supporting

The soldiers of the English army.

## The citizens of Harfleur · supporting

The townspeople on the walls when King Henry demands the town.

## Sir John Falstaff · reference

The old knight of King Henry’s wild years, whose London company Pistol, Bardolph and Nym come from.

After source chapter 5, paragraph 3: The old knight of King Henry’s wild years; Pistol brings his company word that he has died.

## King Henry the Fourth · reference

King Henry’s father, who took the crown from Richard the Second; the play names him only as the King’s father.

## King Richard the Second · reference

The king King Henry’s father deposed, for whose soul King Henry pays priests to sing.

## Henry the Sixth · reference

King Henry’s infant son, crowned King of France and England after him.

## King Edward the Third · reference

King Henry’s great-grandfather, from whom the claim to France descends.

## Edward the Black Prince · reference

King Henry’s great-uncle, victor over the French at Crécy.

## The King of Scots · reference

A Scottish king taken prisoner by the English and sent into France.

## The Emperor · reference

The Holy Roman Emperor, expected in England to mediate the peace; he is never named.

## The Earl of Suffolk · reference

An English nobleman who dies at Agincourt beside the Duke of York.

## Sir Richard Ketly · reference

An English knight named among the dead at Agincourt.

## Davy Gam · reference

A Welsh esquire named among the English dead at Agincourt.

## Talbot · reference

An English commander named in King Henry’s roll of remembered names.

## Doll Tearsheet · reference

A London woman of Pistol’s old company, whose death he hears of at the end.

## Pharamond · reference

The legendary Frankish king the French cite as the founder of the Salic law.

## Charlemagne · reference

The Frankish emperor who subdued the Saxons and settled the French beyond the Sala.

## Charlemain · reference

A later Frankish king in Canterbury’s pedigree, father of Lady Lingare; the text keeps him distinct from Charlemagne.

## King Pepin · reference

The Frankish king who deposed Childeric and claimed France through the female line.

## Childeric · reference

The Frankish king deposed by Pepin.

## Blithild · reference

King Clothair’s daughter, from whom Pepin claimed descent.

## King Clothair · reference

A Frankish king, Blithild’s father.

## Hugh Capet · reference

The French king who took the crown from Charles, Duke of Lorraine.

## Charles, Duke of Lorraine · reference

The last male heir of Charlemagne’s line, displaced by Hugh Capet.

## Lady Lingare · reference

Charlemain’s daughter, from whom Hugh Capet traced his title.

## Lady Ermengare · reference

Daughter of Charles, Duke of Lorraine, and ancestress of Queen Isabel the grandmother.

## Lewis the Emperor · reference

A Frankish emperor in Canterbury’s pedigree, son of Charlemagne.

## King Lewis the Tenth · reference

The French king who wore the crown uneasily until his descent through the female line satisfied him.

## Queen Isabel · reference

King Lewis the Tenth’s grandmother in Canterbury’s pedigree; a different Queen Isabel from the French queen in the play.

## Alexander the Great · reference

The Macedonian king, whom Fluellen compares to King Henry.

## Philip of Macedon · reference

Alexander the Great’s father.

## Cleitus · reference

The friend Alexander killed in drink.

## Pompey the Great · reference

The Roman general whose camp Fluellen holds up as a model of discipline.

## Mark Antony · reference

The Roman commander Fluellen names as a byword for valour.

## Agamemnon · reference

The Greek commander at Troy, Fluellen’s measure of greatness of soul.

## Caesar · reference

The Roman conqueror whose triumphal return the Chorus likens King Henry’s to.

## Brutus · reference

The Roman who hid his purpose under an appearance of folly.

## Herod · reference

The king of Judea whose soldiers killed the children, in King Henry’s threat to Harfleur.

## Adam · reference

The first man, whose offending nature the Archbishop says was whipped out of the King.

## Arthur · reference

King Arthur; the Hostess puts Falstaff in “Arthur’s bosom”, meaning Abraham’s.

## Cadwallader · reference

The Welsh king Pistol invokes when he refuses Fluellen’s leek.

## Saint George · reference

The patron saint of England, King Henry’s battle cry.

## Saint David · reference

The patron saint of Wales, whose day the Welsh mark by wearing the leek.

## Saint Denis · reference

The patron saint of France.

## Saint Crispin and Saint Crispian · reference

The paired saints whose feast day falls on the day of the battle.

## Lucifer · reference

The chief of the fallen angels, in Fluellen’s oath.

## Beelzebub · reference

A devil named beside Lucifer in Fluellen’s oath.

## Barbason · reference

A demon whose name Nym says cannot be used to conjure him.

## Cressid · reference

The faithless lover of the Troy story, invoked in Pistol’s insult.

## Jove · reference

The Roman king of the gods, who comes in thunder.

## Mars · reference

The Roman god of war.

## Phoebus · reference

The sun god, also called Hyperion here.

## Hyperion · reference

The sun god whose horse the labourer helps to saddle at dawn.

## Hermes · reference

The Greek god whose pipe the Dauphin says his horse outplays.

## Mercury · reference

The winged messenger god; the Chorus calls the English youth “English Mercuries”.

## Pegasus · reference

The winged horse of Greek myth.

## Perseus · reference

The Greek hero who rode Pegasus.

## Parca · reference

The fate who spins and cuts the thread of life.

## Fortune · reference

Fortune painted blind on her rolling stone, turning men’s luck.

## The gracious empress · reference

The reigning queen the Chorus addresses his own London audience about; she is not named in the play.

## The general from Ireland · reference

The commander the Chorus imagines returning from Ireland; the play does not name him.

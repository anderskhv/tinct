# Henry V character package

All twenty-three scenes in both actual English editions: 129 recognition cards in the original and 127 in the modern. The cards.md review copy shows Central, Major, Supporting or Reference beside every entry. King Henry is Central; the French king, the Dauphin, the Constable, Fluellen, Pistol, Exeter, Katharine, the Chorus, Montjoy, Canterbury, Orleans, Gower and Williams are Major; the rest of the speaking and stage cast, and the four named groups, are Supporting; the historical pedigree, the battle rolls of the dead, and the classical, biblical and mythological figures are Reference.

Ordinary identity is supplied at first mention: Gloucester, Bedford and Clarence are the King's brothers, Exeter his uncle, Katharine the French king's daughter, the Hostess the woman who keeps a London tavern. Later cards stay short. The name Harry le Roy unlocks after 14:22, where the King gives it to Pistol; Agincourt after 21:22, where Montjoy names the castle; Pistol's marriage to the Hostess after 3:11; Falstaff's death after 5:2; Bardolph's condemnation after 12:32; Katharine's place in the terms after 23:13. Coordinates are reading-unit number and zero-based paragraph, with gates at paragraph end.

Nothing anticipates the ending. King Henry's disguise in the camp is not a concealed identity to the reader, who watches him borrow Erpingham's cloak, so the cards say plainly who he is. Katharine's cards never call her a wife: at 23:81 the King kisses her as his queen and at 23:85 the wedding is still being prepared. The Chorus's own epilogue mentions Henry the Sixth and the loss of France; that is text, released where it stands, and it is not imported into any earlier card.

## Binding review

- **The play repeats first names constantly, and none is bound globally.** Four Edwards (Edward the Third, the Black Prince, Edward Duke of Bar, Edward Duke of York), four Johns (Falstaff, John Bates, John Duke of Bourbon, John Duke of Alençon), three Richards (the Earl of Cambridge, King Richard the Second in the King's prayer at 14:91, Sir Richard Ketly), two Thomases (Sir Thomas Grey, Sir Thomas Erpingham), two Henrys besides the King (Lord Scroop of Masham, Henry the Sixth). Each is bound at its own occurrence by the full form the text actually prints.
- **Two Alexanders.** Alexander Court is a private soldier who enters at 14:45 and speaks once; Alexander the Great is the subject of Fluellen's comparison in scene 20. The soldier's name is never bound to the Macedonian, and a test asserts it.
- **Two Dauphins.** The king's son, and Sir Guichard Dauphin, Great Master of France, in the roll of the dead at 21:37. The surname there is bound to its own entry and the prince is excluded from that paragraph.
- **Two Queen Isabels.** Canterbury's pedigree at 2:9 names "fair Queen Isabel, his grandmother", a French queen four generations back; the Queen Isabel who enters at 23:0 is Katharine's mother. They are separate entries and neither is bound in the other's paragraph.
- **Charlemagne and Charlemain are two men in one speech.** The original prints "Charles the Great" and, separately, "Charlemain, who was the son To Lewis the Emperor"; the modern prints "Charlemagne" and keeps "Charlemain". Both settings preserve the distinction and so does the binding. The genealogy is Shakespeare's, taken from Holinshed; the cards report what the speech claims and do not present it as settled history.
- **"Bar" at 11:8 is a verb once and a duke once.** The French king lists "Alençon, Brabant, Bar, and Burgundy" and eight lines later says "Bar Harry England". Only the peer in the list is bound, and only in the original, since the modern rewrites the verb as "Block this Harry of England".
- **Two heralds.** Montjoy holds every "herald" in the play except one: at 20:14 King Henry sends out an English herald of his own, one paragraph before Montjoy enters at 20:15. The English Herald who brings the count of the dead at 21:32–21:38 is that same office. The plural "Heralds" at 20:35 is left unbound rather than assigned to either.
- **Two embassies.** The Ambassadors at 2:26 and 2:34 are France's to England; the Ambassadors named at 6:7 are England's to France, that is, Exeter's party. They are separate groups. The lowercase "the ambassador from the French" in the Chorus at 6:28 is left unbound and documented here rather than guessed at.
- Messengers are scene-local: the one at the French court in scene 6 and the one in the French camp across scenes 13 and 15 are separate entries. Treating the scene-13 and scene-15 messenger as one man is a reviewed judgment — the same camp, the same night into morning — and is stated here rather than left implicit.
- The FIRST AMBASSADOR and AMBASSADOR cues at 2:28 and 2:30 are treated as one speaker continuing after the King tells him to speak plainly. The source distinguishes no second ambassador. This is a reviewed judgment, recorded here.
- The Duke of Burgundy is one entry. The text does not distinguish the duke who brokers the peace in 1420 from the duke whose brother is named among the dead at Agincourt in 1415, and no distinction has been invented.
- Nell Quickly and the Hostess are the same woman, bound together from 3:7.
- The Emperor: "Lewis the Emperor" at 2:9 is the longer span and wins there; "the Emperor" at 14:19 and 21:48 is the Holy Roman Emperor, unnamed.
- The Chorus's fifth prologue speaks of the Chorus's own London: "our gracious empress" and "the general of our gracious empress ... from Ireland coming" are bound as two unnamed people. The cards say the play does not name them. The usual identifications are Elizabeth the First and the Earl of Essex; neither is asserted as fact in the card copy.
- King Henry's father and Richard the Second are never named together. "My father" and its variants at 1:9, 4:11, 14:91 and 23:41 are bound to Henry the Fourth; "Richard's body" and "Richard's soul" at 14:91 are bound to Richard the Second.
- Generic titles are not bound. Bare "King" and "king" are left alone throughout: the play uses them for King Henry, the French king, the King of Scots, the bee-king of Canterbury's simile and kings in general, and no context rule can carry that safely. Deities and the constant oaths on God, heaven, Jesu and Christ are likewise not bound.

## Source review

Both source files have 23 aligned scenes and 883 paragraphs. No source edits. Two documented differences change what can be bound:

1. **Two figures exist only in the original.** At 3:19 Nym says "I am not Barbason"; the modern reads "I am no demon". At 22:6 Pistol speaks of "Parca's fatal web"; the modern reads "Fate's fatal web". Both are recorded in `validation-report.json` as `omittedEntities` for `modern-en`, which is an entity genuinely absent from that edition rather than an unresolved binding. A test asserts the omission list is exactly these two.
2. **Systematic modernizations that affect aliases.** The speech cue is `KING HENRY.` in the original and `KING HENRY V.` in the modern; `KATHARINE.` becomes `KATHERINE.`; "Ancient/Anchient Pistol" becomes "Ensign Pistol"; "Charles the Great" becomes "Charlemagne"; "Saint Davy's day" and "Saint Tavy's day" both become "Saint David's day"; "Lucifier"/"Belzebub" become "Lucifer"/"Beelzebub"; "Tartar" becomes "Tartarus". Each is carried as an edition-appropriate alias and covered by a test.

One modernization is recorded but deliberately **not** bound: the modern edition renders the interjection "Marry" as "By Mary" in six places (8:30, 8:32, 12:30, 12:32, 13:53, 23:29). That turns a mild oath into what looks like a name for the Virgin. Binding it would put a saint's card on an interjection, so it is left alone in both editions. If the release owner would rather the modern text read "Marry", that is a source change, and it would invalidate the modern hash and require a rebuild.

Neither point blocks safe enablement. The French-language passages in scenes 10, 17 and 23 are bound only where a name appears in them (Alice, Le Fer, Fortune); no attempt is made to bind French common nouns.

The local complete text supplies the card evidence. Folger is a comparison edition, not the offset source.

# On Liberty: recognition references

All five chapters, both English editions; 33 entries each. All categories are Reference. Named thinkers, rulers, reformers and the literary George Barnwell retain short identities; no evolving argument recaps. Marcus Aurelius and Antoninus are one person here. Wilhelm von Humboldt is the scholar/statesman, not Alexander von Humboldt. Lord Stanley is Edward Henry Stanley, rather than his father or a later bearer of the title; see the [University of Victoria historical biography](https://hcmc.uvic.ca/~coldesp/stanley.html).

God and Christ are separate entries with contextual bindings: the Almighty at 2:12, the crucified God at 2:13, and Paul’s Master at 2:36 refer to Christ. Other invocations of God retain the God entry. Czar in the bureaucracy argument identifies the Russian imperial office without conjecturing a particular emperor as the subject of that general claim. George Barnwell is identified as a literary figure, not presented as an independently verified historical criminal.

The local editions omit some annotations and introductory matter found in [other editions of On Liberty](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm). Do not import unprinted names from these. Unnamed legal-case participants, the temperance secretary and generic examples are not assigned speculative personal names. Schools, religious communities and Newtonian/Goethian/Fichtean adjectives are not individual-name matches. Source wording, including its historical claims and judgments, is unchanged; those judgments are not automatically adopted as card descriptions.

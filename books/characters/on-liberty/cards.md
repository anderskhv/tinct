The French philosopher whose system of social organization Mill criticizes.

The Greek philosopher and pupil of Socrates.

The Greek philosopher and pupil of Plato.

The early Christian apostle, formerly a persecutor of Christians.

The Roman emperor and Stoic thinker, also called Antoninus here.

The Roman emperor associated with Christianity’s adoption by the imperial state.

The English writer and critic quoted on religious persecution.

The medieval religious reformer cited among those suppressed before Luther.

The Italian religious dissenter cited among the predecessors of the Reformation.

The English queen whose reign is discussed in relation to Protestantism.

The English queen who succeeded Mary I.

The Roman orator and statesman, cited for studying both sides of an argument.

The Protestant reformer whose religious teaching Mill discusses.

The Scottish Protestant reformer.

The philosopher whose criticism of modern civilization challenged prevailing opinion.

The German scholar and statesman whose writing on individual development Mill draws on.

The Athenian statesman and general, cited as an example of forceful individuality.

The Athenian statesman whom Mill invokes as a model of developed character.

The French political thinker cited on growing social uniformity.

The British philosopher and legal reformer cited on evidence.

The German philosopher named alongside Locke in the education example.

The central figure of Christianity, whose teachings and treatment Mill discusses.

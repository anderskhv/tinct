# The Divine Comedy: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Dante · central

The poet who is also the pilgrim of his own poem. He names himself exactly once in the whole Comedy, when Beatrice calls him by name in the earthly paradise.

## Virgil · central

The Roman poet of the Aeneid, sent to guide Dante through Hell and Purgatory; a soul of Limbo, saved from damnation but not from exclusion. Longfellow spells him Virgilius.

## Beatrice · central

The Florentine woman Dante loved, now among the blessed; she sends Virgil to his aid and takes over as his guide in the earthly paradise and through Heaven.

## Statius · major

The Roman poet of the Thebaid, who joins the travellers in Purgatory and reveals that Virgil’s own verses converted him.

## Lucifer · major

The emperor of the kingdom of grief, fixed in ice at the centre of the earth; also called Dis, Satan and Beelzebub.

## Charon · supporting

The ancient ferryman with wheels of flame about his eyes, who carries the damned across Acheron.

## Minos · supporting

The judge of the damned, who winds his tail about himself to sentence each soul to its circle.

## Cerberus · supporting

The three-throated dog who howls over the gluttons in the rain.

## Plutus · supporting

The hoarse-voiced guardian of the fourth circle, where the misers and spendthrifts roll their weights.

## Phlegyas · supporting

The boatman who ferries Dante and Virgil across the marsh of Styx.

## Megaera · supporting

# Discourse on the Origin of Inequality: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Isaac Rousseau · reference

Rousseau’s father, the Geneva craftsman whose example he recalls in the dedication.

## Tacitus · reference

The Roman historian among the authors read by Rousseau’s father.

## Plutarch · reference

The Greek biographer whose works Rousseau recalls beside his father’s tools.

## Hugo Grotius · reference

The Dutch jurist and political thinker cited on law and property.

## Aristotle · reference

The Greek philosopher used as a model for inquiry into humanity and nature.

## Pliny the Elder · reference

The Roman natural historian invoked as a model for scientific inquiry.

## Pliny the Younger · reference

The Roman writer quoted addressing the emperor Trajan.

## Jean-Jacques Burlamaqui · reference

The Genevan jurist cited on the meaning of natural right.

## Persius · reference

The Roman satirist quoted in the Latin verses introducing the inquiry.

## Moses · reference

The biblical lawgiver whose writings Rousseau invokes in discussing the state of nature.

## Plato · reference

The Greek philosopher invoked as an imagined judge of the discourse and cited on medicine and government.

## Xenocrates · reference

The Greek philosopher and head of the Academy, imagined alongside Plato as a judge of the discourse.

## Thomas Hobbes · reference

The English political philosopher whose account of humanity in the state of nature Rousseau challenges.

## Montesquieu · reference

The French political thinker referred to as the other illustrious philosopher in the discussion of natural fear.

## Richard Cumberland · reference

The English philosopher and bishop cited on natural law and human behavior.

## Samuel Pufendorf · reference

The German natural-law thinker whose views on fear, liberty and slavery Rousseau examines.

## Francis Correal · reference

The travel-account author cited on the Caribs of Venezuela.

## Hippocrates · reference

The ancient Greek physician associated here with dietary treatment.

## Celsus · reference

The Roman medical writer cited on the history of diet as a treatment.

## Étienne Bonnot de Condillac · reference

The French philosopher whose account of language Rousseau discusses.

## Bernard Mandeville · reference

The author of The Fable of the Bees, cited in the argument about compassion.

## Sulla · reference

The Roman general and dictator used in an example of compassion coexisting with cruelty.

## Alexander of Pherae · reference

The Thessalian tyrant whose response to tragedy is contrasted with his cruelty.

## Juvenal · reference

The Roman satirist quoted in the discussion of compassion.

## Socrates · reference

The Athenian philosopher invoked in the discussion of virtue and reason.

## John Locke · reference

The English philosopher cited on property and political authority.

## Lycurgus · reference

The traditional lawgiver of Sparta, used as an example of founding political institutions.

## Trajan · reference

The Roman emperor addressed in the quotation from Pliny the Younger.

## Brasidas · reference

The Spartan commander to whom Rousseau attributes the reply about the pleasures of liberty.

## Algernon Sidney · reference

The English republican thinker cited against deriving absolute government from fatherhood.

## Louis XIV · reference

The French king whose 1667 edict is quoted in the argument about rulers and law.

## Jean Barbeyrac · reference

The jurist cited against surrendering liberty to arbitrary power.

## Lucan · reference

The Roman poet quoted to illustrate obedience to a ruler’s criminal commands.

## Diogenes · reference

The Cynic philosopher invoked through his search for an honest human being.

## Cato the Younger · reference

The Roman republican statesman used as an example of virtue at odds with its age.

## The Tarquins · reference

The royal family associated with the last kings of Rome, whose expulsion Rousseau discusses.

## Glaucus · reference

The sea god whose weathered statue illustrates the difficulty of recognizing humanity’s original nature.

## God · reference

The divine creator invoked in the discussion of human nature, liberty and religious authority.

## Adam · reference

The first man in the biblical creation story, invoked in the discussion of the state of nature.

## Podalirius · reference

The Greek healer at Troy, paired with Machaon in the example about ancient medicine.

## Machaon · reference

The Greek healer at Troy mentioned alongside Podalirius.

## Andromache · reference

Hector’s wife in Trojan legend, invoked as a figure of tragic suffering.

## Priam · reference

The king of Troy, invoked alongside Andromache as a figure of tragic suffering.

## Ceres · reference

The Roman goddess of agriculture, cited through her association with law and land distribution.

## The Persian satrap · reference

The Persian official in the anecdote Rousseau tells about Brasidas and liberty.

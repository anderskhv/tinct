# Measure for Measure character package

All seventeen scenes in both actual English editions: 65 recognition cards in each. The cards.md review copy shows Central, Major, Supporting or Reference beside every entry. The Duke, Angelo and Isabella are Central; Claudio, Lucio, Escalus, Mariana, the Provost and Pompey are Major; local speakers, stage roles and the four named groups are Supporting; people who are only discussed, the prison-list names and the classical and religious allusions are Reference.

Ordinary identity is supplied at first mention: Isabella is Claudio's sister and a novice of Saint Clare who has *not* taken her vows; Juliet is the woman Claudio has got with child; Pompey is Mistress Overdone's tapster. Later cards stay short. The Duke's friar disguise unlocks after 3:7, where he asks for the habit, and the assumed name Lodowick only after 17:42, where Isabella first speaks it. Angelo's marriage to Mariana unlocks after 17:145, where the Duke calls him "this new-married man"; Mariana's own wife card unlocks at the same point. Mariana's betrothal to Angelo unlocks after 9:69; Juliet's private contract after 2:79 and the adopted-cousin tie after 4:20; Pompey's new work for the executioner after 12:4; Barnardine's Bohemian birth and murder after 12:62. Coordinates are reading-unit number and zero-based paragraph, with gates at paragraph end.

Nothing anticipates the ending. Claudio's cards never call him dead, though the play tells Isabella and the audience so; the Duke's proposal to Isabella at 17:174 and 17:184 gets no card, because she never answers it; Lucio's ordered marriage and Claudio and Juliet's ordered union are instructions, not weddings, and are not written as accomplished. Mariana's threatened widowhood at 17:147 is likewise not an outcome.

## Binding review

- **The original's markup breaks the shared alias matcher.** Speech cues are abbreviated and italic-wrapped (`_Mrs Ov._`, `_Fri. P._`), and stage names are italicised (`_Officers_`, `[_To Provost_]`). Underscore is a word character, so `reviewed_aliases` can see neither end of those names. This book binds with letter/digit boundaries instead, and adds the 21 abbreviated cues as original-only aliases. The modern setting expands every cue to capitals and carries no abbreviations, so `reviewed-cue` mentions exist in the original only.
- **Justice is three different things.** The magistrate is bound only at the scene-5 entry direction and at his three `_Just._` / `JUSTICE.` cues. "Justice or Iniquity" at 5:70 is the morality-play pair, "he is indeed Justice" at 10:86 is Angelo, and "Justice, O royal Duke!" at 17:8 is Isabella's plea. None of the three is bound to him. Iniquity, the Vice, is bound at 5:70; the word Justice beside it is left unbound rather than given an invented second entity.
- **Thomas is not the friar.** "Thomas tapster" (original 2:61) and "Thomas barman" (modern) are Mistress Overdone addressing Pompey. Friar Thomas is bound only by his full name and his `_Fri. T._` cue. The play never confirms Thomas as Pompey's given name and the card does not claim it.
- **Pompey the Great is the pun, not a Roman.** Every "Pompey" span denotes the bawd, including 5:94. Julius Caesar has his own entry, bound at Escalus's threat (5:104, printed `Cæsar` in the original and `Caesar` in the modern) and at Lucio's triumph joke (10:15).
- **Crassus, Flavius, Valentius and Rowland are the Duke's Vienna contacts**, not their classical namesakes; the Crassus card says so explicitly.
- The disguised Duke is bound through "Lodowick" from 17:42, which is safe because the reader has watched him take the habit since scene 3. The public unhooding at 17:128 needs no card.
- Mariana is **not** bound at the Duke's "a poor wronged lady" (9:65). Her name and her situation arrive at 9:67 and 9:69, and binding the earlier phrase would have released her identity four paragraphs early. This is a deliberate omission, not a miss.
- Elbow's wife is bound at "Mistress Elbow" (5:35) and at "Elbow's wife" (5:42, 5:53), the longer span winning over the bare surname. She is Reference: the whole hearing is about her and she never appears.
- Generic titles that denote exactly one person in this play are bound: every "Duke"/"duke", "Deputy"/"deputy" and "provost" was checked against every occurrence. The plurals at 2:1 ("the other dukes", "all the dukes") and 10:36 ("dukes it well") are rulers at large and stay unbound; "the king" at 2:1 is bound to the King of Hungary just named, while "the king's crown" at 6:37 is generic and is not.
- The two gentlemen are bound individually by cue. The stage word "Gentlemen" (2:0, 2:42, 2:67) is left unbound rather than given a group entity that would duplicate them.
- The prioress is one person under two labels: "the prioress" (4:6) in both editions, then "the Mother" in the original and "the Mother Superior" in the modern at 4:32.
- Deities and the constant "heaven" exclamations are not bound; there is no useful individual behind them here. The unnamed saint at 12:77 ("by the Saint whom I profess") is left unidentified, as the text gives no basis. The "sanctimonious pirate" of Lucio's joke at 2:4 is a stock figure, not a participant, and is not bound.
- Saint Luke's (9:77) is the moated grange's location, not the evangelist, and is not bound as a person.

## Source review

Both source files have 17 aligned scenes and 1,006 paragraphs, and the same 24 speaking roles. No source edits were made. Four defects are recorded and preserved:

1. **Compositor errors in `original-en`.** `Clandio` for Claudio at 12:29 and `Angclo` for Angelo at 12:64. Both are bound to the right person with resolution `reviewed-source-typo`; the modern text spells both correctly. Do not correct these without rebuilding the package, since the source hash pins the bytes.
2. **Speech cues merged into the previous paragraph.** At 8:13 Isabella's speech is printed inside Angelo's paragraph, and at 17:20 the Duke's reply is printed inside Isabella's. Both editions carry the defect at the same coordinates. Both speakers are bound, but any paragraph-level speaker attribution downstream will be wrong at these two places.
3. **Printed line numbers inside the prose of `original-en`.** Numbers such as `150` sit between words after normalization. At 5:64 this splits "the constable's 150 wife", so that phrase is not a contiguous span and is bound in neither edition, keeping the two editions consistent. The numbers were deliberately **not** stripped: stripping them would move every UTF-16 offset in the file.
4. **Original brace cue at 17:2.** `_Ang._ } … _Escal._}` is the two-speaker line the modern prints as `ANGELO and ESCALUS.` Both speakers are bound in both editions.

None of the four blocks safe enablement. They are cosmetic in the reader and do not affect any binding's correctness; a test asserts each one stays as reviewed. If the release owner repairs the typos or strips the line numbers, the source hashes and every offset change, and the package must be re-reviewed and rebuilt.

The local complete text supplies the card evidence. [Folger's complete play](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/measure-for-measure/read/) and its [editorial cast list](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/measure-for-measure/read/characterList/) were consulted for speaking-role distinctions and for the standing of Isabella as a novice rather than a professed nun. Folger is a comparison edition, not the offset source. The Duke is unnamed in both repository texts; the traditional name Vincentio comes from the Folio's dramatis personae, is absent here, and is deliberately not used in any card.

Run `python3 books/characters/build_measure_for_measure.py --check` and `python3 -m unittest discover -s books/characters -p test_measure_for_measure.py`. Nine tests cover source hashes, exact UTF-16 spans, original-only cue expansion, the three senses of Justice, the Thomas and Pompey namesakes, the preserved compositor errors, the Cæsar/Caesar and Mother/Mother Superior edition differences, ordinary identity against withheld reveals, and every paragraph-end snapshot.

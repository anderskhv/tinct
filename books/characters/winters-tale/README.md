# The Winter's Tale character package

All fifteen scenes in both actual English editions: 59 recognition cards in the original and 60 in the modern. The cards.md review copy shows Central, Major, Supporting or Reference beside every entry. Leontes and Hermione are Central; Polixenes, Paulina, Camillo, Perdita, Florizel, Autolycus, the old Shepherd, the Clown, Antigonus and Time are Major; the rest of the speaking and stage cast and the seven named groups are Supporting; the gods, the Italian master and the people named only in report are Reference.

Ordinary identity is supplied at first mention: Leontes is King of Sicilia and Hermione's husband, Paulina is Antigonus's wife, the Clown is the old Shepherd's son, Florizel is Polixenes's son. Later cards stay short. Camillo's flight to Bohemia unlocks after 2:123, his disguise at the feast after 12:10, where he and Polixenes enter disguised; Florizel's assumed name Doricles after 12:30, where Perdita first uses it, and his change of clothes after 12:212; Perdita's growing up in Bohemia after Time's chorus at 9:1 and her acknowledgement in Sicilia after 14:7; the Shepherd's and Clown's promotion after 14:30. Coordinates are reading-unit number and zero-based paragraph, with gates at paragraph end.

## The two reveals

**Hermione is reported dead at 7:50 and is alive.** No card says either thing until the statue comes down. Her only card before that point identifies her as Leontes's queen and Mamillius's mother; a test asserts the words "dead", "died", "statue" and "alive" appear in none of it. The single later card unlocks after 15:33, the stage direction "Hermione comes down from the pedestal", and is the first place her survival is said. Paulina's assertion at 7:50 that she is dead is a character's claim inside the fiction and is not written into any card as fact — the same rule that governs Hero in Much Ado. Mamillius's death at 7:37 is different: the play confirms it and never reverses it, but it gets no card either, because his identity as Leontes's young son remains the whole of what a reader needs to recognise him.

**Perdita's parentage is not a secret from the reader.** The audience watches Antigonus name the child at 8:8 and hears Time say at 9:1 that she is grown; only the people in the play are ignorant. Her cards therefore identify her as Leontes and Hermione's daughter from her first mention, which is the ordinary-identity allowance working the way the policy intends, and the card at 14:7 marks the point where the court learns it too. Returning to a passage before 8:8 shows no Perdita card at all, since she is not named there.

## Binding review

- **Four different servants share one cue.** SERVANT speaks in scenes 5, 7, 12 and 13, in Leontes's palace, at the trial, at the Bohemian sheep-shearing and at the Sicilian court sixteen years later. They are four scene-local entries, and a test asserts none of them appears outside its own scene. The FIRST LORD of scenes 3, 5 and 7 is one man at Leontes's court before the gap; the bare LORD cue in scene 13 is after it and is a separate entry.
- **Doricles is Florizel.** The name is his own invention at the feast and the reader has watched him take it, so all four occurrences bind to him; there is no shepherd of that name to confuse him with.
- **Dame Partlet and Lady Margery are Paulina.** Leontes throws both at her at 5:29 and 5:52. They are the proverbial names of a hen and a goose, not two more women at court, and both bind to Paulina rather than becoming invented cast.
- **Rogero is the Second Gentleman.** The gentleman who enters at 14:5 is greeted "The news, Rogero?" and speaks at 14:7 under the SECOND GENTLEMAN cue. The Third Gentleman is identified in his own scene as Paulina's steward.
- Hermione's father is named only by his title, "The Emperor of Russia", at her trial; that phrase is bound and no personal name is supplied for him.
- The play's Bohemia has a sea coast and Delphos is an island. Neither is corrected, and neither is bound as a person.
- Generic titles are not bound. Bare "king", "queen", "prince" and "princess" are left alone: the play uses them for both kings, for Hermione, for Mamillius, for Florizel and for Perdita, and no context rule carries that safely. The recurring oaths on the gods and on "Grace" are likewise not bound.

## Source review

Both source files have 15 aligned scenes and 911 paragraphs. No source edits. Two differences are recorded:

1. **Judas is named only in the modern setting.** At 2:118 Polixenes says his name should be "yok'd with his that did betray the Best"; the modern reads "yoked with Judas's that betrayed the Best". The person is referred to in both editions but named in only one, so `judas` is recorded in `validation-report.json` as an `omittedEntities` entry for `original-en`. That is an entity genuinely unnamed in that edition, not an unresolved binding: no name-bound runtime can show a card where no name is printed. A test asserts the omission list is exactly this one entry.
2. **A missing stop in the original's speech cues.** Cleomenes's seven speeches are printed `CLEOMENES` without the period every other cue in the file carries (6:1, 6:3, 6:5, 13:1, 13:5, 13:19, 13:21). The binding is unaffected, because the name itself is intact and is matched as an alias. It is cosmetic in the reader and does not block enablement. Repairing it would change the original's bytes and require a rebuild.

The local complete text supplies the card evidence. [Folger's complete play](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/the-winters-tale/read/) and its [editorial cast list](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/the-winters-tale/read/characterList/) were consulted for the speaking-role distinctions, for Rogero as the Second Gentleman and the Third Gentleman as Paulina's steward, and for Dame Partlet and Lady Margery as by-names rather than characters. Folger is a comparison edition, not the offset source.

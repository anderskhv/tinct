# The Winter’s Tale: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Leontes · central

The King of Sicilia, Hermione’s husband and Mamillius’s father.

## Hermione · central

Leontes’s queen, Mamillius’s mother, and pregnant as the play opens.

After source chapter 15, paragraph 34: Leontes’s queen, alive after sixteen years, coming down from the pedestal where she stood as her own statue.

## Polixenes · major

The King of Bohemia, Leontes’s friend since boyhood and Florizel’s father.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 11: The King of Bohemia, at his son’s sheep-shearing feast in disguise.

## Paulina · major

A lady of Hermione’s court and Antigonus’s wife, plain-spoken to the King’s face.

## Camillo · major

A Sicilian lord in Leontes’s confidence.

After source chapter 2, paragraph 124: The Sicilian lord who has fled to Bohemia with Polixenes and entered his service.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 11: Polixenes’s Bohemian counsellor, at the sheep-shearing in disguise beside his king.

## Perdita · major

Leontes and Hermione’s newborn daughter, whom Antigonus names as he leaves her.

After source chapter 9, paragraph 2: Leontes and Hermione’s lost daughter, grown up in Bohemia and taken for the old Shepherd’s child.

After source chapter 14, paragraph 8: Leontes and Hermione’s daughter, known now at her father’s court in Sicilia.

## Florizel · major

Polixenes’s son, prince of Bohemia.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 31: Polixenes’s son, courting Perdita at the feast under the name Doricles.

After source chapter 12, paragraph 213: Polixenes’s son, escaping Bohemia in the clothes he has taken from Autolycus.

## Autolycus · major

A pedlar, ballad-seller and pickpocket on the Bohemian roads, once in Prince Florizel’s service.

## The old Shepherd · major

The Bohemian shepherd who finds the abandoned child on the shore.

After source chapter 14, paragraph 31: The Bohemian shepherd who found the child, made a gentleman at the Sicilian court.

## The Clown · major

The old Shepherd’s son.

After source chapter 14, paragraph 31: The old Shepherd’s son, made a gentleman at the Sicilian court.

## Antigonus · major

A Sicilian lord and Paulina’s husband.

## Mamillius · supporting

Leontes and Hermione’s young son, prince of Sicilia.

## Time · major

Time as Chorus, who carries the play across the sixteen years between its halves.

## Archidamus · supporting

A Bohemian lord attending Polixenes on his visit to Sicilia.

## Cleomenes · supporting

A Sicilian lord sent with Dion to consult Apollo’s oracle at Delphos.

## Dion · supporting

A Sicilian lord sent with Cleomenes to consult Apollo’s oracle at Delphos.

## Emilia · supporting

A lady attending Hermione.

## Mopsa · supporting

A shepherdess at the sheep-shearing, courted by the Clown.

## Dorcas · supporting

A shepherdess at the sheep-shearing, and Mopsa’s rival for the Clown.

## The First Lord · supporting

A lord of Leontes’s court in Sicilia.

## The lord · supporting

A lord attending Leontes sixteen years later.

## The First Lady · supporting

A lady attending Hermione and Mamillius.

## The Second Lady · supporting

A lady attending Hermione and Mamillius.

## The First Attendant · supporting

An attendant on Leontes.

## The officer of the court · supporting

The officer who reads the indictment and the oracle at Hermione’s trial.

## The Gaoler · supporting

The keeper of the prison where Hermione is held.

## The Mariner · supporting

The seaman who lands Antigonus on the Bohemian coast.

## Leontes’s servant · supporting

A servant of Leontes’s household in Sicilia.

## The servant at the trial · supporting

The servant who brings Leontes word of his son.

## The Shepherd’s servant · supporting

The servant who announces the pedlar at the sheep-shearing.

## The servant at the Sicilian court · supporting

A servant of Leontes’s household sixteen years later.

## The First Gentleman · supporting

A gentleman of the Sicilian court who saw the kings meet.

## Rogero · supporting

A gentleman of the Sicilian court, named Rogero.

## The Third Gentleman · supporting

Paulina’s steward, who describes the reunion.

## The lords · supporting

The lords of the Sicilian and Bohemian courts.

## The ladies · supporting

The ladies attending Hermione.

## The officers · supporting

The officers of the court at Hermione’s trial.

## The guards · supporting

The guards attending the trial.

## The attendants · supporting

The attendants on the two kings.

## The shepherds and shepherdesses · supporting

The country people at the sheep-shearing feast.

## The satyrs · supporting

The twelve herdsmen who dance a satyrs’ dance at the feast.

## The Emperor of Russia · reference

Hermione’s father, whom she calls on at her trial; the play gives him no name.

## Julio Romano · reference

The Italian master said to have made the statue of Hermione.

## Mistress Taleporter · reference

The midwife named as a witness in one of Autolycus’s ballads.

## Smalus · reference

The Libyan king Florizel falsely claims as the father of his bride.

## Alexander the Great · reference

The Macedonian king, cited by Cleomenes on leaving a crown to the worthiest.

## Judas · reference

The disciple who betrayed Christ, named by Polixenes as the worst company for a name.

## Deucalion · reference

The survivor of the flood in Greek myth, a byword for the remotest ancestry.

## Apollo · reference

The god whose oracle at Delphos answers Leontes.

## Jove · reference

The Roman king of the gods, also called Jupiter here.

## Jupiter · reference

Jove under his other name, in Florizel’s list of gods who took animal shapes.

## Neptune · reference

The god of the sea.

## Phoebus · reference

The sun god.

## Mercury · reference

The god of thieves, whom Autolycus claims as his birth-star.

## Juno · reference

The queen of the gods, invoked for the whiteness of her hands.

## Cytherea · reference

Venus under her island name, in Perdita’s catalogue of flowers.

## Flora · reference

The goddess of flowers, whom Florizel says Perdita resembles at the feast.

## Proserpina · reference

The goddess carried off by Dis, whose scattered flowers Perdita names.

## Dis · reference

The god of the underworld, who carried Proserpina away in his waggon.

## The Fates · reference

The powers who spin and cut the thread of a life.

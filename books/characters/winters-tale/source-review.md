## original-en · perdita · 8:8

ANTIGONUS. Come, poor babe. I have heard, but not believ’d, the spirits of the dead May walk again: if such thing be, thy mother Appear’d to me last night; for ne’er was dream So like a waking. To me comes a creature, Sometimes her head on one side, some another. I never saw a vessel of like sorrow, So fill’d and so becoming: in pure white robes, Like very sanctity, she did approach My cabin where I lay: thrice bow’d before me, And, gasping to begin some speech, her eyes Became two spouts. The fury spent, anon Did this break from her: “Good Antigonus, Since fate, against thy better disposition, Hath made thy person for the thrower-out Of my poor babe, according to thine oath, Places remote enough are in Bohemia, There weep, and leave it crying. And, for the babe Is counted lost for ever, Perdita I prithee call’t. For this ungentle business, Put on thee by my lord, thou ne’er shalt see Thy wife Paulina more.” And so, with shrieks, She melted into air. Affrighted much, I did in time collect myself and thought This was so, and no slumber. Dreams are toys, Yet for this once, yea, superstitiously, I will be squar’d by this. I do believe Hermione hath suffer’d death, and that Apollo would, this being indeed the issue Of King Polixenes, it should here be laid, Either for life or death, upon the earth Of its right father. Blossom, speed thee well! There lie; and there thy character: there these;

Leontes and Hermione’s newborn daughter, whom Antigonus names as he leaves her.

## original-en · florizel · 9:1

TIME. I that please some, try all: both joy and terror Of good and bad, that makes and unfolds error, Now take upon me, in the name of Time, To use my wings. Impute it not a crime To me or my swift passage, that I slide O’er sixteen years, and leave the growth untried Of that wide gap, since it is in my power To o’erthrow law, and in one self-born hour To plant and o’erwhelm custom. Let me pass The same I am, ere ancient’st order was Or what is now received. I witness to The times that brought them in; so shall I do To th’ freshest things now reigning, and make stale The glistering of this present, as my tale Now seems to it. Your patience this allowing, I turn my glass, and give my scene such growing As you had slept between. Leontes leaving Th’ effects of his fond jealousies, so grieving That he shuts up himself, imagine me, Gentle spectators, that I now may be In fair Bohemia, and remember well, I mentioned a son o’ th’ king’s, which Florizel I now name to you; and with speed so pace To speak of Perdita, now grown in grace Equal with wondering. What of her ensues I list not prophesy; but let Time’s news Be known when ’tis brought forth. A shepherd’s daughter, And what to her adheres, which follows after, Is th’ argument of Time. Of this allow, If ever you have spent time worse ere now; If never, yet that Time himself doth say He wishes earnestly you never may.

Polixenes’s son, prince of Bohemia.

## modern-en · perdita · 8:8

ANTIGONUS. Come, poor baby. I have heard, but never believed, that the spirits of the dead may walk again. If such a thing is so, your mother appeared to me last night: no dream was ever so like waking. To me came a figure, sometimes her head to one side, sometimes the other. I never saw a vessel of such grief, so full and so becoming. In pure white robes, like holiness itself, she approached the cabin where I lay. Three times she bowed before me; gasping to begin some speech, her eyes became two fountains. The fury spent, this broke from her: 'Good Antigonus, since fate, against your better nature, has made you the man who must cast out my poor baby, in keeping with your oath, places remote enough exist in Bohemia: weep there, and leave her crying. And because the baby is reckoned lost forever, please call her Perdita. For this unkind task, put upon you by my lord, you shall never see your wife Paulina again.' And so, with shrieks, she melted into the air. Greatly frightened, I in time collected myself, and thought that this was real, and no dream. Dreams are nothing, yet for this once, yes, superstitiously, I will be guided by it. I do believe Hermione has met her death, and that Apollo would have it that, since this is in truth the child of King Polixenes, she should be laid here, for life or death, on the soil of her rightful father. Blossom, fare thee well! There lie; and there, your tokens; there these;

Leontes and Hermione’s newborn daughter, whom Antigonus names as he leaves her.

## modern-en · florizel · 9:1

TIME. I, who please some and put all to the test—both joy and terror of good and bad alike, who makes errors and unmakes them—now take it upon myself, in the name of Time, to use my wings. Don't count it a crime in me or in my swift passage that I slide over sixteen years and leave untested the growth of that wide gap; since it is in my power to overturn law, and in one self-born hour to plant and overwhelm custom. Let me pass the same as I always was, before the most ancient order existed, or what is now accepted. I was witness to the times that brought those in; and so I am to the freshest things now reigning, and I'll make the glitter of this present go stale, as my tale now seems to it. Allowing this much patience, I turn my glass, and give my scene growth as if you had slept through it. Leontes leaving the effects of his foolish jealousies, so grieving that he shuts himself away, imagine me, gentle spectators, that I may now be in fair Bohemia; and remember well, I mentioned a son of the king's, whom I now name Florizel to you; and with speed I move on to speak of Perdita, now grown in grace equal to wonder. What follows of her I won't prophesy; but let Time's news be known when it's brought forth. A shepherd's daughter, and what attaches to her, that follows after, is Time's argument. Of this be content, if you've ever spent time worse before now; if never, yet Time himself says he earnestly wishes you never may.

Polixenes’s son, prince of Bohemia.

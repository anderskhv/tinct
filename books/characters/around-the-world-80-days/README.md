# Around the World in Eighty Days recognition cards

Complete 37-chapter scope in both English editions, 1,613 paragraphs each, 111 entries each. Source bytes unchanged. Authoring-agent source review, not independent editorial approval; runtime registration pending.

Fogg and Passepartout are Central. Aouda and Fix are Major. Club companions and locally important participants are Supporting. Recalled people, historical names, religious figures and literary allusions are Reference. The generated cards document every category alongside the actual reader copy.

- Ordinary identities are available at first encounter: Passepartout at “new servant” (1:13), Fix’s signed dispatch (5:11), Aouda at the unnamed woman in the procession (12:20), Bunsby at the sailor (20:16), Proctor at the brawny man (25:25), Mudge at his approach to Fix (30:53), and Speedy at the captain’s entrance (32:7). These do not pretend that ordinary names, professions or family backgrounds are concealed mysteries.
- Fogg’s card never identifies him as a criminal. The unnamed actual bank robber is bound to the club’s original discussion and Fix’s later correction, not subsequent passages that apply the accusation to Fogg. James Strand has no early name/card/appearance link: the earlier unknown robber’s identity updates only after 36:0. This preserves recognition before the reveal without leaking the solution through shared identity metadata.
- Fogg and Aouda’s marriage updates only at the end of 37:31, where the marriage is narrated. A proposal or a plan for a ceremony is not treated as a completed marriage. Otherwise their cards remain brief recognition aids; no recurring travel recaps or ending summaries.
- James Forster, the dismissed English valet, is separate from Forster, the American train engineer. The Indian and American conductors, signalman, stoker, Liverpool driver, Fort Kearney captain and sergeant are distinct. General Dodge’s historical title is not bound to the train engineer. The Sioux at the earlier railroad ceremony are not automatically the later attackers.
- Aouda’s Hong Kong relative Jeejeeh is separate from Sir Jametsee Jeejeebhoy. Her deceased husband and merchant father have separate identities. The Parsi guide is not a blanket match for every Parsi person in the novel. The Malabar Hill priests are separate from the Pillaji priests, including during Passepartout’s mistaken accusation in the courtroom.
- Bunsby/Busby are one skipper. Pilot-boat is a vessel, not a person. The Rangoon captain, Hong Kong pilot, Yokohama steamer captain, Tankadere crew and Henrietta crew are separate. Captain Fogg is not relabeled as Captain Speedy.
- Hitch’s religious history is explicitly attributed to his account. Its prophet, son Mormon, heavenly messenger, Smith’s father and mummy showman are not silently harmonized into a corrected religious genealogy. Joseph the biblical patriarch is separate from Joseph Smith and the Reverend Decimus Smith. Hiram is identified as Smith’s brother Hyrum.
- Robert Stephenson is the engineer who opposed the Suez Canal, not his father George: checked against [the parliamentary record](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1874-06-26/debates/e0f5ef59-cf29-4a72-8b50-97b2992a7901/Resolution). Ucaf Uddaul remains the poet-king named by the narrator without a speculative historical expansion. Imperial titles retain their office-based identity. Historical and religious claims in the novel are not all endorsed as factual biography.
- Ship names such as General Grant, place names such as Cornwallis’s tomb and City of God, Madame Tussaud’s museum, the Bradshaw guide, Leroy’s chronometer, and the planets Uranus and Neptune are not miscast as people. The acrobatic Car of Juggernaut is distinct from the earlier religious reference.

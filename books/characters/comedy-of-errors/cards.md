# The Comedy of Errors: recognition cards

Editorial review copy; source excerpts and later identities may contain spoilers.

## Antipholus of Syracuse · central

Egeon’s son, the visiting twin searching for his mother and brother.

## Antipholus of Ephesus · central

The twin established in Ephesus, married to Adriana.

## Dromio of Syracuse · central

The servant travelling with Antipholus of Syracuse; he too has a twin brother.

## Dromio of Ephesus · central

The servant of Antipholus of Ephesus and twin brother of the other Dromio.

## Egeon · major

The merchant from Syracuse and father of the Antipholus twins.

## Adriana · major

Antipholus of Ephesus’s wife and Luciana’s sister.

## Luciana · major

Adriana’s unmarried sister.

## Solinus · supporting

The Duke of Ephesus.

## Egeon’s wife · supporting

The mother of Egeon’s twin sons, separated from him in the shipwreck.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 120: Egeon’s wife and the twins’ mother, now identified as the abbess.

## The abbess · supporting

The woman who heads the religious house in Ephesus.

After source chapter 11, paragraph 120: The abbess of Ephesus, Egeon’s wife and the twins’ mother.

## Angelo · major

The goldsmith making a chain for Antipholus of Ephesus.

## Balthasar · supporting

The merchant accompanying Antipholus of Ephesus to dinner.

## The first merchant · supporting

The merchant who knows Antipholus of Syracuse and warns him about the city’s laws.

## The second merchant · supporting

The merchant to whom Angelo owes money.

## The courtesan · supporting

The courtesan at the Porpentine, an acquaintance of Antipholus of Ephesus.

## Luce · supporting

A maid in Adriana’s household who speaks from inside the house.

## Nell · supporting

The kitchen maid promised to Dromio of Ephesus, also jokingly called Dowsabel.

## Doctor Pinch · supporting

The schoolmaster called in as an exorcist.

## Egeon’s jailer · supporting

The jailer charged with holding Egeon.

The officer enforcing the debt claims involving Angelo and Antipholus of Ephesus.

# The Comedy of Errors recognition cards

All eleven scenes and 690 paragraphs in each English edition: 51 entries each, 886 original / 897 modern exact mentions. Source editions unchanged. Authoring-agent review, not independent editorial approval; runtime registration pending.

Both Antipholuses and both Dromios are Central. Adriana, Luciana, Egeon and Angelo are Major. Other speaking and locally important participants are Supporting. Recalled tradespeople, relatives, minor names, divine figures and personifications are Reference. Only the two appearances associated with the concealed mother receive a later identity reminder; the rest remain one short card throughout.

- Ordinary identities include Adriana’s marriage, Luciana’s sisterhood, Angelo’s trade and the pairs of twins. The audience knows the twin premise before the city’s characters understand it. Cards do not pretend their confusion conceals the premise from the reader.
- Egeon’s wife in his opening account and the later abbess remain separate appearance records until the abbess’s statement at 11:119. Before that paragraph ends, the abbess has no family identity in her card. Afterward both reminders identify Emilia and their connection. No early shared entity identifier or gallery entry gives away the reunion.
- Fully specified names and speaker labels bind each twin separately. Bare names bind the actual addressee or an unambiguous recalled participant. Thus Adriana addressing the visiting Antipholus resolves to Syracuse, while Luciana’s recalled dinner messenger at 4:60 resolves to Dromio of Ephesus. Dromio here at 10:50 means the Ephesian being addressed; the messenger recalled at 11:138 is Syracusan.
- The conflated accounts at 7:2 and 9:30, and the duke’s uncertain question at 11:123, do not receive an arbitrary single-person bare-name binding. Luce’s ambiguous Dromio address at 5:27 and plural twin names remain unbound. This deliberate restraint does not drop their explicit nearby speaker identities.
- The first merchant who warns the visitor is distinct from Angelo’s creditor. Egeon’s jailer is distinct from the debt-enforcing officer, even when the latter is called jailer. Solinus is distinct from the Duke of Syracuse and Duke Menaphon.
- Luce and Nell have separate records because this source does not explicitly establish that they are one woman. Dowsabel is the joking name for Nell. Recollections of the maid speaking at the locked door point to Luce. No claim is made to settle casting traditions or editorial disagreements about the maids.
- Adam in the allusion is the biblical figure; the second Adam in 9:5 is expressly the prison officer in Dromio’s joke. Satan and Circe remain allusions, not assertions that the courtesan or the citizens are supernatural. Father Time is scoped to personification. Centaur, Phoenix, Tiger and Porpentine are establishments; Expedition and Delay are ship names/wordplay, not characters.
- The six women called for at the door retain their names without invented biographies or a claim that each is demonstrably an employee. Minor tradespeople and recalled rescuers are retained. No exhaustive pronoun, generic-title or unnamed-crowd coverage is claimed.

The quiet return of the river otter

By Maren Holt

2 November 2026
Share on X Share on Facebook Email

For most of the last century, the river that runs through the old mill town was a place people walked past rather than looked into. The water was brown, the banks were steep with rubble, and the only animals anyone remembered seeing were rats and the occasional heron that seemed to have lost its way.

That changed slowly, and then, according to the volunteers who count such things, rather quickly. Last spring a fisherman photographed a pair of otters sliding down a mud bank below the footbridge. By autumn the survey team had found spraints — otter droppings, the surest sign of a resident animal — at eleven sites along a nine-mile stretch.

“People think of otters as shy,” said Ilse Brand, who has led the survey since 2019. “They are shy of people, but they are not shy of a town. What they cannot live with is dirty water and no fish. Give them those two things back and they will find the rest themselves.”

The water did come back, though not by accident. A treatment works upstream was rebuilt in 2014, two weirs were given fish passes, and farmers along the upper valley were paid to leave strips of grass between their fields and the river so that soil and fertiliser stopped washing in after every storm.

None of it was done with otters in mind. The fish passes were meant for salmon and the grass strips for drinking water. But a river is a single system, Brand said, and an otter is simply what a working river looks like from the outside.

Not everyone is delighted. A man who keeps ornamental carp in a pond beside the river has already lost several, and the angling club has asked whether the otters will eat the trout it stocks each year. The survey team has offered advice on fencing, and so far, Brand said, the conversations have been friendly.

For now the otters keep their own hours. They are most often seen at dawn, when the town is quiet, moving in short bursts between the reeds and the shadow of the bridges. Most residents have never seen one. Many of them, Brand suspects, would not believe it if they did.

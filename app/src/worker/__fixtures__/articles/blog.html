Notes on keeping a commonplace book

Written by Tomas Ward on 3 March 2026

For about ten years I have kept a commonplace book: a plain notebook into which I copy, by hand, passages from whatever I am reading that seem worth keeping. It is an old habit, older than printing, and it has survived because it does something that highlighting and bookmarking do not.

Why by hand

Copying a passage takes time, and the time is the point. You cannot copy a paragraph without reading it again, slowly, word by word. Often I find on the second reading that the passage says something slightly different from what I thought, or that the sentence I loved depends on the one before it.

It also forces a choice. A highlighter costs nothing, so everything gets highlighted. A pen and ten minutes cost something, so only the passages I would really miss make it into the book.

What I copy

  • Sentences I want to remember the shape of, not just the idea.
  • Arguments I disagree with, stated at their strongest.
  • Short descriptions of places and weather, which I am bad at writing myself.

Organising it

The traditional method was to write headings at the top of pages and file each passage under one. I tried that and gave up within a month: I never knew in advance how much space a heading would need. Now I copy passages in the order I find them and keep an index at the back, a page of keywords with page numbers beside them.

Read not to contradict and confute, nor to believe and take for granted, but to weigh and consider.

That line from Bacon is on the first page of every notebook I have filled. It is a reminder that the book is not a collection of things I agree with, but a record of what I have weighed.

index:
  weather .......... 4, 17, 31
  attention ........ 9, 22

If you want to start, buy a notebook you are not afraid to spoil, and copy one passage this week. The habit grows from there, slowly, the way reading itself does.

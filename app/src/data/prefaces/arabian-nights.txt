A sultan who has decided to marry a new wife each day and have her put to death at dawn meets Scheherazade, the vizier’s daughter, who begins a story and leaves it unfinished. This selection, edited by Andrew Lang in 1898, translates stories from Antoine Galland’s French version of the Nights. Lang shortened the stories in places and left out most of the verse.

The tales nest inside one another. A merchant is saved by the stories of three old men, three princes tell their own histories, and Sindbad the Sailor recounts seven voyages to an audience that includes a porter. Genies, caliphs and viziers appear alongside fishermen, tailors and merchants. Aladdin and the Wonderful Lamp, the Enchanted Horse and Ali Cogia are here, together with some less familiar stories.

A short preface by Lang explains how the book came to Europe. You can read each tale as a separate story or follow the thread of the frame story from the beginning.

Kenneth Grahame (1859–1932) published The Wind in the Willows in 1908. Mole leaves his spring-cleaning for the river, where he meets Rat, who takes him boating and picnicking. The two visit gruff Badger in the Wild Wood and try, again and again, to keep their friend Toad out of trouble as he moves from caravans to motor-cars to prison to a bold return to Toad Hall.

The book is a string of episodes about friendship, home and the pull of the open road. Some chapters are comic, some quiet, and some almost solemn. Songs and poems are scattered through it, and the language is Edwardian; this edition gives it a clear modern rendering.

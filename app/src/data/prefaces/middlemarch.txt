In an English provincial town on the threshold of political reform, Dorothea Brooke wants a life of useful devotion and Tertius Lydgate wants to change medicine. Their ambitions enter a web of marriages, inheritances, debts, professional rivalries, and neighbors’ opinions. Other lives continually interrupt and reshape their plans.

George Eliot follows several households at once, allowing a person who seems selfish or ridiculous in one scene to become understandable in another. The narrator asks readers to examine the distance between intention and action, sympathy and judgment, and the lives people imagine and those they can actually live.

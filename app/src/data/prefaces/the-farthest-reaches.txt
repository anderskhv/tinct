The Farthest Reaches is Brett Hall's argument that people are the most important entities in the universe. Its central idea, taken from David Deutsch's The Beginning of Infinity, is explanatory universality: people are systems that can explain anything that can be explained, so there is only one kind of person and no mind exists that could understand what ours cannot. Hall, an educator and host of the ToKCast podcast, has been speaking and writing about Deutsch and Karl Popper for almost three decades, and the book grew out of a podcast series of the same name.

The preface starts from cosmology: why do people feel insignificant beside the size of the universe? Three short chapters define universality and its reach. The rest apply it, one chapter at a time, to contested questions: education and IQ, adopting a culture, immigration, ethnicity and multiculturalism, mental illness, sexuality and the trans debate, the 'woke' left and right, artificial intelligence, free speech and evolutionary psychology. In each case Hall argues that differences between people come from differences in knowledge, which can change, and not from fixed limits on the mind.

A concluding chapter is followed by Hall's commentary on an edited transcript of a 2025 conversation between Peter Boghossian and Richard Dawkins about whether some things are 'queerer than we can suppose'. An afterword addresses AI doom arguments. The writing is direct and polemical. Read for a single idea pressed into many debates, and for where you think it reaches too far or not far enough.

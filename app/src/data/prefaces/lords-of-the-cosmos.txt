Lords of the Cosmos began as the script for Arjun Khemani's documentary of the same name, written with Logan Chipkin and drawn heavily from David Deutsch's The Beginning of Infinity. Its argument is that the universe, and then human society, stayed almost unchanged for nearly all of its history, until the Enlightenment discovered how to make progress continuous.

The first six chapters move from the cosmic 'Great Monotony' through Sparta and Athens and the Enlightenment to the ideas the authors call the enemies of civilization. They end with Deutsch's principle of optimism: every evil is caused by a lack of knowledge. Chapter 7, the book's main addition to the film, retells the history of everything as a series of bottlenecks in knowledge, from the Big Bang and the first replicator to language, money, the scientific revolution and the universal computer.

Chapter 8 looks forward to immortality, artificial general intelligence, anarcho-capitalism, Taking Children Seriously and the universal constructor. Chapter 9 closes on the Anthropocene and the place of people in the cosmos, and an organized reading list follows. The authors write as advocates. Their politics are libertarian, and they name socialism, environmentalism and the degrowth movement among the enemies of progress. Read for a compact, openly opinionated tour of Deutsch's worldview, and for the arguments you would want to test.

Bold Conjectures, Volume II gathers thirteen essays by fourteen physicists and philosophers of physics, edited by Logan Chipkin. Each essay either explains a development at the edge of current research or corrects an idea that popular accounts usually get wrong. The preface states the questions: where gravity fits in a quantum multiverse, how quantum theory bears on the problem of time, how the world can be local despite quantum strangeness, whether entropy is subjective, and how probability relates to reality.

Several essays draw on the many-worlds and constructor-theory research associated with David Deutsch and Oxford. Sam Kuypers asks what many worlds means for gravity. Chiara Marletto and Vlatko Vedral recast quantum physics and general relativity as 'superinformation'. Antonia Weber and Giuseppe Di Pietra describe experiments that could test whether gravity, or a living system, is non-classical. Charles Alexandre Bédard gives a local account of Bell's theorem, and David Wallace argues that probability cannot be understood without many-worlds. Others deal with the subjectivity of entropy (Maria Violaris), string theory's history (Aditya Iyer), time (Simone Rijavec), entanglement witnesses (Mia Stamatova), effective field theory (Abdullah Afzal and Hassaan Saleem) and gravity without absolute space or time (Julian Barbour).

The essays are written for non-specialists but do not avoid equations, figures or references. Each stands alone. Read them in any order, for a picture of what physicists in this tradition think is unsettled.

To the Lighthouse follows the Ramsay family and their guests at a summer house on the Isle of Skye, looking across the bay to a lighthouse. In the first part, The Window, very little happens outwardly across a single afternoon and evening: six-year-old James longs to sail to the Lighthouse tomorrow and his father says the weather will not allow it; Mrs. Ramsay knits a stocking for the keeper's boy, tries to arrange a marriage, and presides over a long dinner; Lily Briscoe, a guest, struggles to paint Mrs. Ramsay sitting in the window. The second part, Time Passes, moves through ten years in a few pages while the empty house decays. The third part, The Lighthouse, returns to the house, where the long-delayed trip is taken up again and Lily goes back to her unfinished picture.

Virginia Woolf published the novel in 1927, between Mrs. Dalloway and Orlando. She drew the Ramsays from her own parents, Leslie Stephen and Julia Stephen, and the setting from the family's summers at Talland House in St Ives, Cornwall, moved here to the Hebrides. The story is told almost entirely from inside its characters' minds, passing from one consciousness to the next, often within a paragraph, so that the same moment is seen by several people at once. What happens matters less than how it is perceived, remembered and made to last.

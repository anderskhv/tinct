Werther experiences feeling as evidence that life is being lived fully. A landscape, a book, or an encounter can seem to reveal a significance that practical people have trained themselves to miss. Goethe gives this openness an extraordinary attraction before asking what happens when the intensity of an experience becomes its own authority.

The novel's letters make the world arrive through Werther's attention. His delight is infectious, and so is his frustration with social arrangements that appear to reward rank and compliance more readily than imagination. There are real reasons for his sense of confinement. Sensitivity can disclose what a well-adjusted person has ceased to notice.

It can also make another person's independent life difficult to accept. Love becomes entangled with the need for an experience that confirms the lover's account of himself. The beloved is a particular person with obligations and choices, but desire keeps trying to give her a place in a more absolute story.

Nature changes with the state of the mind describing it. The same capacity for absorption that makes the world radiant can make it seem unbearable. Goethe's prose allows the emotional transformation to be felt while leaving room to question the interpretation Werther places upon it.

Werther wants to live with a depth that ordinary routines seem to forbid, and finds it tempting to regard compromise as a betrayal of that wish. Werther's voice preserves the beauty of wanting life to mean everything, along with the difficulty of sharing a world with people whose meanings will not always coincide with his.

In 1801, a tenant named Lockwood visits his landlord on the Yorkshire moors and mistakes a hostile household for a promising refuge from society. A snowbound night, a name scratched on a window ledge and the housekeeper Nelly Dean’s memories draw him into the history of two neighbouring homes: Wuthering Heights and Thrushcross Grange.

At the centre are Heathcliff, a child brought into the Earnshaw family without a known history, and Catherine Earnshaw, whose attachment to him collides with her wish for social standing. Their story reaches beyond a single courtship. Marriage, education, property and the treatment of children carry its consequences into another generation.

Emily Brontë’s only novel, published in 1847 under the name Ellis Bell, puts these lives inside accounts told by people who have reasons to defend themselves. Lockwood misunderstands what he sees; Nelly remembers events in which she also took part. Keep an eye on who is speaking, and keep the two Catherines distinct: the repeated names are part of the family history.

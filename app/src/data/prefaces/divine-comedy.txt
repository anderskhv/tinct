The Divine Comedy begins with a man who has lost the way in the middle of his life. He cannot simply decide to go back and start again. Dante makes this recognisable predicament the entrance to a journey through the entire moral universe, where every account a person has given of living must face something larger than personal conviction.

The scale is astonishing. Classical heroes, Christian saints, philosophers, lovers, rulers, and people scarcely remembered outside the poem inhabit the same creation. Homer and the Bible belong to one field of thought, though their claims do not always sit comfortably together. Dante attempts to give history, desire, suffering, and salvation a place in a single work.

His traveller is far less secure than this architecture might suggest. He admires eloquence, is overcome by pity, and can mistake the beauty of a person's account for the goodness of what it describes. The poem repeatedly makes feeling and judgment difficult to reconcile. Someone can speak magnificently about a desire that has consumed everything else in life.

Hell is only one part of that inquiry. Purgatory considers the possibility of becoming different, including the effort, companionship, and hope such change might require. Paradise asks poetry to approach forms of understanding and love that exceed the traveller's ordinary capacities. The journey's changing atmosphere matters: Dante is attempting to imagine more than punishment, and more than the exposure of human failure.

For a poem with such immense ambitions, its perceptions can be wonderfully small. A gesture, the movement of birds, the expression of a child, or a remembered landscape gives something almost impossible to conceive a bodily presence. The spiritual world is made visible through the earthly world Dante knew and loved.

The direction of a life deserves every resource poetry possesses, as far as Dante is concerned. Its convictions can be argued with, but its ambition is difficult to diminish: to understand where human wanting leads, and what it would mean for that wanting finally to find its proper object.

The Art of War — chapter by chapter

All 13 chapters — from the preliminary reckoning to the employment of spies.

The Art of War is built as thirteen self-contained teachings. The opening chapters set out the foundations: the reckoning made before a campaign, the cost of keeping troops on campaign, and the preference for stratagem over assault. The middle chapters move into operations — formation, the spirit of the troops, the use of emptiness and strength, marching and ground. The closing chapter, on spies, returns to the opening theme of knowledge and reads as a summary of the whole. The book can be read straight through in an afternoon or consulted one chapter at a time.

Chapters 1–3 · Foundations

Reckoning, cost, and the ranking of strategies.

Chapter 1 — Preliminary Reckoning

Preliminary Reckoning

War is a great matter for the state. Five matters govern it and seven comparisons predict its outcome. War is a thing of pretence. The opening chapter sets the ground for everything after it.

Chapter 2 — Operations of War

Operations of War

What war costs. Long campaigns drain the state. Speed matters. Feed the army from the enemy's stores and turn captured chariots to your own use. No country has ever gained from a prolonged war.

Chapter 3 — The Attack by Stratagem

The Attack by Stratagem

The ranking of strategies: defeat the enemy's plans, keep his forces apart, engage his army, and only then besiege his walls. Five signs of victory. The formula on knowing both sides that has shaped strategy ever since.

Chapters 4–6 · Form and initiative

Formation, spirit, and control of the engagement.

Chapter 4 — The Order of Battle

The Order of Battle

Make yourself invincible first; the enemy supplies the chance to win. The able warrior wins without bloody engagements and gains no reputation by it, because his victory was settled before the battle.

Chapter 5 — The Spirit of the Troops

The Spirit of the Troops

The enemy is engaged with the normal force and defeated by the abnormal. Their combinations are endless. The good fighter is like a stretched crossbow. The round stone rolled from a high mountain: that is the nature of shi.

Chapter 6 — Emptiness and Strength

Emptiness and Strength

Initiative: arrive first and wait, and you are fresh; arrive late and hurry, and you are spent. Unite while the enemy divides. Turn from strength and attack emptiness. An army may be likened to water.

Chapters 7–8 · Manoeuvre

Moving armies and knowing when to depart from the rules.

Chapter 7 — Battle Tactics

Battle Tactics

Nothing is harder than battle tactics. Turn the roundabout route into the fast one. Swift as the wind, calm as the forest, devastating as fire, firm as a mountain. Strike when the enemy's spirit flags.

Chapter 8 — The Nine Changes

The Nine Changes

The chapter of exceptions. Some roads must be avoided; some castles must not be besieged; some orders must not be obeyed. Five faults that destroy generals. The skill lies in knowing when the rule does not apply.

Chapters 9–11 · Ground

Marching, reading the enemy, and the kinds of ground.

Chapter 9 — Movement of Troops

Movement of Troops

The most concrete chapter. Mountains, rivers, marshes and open country each have their rules. Reading the enemy from birds, beasts and dust. Treat soldiers humanely, then hold them to authority.

Chapter 10 — Ground

Ground

Six kinds of ground and six calamities among the troops. Both lists end with the same charge: the general must study them. Ground is the handmaid of victory — for a general who knows how to read it.

Chapter 11 — The Nine Grounds

The Nine Grounds

The longest chapter. Nine kinds of ground, each with its rule. Soldiers with no refuge fight their hardest. The snakes of Mt. Chang: strike one end and the other strikes back.

Chapters 12–13 · Fire and intelligence

Attack by fire and the use of spies.

Chapter 12 — Assault by Fire

Assault by Fire

Five kinds of fire attack and the conditions for each. Then the closing rule: do not make war in passion. Anger passes. A country once overturned cannot be restored. The dead cannot be brought to life.

Chapter 13 — The Employment of Spies

The Employment of Spies

The closing argument. Five kinds of spy: village, inner, converted, death and living. Used together, they form the Mysterious Thread. Knowledge of the enemy comes only through men, and every movement of the army depends on it.

Revelation 21

The first heaven and earth have passed away; the holy city descends from heaven as a bride; God will dwell with humanity and wipe every tear from their eyes.

Summary

Chapter 21 is among the most carefully constructed chapters in the book. The statement that the sea was no more (21:1) is theologically significant in the context of Revelation's world, where the sea is the source of the beast (chapter 13) and the symbol of chaos (following the ancient Near Eastern cosmological pattern in which the chaotic waters are subdued at creation). The absence of the sea in the new creation is the absence of the chaotic threat. The voice from the throne that announces God's dwelling with humanity (21:3) uses the Greek word skēnē (tabernacle, tent), invoking the wilderness tabernacle in which the divine presence dwelt among Israel during the exodus. The same verb (skēnoō, to tabernacle) appears in John 1:14, where the Word tabernacled among us. The new creation is the consummation of the pattern the tabernacle and incarnation both began: God dwelling with his people. The promise that every tear will be wiped away draws on Isaiah 25:8, which itself is the culmination of Isaiah's vision of God's eschatological feast on the mountain for all peoples.

The extended description of the New Jerusalem (21:9-27) is the positive counterpart to the extended description of the whore of Babylon in chapter 17-18, and an angel from the same group of bowl-angels shows it to John — a deliberate structural parallel. The city's dimensions are those of a perfect cube — twelve thousand stadia on each side and in height — recalling the Holy of Holies in the Jerusalem temple, which was also a perfect cube (1 Kings 6:20). The implication is that the entire city is the Holy of Holies: the place of the divine presence expanded to universal scale. The material description of the foundations draws on Isaiah 54:11-12, where God promises to rebuild Jerusalem with precious stones after the exile. The twelve foundation stones (21:19-20) correspond to the twelve stones of the high priest's breastplate in Exodus 28:17-20 — the entire priestly intercession system is now built into the foundations of the city itself. The statement that there is no temple in the city (21:22) reverses the expectation of both Jewish apocalyptic texts and Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 40-48), where the climactic new Jerusalem is centered on a restored temple. In Revelation the temple is unnecessary because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple — the presence that the temple was designed to house is now immediately, totally, and permanently present.

Revelation 11

Two prophetic witnesses preach for 1,260 days, are killed, lie in the street three days, and rise — then the seventh trumpet announces the kingdom.

Summary

The two witnesses of chapter 11 are the most enigmatic figures in the book after the Lamb and the dragon. The imagery that surrounds them conflates Moses and Elijah — the plague power and turning water to blood recall Moses; the shutting of the sky recalls Elijah's drought in 1 Kings 17. The description as two olive trees and two lampstands draws on Zechariah 4, where two anointed figures (Zerubbabel and Joshua) stand before the Lord of all the earth. Some interpreters take the witnesses as literal eschatological prophets yet to appear; others as symbolic figures for the prophetic church or for the law and the prophets; others as Elijah and Moses themselves, who have been interpreted in Christian tradition as the figures at the transfiguration. The 1,260-day period of their ministry is the same period as in 12:6 and 13:5 — the three-and-a-half-year time of tribulation from Daniel — and its recurrence across the book ties the various scenes of suffering and prophecy together into a single bounded period.

The beast from the bottomless pit makes its first brief appearance in this chapter (11:7) before its extended portrayal in chapter 13 — a signal that the book's visions are not strictly linear but recursive, each vision introducing figures that will be developed later. The three-and-a-half-day period in which the bodies lie unburied in the street of the great city — identified as where their Lord was crucified, establishing the scene in Jerusalem — inverts the three-and-a-half-year period of their ministry and recalls the three days of Christ's burial. Their resurrection and ascension in a cloud before their enemies deliberately echo the resurrection and ascension of Christ. The seventh trumpet at the end of the chapter (11:15-18) is premature from a narrative standpoint — the bowl judgments of chapters 15-16 still lie ahead — but structurally it announces the eschatological resolution that the rest of the book will show arriving: the kingdom of the world becoming the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ. The chapter functions as a compressed recapitulation of the entire book's movement within the trumpet framework.

Ezekiel 37

The hand of the Lord set the prophet in a valley full of very dry bones, and asked him: can these bones live? Ezekiel answered: O Lord God, you know.

Summary

Chapter 37 is the most quoted chapter in Ezekiel and contains two of the most concentrated restoration visions in the prophetic tradition. The vision of the dry bones opens with the prophetic transport formula: the hand of the Lord was upon me. The prophet is carried out and set in the middle of a valley, and the valley was full of bones — a great many, and they were very dry. The Lord asks: Son of man, can these bones live? And Ezekiel, in the most careful and precisely right answer in the book, says: O Lord God, you know. He is told to prophesy to the bones. There is a rattling — bone comes to bone. Sinews appear on them, flesh covers them, skin closes over them. But there is no breath. He is then told to prophesy to the four winds — the same Hebrew word as spirit and breath — and the breath comes into them and they live, and stand upon their feet, an exceeding great army.

The vision is interpreted explicitly and immediately: these bones are the whole house of Israel, who say 'our bones are dried, and our hope is lost; we are completely cut off.' The Lord will open their graves and bring them up and return them to the land of Israel. The vision of the dry bones has been one of the most continuously interpreted passages in biblical tradition: in the rabbinic literature as a proof text for bodily resurrection; in Pauline theology as a figure of Israel's eschatological restoration; in African American spiritual tradition as the source of the folk song Dem Bones; in modern Israel as an image of national revival. The second vision of chapter 37 is the two sticks. The prophet joins two sticks — one labelled Judah, one labelled Ephraim (the northern kingdom, Israel) — into one in his hand. The interpretation: the Lord will reunite the divided kingdoms into one nation under one king, his servant David. One nation, one king, one sanctuary, one covenant forever. The two promises of chapter 37 — national resurrection and political reunification — form the climax of the restoration prophecies before the war with Gog.

Daniel — chapter by chapter

All 12 chapters — court tales (1–6) and apocalyptic visions (7–12).

Daniel divides cleanly in half. Chapters 1–6 are the court tales: Daniel refuses the king's food (1), interprets the dream of the great image (2), watches his companions walk out of the furnace (3), interprets the second dream and the madness (4), reads the writing on the wall at Belshazzar's feast (5), and survives the lions' den under Darius the Mede (6). Then the book shifts register entirely. Chapters 7–12 are apocalyptic visions: the four beasts and the son of man (7), the ram and the goat (8), the seventy weeks and Gabriel's oracle (9), the delayed heavenly messenger (10), the detailed account of the wars of the kings (11), and the resurrection of the dead (12). The Hebrew-Aramaic-Hebrew language pattern (Hebrew in 1:1–2:4a and 8–12; Aramaic in between) is one of the book's structural mysteries.

Court tales · Chs 1–6

Exile, refusal, deliverance. Four young men in the courts of Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, and Darius the Mede.

Daniel 1

Daniel 1

Four Jewish exiles in Babylon refuse the king's food, thrive on vegetables and water, and surpass every rival at the royal court — establishing the terms of the whole book.

Appears: Daniel · Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah · Nebuchadnezzar
Daniel 2

Daniel 2

Nebuchadnezzar's dream of a great image — four metals, struck by a stone cut without hands — is interpreted by Daniel as the succession of four world empires before God's everlasting kingdom.

Appears: Daniel · Nebuchadnezzar · Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah
Daniel 3

Daniel 3

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refuse the king's idol, walk out of his furnace unharmed, and deliver one of the most concentrated speeches of faithful witness in the Hebrew Bible.

Appears: Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah · Nebuchadnezzar
Daniel 4

Daniel 4

Nebuchadnezzar's second dream — a great tree felled to a stump — presages his seven-season madness and restoration, narrated in the Bible's only first-person account by a foreign king.

Appears: Nebuchadnezzar · Daniel
Daniel 5

Daniel 5

At Belshazzar's blasphemous feast, a disembodied hand writes on the palace wall — and Daniel translates: 'Numbered, weighed, divided.' That night the king is killed and Babylon falls.

Appears: Belshazzar · Daniel · Darius the Mede
Daniel 6

Daniel 6

Daniel prays toward Jerusalem in defiance of Darius's decree, survives the lions' den through God's protection, and his accusers are destroyed — Darius himself issuing a decree of acknowledgement.

Appears: Daniel · Darius the Mede

Apocalyptic visions · Chs 7–12

Beasts from the sea, a throne of fire, a heavenly messenger, and the resurrection of the dead.

Daniel 7

Daniel 7

The book's pivotal vision: four world empires as beasts from the sea, the divine throne of the Ancient of Days, and one like a son of man receiving an eternal kingdom — founding the genre of biblical apocalyptic.

Appears: Daniel · The Son of Man
Daniel 8

Daniel 8

The ram (Persia) and goat (Greece) of Daniel's second vision culminate in a 'little horn' who desecrates the temple — the most explicit symbolic portrait of Antiochus IV Epiphanes in the book.

Appears: Daniel
Daniel 9

Daniel 9

Gabriel's oracle of the seventy weeks — the most-calculated passage in Daniel — answers Daniel's prayer over Jeremiah's prophecy with a determinate timetable for sin's end and everlasting righteousness.

Appears: Daniel
Daniel 10

Daniel 10

Daniel's three-week fast is interrupted by a dazzling angelic figure whose arrival was delayed by a heavenly battle — introducing the cosmic-war framework of chapters 10-12.

Appears: Daniel
Daniel 11

Daniel 11

The most historically precise chapter in the Bible: the wars of the Ptolemaic and Seleucid kingdoms, tracked verse by verse to Antiochus IV's desecration of the temple — the longest vaticinium ex eventu in Scripture.

Appears: Daniel
Daniel 12

Daniel 12

The book's final chapter delivers the Hebrew Bible's first explicit promise of bodily resurrection — many who sleep in the dust shall awake — before Daniel is told to go his way and rest until the end.

Appears: Daniel

Daniel 12

Michael stands up for Daniel's people at the time of unparalleled trouble — and the vision ends with the first explicit promise of bodily resurrection in the Hebrew Bible.

Summary

The twelfth chapter of Daniel concludes both the long vision of chapters 10-12 and the book as a whole, and its opening verses contain the most theologically consequential statement in the book: the first explicit promise of bodily resurrection in the Hebrew Bible. The transition from the historical panorama of chapter 11 to the eschatological events of chapter 12 is abrupt and deliberate. Michael's standing up marks the beginning of the end-time events; the time of trouble surpassing anything before recalls the language of Jeremiah 30:7 and Joel 2:2, and is echoed in Jesus's apocalyptic discourse in Mark 13:19. The book of life — the written record of those who will be delivered — appears already in Exodus 32:32 and Psalm 69:28, and will reappear as the Lamb's book of life in Revelation 21:27. The resurrection passage (12:2-3) is brief and precise: many (not all — the scope is debated) of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake; the wise and those who turn many to righteousness shall shine like stars for ever. The language of shining like stars may draw on the tradition of stellar immortality in Hellenistic culture, transformed into a Jewish eschatological framework.

The first-century Pharisees cited Daniel 12 as a primary scriptural basis for their doctrine of bodily resurrection, a doctrine the Sadducees rejected. The passage became the foundational Old Testament text for the Christian doctrine of resurrection, and the image of the wise shining like stars was appropriated in a wide range of early Christian and later mystical texts. The cryptic time periods at the end of the chapter — twelve hundred and ninety days (12:11) and twelve hundred and thirty-five days (12:12) — are connected to the desecration of the temple and seem intended to give slightly different ends to the period of persecution; both figures have been calculated against the actual dates of Antiochus's persecution and found to correspond approximately to the period 167-164 BCE, with an additional thirty- and sixty-five-day margin beyond the twelve hundred and sixty days of chapter 7. The chapter closes with the instruction to Daniel to go his way: the book is sealed until the time of the end, and Daniel himself is promised rest and a place among those raised at the final day. The sentence — You shall rest, and you shall stand in your lot at the end of the days — is the last in the Hebrew Bible's only sustained apocalyptic book, and it addresses the seer personally with the same promise of resurrection it has just delivered for the many.

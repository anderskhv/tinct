Exodus — chapter by chapter

All 40 chapters — from the decree to drown Hebrew boys to the glory filling the tabernacle.

Exodus moves in three large arcs. Chapters 1–15 are the deliverance: oppression, Moses's call, the plague contest with Pharaoh, the Passover, and the crossing of the sea. Chapters 16–18 are the wilderness: manna, water from the rock, the battle with Amalek, Jethro's visit and his judicial advice. Chapters 19–40 are Sinai and its aftermath: the theophany, the Decalogue, the Book of the Covenant, the detailed tabernacle instructions, the golden calf disaster, Moses's intercession and the renewal of the covenant, and finally the construction and consecration of the tabernacle so that God can dwell among the people as they travel.

Oppression and deliverance · Ch 1–15

Egypt, the plagues, the Passover, and the crossing of the sea.

Chapter 1

Exodus 1

A new pharaoh, threatened by a people's growth, answers with forced labour, infanticide orders, and a command to drown Hebrew boys in the Nile.

Appears: Pharaoh
Chapter 2

Exodus 2

Moses is born, hidden, saved by Pharaoh's daughter, raised at court, flees to Midian after killing an Egyptian, and marries Zipporah — while Israel's cry rises to God.

Appears: Moses · Miriam · Zipporah · Jethro · Pharaoh
Chapter 3

Exodus 3

At Horeb, God appears in a burning bush, reveals the divine name as I Am Who I Am, and commissions the reluctant Moses to deliver Israel from Egypt.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 4

Exodus 4

Moses, armed with miraculous signs and his brother Aaron as spokesman, makes the dangerous return to Egypt — but God nearly kills him at a roadside inn in a scene that resists explanation.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Zipporah · Jethro
Chapter 5

Exodus 5

Pharaoh refuses Moses's first request, increases the workload by withholding straw, and the Israelite foremen turn furiously on Moses for making things worse.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh
Chapter 6

Exodus 6

God answers Moses's despair with seven covenant promises, renews the commission with Aaron as Moses's spokesman, and the Priestly genealogy establishes Moses and Aaron's lineage.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 7

Exodus 7

Aaron's staff swallows the court magicians' serpents; the Nile turns to blood and the magicians match even this — but Pharaoh walks away unmoved, the hardening begins in earnest.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh
Chapter 8

Exodus 8

Three plagues: frogs, then gnats the magicians cannot match, then flies that spare Goshen. Pharaoh twice negotiates and twice hardens — establishing the pattern of the whole contest.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh
Chapter 9

Exodus 9

Disease kills Egyptian livestock but spares Israel's; boils silence the magicians for good; catastrophic hail destroys crops and trees — and Pharaoh's brief repentance dissolves the moment the storm stops.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh
Chapter 10

Exodus 10

Locusts eat what hail left; palpable darkness covers Egypt for three days; Pharaoh's last offer — leave your animals — is refused, and Moses and Pharaoh part for the last time.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh
Chapter 11

Exodus 11

Moses announces to Pharaoh the death of every firstborn in Egypt at midnight — from Pharaoh's heir to the slave girl's son — while not a dog will bark at any Israelite.

Appears: Moses · Pharaoh
Chapter 12

Exodus 12

The Passover is instituted in detailed liturgical law; at midnight every Egyptian firstborn dies; Israel leaves Egypt before dawn — six hundred thousand men plus dependants — four hundred and thirty years to the day.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Pharaoh · Miriam
Chapter 13

Exodus 13

Israel consecrates its firstborn to God, takes Joseph's bones from Egypt, and is led into the wilderness by a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 14

Exodus 14

Pharaoh pursues with six hundred chariots; Moses stretches his staff over the sea; Israel crosses on dry ground; the Egyptian army is drowned in the returning waters and lies dead on the shore.

Appears: Moses · Pharaoh
Chapter 15

Exodus 15

The Song of the Sea celebrates the drowning of Pharaoh's army in some of the oldest surviving Hebrew verse; Miriam leads the women's dance; three days later the wilderness water is bitter and the murmuring begins.

Appears: Moses · Miriam

Wilderness journey · Ch 16–18

Manna, water, battle with Amalek, Jethro's visit.

Chapter 16

Exodus 16

Israel murmurs in hunger; quail arrive in the evening and manna each morning — exactly enough for one day, double on the sixth — and the sabbath is instituted through the manna's logic.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 17

Exodus 17

Moses strikes the rock at Horeb and water flows; Israel's first battle, against Amalek, is decided by whether Moses keeps his arms raised, with Aaron and Hur holding them up until sunset.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 18

Exodus 18

Jethro brings Zipporah and Moses's sons to the wilderness, hears Moses's account, blesses the Lord, and then advises Moses to delegate judicial work — and Moses implements the entire system before Jethro leaves.

Appears: Moses · Aaron · Jethro · Zipporah

Sinai covenant · Ch 19–24

Theophany, Decalogue, Book of the Covenant, covenant ratification.

Chapter 19

Exodus 19

At Sinai, God offers Israel a covenant as a kingdom of priests and holy nation; the mountain erupts in fire, thunder, and trumpet blasts; Moses alone goes to the summit to receive what follows.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 20

Exodus 20

The Decalogue: ten utterances from Sinai, beginning with the declaration that the God now speaking is the one who freed them from Egypt, followed by the commands on which all later Western law acknowledges descent.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 21

Exodus 21

The first law code after Sinai governs slaves, injuries, and liability — law as the shape of a delivered people.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 22

Exodus 22

From stolen oxen to orphaned widows: the covenant law folds economic and social ethics into the same legal logic.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 23

Exodus 23

Courts, sabbath years, three annual festivals, and the angel who will go before them into the land — the covenant closes with promise.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 24

Exodus 24

The covenant between God and Israel is sealed in blood thrown on both altar and people, followed by a meal before God on the mountain.

Appears: Moses · Aaron

Tabernacle · Ch 25–40

Instructions, the golden calf, covenant renewal, construction, dedication.

Chapter 25

Exodus 25

God instructs the making of the ark, table, and lampstand — the furnishings of the sanctuary where he will dwell with his people.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 26

Exodus 26

The tabernacle's curtains, frames, and inner veil divide holy space from most holy space in a portable architecture of divine presence.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 27

Exodus 27

The bronze altar for burnt offerings and the linen courtyard define the outer perimeter of the sanctuary; oil keeps the lampstand burning night after night.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 28

Exodus 28

Aaron's high-priestly vestments — ephod, breastpiece, robe, turban — bear the twelve tribal names as he carries Israel before God.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 29

Exodus 29

Aaron and his sons are washed, robed, anointed with oil, and consecrated with blood over seven days before the daily sacrifice is established.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 30

Exodus 30

The incense altar, census tax, bronze laver, and the formulas for anointing oil and incense complete the tabernacle's consecrated equipment.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 31

Exodus 31

God names Bezalel and Oholiab as the spirit-filled artisans who will build the tabernacle, then insists that Sabbath rest interrupts even sacred construction.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 32

Exodus 32

The golden calf shatters the covenant before it is sealed; Moses argues God back from destruction and offers himself in the people's place.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 33

Exodus 33

God threatens to withdraw his presence after the golden calf; Moses demands to see God's glory and is shown his back — no one sees God's face and lives.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 34

Exodus 34

The covenant is renewed on new tablets; God proclaims the thirteen attributes of mercy; Moses descends with his face shining and veiled.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 35

Exodus 35

Israel brings freewill offerings of gold, linen, and skill for the tabernacle — more than is needed — in a collective act of generosity.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 36

Exodus 36

Bezalel's craftsmen begin building; the people bring too much, and Moses has to command them to stop giving.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 37

Exodus 37

Bezalel makes the ark, the mercy seat and cherubim, the table, the lampstand, and the incense altar — all from gold and acacia wood, exactly as commanded.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 38

Exodus 38

The altar and courtyard are completed; a full inventory of gold, silver, and bronze consumed in construction closes the chapter.

Appears: Moses
Chapter 39

Exodus 39

Every priestly vestment is made exactly as commanded; Moses inspects the completed work and blesses those who built it.

Appears: Moses · Aaron
Chapter 40

Exodus 40

Moses erects the tabernacle, anoints its furnishings, and consecrates the priests; the glory of the Lord fills it so fully he cannot enter.

Appears: Moses · Aaron

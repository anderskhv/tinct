Exodus 19

Three months out of Egypt, Israel camps at the foot of Sinai; God offers them a covenant, the mountain erupts in smoke and fire, and Moses alone is summoned to the top.

Summary

Exodus 19 is the staging of the Sinai revelation, and it works as theatre as much as theology. The three-day preparation period, the washing, the prohibition on approaching the mountain, the physical phenomena — fire, smoke, earthquake, trumpet — are all designed to establish that what is about to happen at the mountain exceeds ordinary human experience and cannot be approached casually. The repeated boundary warnings, and the penalty of death for touching the mountain, mark out a zone of holiness that ordinary contact would violate.

The offer embedded in the chapter — if you obey me, you will be my treasured possession, a kingdom of priests, a holy nation — is one of the defining statements of Israel's identity in the Hebrew Bible and its resonance extends into Christian and Islamic self-understanding. The people's response, we will do everything the Lord has said, is given before they have heard what the Lord will say. The covenant is accepted in advance of the terms, which places the Decalogue that follows in chapter 20 in the position of the covenant content already agreed to rather than conditions being negotiated. The chapter's structure — repeated ascents and descents, warnings, preparations — enacts the difficulty of the encounter between the holy and the human before the law itself is spoken.

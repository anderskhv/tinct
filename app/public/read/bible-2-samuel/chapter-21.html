Summary

A famine comes upon Israel in David's days for three years. David inquires of the Lord, who says it is for Saul and his bloody house because he slew the Gibeonites. David calls the Gibeonites and asks them what shall be done for them and wherewith shall he make atonement. The Gibeonites say they will have no silver or gold of Saul or his house, nor will they ask the life of any man in Israel. David asks: what do you say I shall do for you? They say: deliver to us the man that consumed us and that devised against us, that we should be destroyed from remaining in any of the coasts of Israel. David says he will give them what they ask. They name seven men of the sons of Saul, and the king takes two sons of Rizpah the daughter of Aiah, Armoni and Mephibosheth, and five sons of Merab the daughter of Saul. He delivers them to the Gibeonites, who hang them in the hill before the Lord. All seven fall together in the days of harvest, in the first days, in the beginning of barley harvest.

Rizpah the daughter of Aiah takes sackcloth and spreads it on the rock, from the beginning of harvest until water drops upon them out of heaven, and suffers neither the birds of the air to rest on them by day nor the beasts of the field by night. David is told what Rizpah has done. He goes and takes the bones of Saul and Jonathan out of Jabesh-gilead — where the men of Jabesh-gilead had secretly taken them from Beth-shan where the Philistines had hanged them — and the bones of the seven executed are gathered up. He buries the bones of Saul and Jonathan in the country of Benjamin in Zelah, in the sepulchre of Kish his father. God is entreated for the land after that. There are more battles with the Philistines in which Abishai kills the giant Ishbi-benob who had thought to slay David; Sibbecai the Hushathite kills Saph; Elhanan kills the brother of Goliath the Gittite; Jonathan the nephew of David kills a man of great stature who had twelve fingers and twelve toes. These four fell by the hand of David and his servants.

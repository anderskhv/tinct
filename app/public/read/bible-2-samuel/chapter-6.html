2 Samuel 6

The king of Israel dances before the Lord with all his might, and his wife watches from a window with contempt.

Summary

David gathers thirty thousand chosen men and goes down to retrieve the ark of God, which has been at Baale of Judah. They carry it on a new cart, Uzzah and Ahio driving. At the threshing floor of Nacon the oxen stumble; Uzzah puts out his hand to steady the ark and is struck dead by God for his error. David is afraid, angry at the interruption, and calls the place Perez-uzzah. He declines to bring the ark to the city of David and leaves it at the house of Obed-edom the Gittite instead. It stays there three months, during which the Lord blesses Obed-edom and all his household. When David hears of this, he goes to bring the ark up to Jerusalem with joy, sacrificing an ox and a fatling every six paces. He dances before the Lord with all his might, wearing a linen ephod. Shouting and trumpets accompany the ark into the city, and David distributes bread, meat, and raisin cakes to the whole assembly.

When David returns to bless his household, Michal, daughter of Saul, comes out to meet him. Her words are sharp: how glorious was the king today, who uncovered himself today in the eyes of the handmaids of his servants, as one of the vain fellows shamelessly uncovereth himself. David's reply is equally sharp: before the Lord, who chose him over your father and all his house, to appoint him ruler over Israel — before the Lord will he play. And he will be yet more vile than this. The handmaids of which she spoke will honour him. The chapter ends with a single, desolate sentence: Michal the daughter of Saul had no child unto the day of her death. The barrenness is the text's verdict on her position. The ark's arrival in Jerusalem marks the completion of the political consolidation; Michal's rebuke and its consequence mark the final severing of any debt to the house of Saul.

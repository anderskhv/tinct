2 Samuel 18

A father orders his generals to deal gently with his rebellious son; the general kills the son, and the father's grief destroys the victory.

Summary

David numbers the people and appoints a third each under Joab, under Abishai the son of Zeruiah Joab's brother, and under Ittai the Gittite. The king says he will go out himself; the people say he shall not, for if they flee the enemy will not care about them, but David is worth ten thousand of them. David agrees to stay in the city. He stands by the gate as all the people go out, and charges the commanders: deal gently with the young man, even with Absalom. All the people hear the king's charge concerning Absalom. The army goes out into the field against Israel and the battle is in the wood of Ephraim. The people of Israel are smitten before the servants of David; a great slaughter of twenty thousand men. The battle spreads over the face of all the country, and the wood devours more people than the sword that day. Absalom meets the servants of David; he is riding on his mule. The mule goes under the thick boughs of a great oak; his head catches in the oak. He is taken up between the heaven and the earth while the mule goes on.

A certain man sees this and tells Joab: Absalom is hanging in an oak. Joab asks why the man did not smite him. The man says: though I should receive a thousand shekels of silver I would not put forth mine hand against the king's son, for the king charged thee and Abishai and Ittai in our hearing, saying, Beware that none touch the young man Absalom. Joab says: I may not tarry thus with thee. He takes three darts in his hand and thrusts them through the heart of Absalom while he still lives in the oak, and ten young men that bear Joab's armour smite Absalom and slay him. Joab blows the trumpet and the people return from pursuing Israel. They take Absalom and cast him into a great pit in the wood and lay a very great heap of stones upon him. All Israel flee every one to his tent. Ahimaaz son of Zadok runs to David with news. A Cushite also runs. David, sitting between the two gates, asks the watchman what he sees. He sees Ahimaaz coming first. He runs and bows before the king and says: all is well. The king says: is the young man Absalom safe? Ahimaaz says: when Joab sent me I saw a great tumult but knew not what it was. The king stands aside. The Cushite arrives with the news of Absalom's death. The king covers his face and cries with a loud voice: O my son Absalom, O Absalom, my son, my son!

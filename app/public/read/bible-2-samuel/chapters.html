2 Samuel — chapter by chapter

All 24 chapters, from the lament for Saul to the altar on the threshing floor.

2 Samuel divides into three movements. Chapters 1–10 are the consolidation: the elegy, the civil war with the house of Saul, the taking of Jerusalem, the ark's procession, Nathan's oracle. Chapters 11–20 are the unwinding: Bathsheba, Uriah, Nathan's confrontation, the death of the child, Amnon, Tamar, Absalom, the rebellion, the death in the oak, Joab's rebuke, the return. Chapters 21–24 are the appendices — famine, plague, a roll call of the mighty men. Twenty-four chapters, two halves, one pivot in chapter 11.

David in Hebron · chs 1–4

The civil war with the house of Saul; Abner and Ish-bosheth.

2 Samuel 1

2 Samuel 1

David receives the news of Saul's death, executes the bearer of supposedly good news, and delivers the elegy 'How are the mighty fallen.'

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 2

2 Samuel 2

David is anointed king at Hebron over Judah while Ish-bosheth rules the north; the two kingdoms' armies clash at Gibeon and the civil war begins.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 3

2 Samuel 3

Abner defects to David after quarrelling with Ish-bosheth over a concubine; Joab kills Abner at the gate of Hebron in revenge for Asahel, complicating David's diplomacy.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 4

2 Samuel 4

Ish-bosheth is murdered in his bed by two captains who bring his head to David; David has them executed, rejecting the assassination's fruits as he rejected the Amalekite's earlier.

Appears: David

Davidic covenant · chs 5–10

Jerusalem taken, the ark brought up, Nathan's oracle, wars on every front.

2 Samuel 5

2 Samuel 5

David is anointed king of all Israel at Hebron, captures Jerusalem from the Jebusites and makes it his capital, and defeats the Philistines in two major engagements.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 6

2 Samuel 6

David brings the ark to Jerusalem with dancing, sacrifices, and celebration; Uzzah's death interrupts the first attempt, and Michal's contempt ends with her barrenness.

Appears: David
2 Samuel 7

2 Samuel 7

Nathan delivers God's promise to David: his dynasty will endure forever, God will be father to his son, and his throne will be established perpetually — the seed of all later messianic hope.

Appears: David · Nathan
2 Samuel 8

2 Samuel 8

A catalogue of David's military victories over surrounding nations and an administrative list of his court officials, recording Israel at the height of its territorial expansion.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 9

2 Samuel 9

David honours his covenant with Jonathan by finding Jonathan's crippled son Mephibosheth, restoring Saul's land to him, and inviting him to eat permanently at the royal table.

Appears: David
2 Samuel 10

2 Samuel 10

David's ambassadors are humiliated by the Ammonites; a war follows in which Joab defeats the combined Ammonite and Aramean forces, leaving the Ammonite capital still under siege.

Appears: David · Joab

Bathsheba and Absalom · chs 11–20

The sin, the parable, the consequences, the rebellion, the death, the return.

2 Samuel 11

2 Samuel 11

David sees Bathsheba bathing, sleeps with her, and when she conceives, arranges the death of her husband Uriah the Hittite; the narrator closes with a single line of divine displeasure.

Appears: David · Bathsheba · Uriah the Hittite · Joab
2 Samuel 12

2 Samuel 12

Nathan confronts David with the parable of the ewe lamb; David confesses; the child of Bathsheba dies despite David's fasting; Solomon is born and loved by God.

Appears: David · Bathsheba · Nathan
2 Samuel 13

2 Samuel 13

Amnon's obsession with Tamar, his half-sister, leads to rape; Amnon's subsequent contempt leads to Absalom's murder of Amnon; Absalom flees to Geshur for three years.

Appears: David · Absalom
2 Samuel 14

2 Samuel 14

Joab engineers Absalom's return from Geshur through a woman of Tekoa who tells a parable that draws a favourable ruling from David before he realises whose case he is deciding.

Appears: David · Absalom · Joab
2 Samuel 15

2 Samuel 15

Absalom builds a popular following over four years, has himself proclaimed king at Hebron, and forces David to flee Jerusalem barefoot and weeping, with loyal followers but uncertain prospects.

Appears: David · Absalom · Joab
2 Samuel 16

2 Samuel 16

David flees Jerusalem through three encounters: Ziba's supplies and slander of Mephibosheth, Shimei's public cursing, and Absalom's entry into Jerusalem and occupation of the palace.

Appears: David · Absalom
2 Samuel 17

2 Samuel 17

Ahithophel's precise battle plan is countered by Hushai's flattering advice; Absalom chooses Hushai, giving David time to escape; the thwarted Ahithophel hangs himself.

Appears: David · Absalom · Joab
2 Samuel 18

2 Samuel 18

Joab's forces rout Absalom's army; Absalom is caught by his hair in an oak tree and killed by Joab against David's explicit order; the king's grief at the news is the book's emotional climax.

Appears: David · Absalom · Joab
2 Samuel 19

2 Samuel 19

Joab scolds David into resuming his kingly duties; David returns to Jerusalem, pardoning Shimei, adjudicating between Mephibosheth and Ziba, and honouring Barzillai the Gileadite.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 20

2 Samuel 20

Sheba the Benjaminite leads a second revolt; Joab murders Amasa and takes command, pursues Sheba north to Abel Beth-maacah, where a wise woman negotiates an end to the siege.

Appears: David · Joab

Appendices · chs 21–24

Famine, plague, the mighty men, a psalm, the threshing floor.

2 Samuel 21

2 Samuel 21

A three-year famine is traced to Saul's breach of Joshua's oath with the Gibeonites; seven of Saul's sons are hanged to atone; Rizpah's vigil prompts David to bury Saul's bones with honour.

Appears: David · Joab
2 Samuel 22

2 Samuel 22

David's long psalm of thanksgiving — nearly identical to Psalm 18 — recounts God's cosmic rescue from enemies and enemies like death, presenting God as rock, fortress, and deliverer.

Appears: David
2 Samuel 23

2 Samuel 23

David's dying oracle describes the just ruler and God's covenant; the roll of the mighty men follows, ending with Uriah the Hittite — the last name placing the ghost of chapter 11 at the book's close.

Appears: David · Uriah the Hittite · Joab
2 Samuel 24

2 Samuel 24

David's census angers God; a pestilence kills seventy thousand; the plague stops at the threshing floor of Araunah, where David builds an altar — the site that will become the temple.

Appears: David · Joab · Nathan

Chapter 33

Elizabeth repeatedly encounters Darcy in her favorite grove — each meeting more unexpected than the last — and then walks with Fitzwilliam, who reveals what Darcy did to separate Bingley from Jane.

Summary

More than once, Elizabeth encounters Mr. Darcy in the park grove she has told him is her favorite walk. The first encounter is a surprise; the second, strange; the third, impossible not to notice as a pattern. He walks with her each time without saying much, asks oddly disconnected questions, and says something that seems to imply she might return to Rosings on some future visit. Elizabeth is unsettled without quite knowing why. She reaches the gate opposite the parsonage with relief.

On a later walk she meets Colonel Fitzwilliam instead, who joins her for the circuit back to the parsonage. Their conversation is easy and pleasant, ranging across various topics, until Fitzwilliam mentions that Darcy recently told him he had done a friend a great service — that he had saved him from the consequences of a most imprudent marriage. The friend had been on the verge of forming an attachment to a young woman very unsuitably placed, and Darcy had taken on himself the task of separating them. Fitzwilliam relates it without any awareness of its significance to Elizabeth.

Elizabeth listens, asks carefully calibrated questions — was the young woman's character at fault, was the attachment real? — and Fitzwilliam gives her the answers she fears. She does not let anything show. She returns to the parsonage, claims a headache to excuse herself from Rosings, and sits alone with Jane's letters. She reads with fresh attention every sentence that hints at unhappiness, and begins to understand that Darcy's boast — his 'most invaluable friend' — means he destroyed her sister's chance at happiness with his own hands and considers it an act of virtue.

Chapter 35 of 37

Fogg does not have to repeat his orders to Passepartout twice

Fogg returns to Saville Row and does not go out. He sits. He has lost — he believes — everything.

Summary

No. 7 Saville Row. The house is unchanged. The house has been closed for eighty days, the staff maintaining it in their normal routine. Fogg enters, goes upstairs, does nothing. He sits.

Passepartout, who has never seen his master not doing something precisely on schedule, understands that this immobility is not rest. Fogg has spent the better part of twenty thousand pounds on the journey — the fare for the Tankadere, the elephantine expense of the rescue, the two thousand pounds bail, the Henrietta purchase and the crew's bonuses. He has returned with almost nothing. And he has returned — he believes — on the wrong day.

Aouda comes to see him. She says something to him that Verne does not fully transcribe, because it does not need to be transcribed. She is there. She has been with him for two months. She has seen more of him than anyone has seen in years. She says something about the future. Fogg receives it with his characteristic stillness. Later, he asks Passepartout to go find a clergyman for the following morning.

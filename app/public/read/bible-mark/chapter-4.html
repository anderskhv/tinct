Mark 4

Jesus teaches in parables from a boat, explains the sower to the disciples alone, and at evening rebukes the wind and the waves to a great calm.

Summary

Mark 4 is the gospel's parable chapter. The setting is deliberate — Jesus in a boat with the crowd on the beach — and the teaching mode is explained within the chapter itself: he did not speak to them without a parable, but privately to his own disciples he explained everything (4:34). The parable of the sower (4:1-9) is given first and at length; the explanation of why Jesus speaks in parables at all (4:10-12) follows, framed around Isaiah 6:9-10 — the passage about hearing without perceiving and seeing without understanding. The explanation of the sower (4:13-20) interprets the four soils as four conditions of the human heart when the word enters it: the path (the word snatched by Satan), the rocky ground (received with joy but rootless, falling away under tribulation), the thorny ground (the word choked by the cares of the world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the desires for other things), and the good soil (hearing and accepting and bearing fruit, thirtyfold, sixtyfold, a hundredfold). The mystery the disciples receive is the parabolic code itself — to those outside, everything remains parable because they have not entered the circle of hearing that constitutes following Jesus.

The seed growing secretly (4:26-29) is unique to Mark among the four gospels: a man scatters seed and sleeps and rises night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows, he knows not how. The earth produces by itself, first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear. When the grain is ripe, at once he puts in the sickle, because the harvest has come. The parable makes the kingdom's growth autonomous — it does not depend on the sower's understanding or effort. The mustard seed (4:30-32) follows: smallest of all seeds, and when it grows it becomes the largest of garden shrubs, so that the birds of the air can make nests in its shade. The crossing of the sea (4:35-41) closes the chapter. The disciples' question — who then is this, that even the wind and sea obey him? — is the gospel's running question about Jesus' identity. The disciples are in the boat with the answer and cannot name him.

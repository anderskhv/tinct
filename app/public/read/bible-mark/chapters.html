Mark — chapter by chapter

All 16 chapters — from the Jordan River to the empty tomb.

Mark divides naturally into three movements. The Galilean ministry (chapters 1–8:21) is the gospel at full speed: calling, healing, exorcism, controversy. The pivot at Caesarea Philippi (8:22–10) turns the gospel south, with three Passion predictions each followed by the disciples' incomprehension. Jerusalem and the Passion (chapters 11–16) bring the entry, the temple controversies, the apocalyptic discourse, the Passion narrative, and the empty tomb.

Galilean ministry · 1–8:21

Calling, healing, exorcism, controversy — and immediate, always immediate.

Mark 1

Mark 1

The gospel opens without preamble: John baptizes, the heavens tear, the Spirit drives, and within forty verses healings and exorcisms have begun in Capernaum.

Appears: Jesus · Peter
Mark 2

Mark 2

Five controversy stories begin: a man lowered through a roof is forgiven and healed, a tax collector is called, and the Sabbath controversies open the gathering opposition.

Appears: Jesus
Mark 3

Mark 3

Pharisees and Herodians begin plotting; Jesus appoints the Twelve, rebuts the Beelzebul charge, and redefines family as those who do the will of God.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples
Mark 4

Mark 4

The parable chapter: the sower, the growing seed, the mustard seed. To the disciples, the mystery is explained. At evening, wind and sea obey a word.

Appears: Jesus · The Disciples
Mark 5

Mark 5

Three healings at the outer limit: a legion expelled into pigs, a twelve-year haemorrhage healed by faith, and a dead girl raised with the words little girl, arise.

Appears: Jesus · Peter
Mark 6

Mark 6

Rejection at Nazareth, the Twelve sent out, John the Baptist beheaded at a banquet, five thousand fed, and Jesus walking on the water to the uncomprehending disciples.

Appears: Jesus · The Disciples · Peter
Mark 7

Mark 7

The purity teaching, the Syrophoenician woman's impertinent faith, and the Aramaic ephphatha: the gospel's Gentile turn is now explicit.

Appears: Jesus · The Disciples
Mark 8

Mark 8

The gospel's centre: the second feeding, the leaven of the Pharisees, a blind man healed in two stages, Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi, and the first prediction of the Passion.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples

Caesarea Philippi pivot to Jerusalem · 8:22–10

Three Passion predictions; three revelations of incomprehension.

Mark 9

Mark 9

The transfiguration on the mountain, the second passion prediction, the possessed boy the disciples cannot heal, and the argument about who is greatest.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples
Mark 10

Mark 10

The third passion prediction, the request for seats of glory, and blind Bartimaeus by the road to Jericho — the way to Jerusalem is also the way of the servant.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples

Jerusalem and the Passion · 11–16

The entry, the temple, the cross, the centurion, the empty tomb.

Mark 11

Mark 11

The entry into Jerusalem on a colt, the clearing of the temple between the cursing and withering of the fig tree, and the challenge to Jesus' authority from the chief priests.

Appears: Jesus · The Disciples · Peter
Mark 12

Mark 12

Five trap questions answered in the temple, the parable of the wicked tenants, the greatest commandment, and the widow whose two coins outgive them all.

Appears: Jesus
Mark 13

Mark 13

The Olivet discourse: temple destruction, false prophets, cosmic signs, the Son of Man in clouds, and the repeated call to watch — for no one knows the day or the hour.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples
Mark 14

Mark 14

The Passion begins: the anointing for burial, the Last Supper's bread and cup, Gethsemane's prayer, the arrest, the Sanhedrin trial, and Peter's three denials.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Disciples · Pilate
Mark 15

Mark 15

The trial, the cross timed by the hour, the cry of dereliction in Aramaic, the curtain torn, and the centurion's confession: the gospel's christological argument reaches its climax at the death.

Appears: Jesus · Pilate · The Centurion at the Cross · Mary Magdalene
Mark 16

Mark 16

The empty tomb, the young man's announcement, the instruction to go to Galilee — and then silence. The gospel ends at 16:8 with the women fleeing in fear, the next sentence the reader's.

Appears: Mary Magdalene · Jesus

Paradiso 3 of 100

Beatrice explains — free will and the vow

Dante has two questions. Beatrice answers: how can Piccarda be fully happy in the lowest sphere? And can a broken vow be compensated by good works?

Summary

Dante has two questions. The first: if Piccarda is fully happy in the lowest sphere, why doesn't her happiness seek more, since she sees plainly that the souls above her enjoy more? Beatrice explains: the capacity to receive the divine light differs at each level, but each level is filled to its capacity. Joy is not diminished by less capacity — it is completed by it. Every blessed soul is a vessel that is exactly full.

The second question: can a broken vow be compensated by other good works, so that the soul does not lose its standing before God? Beatrice is careful here. A vow is the freest possible act — it is the gift of the free will itself to God. Its substance (the will given) cannot be undone. Its form (the specific work promised) can sometimes be changed by church authority, but only with care and only when the substitution is proportionate. Piccarda and Constance could not simply substitute other good works for their vows — the force used against them is the only partial mitigation.

Cacciaguida prophecies Dante's exile

Cacciaguida tells Dante directly what is coming: exile, the bitterness of another's bread, the steep stairs of another's house. He instructs Dante to make the Comedy public regardless of the cost.

Summary

Dante tells Cacciaguida what he has already heard from several shades — allusions to some great misfortune to come. He wants the plain truth, without evasion. Cacciaguida gives it. He sees, in the mind of God, the future: Dante will leave behind everything most dear. That is the first arrow of exile. He will know the taste of another man's bread — how salt it is — and how wearisome it is to climb and descend the stairs of another's house.

His first companions in exile will be foolish, evil, and ungrateful. Then alone, making his own party. His first refuge will be with the great Lombard lord, Cangrande della Scala, whose sign is the Eagle. With him Dante will see things that others will not believe until they happen. Then Cacciaguida names some others who will play roles in Dante's life.

The instruction: all of this is the future Dante must report. Let him shout the whole vision regardless of who scratches their head when they hear it. If his voice is bitter at the first taste, it will give life when well digested. The Comedy is a work of truth; truth hurts those whose lives are lies. Dante should not soften it.

Summary

Hebrews 8 performs a structural pivot. The author has spent three chapters establishing the nature of the christological priesthood through the Melchizedek typology. Now he shifts to the place where that priesthood operates — not the earthly tabernacle built according to the pattern Moses was shown, but the heavenly sanctuary of which the earthly was always a copy and shadow. The move from Melchizedek to the heavenly sanctuary deepens the argument: not only is the priest superior, but the sanctuary in which he ministers is superior. The logic is consistent with the epistle's overall pattern — every element of the old mediation is surpassed by its counterpart in the new.

The Jeremiah 31 citation is the longest Old Testament citation in the New Testament, and its length signals how much work the author wants it to do. By allowing the text of Jeremiah to speak at full length, the author avoids having to argue for the superiority of the new covenant — Jeremiah himself announced it. The specific elements of the new covenant in Jeremiah — laws written on hearts rather than stone, a new intimacy between God and the people, and the complete forgiveness of sins — are each elements that Hebrews will develop in the following chapters. The covenant is internalized, not merely prescribed; the relationship is direct, not merely institutional; and the sacrifice that accomplishes it is once and for all, not repeated.

Hebrews — chapter by chapter

All 13 chapters — from the Son above the angels to the closing benediction.

Hebrews is structured as one continuous argument with a brief practical closing. Chapters 1–7 establish the superiority of the Son as high priest over every previous mediator. Chapters 8–10 develop the new covenant, the heavenly sanctuary, and the once-for-all sacrifice. Chapters 11–12 are the great call to endurance through the cloud of witnesses. Chapter 13 is the closing exhortation and benediction. The warning passages (2:1–4, 6:4–8, 10:26–31, 12:25–29) cut through every section with concentrated severity.

Greater than angels, greater than Moses, the Melchizedek priesthood established.

The new covenant · ch. 8–10

The heavenly sanctuary, the once-for-all sacrifice, and the new covenant written on hearts.

Hebrews 8

Hebrews 8

The main point is announced and the new covenant is established through the longest Old Testament citation in the New Testament — Jeremiah's promise of laws written on hearts, not stone.

Appears: The Son · The Great High Priest Figure
Hebrews 9

Hebrews 9

The earthly tabernacle was always a shadow of the heavenly. Christ entered the true sanctuary once, with his own blood, obtaining not annual atonement but eternal redemption.

Appears: The Son · The Great High Priest Figure
Hebrews 10

Hebrews 10

The law was always a shadow. Christ's single sacrifice ends the need for repetition, and the warnings and exhortations that follow are among the most urgent in the epistle.

The call to perseverance · ch. 11–13

The great cloud of witnesses, the race set before us, and the closing benediction.

Hebrews 11

Hebrews 11

The great catalogue of faith runs from Abel to the unnamed sufferers who were sawn in two. All endured by trusting what they could not yet see — and none received the promise without us.

Appears: The Cloud of Witnesses · Moses
Hebrews 12

Hebrews 12

Look to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of faith. The discipline of suffering is evidence of sonship, and the final warning compares the unapproachable Sinai to the heavenly Jerusalem now accessible.

Appears: The Cloud of Witnesses · The Son · The Addressees
Hebrews 13

Hebrews 13

The great argument closes in practical instruction: hospitality, contentment, endurance, obedience to leaders — and a benediction naming Jesus as the great shepherd of the eternal covenant.

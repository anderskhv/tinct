Summary

The Rostovs go to the opera at Marya Dmitrievna's insistence — a kind of compensation for the disastrous visit to the Bolkonskys. Natasha dresses carefully and looks at herself in the mirror with something close to pleasure, then catches herself thinking of Andrei and the thought produces a sweetness mixed with ache. She would not be silly and frightened if he were here now, she thinks. She would simply embrace him.

The opera house is brilliant with lights and bare shoulders and the warm press of society in its finest clothes. Natasha sits in the box in a state of expectation she cannot fully explain — the opera has not yet started, nothing has happened, and yet something about the atmosphere makes the evening feel charged. She is aware of being looked at. She looks back.

A movement in the stalls below catches her eye. Hélène Bezukhova is in a neighboring box, splendid and lazy, her beautiful shoulders bare, her manner entirely self-possessed. And beside Hélène, speaking to her with the easy intimacy of family — a young man, very handsome, very confident, who looks up at the Rostov box in a way that is both careless and deliberate. Anatole Kuragin has arrived.

Deuteronomy 17

Israel's future king is permitted but strictly constrained: no multiplying horses, wives, silver, or gold—and he must write out the law himself and read it all the days of his life.

Summary

Chapter 17 opens with a prohibition of blemished sacrifices—any ox or sheep with a defect is an abomination to the Lord—and moves to the case of an Israelite who goes and serves other gods. The matter is investigated thoroughly: on the evidence of two or three witnesses the person is stoned to death. The hand of the witnesses shall be first against him. Then follows the appellate procedure for cases too difficult for local courts. You shall come to the Levitical priests and to the judge who is in office in those days and inquire; they shall declare the decision. You shall do according to what they declare and you shall not turn aside from what they tell you. The person who acts presumptuously, not obeying the priest or the judge, shall die.

The chapter's second half legislates the institution of kingship. When you enter the land and decide to set a king over you like all the nations around you, you may indeed set a king over you whom the Lord your God will choose. One from among your brothers you shall set as king over you; you may not put a foreigner over you. He shall not acquire many horses or cause the people to return to Egypt to get more horses, for the Lord has said, you shall never return that way again. He shall not acquire many wives, lest his heart turn away. He shall not acquire excessive silver and gold. And when he sits on the throne he shall write for himself a copy of this law in a book from before the Levitical priests. It shall be with him and he shall read in it all the days of his life, that he may learn to fear the Lord his God by keeping all the words of this law, that his heart may not be lifted up above his brothers. The king is under the law he must personally transcribe.

Summary

Chapter 7 opens as Israel stands on the edge of Canaan. When the Lord your God brings you into the land and clears away seven nations before you—Hittites, Girgashites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites—you must devote them to complete destruction. Make no covenant with them and show no mercy. Do not intermarry with them. Do not serve their gods, for that would be a snare to you. The command is totalizing. The altars are to be torn down, the pillars broken, the Asherim cut down, the carved images burned. Moses explains why: you are a people holy to the Lord your God; the Lord your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession out of all the peoples on the face of the earth.

The chapter then delivers one of the book's most striking theological claims. The choosing is not a reward for virtue or size: it was not because you were more in number than any other people that the Lord set his love on you and chose you, for you were the fewest of all peoples, but it is because the Lord loves you and is keeping the oath he swore to your fathers. Election is sovereign love, not merit. Moses then promises that faithfulness will bring military success—the Lord will deliver the nations before you and will throw them into great confusion—along with agricultural and physical blessing: no barrenness of person or livestock, no disease of Egypt. The nations will be cleared away little by little, not all at once, so the land does not become wild. The chapter closes with instructions to burn the silver and gold of the nations' idols, taking none of it for Israel would be a thing devoted to destruction, an abomination.

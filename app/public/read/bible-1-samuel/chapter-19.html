1 Samuel 19

Saul tells his servants and Jonathan to kill David. Jonathan defends David to his father — and then Saul tries again with a spear.

Summary

Chapter 19 documents Saul's first explicit order to kill David and the network of relationships — Jonathan, Michal, the Spirit itself — that repeatedly protects David from execution. Jonathan's intercession is successful in the short term: he presents David's case to his father, appealing to David's blameless service and the injustice of innocent bloodshed. Saul swears by the Lord that David will not be put to death. The reconciliation lasts through one more military campaign, in which David defeats the Philistines again. Then the evil spirit returns and Saul, raving in his house, reaches for the spear while David plays.

David escapes the spear and flees to his house. Saul sends men to watch it through the night and kill him at daybreak. Michal — Saul's own daughter, David's wife — saves him, letting him down through a window and covering his escape with a domestic deception: she places a household idol in the bed, covers it with garments, and puts goat's hair at its head, telling the messengers David is ill. By the time the deception is discovered, David is gone. He goes to Samuel at Ramah. Saul's efforts to apprehend him there fail in a remarkable way: each group of messengers he sends to Naioth encounters the company of prophets and falls into prophetic ecstasy themselves, unable to fulfil their mission. Saul himself goes, and he too falls under the spirit — stripping off his garments and lying naked all day and all night before Samuel. The proverb — is Saul also among the prophets? — is invoked a second time, now with a different weight.

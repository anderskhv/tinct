Chapter 29 — recovery at Moor House

Jane recovers slowly under Diana and Mary's care. She gives her name as Jane Elliott; she will not say where she has come from or why. St. John questions her closely but does not press.

Summary

Jane lies in bed for three days under Diana and Mary's care, drifting in and out of sleep. They feed her broth and milk; they sit with her; they ask nothing. On the fourth day she sits up. By the end of the first week she is well enough to come downstairs in a borrowed gown and join the sisters in the parlour. The Rivers household is small and warm: Diana, the elder sister, intellectual and warm-tempered; Mary, the younger, gentler and equally well-read; Hannah the servant; St. John the brother, the parson of the local chapel of ease at Morton.

She tells them, when they finally ask, that her name is Jane Elliott; that she was raised by an aunt who is now dead; that she has been employed as a governess; that she has recently lost her position under circumstances she cannot describe; that she has no money and no other living family. The story is true in essentials and false in particulars. Diana and Mary accept it without further questioning. St. John listens with closer attention. He tells her, in the same flat voice he uses for everything, that he reserves the right to learn the rest of her story when she is ready, and that in the meantime she may stay at Moor House until she has recovered her strength.

Jane settles in. She walks in the garden in the mornings; she reads German with Diana in the afternoons; she sits in the parlour in the evenings. The household has been quiet since the Rivers father died a few months earlier — the family has lost most of its small income through a financial reverse; Diana and Mary will have to go out as governesses themselves. Jane, for the first time since Lowood, has a household around her that is intelligent, kind, and on something close to her own intellectual level. She does not yet understand that this household is also, by the strangest coincidence, her own family.

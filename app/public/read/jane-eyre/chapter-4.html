Chapter 4 — Brocklehurst; Jane's first speech

A tall, dark, severe man arrives to interview Jane for Lowood. Mrs. Reed poisons his opinion of her. After he leaves, Jane tells her aunt the truth — for the first time, out loud.

Summary

Months pass. Christmas comes and goes; Jane is excluded from the festivities, kept apart from her cousins, allowed only the leftovers of company. In January, a long-promised visitor arrives. Mr. Brocklehurst is a tall, severe-looking clergyman, "a black pillar" in Jane's description — the proprietor and treasurer of Lowood Institution, a charity school for the orphaned daughters of clergymen. Mrs. Reed has been corresponding with him about taking Jane in. He examines Jane on the catechism. He asks where the wicked go after death; Jane says hell; he asks how she will avoid going there; Jane, who has been thinking, says she must keep in good health, and not die. Brocklehurst is not amused.

Mrs. Reed, before he leaves, draws him aside. She tells him Jane has a "tendency to deceit," that her one defect is "a deceitful nature," that the staff at Lowood must watch her closely and discipline her without indulgence. Brocklehurst nods gravely; Lowood is, he assures her, exactly the place to discipline such tendencies. The arrangement is made. Jane will leave for Lowood within the month. The slander has been planted; it will travel ahead of her.

When Brocklehurst is gone, Jane stands in the middle of the room and looks at her aunt. Something gathers; she does not entirely choose it. "I am not deceitful," she says, "if I were, I should say I loved you; but I declare I do not love you: I dislike you the worst of anybody in the world except John Reed." She tells Mrs. Reed she will never call her aunt again as long as she lives. She tells her she is bad, hard-hearted, deceitful. Mrs. Reed, who has spent ten years keeping this child silent, looks for the first time afraid. Jane, almost trembling, walks out of the room. The chapter ends with the discovery — by Jane, by the reader, by the novel — that this voice can be used out loud, and that using it changes things.

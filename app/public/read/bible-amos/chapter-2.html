Amos 2

The oracle sequence reaches Israel last, with an indictment twice as long as any other: they sell the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes.

Summary

Chapter 2 opens with two more oracles in the nations sequence before the trap closes. Moab is indicted for burning the bones of the king of Edom to lime — a desecration of the dead that crosses a line the ancient Near East recognised even across hostile borders. Judah is indicted not for a specific war crime but for rejecting the law of the LORD and not keeping his statutes, and being led astray by the lies that their fathers followed. This oracle stands out as slightly different in character from the others — more theological, less concrete — and some readers have taken it as a later insertion. Whether or not that is the case, it functions in the sequence as the penultimate step before Israel.

The Israel oracle lands with the full weight of the delayed conclusion. The crimes are not foreign war crimes but domestic economic crimes: the righteous sold for silver, the needy sold for a pair of shoes, the head of the poor trampled into the dust of the earth and the way of the afflicted turned aside. A man and his father go in to the same girl. Garments taken in pledge are laid beside every altar. The wine of those who have been fined is drunk in the house of their God. The LORD then rehearses the history of what he has done for Israel — destroyed the Amorites, brought them out of Egypt, led them through the wilderness, raised up prophets and Nazirites — and announces that all of it will now be pressed down. I will press you down in your place, as a cart full of sheaves presses down. The fast will not save the mighty, the mighty will not save himself, and the one who is stouthearted among the mighty will flee naked in that day.

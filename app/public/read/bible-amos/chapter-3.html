Amos 3

You alone I have known of all the families of the earth; therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities. Privilege becomes the ground of accountability.

Summary

The chapter opens with the first Hear this word address, directed explicitly at the people of Israel, whom the LORD has known from among all the families of the earth. The statement that follows is formally a reversal of what an audience might expect from election: because you alone I have known, therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities. The logic is that intimate relationship implies heightened accountability, not guaranteed protection. This move — election as ground of judgment rather than ground of exemption — will be repeated across the prophetic literature, but Amos states it with unusual directness. He does not soften it with a conditional.

The chapter then proceeds through a sequence of rhetorical questions on causation: do two walk together unless they have agreed? Does a lion roar when it has no prey? Does a bird fall into a snare when there is no trap? Each question establishes the logic of cause and effect, building to the assertion that when the LORD God has purposed something, he reveals it to his servants the prophets. The LORD has roared; the prophet cannot but speak. The oracle then turns to Samaria specifically: the mountains of Samaria, the tumults and oppressions within her, the storehouses that are filled with violence and robbery. The LORD will bring an adversary who will surround the land, strip its defences, and plunder the storehouses. What the wealthy have taken and stored will be taken from them. The fragment of the rescue — a leg or the tip of an ear recovered from the lion's mouth — is the prophet's signal that what survives of Israel will be a remnant barely recognisable as the thing it came from.

Judges 11

The outcast Jephthah is recalled to lead Gilead against Ammon, defeats them decisively, and then returns home to find that his vow has claimed his only child.

Summary

Jephthah the Gileadite is a mighty warrior, the son of Gilead by a prostitute. Gilead's wife also bears sons, and when they grow up they drive Jephthah out — you shall not inherit in our father's house, for you are the son of another woman. He goes to the land of Tob, where worthless fellows gather around him. When the Ammonites make war against Israel, the elders of Gilead go to fetch Jephthah from the land of Tob. If you bring me home to fight the Ammonites and the Lord gives them over to me, I will be your head, Jephthah says. They agree. Jephthah then sends messengers to the Ammonite king asking why they have come against him. The king says: because Israel took our land when they came out of Egypt. Jephthah responds with a lengthy diplomatic and theological argument: Israel did not take Moabite or Ammonite territory; they took only the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who had attacked them first; the Lord God of Israel gave that land to Israel. Three hundred years — why have you not recovered it in all that time?

The king does not listen. The Spirit of the Lord comes on Jephthah. He makes his vow: if you will give the Ammonites into my hand, then whoever comes out of the doors of my house to meet me when I return victorious shall be the Lord's, to be offered up as a burnt offering. He strikes the Ammonites with great slaughter from Aroer to Minnith — twenty cities — as far as Abel-keramim. The Ammonites are subdued. Jephthah returns to Mizpah to his house. His daughter comes out to meet him with timbrels and dancing — his only child; he has no son or daughter but her. He tears his clothes: alas, my daughter, you have brought me very low. I have opened my mouth to the Lord and I cannot take back my vow. She says: if you have opened your mouth to the Lord, do to me according to what has gone out of your mouth. Grant me two months to go and wander on the mountains and bewail my virginity with my companions. He grants it. She returns, and he does with her according to the vow he had made. It became a custom in Israel for the daughters of Israel to go year by year to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in the year.

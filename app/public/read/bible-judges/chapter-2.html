Summary

The chapter opens with the angel of the Lord travelling from Gilgal to Bochim and delivering an accusation before the assembled people of Israel. The Lord had commanded them to make no covenant with the inhabitants of the land and to break down their altars. They did not obey. Therefore the Lord declares he will not drive out the inhabitants before them — those nations will become snares and their gods a trap. The people weep and sacrifice at Bochim. Then the narrative notes the death of Joshua at the age of one hundred and ten, his burial on his own inheritance in the hill country of Ephraim, and the service that the people gave the Lord all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived him and who had seen all the great work the Lord had done for Israel.

Then the generation that knew those things died, and another generation arose who did not know the Lord or the work he had done. The editors articulate the cycle explicitly: the people did evil, served the Baals, abandoned the Lord, followed other gods, and the Lord's anger burned. He gave them into the hand of plunderers who plundered them. Whenever they marched out, the hand of the Lord was against them for evil. Yet whenever the Lord raised up judges for them, he was with the judge and saved them from their enemies all the days of the judge. And when the judge died, they would relapse and behave worse than their ancestors. The chapter closes with the Lord's explanation: the nations Joshua did not drive out will be left to test Israel, to teach war to those who had not known it.

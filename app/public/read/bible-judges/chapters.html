Judges — chapter by chapter

All 21 chapters — from the unfinished conquest to the horror of Gibeah.

Judges is structured in three movements. Chapters 1–2 are the frame: the unfinished conquest and the editors' theological explanation of what follows. Chapters 3–16 are the cycle of judges: six major narratives and six minor figures, each in the repeating pattern of sin, oppression, cry, deliverance, peace. Chapters 17–21 are the two appendices — the migration of Dan and the Levite's concubine — which show what the cycle has produced when it finally runs out of judges altogether.

Introduction (Ch. 1–2)

The unfinished conquest. The editors lay out the pattern the whole book will follow.

Judges 1

Judges 1

The tribes move to inherit their portions after Joshua's death. Judah succeeds in the south; others accommodate rather than displace the Canaanites.

Appears: Deborah · Gideon · Jephthah · Samson · Jael
Judges 2

Judges 2

The book's theological thesis is stated explicitly. A generation arises that does not know the Lord, and the cycle of apostasy, oppression, and deliverance begins.

Appears: Deborah · Gideon · Jephthah · Samson

The Cycle of Judges (Ch. 3–16)

Six major deliverers and six minor ones. Each cycle darker than the last.

Judges 3

Judges 3

Othniel establishes the judge-cycle pattern. Ehud assassinates the Moabite king Eglon in a scene of notable narrative vividness. Shamgar slays six hundred Philistines.

Appears: Gideon · Jephthah · Samson
Judges 4

Judges 4

Deborah judges and prophesies. Barak defeats Sisera at the Kishon. Jael kills the fleeing Sisera in her tent, fulfilling Deborah's prophecy about the hand of a woman.

Appears: Deborah · Jael
Judges 5

Judges 5

Deborah and Barak sing. The song is archaic, fierce, and historically precious. It ends with Sisera's mother watching for a son who will not come home.

Appears: Deborah · Jael
Judges 6

Judges 6

The Midianites have stripped the land for seven years. Gideon is called while hiding in a winepress, argues with the angel, demolishes his father's Baal altar, and tests God twice with a fleece.

Appears: Gideon
Judges 7

Judges 7

Gideon's army is reduced to three hundred men by divine instruction. A night attack with torches, clay jars, and trumpets throws the Midianite camp into fatal confusion.

Appears: Gideon
Judges 8

Judges 8

Gideon captures and kills the Midianite kings, punishes towns that refused him help, refuses the crown but makes an ephod that becomes an idol. He fathers Abimelech and dies.

Appears: Gideon
Judges 9

Judges 9

Abimelech seizes power through massacre and mercenaries. The parable of the trees is spoken from Mount Gerizim. Three years later Abimelech dies under a millstone thrown by a woman.

Appears: Gideon
Judges 10

Judges 10

Two minor judges serve. Israel serves seven foreign gods; the Lord refuses to deliver them and lists all the times he already has. Israel repents, and Gilead looks for a commander to face Ammon.

Appears: Jephthah
Judges 11

Judges 11

Jephthah, expelled as a youth, returns as commander. He defeats the Ammonites but must fulfill a vow that costs him his only daughter. She is mourned annually by the daughters of Israel.

Appears: Jephthah
Judges 12

Judges 12

Jephthah defeats the Ephraimites at the Jordan fords, using dialect to identify and kill forty-two thousand. Three minor judges — Ibzan, Elon, Abdon — serve and die.

Appears: Jephthah
Judges 13

Judges 13

A barren woman is visited by the angel of the Lord and told she will bear a son to be a Nazirite from the womb. Samson is born; the Spirit of the Lord begins to stir him.

Appears: Samson
Judges 14

Judges 14

Samson kills a lion barehanded, finds honey in its carcass, poses a riddle at his wedding. His Philistine bride tells the answer under pressure. Samson kills thirty men of Ashkelon and returns home.

Appears: Samson
Judges 15

Judges 15

Samson burns Philistine crops with foxes and torches. His own tribe hands him over to the Philistines to avoid reprisals. He breaks free and kills a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone.

Appears: Samson
Judges 16

Judges 16

Samson tells Delilah the secret of his Nazirite hair. Captured, blinded, and imprisoned in Gaza, he pulls down the temple of Dagon, dying with three thousand Philistines.

Appears: Samson

Appendices (Ch. 17–21)

What happens when the cycle runs out. The migration of Dan. The Levite's concubine.

Judges 17

Judges 17

A private shrine, household idols, and a hired Levite priest. Micah believes the Lord will bless him for the arrangement. The editors' verdict is in the chapter's closing line.

Appears: The Levite's Concubine
Judges 18

Judges 18

The Danites, unable to hold their territory, spy out Laish in the far north, steal Micah's priestly apparatus, and migrate. They burn Laish, rebuild it as Dan, and establish an unauthorized shrine.

Appears: The Levite's Concubine
Judges 19

Judges 19

A Levite and his concubine stop at Gibeah in Benjamin. The men of the city commit violence against the concubine. She dies at the threshold. The Levite calls all Israel to account.

Appears: The Levite's Concubine
Judges 20

Judges 20

Four hundred thousand Israelites assemble against Gibeah. Benjamin refuses to surrender the perpetrators. A three-day civil war nearly annihilates the tribe. Six hundred men escape to the rock of Rimmon.

Appears: The Levite's Concubine
Judges 21

Judges 21

Israel grieves for Benjamin and procures wives for the six hundred survivors by killing Jabesh-gilead and seizing virgins from Shiloh. The book closes: there was no king in Israel.

Appears: The Levite's Concubine

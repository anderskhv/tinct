Job — chapter by chapter

All 42 chapters — from the wager in heaven to the restored ending that refuses to be tidy.

Job moves in five large arcs. The prose prologue (chapters 1–2) sets up the wager. The poetic dialogue (3–27) runs in three rounds of debate. The wisdom hymn (28) stands alone. Job's long oath of innocence (29–31) closes his case. Elihu's interruption (32–37) fills the silence before God speaks. The Voice from the Whirlwind (38–41) is the climax. Then the epilogue (42) restores Job's fortunes in a manner the poem has made impossible to read without complication.

Wisdom hymn

Where is wisdom to be found? A poem that stands apart from the dialogue.

Job 28

Job's monologue and oath

Job remembers what he was, surveys what he has become, and swears a detailed oath of innocence.

Job 29

Job 30

Job 30

Job contrasts his former dignity with his present degradation: mocked by outcasts, targeted by God, crying out without answer, disfigured, cast into the mire.

Job 31

Job 31

Job swears a comprehensive oath of innocence covering lust, falsehood, treatment of servants and the poor, trust in wealth, and sexual integrity — a complete moral inventory.

Elihu speeches

A young man who held his peace now speaks — six chapters before the whirlwind arrives.

Job 32

Job 32

Elihu the Buzite, who has been silently present, erupts in anger at both Job's self-justification and the friends' failed theodicy, and announces he will finally speak.

Job 33

Appears: Elihu · Job
Job 34

Job 34

Elihu defends absolute divine justice by appeal to God's role as universal creator and sustainer, and condemns Job's complaint as rebellion multiplied on sin.

Appears: Elihu · Job · The Three Friends
Job 35

Job 35

Job 36

Job 36

The Voice from the Whirlwind

God speaks for four chapters about the wild creation. Job answers twice and lays his hand upon his mouth.

Job 38

Job 38

The LORD speaks from the whirlwind with the first barrage of creation questions: the foundations, the morning stars, the sea's birth, the dawn — where was Job for any of this?

Appears: The Voice from the Whirlwind · Job
Job 39

Job 39

God catalogs the wild creatures — mountain goat, wild ass, wild ox, ostrich, war horse, eagle — none serving human purposes, all governed by God alone.

Appears: The Voice from the Whirlwind · Job
Job 40

Job 40

Job silences himself before God's first speech, then God launches a second, presenting Behemoth — made as Job was made, but ungovernable — as testimony to divine creation beyond human control.

Appears: The Voice from the Whirlwind · Job
Job 41

Job 41

God's whirlwind speech reaches its climax with Leviathan, the sea-dragon: fire-breathing, armored, beyond all human power to approach, king over all the sons of pride.

Appears: The Voice from the Whirlwind · Job

Epilogue

Job's final word; the friends rebuked; the restoration that resolves the plot without settling the question.

Job 42

Job 42

Job's second response acknowledges God's unsearchable power; the friends are rebuked and forgiven through Job; Job is restored with twice his former wealth and three named daughters.

Job 31

Job delivers his closing argument: a sworn itemization of every moral obligation he has met, daring God to bring any charge that will stand.

Summary

Chapter 31 is a judicial document. Job proceeds through the categories of a full moral inventory, swearing conditional curses: if I did this, then let that befall me. The categories are extensive. He has made a covenant with his eyes not to look with desire at a young woman. He has not walked in vanity or hasted to deceit. He has not rejected the complaint of his servant or maidservant when they contended with him, knowing that the same God who made him also made them. He has not withheld the desires of the poor, nor caused the eyes of the widow to fail, nor eaten his food alone without sharing it with orphans, nor seen anyone perish for lack of clothing or the poor without covering. He has not lifted up his hand against the fatherless when he saw his own help in the gate.

The list continues through matters of sexual fidelity — he has not been enticed by his neighbour's wife, or lay in wait at the neighbor's door — through matters of civic honesty, through care of his workers and his land, through enemy relations — he has not rejoiced at the destruction of his enemy or his heart lifted up at his hurt. He has not concealed his transgressions as Adam did. He has not mistreated the land or its workers. And then the peroration: O that one would hear me! Behold, my desire is that the Almighty would answer me, and that mine adversary had written a book. Surely I would take it upon my shoulder and bind it as a crown to me. He invites the indictment. He will wear it. The oath is finished; the words of Job are ended.

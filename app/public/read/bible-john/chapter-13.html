John 13

On the night before his death Jesus kneels and washes his disciples' feet, gives the new commandment to love one another, and identifies his betrayer.

Summary

The setting is the supper before the Passover, and John frames the scene with two statements: Jesus knows his hour has come to depart from this world to the Father, and he loves his own to the end (or to the uttermost — the Greek eis telos carries both meanings). Knowing that the Father has given all things into his hands, and that he came from God and is going back to God, he gets up from supper, lays aside his outer garments, takes a towel, pours water into a basin, and begins to wash his disciples' feet, wiping them with the towel. When he comes to Peter, Peter says Lord, do you wash my feet? Jesus says you do not understand now, but you will afterwards. Peter refuses; Jesus says unless I wash you, you have no share with me. Peter overcorrects and asks to be washed entirely; Jesus says the one who has bathed does not need to wash, except for his feet, but is completely clean. He adds: not all of you are clean — referring to the betrayer.

Back at the table, Jesus explains: do you understand what I have done? You call me Teacher and Lord, and you are right. If I then have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. I have given you an example. He says the servant is not greater than his master, nor is the messenger greater than the one who sent him. He quotes Psalm 41 about the betrayer, then tells them in advance so that when it happens they will believe. Troubled in spirit, he says one of you will betray me. Questioning looks pass between the disciples. Peter signals the Beloved Disciple to ask who it is; he asks; Jesus says it is the one to whom I give this morsel. He gives it to Judas Iscariot. Satan enters into Judas; Jesus tells him what he is doing, do it quickly. Judas leaves into the night. The chapter closes with the new commandment: a new commandment I give to you, that you love one another just as I have loved you. By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another. Peter asks where Jesus is going; Jesus says he cannot follow now but will follow later; Peter insists he will lay down his life; Jesus says the cock will not crow before Peter has denied him three times.

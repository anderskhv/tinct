John 20

Mary Magdalene in the garden hears her name spoken and recognizes the risen Jesus — then Thomas, eight days later, touches the wounds and says my Lord and my God.

Summary

Mary Magdalene comes to the tomb on the first day of the week while it is still dark and finds the stone removed. She runs to Simon Peter and the Beloved Disciple: they have taken the Lord out of the tomb and we do not know where they have laid him. The two disciples run; the Beloved Disciple reaches the tomb first and looks in; Peter enters first and sees the linen cloths lying there and the face cloth rolled up in a place by itself. The Beloved Disciple enters, sees, and believes. They go home. Mary stands weeping outside; she stoops to look in and sees two angels in white sitting where the body had been, at the head and at the feet. Why are you weeping? They have taken my Lord and I do not know where they have laid him. She turns and sees Jesus, not recognizing him. Why are you weeping? Whom are you seeking? Supposing him to be the gardener, she says if you carried him away, tell me where you have laid him. Jesus says Mary. She says Rabboni. He tells her not to cling to him because he has not yet ascended, and sends her to tell the disciples.

That evening, on the first day of the week, the disciples are gathered behind locked doors for fear of the authorities. Jesus comes and stands among them and says peace be with you. He shows them his hands and his side; they are glad. As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you. He breathes on them: receive the Holy Spirit. Whoever's sins you forgive, they are forgiven; whoever's sins you retain, they are retained. Thomas, one of the Twelve, is not present; he refuses to believe the report: unless I see in his hands the mark of the nails and place my finger into the mark of the nails and place my hand into his side, I will never believe. Eight days later the disciples are inside again, and Thomas with them; Jesus comes through locked doors and stands among them. He addresses Thomas: put your finger here, and see my hands; put out your hand and place it in my side. Do not disbelieve, but believe. Thomas says my Lord and my God. Jesus says: because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed. The chapter closes with the purpose statement: these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name.

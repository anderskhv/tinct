John 3 of 21

John 3

A Pharisee comes to Jesus secretly at night and hears the most quoted verse in the gospel — and perhaps in the whole New Testament.

Summary

Nicodemus is a Pharisee and a member of the ruling council who comes to Jesus at night and opens with an acknowledgment: Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs unless God is with him. Jesus does not respond to the compliment but immediately raises the stakes: unless one is born again — or born from above — one cannot see the kingdom of God. Nicodemus takes it literally: how can a man be born when he is old? Jesus's answer distinguishes flesh and spirit: that which is born of the flesh is flesh; that which is born of the Spirit is spirit. The Spirit is like the wind — you hear its sound but do not know where it comes from or where it goes. Nicodemus asks how this can be, and Jesus gently reproaches him: you are the teacher of Israel and you do not understand these things?

The discourse opens wider. As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness — the bronze serpent of Numbers 21 — so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. Then verse 16, followed by its paired verses: God did not send the Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him. Whoever believes is not condemned; whoever does not believe is condemned already. The light has come into the world and people loved the darkness rather than the light, because their works were evil. The chapter closes with the Baptist's final testimony. He is the best man at a wedding, not the groom. He who comes from above is above all. The one who has received his testimony has set his seal to this, that God is true.

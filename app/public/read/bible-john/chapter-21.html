Summary

Seven disciples go night-fishing on the Sea of Tiberias and catch nothing. At daybreak a figure on the shore calls to them and tells them to cast the net on the right side of the boat; they do, and they cannot haul it in for the quantity of fish. The Beloved Disciple says to Peter: it is the Lord. Peter, hearing this, puts on his outer garment and jumps into the sea; the other disciples come in the boat, dragging the net, about a hundred yards from shore. On land there is a charcoal fire with fish on it and bread. Jesus says bring some of the fish you have caught. Peter hauls the net ashore; it holds a hundred and fifty-three large fish, and the net does not tear. Come and have breakfast, Jesus says. No one dares ask who he is; they know it is the Lord. He takes the bread and gives it to them, and likewise the fish.

After the meal Jesus asks Peter three questions, each one Simon, son of John, do you love me? The word Jesus uses in the first two questions is the Greek agapē; Peter answers each time with phileō, the word for friendship. The third time Jesus uses Peter's word; Peter is grieved because he asked a third time. Three times Peter answers yes; three times Jesus assigns a pastoral commission: feed my lambs, tend my sheep, feed my sheep. Jesus then says that when Peter was young he dressed himself and went where he wanted; when he is old he will stretch out his hands and another will dress him and carry him where he does not want to go. This he said to show by what kind of death Peter would glorify God. Follow me. Peter turns and sees the Beloved Disciple following and asks what will happen to him; Jesus says if it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? The rumour spread that this disciple would not die; Jesus had not said that. The chapter ends: this is the disciple who is bearing witness to these things, and who has written these things, and we know that his testimony is true. There are also many other things that Jesus did; were every one of them written, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written.

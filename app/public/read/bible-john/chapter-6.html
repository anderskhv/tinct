John 6

Five loaves feed five thousand, Jesus walks on water, and then the hardest discourse in John: I am the bread of life — my flesh is real food, my blood is real drink.

Summary

The fourth sign begins with a test: Jesus asks Philip where they are to buy bread for the crowd that has gathered around him, knowing what he is about to do. Andrew mentions a boy with five barley loaves and two fish, but what are they among so many? Jesus has the crowd of five thousand sit down. He gives thanks, distributes the loaves and fish, and everyone eats until they are satisfied; twelve baskets of fragments are gathered. The crowd wants to make him king; he withdraws alone to the mountain. That evening the disciples row across to Capernaum in the dark. Jesus comes toward them walking on the sea; they are frightened; he says I am he, do not be afraid, and they take him into the boat, which immediately reaches the shore. The next day the crowd follows to Capernaum and asks when he arrived.

The bread-of-life discourse occupies most of the chapter and is built in stages. You seek me not because you saw signs but because you ate your fill of the loaves. Do not work for food that perishes but for the food that endures to eternal life. The crowd asks what they must do to do the works of God; Jesus says to believe in the one God has sent. They ask for a sign — Moses gave manna in the wilderness. Jesus corrects the reference: Moses did not give the bread from heaven; my Father gives the true bread from heaven, the bread that gives life to the world. I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me shall not hunger; whoever believes in me shall not thirst. The discourse intensifies: I am the living bread that came down from heaven. The bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh. This causes a dispute — how can this man give us his flesh to eat? — and Jesus answers with the language that has driven eucharistic interpretation across twenty centuries: unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you. Many disciples call it a hard saying and stop following him. Jesus asks the Twelve if they want to leave too. Peter says Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.

John — chapter by chapter

All 21 chapters — from the Word in eternity to breakfast on the beach.

John falls into three movements: the Book of Signs (chapters 1–12), in which Jesus performs seven signs and the public controversy escalates; the Book of Glory (chapters 13–20), which begins with the foot-washing and ends with Thomas's confession; and a closing epilogue (chapter 21) on the beach at Tiberias. Twenty-one chapters total.

Book of Signs · Chapters 1–12

The prologue, the seven signs, the public ministry, and the rising controversy.

John 1

John 1

John's opening frames the entire gospel in two movements: the high theology of the prologue — the Word made flesh — and the quiet, personal scene of the first disciples being called with the words 'come and see.'

Appears: Jesus · John the Baptist · Andrew · Peter · Philip
John 2

John 2

The first sign — water into wine at a Galilean wedding — is followed immediately by the temple cleansing in Jerusalem: two scenes that together open the public ministry with a quiet miracle and a public confrontation.

Appears: Jesus · Mary the Mother
John 3

John 3

Nicodemus comes at night for a secret conversation that becomes one of John's deepest theological passages — ending with 3:16, the gospel's most famous sentence, and the Baptist's last testimony.

Appears: Jesus · Nicodemus · John the Baptist
John 4

John 4

The conversation at Jacob's well — Jesus and a Samaritan woman, alone, about living water, five husbands, and the true place of worship — is the longest single conversation in any of the four gospels.

Appears: Jesus · The Samaritan Woman
John 5

John 5

A Sabbath healing at the pool of Bethesda triggers the first major confrontation of the gospel and the first extended discourse on the relationship of the Son to the Father — judgment, resurrection, and the voice of the living God.

Appears: Jesus
John 6

John 6

The feeding of five thousand launches John's most demanding chapter: the bread-of-life discourse moves from sign to sacrament, from feeding to eating his flesh and drinking his blood, and many disciples walk away.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · Philip · Andrew
John 7

John 7

At the Feast of Tabernacles in Jerusalem, Jesus teaches in the temple, divides the crowd into factions, and delivers the living-water saying about the Spirit — while the officers sent to arrest him return without him.

Appears: Jesus · Nicodemus
John 8

John 8

The escalating controversy of chapter 8 moves from the woman taken in adultery through the light-of-the-world discourse to the most explosive claim in the gospel: before Abraham was, I am.

Appears: Jesus
John 9

John 9

A man blind from birth receives sight — the fifth sign — and the investigation that follows is John's most carefully plotted chapter: the healed man's testimony grows clearer and the religious authorities' vision grows darker.

Appears: Jesus
John 10

John 10

The good-shepherd discourse brings two I am sayings, the note of sacrificial death, and the climactic declaration that the Father and Son are one — which nearly gets Jesus stoned on the spot.

Appears: Jesus
John 11

John 11

Lazarus raised from the tomb after four days is the climax of the public ministry — preceded by the I am the resurrection and the life declaration and followed by the decision of the Sanhedrin to have Jesus killed.

Appears: Jesus · Lazarus · Martha · Mary of Bethany · Thomas
John 12

John 12

The anointing at Bethany, the triumphal entry into Jerusalem, and the announcement that the hour has arrived close the Book of Signs — the Son of Man must be lifted up, and the grain of wheat must die.

Appears: Jesus · Mary of Bethany · Lazarus · Judas Iscariot · Philip

Book of Glory · Chapters 13–20

The foot-washing, the Farewell Discourse, the passion, and the resurrection.

John 13

John 13

The last supper in John has no bread and cup — instead Jesus kneels and washes his disciples' feet, identifies Judas as the betrayer, and gives the new commandment: love one another as I have loved you.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Beloved Disciple · Judas Iscariot
John 14

John 14

The opening of the Farewell Discourse promises many rooms in the Father's house, delivers the I am the way saying, and introduces the Paraclete — the Spirit of truth who will come to the disciples when Jesus is gone.

Appears: Jesus · Thomas · Philip
John 15

John 15

The vine and branches discourse — the seventh and last I am saying — calls the disciples to abide in Jesus and redefines them as friends rather than servants; then comes the sober warning that the world that hated him will hate them.

Appears: Jesus
John 16

John 16

It is better that Jesus leaves — because only then will the Advocate come to convict the world. The disciples' imminent sorrow will turn to a joy no one can take from them; and in the world they will have tribulation, but he has overcome the world.

Appears: Jesus
John 17

John 17

The high-priestly prayer — Jesus praying aloud for himself, his disciples, and all future believers — is the most intimate and comprehensive prayer in any gospel, ending with the petition that all may be one.

Appears: Jesus
John 18

John 18

The arrest, Peter's three denials, and the first Roman trial — with Pilate asking what is truth — open the passion narrative in a gospel that frames the entire sequence as Jesus remaining in control throughout.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · Judas Iscariot · The Beloved Disciple · Pilate
John 19

John 19

The scourging and crowning with thorns, Pilate's refusal to change the inscription, the crucifixion, the entrusting of his mother to the Beloved Disciple, and the burial — John narrates the passion with careful, witnessing restraint.

Appears: Jesus · Pilate · Mary the Mother · Mary Magdalene · The Beloved Disciple
John 20

John 20

Mary Magdalene becomes the first witness to the resurrection in the garden; Thomas, eight days later, moves from refusal to the gospel's highest confession — my Lord and my God — and John states his reason for writing.

Appears: Jesus · Mary Magdalene · Peter · The Beloved Disciple · Thomas

Epilogue · Chapter 21

A morning on the lake and Peter restored.

John 21

John 21

The gospel ends on a beach at dawn: a miraculous catch of fish, breakfast cooked by the risen Jesus, and Peter asked three times if he loves him — one question for each denial — before the final testimony of the Beloved Disciple.

Appears: Jesus · Peter · The Beloved Disciple · Thomas · Nathanael

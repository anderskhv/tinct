Jeremiah — chapter by chapter

All 52 chapters, from the call to the fall and its aftermath.

Jeremiah is not strictly chronological — oracles from different reigns are grouped thematically rather than in sequence. The book falls into four broad movements: the oracles of judgment against Judah in chapters 1–25; the biographical narratives of the prophet's confrontations, imprisonments, and the fall of Jerusalem in chapters 26–45; the oracles against foreign nations in chapters 46–51; and the historical appendix in chapter 52, nearly identical to 2 Kings 24–25, which closes the book with the destruction of Jerusalem and the eventual release of King Jehoiachin from Babylonian prison.

The Call and Early Oracles · chs 1–25

The commission, the indictments of Judah, and the confessions.

Jeremiah 1

Jeremiah 1

God calls a boy who cannot speak to be an iron pillar against kings. The almond branch and boiling pot frame a forty-year mission that cannot be refused.

Jeremiah 2

Jeremiah 2

Israel has swapped a spring of living water for cracked cisterns. The first great indictment traces the full arc of the people's apostasy from wilderness to the present.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 3

Jeremiah 3

God calls faithless Israel back with the legal right to refuse her — and chooses not to invoke it. Mercy overrides statute; but the call goes unanswered.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 4

Jeremiah 4

The destroyer of nations moves south. Jeremiah watches Judah's coming catastrophe as an uncreating of the world — tohu wa-bohu, without form and void, again.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 5

Jeremiah 5

God challenges Jeremiah to find one honest person in Jerusalem. The search fails at every level of society. Even the oaths are lies.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 6

Jeremiah 6

God shows the people the ancient paths and the good way. They refuse. Jeremiah is full of wrath he can barely contain — and the army is already on the way.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 7

Jeremiah 7

Jeremiah stands at the temple gate and tells the crowd that the temple is not a magic charm. It will become like Shiloh — a ruin — if the covenant is not kept.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 8

Jeremiah 8

Is there no balm in Gilead? The chapter circles grief: even migratory birds know their season, but Judah does not know her God. The wound will not close.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 9

Jeremiah 9

Jeremiah wishes he could flee to the desert. God calls for professional mourners. The final word: the only boast worth having is knowing a God who delights in justice.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 10

Jeremiah 10

An idol is a scarecrow bolted to its stand. The living God needs no fastening. Jeremiah ends the chapter with a private prayer: correct me, but not in anger.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 11

Jeremiah 11

The covenant has been broken for generations. Then a smaller shock: Jeremiah's own townspeople of Anathoth plot to silence him. The first confession begins.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah
Jeremiah 12

Jeremiah 12

The second confession brings the oldest complaint: the wicked prosper while the faithful suffer. God's answer: you have not yet seen how hard this gets.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 13

Jeremiah 13

A linen belt hidden by the Euphrates returns ruined and good for nothing. That is what Judah has become — once intimate with God, now spoiled beyond use.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 14

Jeremiah 14

Drought devastates the land. Jeremiah prays for the people; God tells him not to. The false prophets promise peace. God says they are lying and will die by sword and famine.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 15

Jeremiah 15

The third confession accuses God of being a deceptive brook — waters that fail. God's answer is not comfort but a recommissioning: return to me, and you will stand before me.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 16

Jeremiah 16

Jeremiah is forbidden to marry or mourn. His celibacy is not personal preference — it is a public sign that this generation has no future in the land.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 17

Jeremiah 17

The heart is deceitful above all things. The blessed person trusts God like a tree by water. Jeremiah asks to be healed and disclaims any desire for the disaster he has prophesied.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 18

Jeremiah 18

The potter remakes a marred vessel. God can reshape any nation — including Israel. The fifth confession follows: Jeremiah's neighbors plot against him, and he calls for judgment.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 19

Jeremiah 19

A fired clay jar shattered in the valley of Hinnom — unlike soft clay, the shards cannot be rejoined. God will break this city in the same way, beyond mending.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 20

Jeremiah 20

The sixth and seventh confessions: Jeremiah accuses God of deception, then curses the day he was born. The most psychologically extreme passage in any prophetic book.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 21

Jeremiah 21

King Zedekiah asks Jeremiah for a miracle during the Babylonian siege. The prophet's answer: God himself will fight against Jerusalem. Surrender now, or die.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah
Jeremiah 22

Jeremiah 22

Three kings of Judah are indicted in sequence: Jehoahaz will die in exile, Jehoiakim will be buried like a donkey, Jehoiachin is to be written childless. The cedar palace is not kingship.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah
Jeremiah 23

Jeremiah 23

A righteous Branch for David is promised — the Lord is our righteousness. Then the longest polemic against false prophets in the Hebrew Bible: they have not stood in the divine council.

Appears: Jeremiah · Hananiah ben Azzur
Jeremiah 24

Jeremiah 24

Two baskets of figs: the good figs are the Babylonian exiles, whom God will restore with a new heart; the bad figs are those who remained in Jerusalem or fled to Egypt.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 25

Jeremiah 25

Twenty-three years of unheeded prophecy culminates in the number seventy. Then Jeremiah passes God's cup of wrath to every nation on earth, from Jerusalem to the ends of the world.

Appears: Jeremiah · Nebuchadnezzar

Narratives and the Book of Consolation · chs 26–45

The temple sermon aftermath, Hananiah, the new covenant, the siege and fall.

Jeremiah 26

Jeremiah 26

The temple sermon nearly costs Jeremiah his life. He is saved by elders citing the prophet Micah. But another prophet — Uriah — said the same things and was killed. The acquittal is not a triumph.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah
Jeremiah 27

Jeremiah 27

Jeremiah wears a wooden yoke through the streets of Jerusalem, commanding submission to Babylon and warning that false prophets promise peace where there is none.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 28

Jeremiah 28

Hananiah breaks Jeremiah's wooden yoke in the temple and predicts two years to Babylonian defeat; Jeremiah predicts an iron yoke instead, and Hananiah dies within two months.

Appears: Jeremiah · Hananiah ben Azzur · Zedekiah
Jeremiah 29

Jeremiah 29

Jeremiah's letter to the Babylonian exiles commands them to settle in, build homes, pray for Babylon's welfare, and wait seventy years — not the brief return false prophets are promising.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 30

Jeremiah 30

God commands Jeremiah to write his words in a book, and promises restoration after catastrophe — the time of Jacob's trouble will pass, and God himself will heal what no physician can.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 31

Jeremiah 31

The new covenant oracle of chapter 31 — God's law written on hearts, all knowing him from least to greatest, sins forgiven and forgotten — is the source of the phrase New Testament itself.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 32

Jeremiah 32

With Babylon at the walls and himself under arrest, Jeremiah legally purchases his cousin's field at Anathoth — a silver-weighted declaration that there will be a future in the land.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 33

Jeremiah 33

From his prison in the besieged city, Jeremiah receives oracles of a rebuilt Jerusalem, a righteous Davidic ruler, and an eternal Levitical priesthood — as unbreakable as the laws of day and night.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 34

Jeremiah 34

The nobles of Jerusalem free their Hebrew slaves under siege-pressure, then re-enslave them when the army briefly withdraws — and God responds by releasing them to sword, pestilence, and famine.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 35

Jeremiah 35

The Recabites refuse Jeremiah's wine because their ancestor commanded it centuries ago; God cites their faithfulness as an indictment of Judah, which has refused to hear God's own repeated commands.

Appears: Jeremiah · The Recabites
Jeremiah 36

Jeremiah 36

King Jehoiakim burns Jeremiah's scroll column by column in his winter brazier; God tells Jeremiah to dictate it all again, with additions — and the king's name goes onto the list of the condemned.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah
Jeremiah 37

Jeremiah 37

Jeremiah is arrested leaving Jerusalem during a temporary lull in the siege, beaten, and thrown into a dungeon cistern; Zedekiah secretly asks for a prophecy and then moves the prophet to slightly better confinement.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah
Jeremiah 38

Jeremiah 38

Jeremiah is lowered into a muddy cistern to die; an Ethiopian palace servant rescues him with rags and ropes; Zedekiah asks privately for the truth, receives it, and cannot act on it.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah
Jeremiah 39

Jeremiah 39

After eighteen months of siege, Babylon breaches Jerusalem's walls in 587 BCE; Zedekiah watches his sons die, is blinded, and is taken to Babylon; Jeremiah is freed and entrusted to Gedaliah.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 40

Jeremiah 40

Jeremiah chooses to stay in the ruined land with Gedaliah, the Babylonian-appointed governor, as Jewish refugees return from neighboring countries and gather an autumn harvest — but an assassination plot is forming.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 41

Jeremiah 41

Ishmael assassinates Gedaliah, massacres eighty pilgrims, takes Mizpah's population captive toward Ammon; Johanan overtakes him and frees the captives — but Ishmael escapes, leaving chaos and fear.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 42

Jeremiah 42

The frightened remnant solemnly asks Jeremiah whether to flee to Egypt; God answers with unusual directness: stay in the land, fear not — but if you go to Egypt, the sword you fear will meet you there.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 43

Jeremiah 43

The remnant leaders accuse Jeremiah of lying on Baruch's behalf, take the entire community to Egypt against God's command — and Jeremiah buries stones at Pharaoh's gate, announcing Babylon will come here too.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah
Jeremiah 44

Jeremiah 44

The Judahite community in Egypt refuses Jeremiah's warning and argues that things went better when they worshipped the queen of heaven — his last recorded speech predicts their destruction in the land of their refuge.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah
Jeremiah 45

Jeremiah 45

The only oracle in the Hebrew Bible addressed solely to a prophet's scribe — God tells Baruch his grief is understood, but great things are not to be sought in a time of universal disaster; his life is his prize.

Appears: Baruch ben Neriah · Jeremiah

Oracles Against the Nations · chs 46–51

Egypt, Philistia, Moab, Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar, Elam, Babylon.

Jeremiah 46

Jeremiah 46

The nations-oracle collection opens with Egypt — a vivid dramatization of the Carchemish defeat in 605 BCE and a longer oracle of Nebuchadnezzar's coming invasion — ending with comfort for Israel: do not fear.

Appears: Jeremiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 47

Jeremiah 47

The Philistines face waters rising from the north that no army can stop — Gaza shaves in mourning, Ashkelon falls silent, and the sword of the Lord will not return to its sheath until the work is done.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 48

Jeremiah 48

The longest nations oracle in Jeremiah moves city by city through Moab's destruction — pride, complacency, and Chemosh all condemned — ending with God's own lament over the destruction, and a distant promise of restoration.

Appears: Jeremiah
Jeremiah 49

Jeremiah 49

Five nations — Ammon, Edom, Damascus, the Arabian tribes, and Elam — receive oracles of judgment that catalog pride and hostility; Edom faces the most severe indictment, and Ammon and Elam the promise of eventual restoration.

Appears: Jeremiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 50

Jeremiah 50

The great oracle against Babylon opens: Bel is shamed, Marduk dismayed, Israel will return to Zion; the hammer of all the earth will itself be shattered because it rejoiced over Jerusalem's ruin without regret.

Appears: Jeremiah · Nebuchadnezzar
Jeremiah 51

Jeremiah 51

The Babylon oracle reaches its poetic peak — nations mobilize, walls fall, the idols shamed, God the maker of all things contrasted with carved wood — then Jeremiah's scroll of doom is sunk in the Euphrates to enact the prophecy.

Appears: Jeremiah · Baruch ben Neriah · Zedekiah

Historical Appendix · ch 52

The fall of Jerusalem retold; Jehoiachin released from Babylonian prison.

Jeremiah 52

Jeremiah 52

The historical appendix retells the fall of Jerusalem and the destruction of the temple in the words of 2 Kings — verified by catalog of plundered vessels and three deportation counts — and closes with Jehoiachin's quiet release from prison in Babylon.

Appears: Jeremiah · Zedekiah · Nebuchadnezzar

Scene 24 of 28

Country near Dunsinane — Birnam Wood

Malcolm gives the order that turns the witches' prophecy against Macbeth.

Summary

Open country in front of Birnam Wood. The army has arrived: Malcolm at the head, with old Siward and his son, Macduff, Menteith, Caithness, Angus, Lennox, and the rest of the lords, with drums and colours. Malcolm asks how near the day is when "chambers will be safe" — when houses in Scotland will once again be safe to sleep in. Menteith answers: very near.

Malcolm gives the order. "Let every soldier hew him down a bough and bear't before him: thereby shall we shadow the numbers of our host, and make discovery err in report of us." The reasoning is purely tactical. The branches will conceal the size of the advancing army from Macbeth's scouts on the walls; reports will undercount; surprise will be greater. There is no mention, in any of the lines, of the witches' prophecy. Malcolm, Siward, Macduff — none of the army's leadership has heard the prophecy. The order is military.

The audience, who has heard the prophecy, recognises immediately what has happened. The third apparition told Macbeth he was safe "until great Birnam Wood to high Dunsinane Hill shall come against him." Macbeth, hearing it, was exultant: trees do not march. The trees are now, by an entirely ordinary decision, marching. The witches' word, which seemed impossible, is being made true by a sergeant-major's logic in the woods. The scene is roughly fifteen lines long. It is the play's quiet demonstration that the prophecies will all come true, that none of them mean what Macbeth thought they meant, and that the trap Hecate prepared has just snapped shut without anyone in the army knowing they have closed it.

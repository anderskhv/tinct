Summary

A great room of state. The lords are arriving. Macbeth welcomes them with elaborate courtesy; Lady Macbeth, as queen, sits on the throne. Macbeth is moving to mingle when the first murderer appears at a side door, blood on his face. He gets the news quickly: Banquo in a ditch with twenty trenched gashes in his head; Fleance escaped. "Then comes my fit again. I had else been perfect — whole as the marble, founded as the rock." With Fleance alive, the witches' future is still alive. He sends the murderer away.

Macbeth rejoins the table. Lady Macbeth chides him for not playing host. He recovers and proposes a toast to Banquo, "whose absence is to be regretted." As the toast is drunk, the ghost of Banquo enters — silent, bloody, twenty wounds visible — and takes Macbeth's seat. Only Macbeth sees him. The lords see only their king staring at his own empty chair and then shouting at it: "thou canst not say I did it. Never shake thy gory locks at me." Lady Macbeth steps forward; her husband is having a fit, an old affliction; pay no attention; eat. To Macbeth, hissed: "are you a man?" The ghost vanishes.

He recovers; he proposes another toast — and the ghost returns. "Hence, horrible shadow!" The ghost leaves. The lords are by now bewildered. Lady Macbeth dismisses them: "stand not upon the order of your going, but go at once." The Macbeths are alone in the wreckage of the banquet. He is shaken in a way he has not been before. He notes Macduff's absence and announces he will go to the witches. The play's most quoted couplet on commitment to evil is here: "I am in blood stepped in so far that, should I wade no more, returning were as tedious as go o'er."

Scene 18 of 28

A dark cave — the cauldron and the apparitions

"Double, double, toil and trouble." Macbeth comes back to the witches and they are ready for him.

Summary

A dark cave with a boiling cauldron at the centre. The three witches circle it, taking turns adding ingredients and chanting. The lines have become English literature's standard image of witchcraft: "double, double, toil and trouble; fire burn and cauldron bubble." Eye of newt and toe of frog, wool of bat and tongue of dog, scale of dragon, tooth of wolf, finger of birth-strangled babe. Hecate appears briefly to approve and is gone. Macbeth arrives — driven now, no longer hesitating. "Howe'er you come to know it, answer me... answer me to what I ask you."

The witches give him three apparitions in succession. An armed head rises and speaks one warning: "Macbeth, Macbeth, Macbeth: beware Macduff." A bloody child follows: "be bloody, bold, and resolute: laugh to scorn the power of man, for none of woman born shall harm Macbeth." Macbeth is reassured — and yet resolves to kill Macduff anyway, "to make assurance double sure." A crowned child holding a tree branch: "Macbeth shall never vanquished be until great Birnam Wood to high Dunsinane Hill shall come against him." He is exultant. Trees do not march.

He demands one more answer. Will Banquo's descendants ever reign? The witches warn him not to ask. He insists. The cauldron sinks; a procession of eight kings appears, all in Banquo's likeness, the last carrying a mirror. Banquo's ghost follows, smiling and pointing. The future of the Stuart kings — the line for whom the play was written — is being shown to a Macbeth who has spent four acts trying to extinguish it. The witches vanish. Lennox enters: Macduff has fled to England. Macbeth, in cold fury, orders his castle seized and his wife and children put to the sword. The play has just shown Macbeth's future shut to him; his response is to murder a family.

The Captain's Papers

Dr. Livesey opens the packet. Inside is a map of an island with three red crosses and handwritten notes by Captain Flint.

Summary

Dr. Livesey opens the sealed packet under the squire's eye. Inside is a document in two hands: Flint's own record of his financial affairs over many years — a meticulous account — and a map of an island roughly nine miles long, with precise nautical information and three red crosses. The largest cross is marked in a different hand: "Bulk of treasure here." There is also a note giving the orientation and the depth at which the cache was buried.

Squire Trelawney is transfixed. He declares, immediately and with complete confidence, that he will go to Bristol, hire a ship, and they will all sail for Treasure Island. Livesey will be the ship's doctor; Jim will be the cabin boy. He will have the ship provisioned and ready within a week. Livesey, more cautious, asks only one thing: that the squire tell no one. Not a word. The secret must be kept absolutely until they are at sea.

The squire agrees completely and with visible sincerity. Livesey's face as he says goodbye to Jim carries a note of doubt the narrative does not belabor. The squire goes to Bristol. Within days he has hired every man who will go with him — including, as Jim and the reader will later learn, Silver's old shipmates from Flint's crew — and has written a letter so full of details about their destination and purpose that it might as well be an advertisement. The secrecy lasts exactly as long as it takes him to reach the docks.

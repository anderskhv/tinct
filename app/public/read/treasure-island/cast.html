Treasure Island — who's who

The Hispaniola — an honest party and a pirate crew bound for buried gold.

Treasure Island has a relatively small cast: the honest party (Jim, the squire, the doctor, the captain, and a handful of loyal crew), the pirate party (Silver and his men), and the two outliers — Ben Gunn and Billy Bones. The tension is structural: the same ship, the same destination, two irreconcilable purposes. Silver is the joint figure — presenting as honest until he cannot.

The honest party

Narrator
Jim Hawkins
The innkeeper's son

The narrator, fourteen, retrospective. Finds the map, discovers the mutiny, makes three unauthorized decisions that each change the expedition's fate. His honesty about his own fear and his own mistakes is what makes him a reliable narrator and an unusual adventure hero. Ends the book not with celebration but with nightmares about the sea.

Pirate
Long John Silver
Cook; pirate captain

The novel's central problem. Presents as the ship's cook — charming, helpful, fond of Jim — and is simultaneously the leader of a planned mutiny. Manages the pirate crew with the same intelligence he manages the honest party. Receives the black spot twice and talks his way out of it twice. Escapes with a portion of the gold. The one figure neither party can fully account for.

Captain
Captain Smollett
The professional

Hired by the squire and right about the crew from the first hour. Wounded in the stockade attack. Commands the honest party's defense with the tactical intelligence of a man who has been in dangerous situations before. Does not like Jim's unauthorized adventures and says so — which is the response of a professional who knows what uncontrolled variables cost.

Doctor
Dr. Livesey
The physician and co-organizer

Steady, methodical, and the holder of the strategic insight that saves the honest party: he trades the stockade for Ben Gunn's knowledge and gives up ground to win the decisive information. Narrates three chapters (16–18) in Jim's absence. His voice is noticeably different from Jim's — professional, compressed, tactical. Treats wounded pirates without comment. Distinguishes between his enemies and the injured.

Squire
Squire Trelawney
The funder, and the leak

Wealthy, generous, and constitutionally incapable of secrecy. Shares the expedition's purpose with everyone in Bristol, which allows Silver to recruit accordingly. His enthusiasm is real and his incompetence is real and they are the same quality. Brave enough in the stockade; the root cause of the crisis the expedition faces.

Castaway
Ben Gunn
The marooned sailor

Left on the island by Captain Flint three years before the novel begins. Has spent those three years finding, moving, and guarding the treasure. Speaks in the oblique, associative way of a man who has talked to no one for three years. Wants cheese. Is the most important person in the second half of the novel because he is the only one not playing by any code the pirates know.

The pirate crew

Pirate
Israel Hands
Former gunner under Captain Flint

The most competent pirate after Silver. Wounded in the stockade attack, he is left aboard the Hispaniola as Jim boards her and sails her. He and Jim reach an uneasy alliance to navigate the ship to the North Inlet — then Hands attacks Jim in the rigging. Jim shoots him; Hands falls into the sea. One of the few pirate figures drawn with enough specificity to be remembered.

Appears in: Chapter 11 · 23 · 24 · 25 · 26 · 27
Sailor
Billy Bones
The old sea-dog

The first pirate the novel shows, and the one who sets the plot in motion. He arrives at the Admiral Benbow with a sea-chest and a terror of a "seafaring man with one leg" — which is Silver. He dies of a stroke after the black spot is delivered. His chest contains the map. Without him there is no novel, and he is gone before the expedition begins.

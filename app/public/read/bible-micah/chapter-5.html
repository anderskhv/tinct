Summary

The fifth chapter contains the most contested single prophecy in the book and one of the most debated in the entire Hebrew prophetic corpus. The prophecy of Bethlehem Ephrathah — the small clan, the ancient origin, the coming forth from of old — is deliberate in its geography of reversal. Micah has spent the first three chapters naming the failure of Jerusalem's leadership, and chapter 5 locates the future ruler not in the great city but in the small village south of it, of the line that came from there before the Davidic monarchy moved its centre of gravity into the capital. The prophecy is a critique of the existing dynasty as much as it is a hope for its renewal. The ruler from Bethlehem comes from the source, not from the current establishment.

The Christian tradition has read the chapter through Matthew 2:6, where the verse is quoted to Herod's chief priests by the Magi asking where the king of the Jews is to be born. That reading has given the chapter its long afterlife in liturgical use. What is worth reading in the chapter's own context is the full arc from siege (verse 1) to shepherd-king (verses 2-5a) to the defeat of Assyria (verses 5b-6) to the remnant among the nations (verses 7-9) to the purging of the people's armoury and idolatry (verses 10-15). The chapter moves through the historical crisis — the Assyrian siege is present and real — and comes out on the other side in a total reversal. The warrior and the idol-carver are stripped away; what remains is the shepherd and the remnant who wait for him.

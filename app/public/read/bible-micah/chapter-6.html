Summary

The sixth chapter is structured as a formal legal proceeding in which YHWH is both plaintiff and judge. The setting is announced in the first two verses: Arise, contend before the mountains, and let the hills hear your voice. Hear, O mountains, the controversy of the LORD, and you enduring foundations of the earth, for the LORD has a controversy with his people, and he will contend with Israel. The prosecution is gentle in tone and devastating in effect. The LORD does not open with accusation; he opens with a question: what have I done to you? He then recites the acts of deliverance that define the covenant relationship — the exodus, the leadership of Moses, Aaron, and Miriam, the episode of Balak and Balaam in which an enemy king's hired curse was turned into blessing, the crossing from Shittim to Gilgal. The case is a recital of the relationship, not an argument from law.

The people's response and the prophet's answer are the chapter's core. The people's liturgical anxiety mounts through a scale of offering — calves, rams, rivers of oil, the firstborn — and the mounting reveals the assumption they are making: that there is a quantity of sacrifice that will satisfy the LORD's demand. Micah's answer refuses the entire framework. He has shown you, O man, what is good — the showing has already happened; it is not new information the people lack but action they are withholding. What does the LORD require? The Hebrew of the three clauses is precise: to do justice (asot mishpat) requires carrying out the actual judgment in the actual case; to love mercy (ahavat hesed) requires not merely performing the covenant loyalty but cherishing it; to walk humbly with your God (hatznea lekhet im elohekha) requires the careful, attentive discretion of one who knows the company they are in. The line refuses the commercial logic of the offering-scale and replaces it with a description of daily relational life. The chapter's second indictment of dishonest scales and deceitful weights — the commercial fraud of the market — follows immediately and is the proof that the people have not done the thing the prophet has just described.

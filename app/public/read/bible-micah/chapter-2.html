Micah 2

Those who lie awake plotting how to seize the next field will find YHWH has been plotting against them.

Summary

The second chapter develops the economic dimension of the book's indictment. The opening woe is directed at the predatory wealthy who plan their acquisitions during the night and carry them out at dawn, exploiting the legal mechanisms of debt, inheritance claim, and boundary-stone to strip smallholders of their ancestral land. The language of covet fields and seize them echoes the Decalogue's final commandment, and the echo is deliberate: the land-grabbers are not merely economically aggressive, they are violating the covenant order by which YHWH distributed the land to families as their inheritance. Against their scheming, the LORD announces his own: I am devising disaster against this family, from which you cannot remove your necks. The symmetry of divine counter-scheming — they planned against the people; the LORD plans against them — is characteristic of Micah's tight juridical structure.

The chapter's second movement concerns the prophetic office itself. Micah quotes those who tell him, Do not preach — one should not preach of such things; disgrace will not overtake us. The prophetic censorship is the establishment's self-defence: if the warning is silenced, the danger seems to go away. Micah's response names the kind of prophet they actually want — a man of wind and lies who says, I will preach to you of wine and strong drink — and implies they will get him. The chapter closes with a remnant oracle of a different tone: God will surely gather the remnant of Israel, set them together like sheep in a fold, break open the way before them, and go through the gate before them, their king passing on before them, the LORD at their head. The contrast between the midnight schemers and the gathered remnant gives the chapter its structural shape.

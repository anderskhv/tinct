Summary

Zossimov has come to look in on the patient. Razumikhin and Zametov are with him. They settle in around Raskolnikov's sofa with cigarettes and a cheerful disregard for the patient's state. The conversation is general at first — gossip from the bureau, news of mutual friends, comments on the heat. Razumikhin has been talking, with growing irritation, about the official theory of the double murder.

Zametov, the head clerk at the bureau, has the latest. The two painters who had been working below Alyona Ivanovna have been arrested — Mikolka and his apprentice. Mikolka has been making a confused, partial confession of having been "frightened" or having "seen something." A pair of small earrings from the pawnbroker's box have been found, dropped on a staircase, by one of the workmen the next morning. The case, the bureau thinks, is essentially solved. Razumikhin objects loudly. He has been thinking about the case from a distance for days and is convinced the painters cannot have done it — the timing does not fit, the dropped earrings make no sense for a thief who had been in the apartment proper, the violence does not match the temperaments of the men arrested. He works through it with cheerful obstinacy. Zametov listens with growing interest.

Raskolnikov, lying on the sofa, says almost nothing. He breaks in once or twice with quiet questions — about the painters, about the earrings, about Zametov's opinion of the case. He keeps his eyes on Zametov throughout. The chapter is the first scene in which he begins to play, deliberately, with the person investigating him; the second part of that game is the next chapter's entire subject.

Section 1

Modern English

Some things are within our control, others are not. Within our control are our opinions, impulses, desires, and aversions — in short, whatever is our own doing. Outside our control are our body, reputation, possessions, and positions of power — in short, whatever is not our own doing. What is within our control is by nature free, unhindered, unobstructed. What is outside our control is weak, dependent, subject to obstruction — it belongs to others. Remember: if you treat what is naturally dependent as free and mistake what belongs to others for your own, you will be frustrated, you will grieve, you will be in turmoil, blaming gods and men alike. But if you take as your own only what truly is your own, and recognise that what belongs to others belongs to others, no one will ever compel you or obstruct you. You will blame no one, accuse no one, do nothing against your will. You will have no enemies, and nothing can harm you.

Long (1877)

Of things some are in our power, and others are not. In our power are opinion, movement towards a thing, desire, aversion, turning from a thing; and in a word, whatever are our acts. Not in our power are the body, property, reputation, offices (magisterial power), and in a word, whatever are not our own acts. And the things in our power are by nature free, not subject to restraint or hindrance; but the things not in our power are weak, slavish, subject to restraint, in the power of others. Remember then, that if you think the things which are by nature slavish to be free, and the things which are in the power of others to be your own, you will be hindered, you will lament, you will be disturbed, you will blame both gods and men; but if you think that only which is your own to be your own, and if you think that what is another’s, as it really is, belongs to another, no man will ever compel you, no man will hinder you, you will never blame any man, you will accuse no man, you will do nothing involuntarily (against your will), no man will harm you, you will have no enemy, for you will not suffer any harm.

How this edition was made. Tinct's modern English is drafted with AI from George Long's public-domain translation (1877), then checked by a separate AI review against that translation. The original is one tap away, and if you spot a mistake, tell us.

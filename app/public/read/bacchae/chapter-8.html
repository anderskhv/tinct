Third Stasimon

A short, hard song about escape, divine justice and happiness, sung while the king is being dressed inside the palace.

Summary

With Dionysus and Pentheus inside, the chorus sing the third stasimon. It begins with longing for the all-night dances and the dew on their necks. They picture a fawn that has escaped the hunt, past the watchers and the nets, with the hounds and huntsmen behind her, racing across the river plain until the trees close around her in a place no man tracks. Then comes the refrain, which Way renders: "What wisdom's crown, what guerdon, shines more glorious That Gods can give the sons of men, than this— O'er crests of foes to stretch the hand victorious?" The answer is that honour is always precious. In this setting the image is pointed: the enemy is Pentheus.

The second stanza is a warning. The power of heaven moves slowly but surely against people who put their own unbelief on a throne and belittle the divine. The gods wait in ambush; time passes, but their purpose does not change, and fate tracks the godless down like a hound. No one should set himself above custom and tradition. It costs little, the chorus sing, to accept that what comes from heaven, and what has been held through the ages, has authority. The refrain returns.

The final short stanza is about happiness. Blessed is the one who escapes the sea and reaches harbour, and the one who succeeds after hard labour. People rise above one another in wealth and power, and countless hopes burn in countless hearts; some are fulfilled and some come to nothing. Happy is the one whose good fortune grows from day to day. The song ends. Dionysus comes out of the palace and calls Pentheus to follow him.

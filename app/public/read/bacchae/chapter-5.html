Scene 5 of 11

The arrest and the interrogation

The stranger brought in bound, and the king who questions him without understanding who is in front of him.

Summary

A servant leads in the prisoner and reports to Pentheus. The capture was easy. The stranger did not run, did not turn pale, and held out his hands, smiling, while they tied them. The servant was ashamed enough to tell him that he was acting under the king's orders, not by choice. There is more. The women Pentheus had locked in the common prison have escaped to the meadows and are dancing again. Their fetters fell away and the doors unbarred themselves. The servant says the man has come to Thebes full of wonders and leaves the rest to the king.

Pentheus inspects the prisoner. He is handsome enough to tempt women, Pentheus says; his long hair shows he is no wrestler, and his skin is pale from staying out of the sun. The questioning that follows is the first direct contest of the play. The stranger says he comes from Lydia, near Mount Tmolus. Dionysus himself initiated him, face to face and awake. The rites cannot be described to the uninitiated; they are mostly held at night. When Pentheus says these answers tell him nothing, the stranger replies, in Way's line, "Wise answers seem but folly to a fool."

Pentheus threatens to cut off his hair; the stranger says it is sacred to the god. Pentheus demands the thyrsus; the stranger tells him to take it, since it belongs to Dionysus. Pentheus says he will keep him in his dungeon; the stranger says the god will free him whenever he wishes, and that the god is close by, watching, though Pentheus cannot see him. Pentheus orders him bound and penned in the horse-stalls in darkness, and says the women who came with him will be sold or put to work at the loom. Dionysus goes quietly, warning that the god Pentheus denies will make him pay for this.

The Bacchae — chapter by chapter

All 11 scenes summarized — a god's revenge on the city that refused him.

The Bacchae is structured around two long messenger speeches that report what is happening on Mount Cithaeron, bracketed by Dionysus's prologue at one end and the recognition scene at the other. Scenes 1–4 are the arrival: the god, the chorus, the two old men in fawn-skins, the young king's refusal. Scenes 5–8 are the contest: Dionysus arrested, the prison shattered, the first messenger's report, and the seduction of Pentheus. Scenes 9–11 are the killing and the recognition: Pentheus dressed as a woman, the second messenger's report from the mountain, Agave with the head, and the long quiet judgement that ends the play.

Scenes 1–4 · The arrival

The god in the city of his birth, the chorus from the east, the two old men, the young king's refusal.

Scene 1

Dionysus's prologue

The god alone beside his mother's tomb, in mortal disguise, laying out the plan. His aunts denied that Zeus was his father; he has driven them and every woman of Thebes onto Cithaeron. Pentheus, the young king, excludes him from offerings and prayers, and Dionysus promises to show him and the whole city what he is. He calls in the women he brought from Lydia and goes up to the mountain.

Appears: Dionysus · The Chorus (entering)
Scene 2

Parodos

The chorus of Asian women sing the long entrance song. They bless the initiate, retell the god's birth from his father's thigh, call on Thebes to put on ivy and fawn-skin, and describe the mountain: the hunt, the raw feast, the ground running with milk and wine and honey, the dancer racing like a foal among grazing mares. The song ends as the blind prophet Tiresias arrives at the palace door.

Appears: The Chorus · Tiresias (entering)
Scene 3

Tiresias, Cadmus, Pentheus

Tiresias and Cadmus, both old, both in fawn-skins, set out to dance for the new god on foot. Pentheus arrives, enraged by the news from the mountain, and sneers at them. Tiresias defends the god as the giver of wine and the rest it brings; Cadmus reminds him of Actaeon, torn apart by his own hounds. Pentheus orders the prophet's seat of augury overturned and the stranger brought in chains to be stoned.

Appears: Tiresias · Cadmus · Pentheus · The Chorus
Scene 4

First Stasimon

The chorus's first ode. They ask Sanctity whether she has heard the king's blasphemy, and list the god's gifts: dance, music, laughter, release from care, sleep. They warn that unrestrained speech ends in disaster and that cleverness is not wisdom. They long for Cyprus and the Muses' Pieria. The song ends with the stranger led in bound.

Appears: The Chorus

Scenes 5–8 · The contest

The arrest, the broken prison, the first report from the mountain, the seduction.

Scene 5

The arrest and the interrogation

The servant who arrested the stranger reports that he came willingly, and that the women already jailed have walked free, their chains loosened and doors unbarred without a hand on them. Pentheus mocks the prisoner's looks and questions him. The stranger says he is from Lydia, that the god taught him the rites in person, and that the god is standing beside them now. Pentheus takes his thyrsus and has him locked in the stables. The prisoner is Dionysus.

Appears: Pentheus · Dionysus (in disguise) · The Soldier · The Chorus
Scene 6

The earthquake

The chorus appeal to Dirce, condemn Pentheus as the offspring of the dragon-born Echion, and ask where their god is lingering. A voice answers from inside the palace. The ground shakes, the palace lintels sway, and fire flares around Semele's tomb. The chorus fall flat in fear and worship, and the stranger walks out free.

Appears: The Chorus · Dionysus
Scene 7

The first messenger and the hinge

The god describes his escape: the bull Pentheus bound, the phantom he stabbed, the palace in ruins. Pentheus storms out. A herdsman reports from the mountain: the women sleeping peacefully, wine and milk rising from the earth, then the rampage when the herdsmen tried to catch Agave. Pentheus orders out his army. The stranger asks one question: would he like to see them? Pentheus says yes, whatever it costs. The hinge.

Appears: Pentheus · Dionysus · The First Messenger · The Chorus
Scene 8

Third Stasimon

The chorus long for the night dances and picture a fawn escaping the hunt into the shelter of the woods. Their refrain asks what prize from the gods is greater than holding a victorious hand over an enemy's head. They sing of the slow, sure reach of divine power against the godless, and of the happiness of a life that improves day by day. Behind the palace door, the king is being dressed.

Appears: The Chorus

Scenes 9–11 · The killing and the recognition

Pentheus dressed as a woman, the second messenger's report, Agave with the head.

Scene 9

Pentheus dressed

The stranger calls Pentheus out. The king is dressed as a Bacchant and no longer sane: he sees two suns, a double Thebes, a bull where the stranger stands. The god fixes his hair, his belt and his hem, and corrects how he holds the thyrsus. Pentheus boasts he could lift the mountain, settles on hiding in the pines, and hears that he will return carried in his mother's hands. Dionysus sends him off and calls on Cadmus's daughters to meet him.

Appears: Pentheus · Dionysus · The Chorus
Scene 10

Fourth Stasimon

The chorus abandon the restraint of their earlier songs. They call on the hounds of Madness to set the women on the spy, imagine Agave's cry when she sees him, and summon Justice with her sword to cut his throat. They ask the god to come as a bull, a dragon or a lion wrapped in flame and catch the hunter in his own net. A messenger arrives from the mountain.

Appears: The Chorus · The Second Messenger (entering)
Scene 11

The messenger, Agave, the judgement

The second messenger describes the killing: the bent pine, the voice from the sky, the women uprooting the tree, Pentheus begging his mother, the dismemberment. Agave enters with her son's head, sure it is a lion's. Cadmus arrives with the remains and questions her until she sees what she is holding. Dionysus declares their punishment. Father and daughter say goodbye and go into exile, and the chorus close the play.

Appears: The Second Messenger · The Chorus · Agave · Cadmus · Dionysus

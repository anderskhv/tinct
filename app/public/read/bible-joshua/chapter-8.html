Joshua 8 of 24

The Taking of Ai and the Altar at Ebal

With Achan's sin removed, Ai is taken by a feigned retreat and an ambush — then Joshua builds an altar and reads the whole law aloud.

Summary

The contrast between the taking of Jericho and the taking of Ai is deliberate and structural. Jericho: miraculous, liturgical, no battle plan, walls fall at a shout. Ai (second attempt): conventional, tactical, ambush and feigned retreat, city taken by the oldest trick in warfare. Both are presented as equally valid under God's direction, but the difference illustrates the book's implicit argument: the mode of giving can vary, but the requirement of purity in the recipient of the gift is constant. Jericho fell when Israel was clean; Ai failed when Israel was contaminated; Ai fell when Israel was clean again. The victory conditions are moral and cultic, not military.

The ceremony at Mount Ebal is the most surprising element of the chapter. In the middle of a military campaign, the entire community — men, women, children, resident aliens — gathers for a covenant renewal ceremony explicitly commanded in Deuteronomy 27. Joshua builds an altar of unhewn stones and plasters them and writes on them a copy of the law. Burnt offerings and peace offerings are made. Then Joshua reads the whole law — every word of the blessing and the curse — aloud to the whole assembly. The scene interrupts the conquest narrative and places the law at the center of what the conquest is about. The people are not simply taking land; they are taking land as a law-bound community, and the law is being inscribed and proclaimed in the territory before the territory is fully secured.

Joshua — chapter by chapter

All 24 chapters — the crossing, the conquest, the allotment, and the farewell.

Joshua has three large movements. Chapters 1–12 are the conquest narrative: the commissioning of Joshua, Rahab's sheltering of the spies, the crossing of the Jordan with the ark, the fall of Jericho, the disaster at Ai because of Achan's hidden plunder, the Gibeonite deception, the long day at Aijalon when the sun stands still, and the southern and northern campaigns. Chapters 13–22 are the apportionment of the land among the twelve tribes, with the Levites given forty-eight cities and six cities of refuge designated. Chapters 23–24 are Joshua's farewell and the covenant renewal at Shechem.

The conquest (ch. 1–12)

From the commissioning of Joshua to the list of defeated kings.

Joshua 1

The Commissioning of Joshua

The formal commissioning of Joshua as Moses's successor — God, the officers, and the eastern tribes all confirm his authority before anyone crosses the river.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 2

Rahab and the Spies

Rahab shelters the Israelite spies, extracts a promise of safety, and receives the scarlet cord that will mark her household for preservation when Jericho falls.

Appears: Rahab · Joshua
Joshua 3

The Crossing of the Jordan

The priests carry the ark into the flooded Jordan and the waters halt — a crossing explicitly designed to repeat and complete the Exodus from Egypt.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 4

Twelve Stones at Gilgal

Twelve stones from the Jordan riverbed are set up at Gilgal — a permanent monument engineered to provoke a question children will ask for generations.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 5

Circumcision and Passover at Gilgal

Before battle, the whole camp is circumcised, the Passover is observed, and the manna ceases — then Joshua meets an armed stranger who identifies himself as the Lord's commander.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 6

The Fall of Jericho

Jericho falls not by military force but by seven days of liturgical procession — and the city's total destruction inaugurates the book's most difficult ethical territory.

Appears: Joshua · Rahab
Joshua 7

Achan's Sin and the Defeat at Ai

One man's hidden violation of the herem contaminates the whole camp — Achan's confession and execution in the Valley of Achor frames the book's starkest claim about collective integrity.

Appears: Achan · Joshua
Joshua 8

The Taking of Ai and the Altar at Ebal

A feigned retreat and ambush take Ai by conventional tactics — then Joshua pauses the conquest to build an altar, write the law on stones, and read it to the whole assembly.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 9

The Gibeonite Deception

The Gibeonites' theatrical deception tricks Israel into a binding treaty, illustrating the irreversibility of an oath sworn in God's name — and the danger of deciding without consulting God.

Appears: Joshua · The Gibeonites
Joshua 10

The Long Day at Aijalon

The southern campaign culminates in the Long Day at Aijalon, where the sun halts in the sky to give Israel time to complete its victory — and five Amorite kings are drawn from a cave and executed.

Appears: Joshua · The Gibeonites
Joshua 11

The Northern Campaign

The northern coalition under King Jabin of Hazor is crushed at Merom — Joshua burns the chariots and hamstrings the horses, ending the military conquest with Hazor's fall.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 12

List of Defeated Kings

A register of thirty-one defeated kings east and west of the Jordan — the archival drumbeat that closes the conquest narrative before the allotment begins.

Appears: Joshua

The allotments (ch. 13–21)

The land divided tribe by tribe, the Levitical cities, the cities of refuge.

Joshua 13

Territories Yet to Be Taken

Before distributing the western land, God acknowledges to Joshua that much remains unconquered — then confirms the eastern allotments Moses had already made.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 14

Caleb Claims Hebron

Caleb claims the hill country of Hebron at eighty-five, citing a forty-five-year-old promise and declaring himself as fit for war as the day Moses first sent him.

Appears: Caleb · Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 15

Judah's Allotment

Judah's vast and precisely bounded territory includes Caleb's hill country of Hebron — and Jerusalem itself remains unconquered, held still by the Jebusites.

Appears: Caleb · Joshua
Joshua 16

Ephraim's Allotment

Ephraim's allotment in the central highlands ends with a candid note: the Canaanites at Gezer were not driven out but remain as forced labourers.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 17

Manasseh's Allotment

Manasseh's allotment includes a ruling for Zelophehad's daughters and a frank admission of failed dispossession — then the Joseph tribes complain about space and Joshua tells them to clear their own forest.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 18

The Survey at Shiloh

Seven tribes have not yet received their land — Joshua commissions a written survey, casts lots at Shiloh, and begins distributing the remaining portions starting with Benjamin.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 19

Allotments for Six Remaining Tribes

The remaining six tribes receive their allotments — including Dan, which cannot occupy its territory and migrates north — and Joshua himself is given the last inheritance, the town of his own choosing.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 20

The Cities of Refuge

Six cities of refuge are established across the land — a legal institution interposing public sanctuary between accidental killing and the private vengeance of the victim's family.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 21

Levitical Cities and the Promise Fulfilled

Forty-eight cities scattered through every tribe are given to the Levites — and the chapter closes with the declaration that every promise God made to Israel has come to pass.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar

Covenant renewal (ch. 22–24)

The altar dispute, Joshua's farewell, and the choice at Shechem.

Joshua 22

The Altar by the Jordan

The eastern tribes build an altar at the Jordan and nearly trigger civil war — their explanation reveals it is a monument of inclusion, not apostasy, built to assert their ongoing share in the God of Israel.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar
Joshua 23

Joshua's First Farewell

Joshua's first farewell warns the leaders that the same faithfulness that brought them into the land can expel them from it — covenant cuts both ways.

Appears: Joshua
Joshua 24

Covenant at Shechem — Choose This Day

The covenant renewal at Shechem — recited history, the challenge to choose, the great stone witness, and three burials — closes the book with a promise made and a future already in doubt.

Appears: Joshua · Eleazar

Summary

Dawn. Thetis brings the new armor and lays it before Achilles. He arms. The bronze rattles like life coming back into him. He calls the assembly. The captains gather; the wounded — Diomedes, Odysseus, Agamemnon — limp in. Achilles speaks first: he renounces his wrath. He says it formally. What he had against Agamemnon, he sets aside. Patroclus is dead and the Trojans are still standing; nothing else matters now.

Agamemnon then speaks his own awkward speech of restitution. He blames Atē — Folly, Delusion — for the original quarrel; not really himself. The accepted gifts of restitution are brought from his ship. Achilles waves them off impatiently — he doesn't care; he wants to go fight now, without waiting to eat. Odysseus, quietly, insists the men have to eat. Soldiers cannot fight all day on empty stomachs. Achilles agrees, but he himself will not eat. While the army eats, Athena slips down secretly and fills him with nectar so he will not weaken.

Then the arming. Achilles puts on the bronze greaves, the silver-studded breastplate, the helmet, the new shield — each piece described as he puts it on. He picks up the great spear of Peleus, the only piece of his old gear he still has. He climbs into his chariot. His horse Xanthus, gifted with speech for one moment by Hera, looks back at him and warns him: today is the day you die. Achilles answers, calmly, that he knows. He has known for a long time. He drives the chariot out of the camp. The war's last day for him has begun.

Isaiah 11

A shoot springs from the stump of Jesse, filled with the spirit of wisdom and knowledge; the wolf dwells with the lamb; the earth is full of the knowledge of the Lord.

Summary

Chapter 11 opens with the stump image that closed chapter 10, but the direction is reversed: chapter 10's forest of Assyrian pride is cut down as ending; chapter 11's stump of Jesse is cut down as beginning. The Davidic dynasty — in Isaiah's time still ruling, but the oracle imagines a future collapse — will be reduced to a stump. From that stump, a new growth. The word for branch (choter) is different from the tsemach of chapter 4 and the neser of chapter 53 — Isaiah uses several different plant-metaphors for the coming restoration, all centering on new life from apparent death. The spirit-endowment of the coming king uses a catalogue of three paired gifts (wisdom/understanding, counsel/might, knowledge/fear of the Lord) that have been influential in later Jewish and Christian angelology and in descriptions of spiritual gifts. The point is not that this king will have unusual intelligence but that his governance will be God-shaped — he will know and fear the Lord in a way that Ahaz and others did not.

The peaceable kingdom vision (11:6-9) is among the most beloved and contested passages in the prophetic tradition. The animal pairs — wolf/lamb, leopard/kid, calf/lion — are all predator/prey relationships in the natural order, and the vision describes their abolition. A little child shall lead them — the child figure that has been present throughout the first eleven chapters (Immanuel, Maher-shalal-hash-baz, the child of chapter 9) appears here in its most peaceful form. Whether the vision is literal (a transformation of the natural order) or metaphorical (the transformation of human social violence) has been debated through the tradition. The founding statement of verse 9 — 'the earth will be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea' — suggests the epistemological root: it is the universal knowledge of the Holy One that makes the peaceable kingdom possible. The second half of the chapter (vv. 10-16) develops the gathering of the remnant from the diaspora — from Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, the coastlands. A second exodus will bring them home, the Lord's hand lifted a second time, a highway lifted up for the returning remnant. The chapter thus moves from the messianic king to the messianic age to the eschatological return — a sustained vision of the future that draws on the whole theological vocabulary of the book.

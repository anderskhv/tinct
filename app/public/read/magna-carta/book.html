Magna Carta (1215)

Modern English

John greets his archbishops, his bishops and abbots, his earls and barons, his judicial officers and forest officers, his sheriffs, stewards and servants, and every bailiff and subject who owes him loyalty. Through God’s grace he reigns as England’s king, as Ireland’s lord, as duke in Normandy and in Aquitaine, and as Anjou’s count. Let everyone know that we have made the grants set out here out of regard for God, to secure salvation for ourselves, for our ancestors and for our heirs, to honour God, to advance holy Church and to reform our kingdom. We have acted with advice from our revered fathers: Stephen, the archbishop at Canterbury, who is primate for all England and a cardinal serving the holy Roman Church; Henry, the archbishop at Dublin; and these bishops: William of London, together with Peter of Winchester; Jocelyn of Bath and Glastonbury, together with Hugh of Lincoln; Walter of Worcester, together with William of Coventry and Benedict of Rochester. Our advisers also include master Pandulf, a subdeacon whose service is in the household belonging to our lord the Pope, and brother Aymeric, master for England of the order called the Knights of the Temple. We have also consulted these distinguished men: William Marshal, who is earl at Pembroke; William, who is earl at Salisbury; William, who bears the title earl Warenne; William, who is earl at Arundel; Alan of Galloway, Scotland’s constable; Waren Fitz Gerald, and also Peter Fitz Herbert; Hubert de Burgh, the seneschal in Poitou; Hugh de Neville, and also Matthew Fitz Herbert; Thomas Basset, and also Alan Basset; Philip d’Aubigny, and also Robert of Roppesley; John Marshal, and also John Fitz Hugh; with others who owe us allegiance.

McKechnie (1914)

John, by the grace of God, king of England, lord of Ireland, duke of Normandy and Aquitaine, and count of Anjou, to the archbishops, bishops, abbots, earls, barons, justiciars, foresters, sheriffs, stewards, servants, and to all his bailiffs and liege subjects, greeting. Know that, having regard to God and for the salvation of our souls, and those of all our ancestors and heirs, and unto the honour of God and the advancement of holy Church, and for the reform of our realm, [we have granted as underwritten] by advice of our venerable fathers, Stephen, archbishop of Canterbury, primate of all England and cardinal of the holy Roman Church, Henry archbishop of Dublin, William of London, Peter of Winchester, Jocelyn of Bath and Glastonbury, Hugh of Lincoln, Walter of Worcester, William of Coventry, Benedict of Rochester, bishops; of master Pandulf, subdeacon and member of the household of our lord the Pope, of brother Aymeric (master of the Knights of the Temple in England), and of the illustrious men William Marshal, earl of Pembroke, William, earl of Salisbury, William, earl Warenne, William, earl of Arundel, Alan of Galloway (constable of Scotland), Waren Fitz Gerald, Peter Fitz Herbert, Hubert de Burgh (seneschal of Poitou), Hugh de Neville, Matthew Fitz Herbert, Thomas Basset, Alan Basset, Philip d’Aubigny, Robert of Roppesley, John Marshal, John Fitz Hugh, and others, our liegemen.

Luke 23

Pilate finds no fault, Herod sends him back dressed in fine clothes, and he dies with a psalm on his lips promising paradise to a thief.

Summary

The trial before Pilate unfolds in a pattern of repeated declarations of innocence. The assembled crowd accuses Jesus of three political charges — perverting the nation, forbidding tribute, and claiming to be the Messiah king. Pilate interrogates him and finds no guilt in this man. The crowd insists on Galilean origins, which gives Pilate the opportunity to send him to Herod Antipas, who is in Jerusalem for the festival. Herod, who has long wanted to see Jesus, questions him at length and hopes to see a sign; Jesus offers him nothing. Herod and his soldiers treat him with contempt, dress him in elegant clothing, and send him back. Luke adds the note that Herod and Pilate became friends with each other that day, having previously been at enmity. Pilate reconvenes the chief priests and the rulers and the people and again declares that he has found no guilt, that Herod has found nothing deserving death, and that he will therefore flog him and release him. The crowd demands the release of Barabbas and the crucifixion of Jesus; Pilate gives the verdict they require.

Simon of Cyrene is pressed into carrying the cross. Jesus addresses the weeping women of Jerusalem — weep not for me but for yourselves and your children — with a prediction of the city's destruction. At the place called The Skull he is crucified, with criminals on either side. Luke alone records the prayer — Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do — which the soldiers and the rulers and one of the criminals mock. The second criminal rebukes the first: we are receiving what our deeds deserve, but this man has done nothing wrong. He asks to be remembered; Jesus answers: today you will be with me in paradise. At noon darkness covers the land for three hours; the curtain of the temple is torn down the middle. Jesus prays — Father, into your hands I commit my spirit — and dies. Luke replaces Mark's cry of dereliction with a psalm verse of confident commitment. The centurion, seeing what has happened, declares this man was certainly righteous. The watching crowd beats their breasts and returns home. The disciples stand at a distance. Joseph of Arimathea, a council member who had not agreed to the decision against Jesus, goes to Pilate and asks for the body, wraps it in linen, and lays it in a rock-cut tomb where no one had yet been laid. The women who have come from Galilee observe and see where the body is laid.

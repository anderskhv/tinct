Luke 21 of 24

Luke 21

A widow's two coins outweigh the rich men's gifts, the beautiful temple is predicted to be rubble, and signs of the end are catalogued.

Summary

The observation about the widow's offering is triggered by Jesus watching the wealthy put large amounts into the temple treasury. The widow's two lepta — the smallest denomination of coin — are declared to surpass all other contributions because they represent her entire livelihood rather than a portion of abundance. The scene is brief but resonant: it is one of the few moments in the Jerusalem chapters where the crowd recedes and an individual, overlooked figure is noticed. As the disciples admire the temple, Jesus predicts total architectural destruction. When asked for a sign, he warns first against deception — many will come in his name — and then describes wars, earthquakes, famines, and pestilences. Before all this, he tells them, you will be handed over to authorities, an occasion for bearing testimony; settle in your minds not to meditate beforehand on your defense, for he will give you words and wisdom.

Luke's version of the Jerusalem discourse differs from Mark's at the crucial middle passage. Where Mark describes an abomination of desolation standing where it ought not, Luke writes: when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, know that its desolation is near. Then flee — those in Judea to the mountains, those in the city out, those in the country not into the city — for these are the days of vengeance, when all that is written must be fulfilled. Woe to those with child and those nursing in those days. This will be great distress upon the earth and wrath against this people; they will fall by the sword and be led away captive among all nations, and Jerusalem will be trampled by the Gentiles until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled. Then the cosmic signs: sun, moon, stars in distress; roaring seas; people fainting from fear. And then the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory. The parable of the fig tree makes the point that visible signs indicate nearness: when the tree puts out leaves, summer is near; when these things happen, the kingdom of God is near. This generation will not pass away before all these things take place. Heaven and earth will pass away, but his words will not. The chapter ends with Jesus teaching in the temple by day and sleeping on the Mount of Olives by night, with the people coming early in the morning to listen.

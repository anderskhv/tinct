Romans — chapter by chapter

All 16 chapters summarized — from the greeting and thesis through to the closing names and benediction.

Romans is structured in four movements. Chapters 1-4 are the diagnosis and the prescription: universal sin, justification by faith, Abraham as the demonstration. Chapters 5-8 are the consequences for believers: peace, hope, the new life in the Spirit — culminating in the great "no condemnation" of chapter 8. Chapters 9-11 are the hardest meditation on Israel. Chapters 12-16 are the practice: living sacrifice, love, governing authorities, the strong and the weak, and the long closing greetings.

Peace, hope, the divided I, and the great climax of chapter 8.

The law is good, but the I cannot keep it — the most quoted description of moral self-division in the New Testament, ending in a cry and a thanks.

Romans 8

Israel · 9-11

The hardest question. Has God abandoned his promises? Paul's anguished three-chapter answer.

Romans 9

Romans 9

Paul could wish himself damned for Israel's sake; God's election has always been free, and that freedom is the ground of mercy, not injustice.

Romans 10

Appears: Paul

Romans 11

Living sacrifice, love, governing authorities, the strong and the weak — and the names.

Romans 12

Romans 13

Romans 14

Romans 15

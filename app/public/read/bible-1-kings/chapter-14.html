1 Kings 14

Jeroboam's sick child prompts a disguised royal visit to the prophet Ahijah, blind but sharp, who delivers a devastating oracle against the house of Jeroboam.

Summary

The Ahijah episode is a masterpiece of prophetic-narrative irony. Jeroboam's wife disguises herself, but the Lord has told the blind prophet who is coming before she arrives. The disguise is theatrical and pointless; the prophet sees what she cannot hide. The oracle he delivers is total: the cutting off of every male of the line of Jeroboam, the denial of burial rites to the whole house, dogs and birds as the agents of dishonour. The exception — the sick child Abijah, who will be buried and mourned because something good was found in him toward the Lord — is the oracle's most poignant element. He is the only member of the house for whom a dignified death is given, and he receives it by dying.

The Rehoboam section introduces the alternating format that will characterise the rest of the book: the historian moves between the two kingdoms, giving each king a summary evaluation before moving to the other. Rehoboam's evaluation includes the details of cultic heterodoxy — high places, Asherim, the male cult prostitutes associated with Canaanite religion, abominations of the nations the Lord drove out before Israel. Shishak's raid, which removed the gold shields Solomon had made for the House of the Forest of Lebanon and required their replacement with bronze ones, is both historical event and symbolic commentary: the kingdom is now in bronze where it was gold. The Shishak campaign is corroborated in Egyptian records of Pharaoh Sheshonq I and is one of the earliest external archaeological confirmations of events narrated in Kings.

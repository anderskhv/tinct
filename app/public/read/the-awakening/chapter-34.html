Chapter 34 — the awkward dinner with Robert

Robert and Edna dine awkwardly together. He cannot quite explain his return or his restraint.

Summary

Robert sits across from Edna in the small parlor. He has come back from Mexico. He is in the same suit he left in. His face is more sunburned than she remembers. He is, she sees in five minutes, more uncomfortable than she had imagined he could be. The dinner Celestine produces is good but neither of them eats much. Edna asks the obvious questions and gets evasive answers. Why has he come back? Vague reasons. Why did he never write? Vaguer ones. Why has he not come to see her until tonight? He starts a sentence and does not finish.

Edna goes into her bedroom briefly and brings out a small book Robert had given her one morning at Grand Isle, with a marker still at the page they had been on. She returns it to him. He takes it. The gesture is small and devastating. He has no place to put the book. He sets it on the table next to Arobin's tobacco pouch — left there from the night before, casual, visible. Robert sees it. His face does not change. They both look at it. They both look away.

He leaves earlier than Edna wants. He says he will call again. The restraint is what wounds her — she had been preparing for confession or accusation or longing, and she has gotten politeness. After he is gone she sits a long time on the small lounge looking at the closed door. The chapter is the careful tightening of the spring. The novel is preparing the next chapter to land.

Chapter 61 — Stubb Kills a Whale

The first successful kill: Stubb and his boat pursue and kill a sperm whale in the Indian Ocean.

Summary

Ishmael is dozing at the foremast-head when a sperm whale surfaces. Stubb's boat is in the water within moments. Ishmael gives us the boat's crew at their oars, the harpooner in the bow, the increasing speed as they close the distance — the rhythm of a whale-boat at full race.

Stubb buries the harpoon. The whale runs. The line whips out. The boat is dragged through the water at speed. Eventually the whale slows; Stubb moves up to the bow and works the lance, probing for the whale's life — the vital spot. The whale's blood begins to spout. Its swimming becomes irregular. The final flurry: violent thrashing, the sea turned red, and then the whale rolls over.

The kill is described with complete accuracy and no sentimentality. The chapter is not about Ahab or the white whale — it is about ordinary whaling, what the Pequod's crew is actually there to do. And Melville makes it magnificent: violent, skilled, exhausting, and strange.

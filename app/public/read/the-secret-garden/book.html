There Is No One Left

Modern English

Everyone said that Mary Lennox was the most disagreeable-looking child they had ever seen. They said it when she was sent to Misselthwaite Manor to live with her uncle, and it was true. She had a thin little face and a thin little body, with thin, light hair and a sour look. Her hair was yellow, and so was her face, because she had been born in India and had always been sick with one thing or another. Her father had worked for the English Government and had always been busy and unwell himself. Her mother had been a great beauty who cared only about going to parties and having fun with cheerful people. She had not wanted a little girl at all. When Mary was born, she handed her over to an Ayah, who was made to understand that if she wanted to please the Mem Sahib, she must keep the child out of sight as much as she could. So when Mary was a sickly, fussy, ugly baby, she was kept out of the way. When she grew into a sickly, fussy toddler, she was kept out of the way too. The only faces she ever remembered seeing every day were the dark faces of her Ayah and the other native servants. They always obeyed her and let her have her own way in everything, because the Mem Sahib would be angry if her crying disturbed her. So by the time Mary was six years old, she was as bossy and selfish a little pig as ever lived. The young English governess who came to teach her to read and write disliked her so much that she quit after three months. When other governesses came to fill the job, they always left even sooner than the first. So if Mary had not decided that she really wanted to learn to read books, she would never have learned her letters at all.

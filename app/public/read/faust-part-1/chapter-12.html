Promenade

The jewels have been confiscated by a priest. Mephistopheles is furious. A second casket will be left.

Summary

Mephistopheles catches up with Faust on the promenade with news that has clearly been irritating him for hours. The jewels: Gretchen's mother, being a woman with a fine moral nose, grew uneasy about the gift and called in the priest. The priest arrived, inspected the casket, acknowledged that it was a very fine thing, promised heavenly rewards, and carried the entire treasure off to the church. Gretchen's mother felt better. The church was delighted. Mephistopheles is outraged.

Faust takes it calmly. He has seen kings and Jews do similar things. Mephistopheles does not find this soothing — the principle bothers him. He has obtained a fine piece of jewelry through appropriate supernatural effort and an ordained representative of God has simply walked off with it. He cannot even revenge himself properly.

The solution is straightforward: a second, larger casket will be arranged. But this time, rather than leaving it in the mother's custody, it will go through the neighbor — Marthe, a widow of worldly tastes who will not feel obliged to check with the priest before enjoying a gift. Mephistopheles will handle the introduction. The engineering continues.

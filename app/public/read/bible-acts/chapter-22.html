Acts 22 of 28

Acts 22

Standing on the barracks steps with Roman soldiers at his back, Paul tells the crowd in Aramaic the story of his conversion on the Damascus road; the crowd listens until he mentions the Gentiles, then demands his death.

Summary

Paul addresses the crowd from the barracks steps in Aramaic: I am a Jew, born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but brought up in this city, educated at the feet of Gamaliel according to the strict manner of the law of our fathers, being zealous for God as all of you are this day. I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and delivering to prison both men and women, as the high priest and the whole council of elders can bear me witness. From them I received letters to the brothers, and I journeyed toward Damascus to bring those who were there in bonds to Jerusalem to be punished. As I was on my way and drew near to Damascus, about noon a great light from heaven suddenly shone around me. I fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to me: Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? I answered: who are you, Lord? He said: I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you are persecuting. Those who were with me saw the light but did not understand the voice. I said: what shall I do, Lord? The Lord told me to go to Damascus and there it would be told me what to do.

Paul continues: Ananias, a devout man according to the law, well spoken of by all Jews living in Damascus, came to me and said: Brother Saul, receive your sight. In that very hour I received my sight and saw him. He said: the God of our fathers appointed you to know his will, to see the Righteous One and to hear a voice from his mouth; for you will be a witness for him to everyone of what you have seen and heard. When I had returned to Jerusalem and was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance and saw Jesus, who said to me: make haste and get out of Jerusalem quickly, because they will not accept your testimony about me. Lord, I said, they know that I imprisoned and beat those who believed in you. When the blood of Stephen your witness was being shed, I myself was standing by and approving and watching over the garments of those who killed him. He said to me: go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles. Up to this word they listen; then they raise their voices: away with such a fellow from the earth! The commander orders Paul to be examined by flogging. When they stretch him out with straps, Paul asks the centurion: is it lawful for you to flog a man who is a Roman citizen and uncondemned? The centurion goes to the commander: what are you about to do? For this man is a Roman citizen. The commander asks Paul: tell me, are you a Roman citizen? He says yes. The commander says he bought this citizenship for a large sum; Paul replies he was born a citizen. Those who were about to examine him withdraw; the commander is alarmed that he had bound a Roman citizen. The next day he has Paul brought before the chief priests and the council.

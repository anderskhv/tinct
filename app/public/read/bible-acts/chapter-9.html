Acts 9 of 28

Acts 9

The church's most determined persecutor rides toward Damascus to arrest more Christians and is struck blind by a light he cannot look at and a voice that asks why he is persecuting it.

Summary

Saul, still breathing threats and murder against the disciples, goes to the high priest for letters authorizing him to arrest anyone belonging to the Way in Damascus and bring them to Jerusalem. Near Damascus, a light from heaven suddenly flashes around him and he falls to the ground. A voice says: Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? Who are you, Lord? I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting. Rise and go into the city; you will be told what you must do. The men travelling with him hear the voice but see no one. Saul rises, opens his eyes, and cannot see. They lead him by the hand into Damascus, where he remains blind for three days, neither eating nor drinking. In a vision he sees a man named Ananias coming and restoring his sight.

The Lord tells Ananias in a vision to go to Saul in the street called Straight. Ananias objects: Lord, I have heard about this man — how much evil he has done to your saints in Jerusalem, and he has authority to arrest all who call on your name. The Lord replies: go, for he is a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel, and I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name. Ananias goes, lays his hands on Saul, and says: Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus who appeared to you on the road has sent me so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit. Immediately something like scales falls from Saul's eyes, he regains his sight, rises, and is baptized. He takes food, is strengthened, and within days begins proclaiming in the synagogues that Jesus is the Son of God. Those who hear are astonished: is this not the man who used to make havoc among those who called on this name? Saul increases in strength and confounds the Damascus Jews, proving from their own scriptures that Jesus is the Messiah.

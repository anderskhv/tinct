Acts 27 of 28

Acts 27

Paul sails for Italy under guard in late sailing season; a two-week hurricane drives the ship across the Adriatic; it runs aground on an unknown island and breaks apart, but all 276 people aboard survive.

Summary

When it is decided that they are to sail for Italy, Paul and some other prisoners are handed over to a centurion named Julius of the Augustan Cohort. They sail in a ship from Adramyttium and find an Alexandrian ship bound for Italy at Myra. The winds are unfavourable; they sail with difficulty to Fair Havens on Crete. Paul advises them that the voyage will be disastrous, but the centurion follows the advice of the pilot and owner rather than Paul. Since the harbour is unsuitable for wintering, they decide to sail on and try to reach Phoenix on Crete. A moderate south wind tempts them to press on, but a violent northeastern wind, Euroclydon, strikes the ship. Unable to face the wind, they give way and are driven along. The crew jettisons cargo and then the ship's tackle. When neither sun nor stars appear for many days and the storm rages, all hope of survival is abandoned. Paul stands among them: men, you should have listened to me and not sailed from Crete and incurred this injury and loss. I urge you now to take heart, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship. Last night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood before me and said: do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has granted you all those who sail with you. Take heart, men, for I have faith in God that it will be exactly as I have been told.

After fourteen nights drifting across the Adriatic, the sailors sense they are approaching land. They take soundings and find twenty fathoms, then fifteen, and fearing they will run on rocks, they drop four anchors from the stern and pray for day. The sailors plan to escape from the ship on the pretext of laying anchors from the bow. Paul says to the centurion and soldiers: unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved. The soldiers cut away the ropes of the ship's boat and let it fall. As day comes, Paul urges all to eat: today is the fourteenth day that you have been waiting and going without food. Not a hair will perish from the head of any of you. He takes bread, gives thanks before all, breaks it, and begins to eat. They are all encouraged and eat. There are 276 persons in the ship. They lighten the ship by throwing the wheat into the sea. When day comes, they see a bay with a beach and decide to run the ship ashore there. Striking a shoal, the bow sticks fast; the stern is broken up by the surf. The soldiers plan to kill the prisoners lest any escape. The centurion, wishing to save Paul, prevents this and orders all who can swim to jump first and make for land; the rest to follow on planks or pieces of the ship. So it is that all escape safely to land.

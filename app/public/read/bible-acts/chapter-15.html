Acts 15 of 28

Acts 15

A dispute over circumcision that cannot be settled in Antioch goes to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem; what results is the most consequential decision in the history of the early church.

Summary

Some men come down from Judea to Antioch and teach the brothers that unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved. Paul and Barnabas have no small dissension and debate with them; the church decides to send Paul and Barnabas and some others up to Jerusalem to lay the question before the apostles and elders. They travel through Phoenicia and Samaria, reporting the conversion of the Gentiles and bringing great joy. In Jerusalem they are welcomed by the church and the apostles and elders, and they declare what God has done with them. But some believers who belong to the party of the Pharisees stand up and say: the Gentiles must be circumcised and required to keep the law of Moses. The apostles and elders meet to consider this. After much debate, Peter stands: brothers, you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe. And God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them by giving them the Holy Spirit, just as he did to us; and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith. Why then are you putting God to the test by placing on the neck of the disciples a yoke that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?

The assembly falls silent. They listen to Paul and Barnabas describe the signs and wonders God has done among the Gentiles through them. James speaks: Simeon (Peter) has related how God first visited the Gentiles to take from them a people for his name. This agrees with the words of the prophets, as it is written in Amos: After this I will return, and I will rebuild the tent of David that has fallen. My judgment is that we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God, but should write to them to abstain from the things polluted by idols, and from sexual immorality, and from what has been strangled, and from blood. For from ancient generations Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him. The whole assembly agrees. They choose Judas Barsabbas and Silas to go with Paul and Barnabas to Antioch, bearing a letter: it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to place on you no greater burden than these requirements. In Antioch they read the letter to the congregation, who rejoice at the encouragement. After some time Paul says to Barnabas: let us return and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord. Barnabas wants to take John Mark; Paul refuses, since Mark had deserted them in Pamphylia. The disagreement is so sharp that they part company. Barnabas takes Mark and sails to Cyprus; Paul takes Silas and departs through Syria and Cilicia.

Hamlet · 1603
p. 96 / 214

To be, or not to be, that is the question: whether 'tis nobler in the mind to suffer the slings and arrows of outrageous fortune, or to take arms against a sea of troubles, and by opposing end them?

To live, or to die — that is the real question. Is it nobler to endure the injustices that life hurls at you, or to fight back against them all, and in fighting put them to an end?

To die — to sleep, no more; and by a sleep to say we end the heart-ache and the thousand natural shocks that flesh is heir to: 'tis a consummation devoutly to be wish'd.

To die, to sleep — nothing more — and to say that sleep ends the heartache and the countless shocks that being human forces us to bear: that would be an ending worth longing for.

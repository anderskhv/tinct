Tinct vs Gloss: deep test

I tested 41 actions on the same paper in both products: five passages each for Explain, Simplify, Define, Fact-check, Counterpoint and Ask, three whole-paper questions, three full quizzes and the primer. I scored every answer against a fixed rubric. Averaged over every scored answer, Tinct gets 3.4 out of 5 and Gloss 4.7. The test found nine flaws that the first test did not.

Tested 15 October 2026 against the version live before Coder’s fix PRs, so this is the baseline to measure the fixes against. Tinct was signed in as the test account; Gloss was signed out. I scored all answers myself against a rubric fixed before reading any of them.

How the scoring works

Each answer gets 1 to 5 on five criteria, and its score is the average:

  • Accurate: correct about the paper and the world.
  • Grounded: uses the passage and the paper, and shows where.
  • Useful: helps a reader beyond the text itself.
  • Fits the action: length suits a margin card, and it does what the button says.
  • Speed: time to the last word. 2 s or less scores 5; 4 s scores 4; 7 s scores 3; 12 s scores 2; slower scores 1.

Quiz me has no Gloss counterpart, so it is scored on its own: are the questions on the passage, are they good questions, how good is the marking, and how fast.

New flaws found in this test

  • CriticalDefine gives the wrong meaning almost every time in a technical PDF. It was wrong on 5 of 5 words. The sense check never finds a fit, because the dictionary has no technical senses, and there is no fallback to the meaning in this text.
  • MajorDouble-clicking a hyphenated word defines only half of it. “auto-regressive” became “regressive”, which was then defined as a tax term. Double-click should take the whole hyphenated word.
  • MajorThe book switcher breaks for returning readers. /lab/library-add.js was rebuilt without changing its ?v= tag (still 20261014talkwake). It is cached for a day, and served stale for up to a week. Cached copies point at chunks that no longer exist (ownSwitcher-DpiQjfD1.js and quickBookSwitcher-Dkji_CZ5.js, both 404), so the switcher fails to load. Bump the tag on every build, or derive it from the file’s hash.
  • MajorSimplify’s opening line describes instead of simplifying. Every answer starts “The passage explains…”. That is what our prompt asks for, but it reads worse than Gloss, which leads with the plain version. Change the prompt so the first sentence is the simplest faithful restatement.
  • MajorExplain breaks its own limits every time: 5 of 5 ran four paragraphs, and most added a “Notation” paragraph.
  • MinorFact-check repeats its verdict. In three of five, the bottom line (“Holds. …”) is followed by a first paragraph that starts “Holds.” again.
  • MinorCounterpoint invents evidence. In one of five, the author’s reply cited findings the paper does not contain. Add to the prompt: “Give the author’s reply only from what the document says or plainly implies.”
  • MinorThe whole-paper card opens empty, then fills. When it opens, it shows no messages for a moment while the earlier conversation loads, so a reader may think their history is gone.
  • MinorQuiz me counts “Show answer” as wrong without saying so. The end card reads “0 of 3” even after an answer was revealed. Say “2 of 3 answered, 1 shown”, or leave revealed questions out of the score.

Flaws confirmed again

  • Whole-paper questions look at one page: 1 of 3 answers here, and both in the first test.
  • Markdown and em dashes: asterisks in three answers, bullet lists in two, em dashes in most.
  • Quiz question 1 is off the selection: 3 of 3 quizzes.
  • Speed: Tinct takes 3 to 12 seconds where Gloss takes 1 to 3. PDF Explain still goes through the slow path.
  • Primer: it covers only the first section, and its cautions describe our excerpt.

Where Tinct already does well

  • Fact-check content has improved. It now flags outdated claims and names the right later research.
  • Counterpoints are strong. They are well-informed objections with a fair reply, and better argued than most of Gloss’s, though less specific.
  • Ask answers are thorough and correct, just too long for a card.
  • Quiz marking is genuinely good teaching. No other product here has it.

What this adds to the fix plan

  1. Define. When no dictionary sense fits, show the meaning in this text first, and select whole hyphenated words. This now outranks most items in PR C.
  2. Book switcher. Fix the stale cache tag on library-add.js and give every lazily loaded engine a tag tied to its content.
  3. Simplify. The first sentence becomes the plain restatement itself.
  4. Prompt tightening. Hold Explain to three paragraphs, no notation paragraph and about 120 words. Fact-check states its verdict once. Counterpoint takes the author’s reply only from the document. Ask labels speculation as such, as Gloss does (“the paper does not explain why”).
  5. Quiz me. Count “Show answer” separately on the end card.

Re-run this same test after each fix PR. The test set, passages and scripts are saved, so the next scores are directly comparable with these.

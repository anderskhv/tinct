The new PDF reader, retested

I ran the same tests again on the reader as it went live today (release trains 1 and 2, plus #455 and #456). When an answer loads, Tinct now scores 4.1 out of 5 on the Attention paper and 4.2 on the GAN paper, up from 3.4 and 3.8. Gloss scored 4.7 and 4.6 on the same tests. The biggest gains are in the primer, whole-paper questions, Explain and scans. The new problem is reliability: 7 of about 70 actions ended with “The answer could not be loaded”.

Tested 15 October 2026, evening, on the live site with the test account flagged internal. The Gloss scores are the baselines from this morning; Gloss has not changed. Same passages, same questions, same rubric (accurate, grounded, useful, fits the action, speed).

The new problem: answers that do not load

7 of about 70 actions ended with “The answer could not be loaded. Try again.” That was 1 on Attention and 6 on GAN (two Simplify, one Define, two Fact-check, one Ask, plus one quiz that never opened). The browser logged 502 /api/chat. I was running up to three browsers at once, so at most about three requests at the same time, which the Worker should handle easily. Nothing retries automatically, so the reader just sees a failed card.

  • Retry once automatically on a 502 or a dropped stream before showing the error.
  • Log the cause on the server: upstream overload, a timeout, or the new cache-prefix requests.

Lookups got faster, but the main-model actions got slower. Fact-check and Counterpoint run 3–15 s over their targets, and Fact-check still waits for the search before it writes anything. The likely causes are the higher effort settings on Sonnet 5.5 and the whole-document prompt. Worth checking usage.cache_read_input_tokens to see whether the cache is actually being hit.

Still leaking: wording and plumbing

  • Pipeline wording: “the text I have”, “falls past the text I have”, “the supplied text”, “the search note I drew on”.
  • Raw citation tags: “{p. 1}”, “{pp. 5}”, “(p. 2)” in Define and Explain, and the merged “p. 5p. 6”.
  • Self-reference in the whole-PDF thread: “My first answer cited page 445, which was wrong”, “Same question as before”. Honest, but it reads like a log.
  • Fact-check verdict stated twice (“Partly holds.” then “Partly holds.”).

Your UI notes, checked live

  • Fixed: nothing has the typing focus when the reader opens, so there is no blinking cursor until you click into a field. The “Your own book” pop-up has one button and an × (#455).
  • Not built yet (in Coder’s batch): the whole-PDF bar is still there; the primer appears at full size in one go; cards have no delete button; “Tinct text” still shows instead of Aa; the scrollbar sits between the page and the margin.
  • New: dragging over a figure caption now brings up the figure chip instead of the bar, so you can no longer highlight or note a caption.

What Coder should do next

  1. Reliability first. Retry once on a 502 or a dropped stream, and log the cause. Aim for zero “could not be loaded” in a full test run.
  2. Speed of the main model. Get Fact-check’s first words under 4 s, by writing from the document while the search runs, and Counterpoint and Quiz under 5 s. Check the cache hit rate, and lower effort to low for Counterpoint and Quiz if it is higher.
  3. Strip the pipeline wording in the prompts (“text I have”, “supplied”, “search note”), and render or strip every citation tag form: {p. N}, {pp. N}, (p. N), and no merged “p. 5p. 6”.
  4. Define: run the “In this text” check even when the dictionary has the word (ensemble, perplexity, saturate). Drop the novel-character wording for people’s names.
  5. Simplify: never return the passage unchanged, and no meta closing line.
  6. Long books: when search finds a section, send its full text (several pages), not only its first lines. “Go to page” should accept the printed number.
  7. Figures: fix the partial Figure 2 region and the Table 8/Figure 6 mix-up. Attach a figure named in a question even when it is on another page. Detect diagrams with text labels (TD-Gammon). Let a caption be highlighted.

Tinct is now close to Gloss on answer quality, and ahead of it on figures, scans with the margin, whole-paper reasoning and long books. Gloss still leads on speed and reliability. Those two, plus the leaking wording, are what stand between us and parity.

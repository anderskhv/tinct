Literature

  • A literary PDF opens in the book reader by default (“Tinct text”), so readers do not get the margin there unless they switch layout. The EPUB also opens in the book reader: one-line Explains in about 0.4 s from the old popup (Explain, Chat, Highlight, Copy), and no Simplify, Define, Fact-check or Quiz me.
  • Metamorphosis (71 pages, in the margin): whole-book answers are excellent. They trace Grete across all three parts with pages, explain who the three gentlemen are and why they leave, and correctly name the father as the one who threw the apple. Define gave vermin, plaintively and intercede in their story senses.
  • The fiction gaps: Simplify pastes the text back; Fact-check opens with “there is no factual claim”; Counterpoint sometimes agrees with the passage; and the Explain notation template leaks into stories.
  • Ligatures: PDFs set with ligatures (ﬁ, ﬂ, ﬀ) put those characters into the text layer (“inﬂuence”). Answers were not affected, but a search or quote may be.

Phones (390 × 844, touch)

  • Works well. Selecting text brings up the bar just under the selection, with 40-pixel-high buttons. Answers open as bottom sheets, More is a clean menu, Define is a dark card with “In this text”, the quiz opens in a sheet, and the primer comes up from below. The layout check found no overlaps and nothing off-screen.
  • Speed on the phone matched the desktop: Explain 2.5–3.7 s, Simplify under 2 s, Fact-check’s first words 2.5 s, Ask 7–8 s.
  • Whole document: it lives under t menu → “Librarian” (5.7 s, correct). The release notes call it Chat, so readers looking for “Chat” will not find it.
  • PDF pages are too small to read on a phone at fit-to-width. On phones, Tinct text or a zoomed column view should probably be the default.
  • Header: the title truncates (“Attentio…”), and the page pill collapses to an empty “∨”.
  • Figures on the phone: tapping a figure brings up the chip (Explain, Chat, Highlight, Copy). Good.
  • Two devices at once: the phone and desktop runs on the same book produced 409 conflicts on cards and places. No cards were lost, but it is worth confirming that the conflict path adopts the server copy without a visible error.

What Coder should do next

  1. Simplify: never return the passage or the following sentences unchanged, and never print the same line twice. Fiction gets one plain restatement plus, at most, one line on what it means.
  2. Fiction prompts:
    • Fact-check: no “there is no factual claim” opener. Check the real-world references, or say in one line that there is nothing outside the story to check.
    • Counterpoint: give an alternative reading that differs from the obvious one, with no “author’s reply” template.
    • Explain: drop the notation paragraph for prose.
  3. Fact-check length: drop the opening “The passage claims… the document offers…” summary. Start with the verdict, which is already streaming fast.
  4. Quiz me speed: show the first question in under 5 s (it is 5–32 s now); stream it as planned.
  5. Accuracy: the Table 1 complexity slip (self-attention is O(n²·d)). Remove the leaks “The image confirms this passage reads as printed” and the raw “sec. 3” tag.
  6. Phones: name the whole-document chat consistently (Chat or Librarian), make PDF pages readable on a phone, and fix the header title and page pill.
  7. Still open from before: long-book search sends the full section; “Go to page” takes printed numbers; the figure items (Figure 2 region, Table 8/Figure 6, other-page figures, TD-Gammon); reading order around figures.

Where this leaves the normal-reader question: reliability and Fact-check speed, the two blockers from this evening, are fixed. What remains is prompt polish, Quiz me’s speed and phone gaps. A novel in the margin works well apart from items 1–2. One more clean round and the new menu is ready to try in the book reader for Anders and the test accounts.

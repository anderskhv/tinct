Scans, figures and long books: Tinct vs Gloss

These are the three cases the earlier tests did not cover. Tinct is clearly ahead on figures and long books, and clearly behind on scans. Gloss shows no figures and will not open the long book without the reader’s own paid key, while its scans just work. Each case also turned up bugs on our side; the worst is that a scanned PDF loses the margin even after its text has been recognised.

Tested 15 October 2026 on the live site, before Coder’s fix PRs. Files: “Deep Residual Learning” (ResNet, 12 pages, 7 vector figures), the GAN paper turned into an image-only scan (9 pages), and Sutton and Barto’s “Reinforcement Learning” (548 pages, 73 MB). Tinct used the test account (which has the trial); Gloss was signed out. I scored the answers myself.

Figures

Figure bugs in Tinct

  • MajorThe chip runs off the page. On Figure 2 the chip sat at the left edge, so “Explain” was off-screen and could not be clicked. Detection had also found only the lower part of the diagram.
  • MajorDiagrams with text labels go undetected. The TD-Gammon network (Figure 16.1 in the long book) gets no chip anywhere on the page. Detection blanks out every text span of four or more letters, and diagram labels such as “hidden units” are text spans, so the diagram falls apart into pieces too small to count.
  • MajorFigure answers go to the whole-PDF card. There is no card by the figure, no thumbnail, and every figure shares one thread. That shared thread leaks: one answer opened “This is Figure 7, the one you asked about earlier”, about a figure never asked about before.
  • MajorThe two other routes cannot see figures. Selecting the caption and asking, or asking a whole-PDF question about “Figure 1”, sends no picture, and the answer says so. Turning passage pictures on (PR A item 4) will not fix this, because the crop is the selected caption, not the figure.
  • MinorOne misreading in Explain: it said ResNet-34 drops below ResNet-18 “after the second rate drop around iteration 30”. The plot shows it below from early on.

What this adds to the fix plan

Scans (a new PR D, after PR B)

  1. First-open race. The status call answers 404 before the account copy exists, and the reader treats that as recognition being switched off. Answer { state: "none" } for a book that is not stored yet, or ask again once “Saved to your account” fires. Never show the “can’t be read aloud” line while the switch is on.
  2. Recognised scans stay in the PDF reader with the margin. Lay the recognised words over the page as a text layer (the account already has the page images and the blocks), so selection, the margin bar, the primer and whole-PDF questions all work on the scan itself. Until then, at least give the primer and whole-PDF questions the recognised text.
  3. Keep captions, algorithm boxes and footnotes in the recognised text, as their own kinds. Split chapters at every numbered section heading.

Figures (PR B)

  1. A figure card by the figure: the thumbnail at the top, a leader line to the figure, and a thread of its own per figure. Never the whole-PDF card.
  2. Detection. Keep short label text inside an ink area when deciding what counts as a figure, and detect figures that sit beside text. Fallback: hovering over a “Figure N” or “Table N” caption offers the figure above or beside it.
  3. Keep the chip inside the page and the screen, flipping sides when needed. Add a figure at the left page edge to the layout check.
  4. Send the figure with figure questions. When the selection is a caption, or a question names “Figure N” or “Fig. N” on the current page, attach that figure’s image, not only the caption crop.

Long books (PR A and B)

  1. Above the whole-document limit, send the current chapter plus a contents list with page numbers. Also search the book’s text for the question’s terms and include the best few passages with their pages, so “where is Sarsa introduced” gets a page. Say “from general knowledge” whenever the answer is not from the book.
  2. Primer scope: the chapter the reader is in when they open it. Skip front matter, references and the index, using the nearest real chapter instead, and never make a primer of an index.
  3. Printed page numbers: when a PDF has no page labels, read the number printed in the page header or footer and use it in cards, jumps and the header.
  4. Reading order around figures: keep figure labels and captions out of the body text flow, so selections and AI context read in order.
  5. Large-file open: 6.8 s to reopen a 73 MB book. Show page 1 from the device’s stored copy before the whole file has loaded.

Two decisions for Anders

  • Recognise small scans automatically? Gloss does it for everyone. We ask first, and only on the trial or Premium. A 9-page scan costs very little, so I would recognise scans of up to about 20 pages automatically for signed-in readers within the monthly page allowance, and keep asking for bigger ones.
  • Book-length is where we can lead. Gloss stops at 20 pages without the reader’s own key. Items 8 and 9 (search the book for answers, and a primer of the right chapter) would make whole-book questions work where Gloss does not.

Reading in the margin

Second pass. The PDF stays exactly as published, with its figures and equations. Around it: a shorter selection menu, a definition when you pick a single word, answers and notes as cards in the margin, a primer you open from the margin, and cards that sit near the text on the phone.

1. The selection menu

Ask, Explain, Simplify, more, highlight, note. Explain and Simplify are words, not icons. Highlight is a dot in your current colour; the note is a folded page, so the two never look alike.

A · Selected
Six items, no Talk. Start typing and the words go straight into Ask.
B · Asking
The bar becomes the question, with a mic to say it instead of typing it. Return sends; the answer opens as a card in the margin.
C · More
Define, Fact-check, Counterpoint, Quiz me, then Copy quote and Share as image. Related is gone. One line each on desktop.
D · Highlight
One tap on the dot highlights in your colour. Press and hold, or tap it again, to choose another; the choice becomes the new default.
E · A single word
Pick one word and you get the dictionary first, so it works offline. The sense that fits this text comes first and is set darker. The menu sits along the bottom, for when you wanted something else.

2. The PDF reader, with a margin

The page keeps its place on the left. Answers, explanations and notes become cards in the margin, each tied to its passage by a thin line. One card is open at a time; the rest fold to a line or two.

3. When cards crowd together

Five cards on one stretch of page. Cards keep the order of their passages and push down rather than overlap. The open card keeps its full height. When the column runs out of room, the rest gather into one row at the bottom; clicking a passage brings its card up.

The line to the open card is full strength; lines to folded cards are fainter, so your eye finds the conversation you are in.

4. Before you start

At the start of a file or a chapter, the margin offers one folded card: “Before you start”. Click it, or press B, and it opens into the primer in the same place. Close it and it folds back.

5. On the phone

The menu now sits next to the text, below the selection so it clears the phone’s own copy menu above it. More is a short list of names. Answers and the chat itself open as a card along the bottom.

A · Selected
Right under the selection, with the same six items as desktop.
B · More
Names only, no explanations, opening beside the bar.
C · An answer
A card along the bottom, the passage still underlined above it. Swipe up for the full thread.
D · Chat
Chat as a card too, instead of taking the whole screen. The page stays in view; swipe up when the conversation grows.

6. The new answers

Every action opens the same card in the margin (a bottom card on the phone). What changes is what sits under the quote. Each answer starts with a bottom line in medium weight.

Simplify
Simplify · p. 12×
These results are easiest to obtain by noting that r is the sum of N independent random variables…

Think of the count of heads as ten tiny counts added up, one per toss. Each toss adds 0 or 1. Because the tosses do not affect each other, their spreads simply add, so the total spread is N times the spread of one toss.

No question bubble: the action is the question. The rewrite keeps every claim and its order.
Fact-check
Fact-check · p. 12×
x! ≃ xx e−x √(2πx)

Holds. This is the standard form of Stirling’s approximation.

  • Holds. The relative error falls below 1% from x = 9 and keeps shrinking as x grows.
  • Note. Many texts write the cruder x! ≃ xx e−x; this page includes the √(2πx) term, which matters for small x.

Verdict words, not colours: Holds, Partly holds, Outdated, Disputed, Can’t check, and Note for context. Sources as plain links. While searching, the card says “Checking sources…”.
Counterpoint
Counterpoint · p. 12×
Each toss has mean f and variance f(1 − f).

The model assumes every toss has the same f, and real data rarely does. If f drifts from toss to toss, or tosses influence each other, the spread of r is wider than Nf(1 − f), and the binomial understates the uncertainty.

The author could answer that the binomial is a model to be tested against data, not a claim about every coin.

The strongest objection, then the author’s best reply. For a story rather than an argument, it gives the strongest other reading.

The rules

The PDFAlways the original pages, tinted to the reader’s look as today. No re-flowing, so figures, equations and layout survive. Selection uses the PDF’s own text layer.
Selection menu44px bar, 12px corners, ink fill in every look, system font 14px. Ask with a caret, Explain, Simplify, more, a dot in the current highlight colour, note. Typing with text selected goes into Ask. Ask shows a mic for speaking the question. No Talk in the bar.
Single wordA one-word selection (double-click, or tap on the phone) opens Define first, from Tinct’s offline dictionary: the word, its part of speech and up to three senses. When online, the sense that fits the passage is moved to the top and set in full ink; offline, the dictionary’s order stands. Names and words the dictionary lacks fall back to an AI meaning when online. The menu runs along the card’s foot.
MoreDefine, Fact-check, Counterpoint, Quiz me, then Copy quote and Share as image. Desktop shows a one-line explanation under each; the phone shows names only. Define on a longer selection looks up its key term in the dictionary.
HighlightA tap highlights in the last colour used. A second tap or a long press opens the four colours; the one chosen becomes the default.
Margin column380px right of the page, 56px gap. Cards line up with their passage, keep the page’s order, and push down instead of overlapping, 14px apart. One open card at a time; opening another folds the last.
CrowdingIf the cards no longer fit above the column foot, the remaining ones gather into a single row, “2 more on this page”. Clicking a passage, or the row, brings its card up and folds the others.
Open cardThe shared chat thread inside a card: the passage as a small italic quote, the question as a bubble, the answer plain with its first sentence in medium weight. Page and equation tags are clickable. Copy and close only; a follow-up line at the foot with a mic.
Leader line1px from a 3px dot at the page’s edge, beside the passage, to the card. Full strength for the open card, about half for folded ones.
PrimerOffered as one folded card at the top of the margin at the start of a file or chapter, and with B anywhere. Opens in place; made once and kept with the file.
PhoneThe bar opens just below the selection, with its tip pointing up at it. More is a list of names beside the bar. Answers and chat open as a bottom card at about half height; swipe up for full height.
Narrow windowsBelow about 1180px the column hides. Cards become marks at the page’s right edge and open as a floating card.
NeverNo re-rendered text in place of the PDF, no monospace, no capitals-only labels, no colour beyond the highlight colours. Icons are our own: no bulb, feather or highlighter-pen shapes.

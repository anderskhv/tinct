Reading in the margin

A proposal for the PDF reader first, then the book reader. The PDF stays exactly as published, with its figures and equations, so nothing is lost in a re-render. What changes is the frame around it: a new selection menu, answers and notes as cards in the margin tied to their passage, and a primer before you start. The chat thread that shipped last week lives on inside each card.

1. The selection menu

Select text and the bar appears above it. Start typing and the words go straight into Ask; no click needed. Explain and Simplify answer in one tap. Talk opens a voice conversation about the passage. The rest sits behind the dots.

A · Selected
Ask, Explain, Simplify, Talk, more, highlight, note. The caret after Ask shows that typing works at once. Replaces today’s Explain, Chat, Highlight, Copy, Share.
B · Typing
The bar becomes the question. Return sends; the answer opens as a card in the margin. Escape puts the bar back.
C · More
Six more ways in, each one line of explanation. Quiz me is new: three questions on the passage, answered in the card. Share as image is the quote card that already ships.
D · Highlight
The colours open in place of the bar. One tap highlights in the last colour used; the pen beside it adds a note. Tinct’s four colours, unchanged.

2. The PDF reader, with a margin

The page keeps its place on the left. The docked chat becomes a margin column of cards, each tied to its passage by a thin line. One card is open at a time; the others fold to a line or two and stay beside their passage as you scroll.

Three kinds of card. A folded Simplify card at the example, an open Ask card at the passage on variances, and your own note at Stirling’s formula with its highlight colour. The bottom of the column asks about the whole PDF; press / to start typing there.

3. Before you start

Opening a PDF or a new chapter offers a primer at the top of the margin: the gist, the key claims, what to keep in mind, a map with page numbers, and Quiz me at the end. It is made once and kept with the file. Press B to open it from anywhere.

4. On the phone

There is no room for a margin, so the bar docks above your thumb and a card opens as a short sheet. The page scrolls so the passage sits just above the sheet, still marked.

A · Selected
The bar sits at the bottom, not over the text, so it never fights the phone’s own copy menu. Tapping Ask opens the keyboard with the passage attached.
B · More
The dots open the same six choices as a sheet. Same words, same order as on desktop.
C · An answer
The card is a short sheet. Swipe it up for the full thread; swipe down to return to the page with the passage still underlined.

The rules

The PDFAlways the original pages, tinted to the reader’s look as today. No re-flowing, so figures, equations and layout survive. Selection uses the PDF’s own text layer.
Selection menu44px bar, 12px corners, ink fill in every look (a raised brown-black in Night), system font 14px. Items: Ask with a caret, Explain, Simplify, Talk, more, highlight, note. Typing with text selected goes into Ask. Above the selection, or below it near the top of the screen.
MoreDefine, Fact-check, Related, Counterpoint, Quiz me, then Copy quote and Share as image. Each with a one-line explanation. Fact-check is the only one that searches the web.
Margin column380px to the right of the page, 56px gap. Cards line up with their passage and push down to avoid overlap. One open card at a time; opening another folds the last. A folded card shows its label and two lines.
Open cardThe shared chat thread inside a card: the passage as a small italic quote, the question as a bubble, the answer plain. The answer’s first sentence is set in medium weight as the bottom line. Page and equation numbers appear as small tags the reader can click. Copy and close only; a follow-up line with a caret at the foot.
Leader line1px, ink at about a third. Starts with a 3px dot at the end of the passage and runs to the card’s edge. The passage gets a matching underline while its card is open.
Whole PDFThe foot of the column asks about the whole file (slash key). Its answer opens as a card at the top of the column, labelled Whole PDF, and its history is the full chat thread.
Narrow windowsBelow about 1180px wide the column hides. Cards become small marks at the page’s right edge and open as a floating card on click.
KeysType to ask, slash for the whole PDF, B for the primer, N for notes, Escape to close or step back.
NeverNo re-rendered text in place of the PDF, no monospace, no capitals-only labels, no accent colour beyond the reader’s highlight colours.

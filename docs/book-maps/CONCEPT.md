## What it is

A **Map** tab next to Contents and Notebook shows the whole book as parts and stops, through one lens: what problem the author is solving here.

**Each stop:**
- **The problem:** one line.
- **You will meet:** the key move, idea or scene.
- **Watch for:** the trap or the payoff.
- **Difficulty:** Easy, Steady or Demanding, with a reason.
- **Time:** at the reader's own pace, from the reading card.
- **Essential:** a mark on the chapters most worth reading.

**The book:** the question behind the book, the overall difficulty, and the time left at your pace.

**Your interactions with the book:**
- **Status:** Read, Summarized, You are here, or Ahead.
- **What you did:** highlights, notes and Quiz me scores per chapter.
- **A chapter you have read:** opens to your highlights, your note, Recap and Read again.

**Choosing how to read:**
- **A stop ahead** offers "Read it", "Summarize instead" and "Read this next". Summarizing marks the stop half-filled, and the chapter stays one tap away ("Read it anyway", "Ask about it").
- **Routes:**
  - **Whole book.**
  - **The essentials:** a curated set and order, e.g. The Prince starting at chapter 15.
  - **My order:** the reader's own order, built with "Read this next".

## Rules

- **Fiction:** no spoilers. Stops ahead show only the setup and what the author is doing (introducing, complicating, turning), never what happens later. Summaries only for chapters behind the reader, or chapters the reader explicitly summarizes.
- **Content:** maps are written in sessions and reviewed like editions (zero API spend). They are stored per book and edition; when the edition's text changes, the map is checked again.
- **Free for everyone.** The AI follow-ups (Ask, Recap) keep their usual plan.
- **Reading position:** jumping from the map never loses the reader's place. "Read it" opens at the chapter start; going back returns to where they were.

## Samples inspected (7), spanning Books 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10

### 1. Opening — Book 1 ¶0–4 (the Chief Good, the hierarchy of ends)

SRC ¶0: *"Every art, and every science reduced to a teachable form… aims, it is thought, at some
good: for which reason a common and by no means a bad description of the Chief Good is, 'that which
all things aim at.'"*
MOD ¶0: *"Every art and every systematic branch of knowledge, along with every action and deliberate
choice, seems to aim at some good. This is why people often describe the highest good as 'that at
which all things aim.'"*

SRC ¶1: *"for in some cases they are acts of working, and in others certain works or tangible results
beyond and beside the acts of working"*
MOD ¶1: *"In some cases the end is the activity itself; in others, it is some product beyond the
activity."*

SRC ¶2: *"as under that of horsemanship the art of making bridles, and all that are connected with
the manufacture of horse-furniture in general"*
MOD ¶2: *"as bridle-making and other equipment-making fall under horsemanship"*

**Finding — strong.** "science reduced to a teachable form" → "systematic branch of knowledge" and
"acts of working" → "the activity itself" are exactly the right move: Chase's Victorian coinages for
*epistêmê* and *energeia* replaced with accurate familiar equivalents, no commentary added. Every
example in the ends-list (medicine/health, shipbuilding/ship, strategy/victory, household
management/wealth) is preserved. The technical term *choice-worthy* is retained rather than
paraphrased away — correct; it is a term of art the reader meets repeatedly.

### 2. Book 2 ¶30–35 (virtue as a state; the doctrine of the mean)

SRC ¶32: *"Since then the virtues are neither Feelings nor Capacities, it remains that they must be
States."*
MOD ¶32: *"Since the virtues are neither feelings nor capacities, it remains that they must be
states."*

SRC ¶34: *"the excellence of Man, i.e. Virtue, must be a state whereby Man comes to be good and
whereby he will perform well his proper work"*
MOD ¶34: *"the excellence of the human being — that is, virtue — must be a state that makes him good
and enables him to perform his proper function well"*

SRC ¶35: *"the exactly equal is a mean between excess and defect… suppose ten is too much and two too
little, people take six for the absolute mean"*
MOD ¶35: *"The equal part is a mean between excess and deficiency… If ten is too much and two is too
little, for example, people take six as the absolute mean"*

**Finding — strong, and directly answers the batch's hardest question.** The feelings/capacities/
states trichotomy (*pathê / dunameis / hexeis*) is preserved intact with Chase's own English terms,
not over-explained and not dropped. "defect" → "deficiency" is the model of a point-of-need fix: a
word whose 1847 sense ("falling short") would misread today as "flaw", swapped for the accurate
familiar equivalent, with no gloss added. The 10 / 6 / 2 arithmetic example survives exactly.

### 3. Book 3 ¶0–3 (voluntary and involuntary; the tyrant; goods overboard)

SRC ¶1: *"being such that in it the agent (perhaps we may more properly say the patient) contributes
nothing"*
MOD ¶1: *"such that the agent (or, better, the patient) contributes nothing"*

SRC ¶2: *"if you were ordered to commit some base act by a despot who had your parents or children in
his power"*
MOD ¶2: *"if a tyrant holding your parents or children ordered you to commit some shameful act"*

SRC ¶3: *"with a view to his own and his shipmates' safety any one would who had any sense"*
MOD ¶3: *"with a view to his own and his shipmates' safety, any sensible man would"*

**Finding — strong.** The agent/patient technical distinction — easy to lose, load-bearing for the
whole chapter — is kept. Wind, tyrant, hostages, jettison: all concrete cases preserved. No
interpretive additions about coercion or moral luck, which would be the obvious temptation here.

### 4. Book 5 ¶20–24 (universal vs. particular justice — the densest argument in the book)

SRC ¶23: *"And as the unequal and the more are not the same, but differing as part to whole (because
all more is unequal, but not all unequal more), so the Unjust and the Injustice we are now in search
of are not the same with, but other than, those before mentioned, the one being the parts, the other
the wholes"*
MOD ¶23: *"Just as the unequal and the more are not the same, but related as part to whole (all
'more' is unequal, but not all unequal is 'more'), the unjust and injustice we are now looking for
are not the same as, but other than, those previously mentioned. They are the parts; those were the
wholes."*

**Finding — good, with the batch's characteristic residue.** The part/whole logic and the
"co-extensive with virtue" phrasing survive exactly; the quotation marks added around *'more'*
genuinely help the reader parse the sentence. But this passage remains hard on first read — Chase's
scaffolding is intact, and no help is offered for *co-extensive*. This is the honest limit of the
edition: it removes Victorian awkwardness but does not make Aristotle's hardest arguments easy. Per
our standard that is the correct trade (*"simplify old vocabulary and tangled syntax without
simplifying away the ideas"*), but it means "clarity 5" is not available.

### 5. Book 6 ¶40–44 (sophia vs. phronêsis — a correction to Chase)

SRC ¶41: *"It is plain then that **Science** is the union of Knowledge and Intuition… Accordingly,
**Anexagoras**, Thales, and men of that stamp, people call **Scientific**, but not Practically
Wise"*
MOD ¶41: *"It is plain, then, that **philosophic wisdom** is the union of scientific knowledge and
intuition… Accordingly people call **Anaxagoras**, Thales, and men of that stamp **philosophically
wise**, but not practically wise"*

SRC ¶44: *"Further: **πολιτικὴ** and Practical Wisdom are the same mental state"*
MOD ¶44: *"Further, **political science** and practical wisdom are the same mental state"*

**Finding — a real strength, and the sharpest editorial judgement in my batch.** Chase renders
*sophia* as "Science", which in 2026 is actively misleading — a reader will think laboratory science.
The modern edition corrects it to "philosophic wisdom", the standard modern rendering, keeping the
*sophia / phronêsis* contrast legible. It also silently fixes Chase's typo "Anexagoras" and
translates a stray untranslated Greek word Chase left in the body text. This is informed
modernization, not mechanical substitution — and it is done without adding a word of commentary.

### 6. Book 7 ¶10–13 and Book 8 ¶8–12 (akrasia; the objects of friendship)

SRC 7¶13: *"it is a strange thing, as Socrates thought, that while Knowledge is present in his mind
something else should master him and drag him about like a slave"*
MOD 7¶13: *"Socrates thought it strange that, while knowledge is present in the mind, something else
should master it and drag it about like a slave"*

SRC 8¶8: *"'The earth by drought consumed doth love the rain, And the great heaven, overcharged with
rain, Doth love to fall in showers upon the earth.'"*
MOD 8¶8: *"'The earth by drought consumed doth love the rain, and the great heaven, overcharged with
rain, doth love to fall in showers upon the earth.'"*

SRC 8¶9: *"Heraclitus, again, maintains, that 'contrariety is expedient…'"*
MOD 8¶9: *"Heraclitus holds that 'opposition is expedient…'"*

**Finding — good.** The dragged-like-a-slave image is preserved rather than explained (the standard's
"images replaced by explanations" trap, avoided). *Imperfect Self-Control* — Chase's rendering of
*akrasia* — is retained, correctly: it is already a transparent English gloss, and replacing it with
"akrasia" would raise the barrier. Note that the **Euripides verse quotation is deliberately left
archaic** (*doth love*) while the surrounding prose is modernized. That is a defensible convention
(quoted verse stays quoted verse), but it is worth confirming it is applied consistently across the
book; I did not check every quotation.

### 7. Ending — Book 10 ¶118–123 (the turn to the Politics)

SRC ¶118 and MOD ¶118 are **byte-identical** (46 words) — this is the single identical long
paragraph the Phase 1 scan found. Inspected: Chase's sentence there is already plain, so leaving it
is defensible rather than lazy.

SRC ¶119: *"they who would acquire a knowledge of Statesmanship must have in addition practice"*
MOD ¶119: *"those who would acquire a knowledge of statesmanship must, **in addition to theory**,
have practice"*

SRC ¶120: *"neither would they have conceived it to be **'an easy matter to legislate by simply
collecting such laws as are famous because of course one could select the best,'**"*
MOD ¶120: *"nor would they have thought it easy to legislate by simply collecting the famous laws and
selecting the best"*

SRC ¶123: *"…what kind of Constitution is best, what kind of regulations are best for each, and what
laws and customs."*
MOD ¶123: *"…what kind of constitution is best, what regulations are best for each, and what laws and
customs."*

**Finding — two very small blemishes at the end of the book.** (a) ¶119 adds *"in addition to
theory"*, which is not in Chase; it is a fair inference from context but it is an addition, and the
standard says not to add explanatory material. (b) ¶120 drops Chase's quotation marks around the
sophists' claim, so what the source presents as a *quoted opinion being attacked* reads as the
narrator's own paraphrase. Both are one-line fixes. Everything substantive — the compilations/
physicians analogy, the music and painting comparisons, the programme for the Politics — is intact.

## Phase 3 — human-edition research

The central question for this book is whether **W. D. Ross** should replace Chase.

**Candidate A — W. D. Ross, *Ethica Nicomachea*.**
- Widely regarded as the standard scholarly public-domain-era English Nicomachean Ethics.
- **Publication date verified as 1925, not 1908.** *The Classical Review* (Cambridge Core) records:
  *"The Nicomachean Ethics — The Works of Aristotle (translated into English): Ethica Nicomachea. By
  W. D. Ross… Oxford: At the Clarendon Press, 1925."* Ross dated his own translator's note July 1925.
  It is volume IX of the Oxford *Works of Aristotle Translated into English*.
- **The "1908" claim circulating online is wrong.** `en.wikisource.org/wiki/Talk:Nicomachean_Ethics_
  (Ross)` states only: *"There are two publication dates listed (1908 and 1925) for Ross's
  translation. This copy states 1908 so it is indeed in the public domain."* That reasoning does not
  hold up — 1908 is the date of Ross's *Metaphysics* translation (vol. VIII), not the Ethics. Anyone
  relying on the Wikisource banner is relying on an unverified assertion.
- **Rights — split by jurisdiction, and this is the decisive point:**
  - **United States:** published 1925 → entered the public domain **1 January 2021** (95 years from
    publication). PD in the US today.
  - **Denmark / EU:** term is life + 70. Sir William David Ross was born 15 April 1877 and **died
    5 May 1971** (confirmed: Oriel College Oxford, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Wikipedia).
    Ross's translation therefore remains in copyright in Denmark and the EU **until 1 January 2042**.
- **Assessment: NOT usable.** Tinct operates from Denmark and distributes globally; a text that is
  PD in the US and under copyright in the operator's own jurisdiction for another 16 years is not
  rights-clear for our use. Widely available free copies (Batoche Books PDF, Internet Classics
  Archive, Wikisource) do not change this — free online access is not permission to redistribute,
  and several of those hosts are themselves relying on the erroneous 1908 date.
- Rights status: **permission required** (EU/DK) / public domain (US only). Recorded as **unclear —
  jurisdictionally split**, resolved against use.

**Candidate B — F. H. Peters (1881).** PD worldwide (Peters d. 1921). Complete. A common Victorian
alternative to Chase. I did **not** fetch and read a sample, so I record it as **unverified** on
readability — I am not claiming it is better or worse than Chase, only that it exists and is
rights-clear.

**Candidate C — D. P. Chase (our current core text).** PD worldwide. Complete. Already in place.

**Modern translations not viable:** Terence Irwin (Hackett, 1985/1999), Roger Crisp (Cambridge,
2000), C. D. C. Reeve (Hackett, 2014), Bartlett & Collins (Chicago, 2011) — all fully in copyright,
permission required.

**Conclusion:** no rights-clear human English edition available to a Denmark-based distributor
clearly beats the current pairing of Chase + this modern-en. The one candidate that would (Ross) is
blocked in our home jurisdiction until 2042.

## Recommendation

**KEEP CURRENT MODERN EDITION** — confidence **medium**.

Honest statement of coverage: 7 passages, roughly 3,400 source words of 92,325 (~3.7%), drawn from
Books 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10. I found no substantive omission or invention in any of them, and
the quality was consistent across all seven — including in the two hardest places I could find
(Book 5's part/whole justice argument, Book 6's *sophia/phronêsis* distinction). That is a strong
result **in samples**. It is not a verification of all 1,195 paragraphs, and I am not claiming one.

**Correction scope: none** for the translation itself.

Two optional tidies, neither blocking: revert the *"in addition to theory"* addition at 10¶119 and
restore the quotation framing at 10¶120; and repair the ~21 mid-sentence paragraph breaks, which are
a data-structure artifact of the Gutenberg source rather than a translation defect.

## Limitations of this review

- ~3.7% of the text read at paragraph level. Books 4 and 9 were not sampled at all.
- English-to-English only. I did not check either edition against the Greek. Where modern-en departs
  from Chase (notably the *sophia* → "philosophic wisdom" correction) I judged it against standard
  scholarly usage, not against Aristotle's text.
- I did **not** verify that the verse-quotation-stays-archaic convention is applied consistently
  across all quotations in the book; I saw it in one place (Book 8 ¶8).
- I did not audit `modern-da`.
- Candidate B (Peters 1881) is recorded as **unverified** — I did not open it. "No suitable
  rights-clear alternative found in this search" is what I mean; I am not asserting none exists.
- Onboarding JSON, Cast/threads data and audio for this book were not inspected.

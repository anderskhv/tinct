People form communities to live. Aristotle asks what would make a community serve living well. The difference takes the argument beyond survival into education, property, citizenship and the purpose of authority.

He compares political arrangements and examines why they endure or fall apart. A form of government can serve a common interest or become a way for one group to secure its own advantage. The familiar names of constitutions do not settle which is happening.

Some of his assumptions are profoundly exclusionary, including his defence of natural slavery and restrictions on full citizenship. Reading carefully means confronting those claims alongside the questions that remain productive.

What would you expect institutions to make possible for the people living under them? And who disappears from the answer when a society decides too quickly which people count as its citizens?

Edna Pontellier has a husband, children and a place in respectable society. On holiday by the sea, she begins to feel how much of her life has been arranged around what other people need her to be.

The changes arrive through sensation before they become a programme: music, swimming, solitude, the pleasure of wanting something for herself. Chopin lets us feel the attraction of these discoveries. She also keeps the demands of affection and dependence close by. Other people's needs do not disappear when Edna begins listening to her own.

That makes the novel more difficult than a simple celebration of escape. What counts as a self worth protecting? Which obligations express love, and which have been imposed without asking? How can someone discover what she wants when the available language already calls that wanting selfish?

Read for the small moments in which permission is sought, assumed or refused. The question is uncomfortably recognisable: how long could you live a life that looks right from outside while feeling absent from it yourself?

Athens and Sparta enter a conflict that will draw much of the Greek world into their rivalry. Public explanations multiply: security, honour, allies, necessity. Each side can describe a reason why the next demand must be met.

Thucydides examines how fear and ambition change what people consider reasonable. A city can defend its freedom while limiting someone else's. A speaker can make a dangerous policy sound inevitable by persuading the audience that every alternative would be humiliating.

The history follows campaigns, disease, civil conflict and deliberation. Its speeches present the pressures of political argument; they should not be mistaken for modern verbatim transcripts. The interest lies in how communities reason when their interests and self-image are at stake.

Read for the gradual alterations in what people permit themselves to say and do. At what point does a society stop recognising its own conduct because each step seemed necessary when it was taken?

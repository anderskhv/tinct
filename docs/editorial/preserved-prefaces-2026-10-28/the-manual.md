Someone insults you. A plan fails. A person whose approval matters decides to withhold it. Epictetus asks where, in each event, your own responsibility begins.

The Manual repeatedly separates what depends on us from what does not. That distinction sounds simple until it touches reputation, ambition, illness or the people we love. We may have built a life around securing outcomes we can influence but never command.

The exercises can be bracing and severe. They ask us to examine the judgment added to an event before treating distress as something the event has simply done to us. Read them as a demanding practice, while testing how the advice meets actual obligations and vulnerability.

A short paragraph may be enough for a day's thought. Which of your desires asks the world to make a promise it has never been able to keep?

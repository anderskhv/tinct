A man and a woman reach for forbidden knowledge. A brother kills his brother. A people escape slavery and struggle to learn how to live in freedom. Long before you open the Bible, you have probably encountered some version of its stories.

That is one reason to read the book of books. So much of Western literature speaks in its images and wrestles with its questions: creation and fall, bondage and deliverance, guilt and forgiveness. Milton's Paradise Lost sends us back to Genesis. Shakespeare's plays draw on biblical stories and language. Reading the Bible lets us hear those conversations more clearly, including the voices arguing against it.

The stories have power of their own. Families play favourites, brothers become rivals, rulers abuse the power entrusted to them. People lie, love, bargain and try to begin again. The question of what a human being is capable of remains open in both directions: cruelty and courage, betrayal and faithfulness.

The voices change as you read. Job refuses to let his suffering be explained away. The Psalms give words to gratitude, fear, anger and abandonment. In the Gospels, Jesus asks people to love their enemies and tells stories that make the neighbour, the sinner and the stranger harder to keep at a comfortable distance.

These writings belong to living traditions of faith. The Hebrew Bible is Jewish scripture, with its own enduring life of reading and interpretation. Christians read the Old and New Testaments together, finding their centre in Jesus Christ. Whether you approach with faith, doubt or curiosity, taking the books seriously means meeting their claims about God as well as their accounts of human life.

Reading them can change what you notice in other books. It can also make familiar words less comfortable. What does it mean to forgive someone who really is guilty? What do you owe a stranger? What would change if you believed that even the person you despise bears the image of God?

The cultural inheritance is one reason to begin. The possibility that these questions might become your own is a reason to keep reading.

A friend needs money. Antonio agrees to guarantee a loan, and Shylock proposes an extraordinary condition. Affection has entered a contract whose wording may matter more than anyone wishes.

The play asks what can be bought, promised, owed or forgiven. Its romantic plots make wealth a route towards marriage, while its central dispute exposes the violence inside religious exclusion. Characters who can speak beautifully about mercy do not necessarily offer it without conditions.

Shylock has been made one of literature's most troubling figures, and the play's antisemitism cannot be detached from the treatment he receives. His claims to dignity and his desire for retaliation make the audience's judgment difficult.

Read the speeches about justice and mercy with attention to who can afford each principle. Does generosity remain generous when it requires the recipient to become someone else?

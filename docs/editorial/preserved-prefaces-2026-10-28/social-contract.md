People live under rules they did not personally write. Rousseau asks what could make those rules legitimate, rather than merely powerful enough to enforce.

His answer involves a political community whose members participate in a common authority. The attraction is clear: obedience might express freedom if people are obeying laws they give themselves together. The difficulty lies in what together means when actual citizens disagree.

The general will is not simply whatever a majority happens to want. That distinction makes the argument demanding and potentially dangerous: someone may claim to know the common good better than the people supposedly authorising it. Rousseau's account needs to be tested wherever participation, dissent and coercion meet.

Read with a decision you strongly oppose in mind. What would have to be true of a political process for accepting its result to feel like an exercise of freedom?

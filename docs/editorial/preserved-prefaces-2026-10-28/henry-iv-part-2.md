Prince Hal has a future that his companions cannot share on equal terms. His father worries about the kingdom and the son who will inherit it. Falstaff continues to turn appetite, invention and shamelessness into a way of getting through the day.

The play feels time pressing on everyone. Authority is tired; rebellion persists; bodies age; stories about younger days become a defence against the present. A role that once allowed escape may now become something a person is expected to outgrow.

Shakespeare lets us enjoy Falstaff's vitality without disguising the people he uses. He also makes responsible kingship more complicated than exchanging bad company for good behaviour. Becoming fit for an office can require decisions that look different from the position of those left behind.

Read for the costs hidden inside the phrase growing up. How would you decide which parts of your former life deserve loyalty and which make it impossible to meet your responsibilities?

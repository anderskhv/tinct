Socrates's friends gather in prison on the day he will die. Their conversation turns towards the soul and whether death is what it appears to be. Philosophy has acquired a deadline.

The arguments are not offered in a room untouched by emotion. Hope, grief and the wish for reassurance are present. Plato makes the reader ask how an argument should be judged when believing it would help us bear something otherwise almost unbearable.

Socrates encourages the examination of objections, including the danger of becoming so disappointed by flawed reasoning that we stop trusting reasoning altogether. The conversation therefore concerns both death and the habits needed to think honestly in its presence.

Read with sympathy for the need for consolation and attention to the evidence offered. What would distinguish a belief that helps you face reality from one that helps you avoid it?

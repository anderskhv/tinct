Gilgamesh has strength, authority and a city to rule. These advantages do not make him a good king, and they cannot protect him from the questions that will change his life.

The epic begins in the world of extravagant action: an overbearing ruler, a wild outsider, formidable journeys and the desire to establish a name. Friendship gives ambition a new shape. The wish to become extraordinary meets the discovery that even extraordinary people belong to a mortal world.

That encounter is part of the wonder of reading something so old. Across damaged tablets and unfamiliar gods, we meet recognisable feelings: the exhilaration of having a companion, the fear of losing what makes life bearable, and the hope that somewhere there may be an exception to death.

Read for what Gilgamesh's strength can accomplish and what it cannot. If lasting forever were impossible, what would remain worth building, loving or remembering?

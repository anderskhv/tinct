Aeneas escapes a destroyed city carrying obligations that will not let him begin again as an ordinary man. He must protect those who survive and seek a future promised to people not yet born.

The journey offers chances to rest, to love and to belong. Duty keeps asking whether he is entitled to accept them. Virgil gives that pressure immense emotional force. A future nation may have a splendid story about its origins; the people caught inside those origins experience bereavement, uncertainty and choices they did not ask to make.

The poem asks what founding a world requires, and who gets to name the price necessary. Its beauty can make a destiny feel persuasive even while its grief resists the reassurance that everything happened for the best.

Read for the voices that complicate the hero's task. When someone says that present suffering will be justified by a greater future, how can the people paying the cost answer?

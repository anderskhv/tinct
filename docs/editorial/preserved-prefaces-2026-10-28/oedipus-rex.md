Thebes is suffering, and its people turn to the ruler who once saved them. Oedipus promises to find the cause and remove it. He believes the problem can be solved if people will tell him what they know.

That promise makes the play move like an investigation. A witness hesitates. A recollection fails to fit. An accusation seems like a political attack. Oedipus's impatience is understandable: a leader has work to do, and everyone appears to be obstructing him.

Sophocles makes confidence in one's own judgment both admirable and frightening. The person most committed to discovering the truth may also have the strongest reasons to resist a particular answer. Responsibility becomes difficult to separate from knowledge and intention.

Even if you know the story's reputation, listen to how carefully each exchange changes what can be believed. What would it take to keep asking an honest question after you realised that your identity depended on the answer being wrong?

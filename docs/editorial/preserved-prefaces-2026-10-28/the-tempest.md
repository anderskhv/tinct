Prospero has spent years on an island remembering a betrayal. When the people involved come within reach, he has unusual power to determine what they see, fear and believe.

That power turns the island into a theatre. Some characters are frightened into self-examination; others discover opportunities for schemes of their own. Prospero can manage an encounter, but managing someone is not the same as knowing what they will become.

The play's beauty sits beside uncomfortable claims of ownership. Ariel wants freedom. Caliban disputes the story by which his home has become another man's domain. Miranda meets the visitors with a curiosity her father's plans cannot entirely contain.

Read for the difference between bringing someone under control and making peace with them. If you could arrange a perfect reckoning with the people who wronged you, what would persuade you that the reckoning was complete?

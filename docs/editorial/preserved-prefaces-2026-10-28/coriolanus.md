Coriolanus has proved himself in battle. Turning that distinction into public office requires another skill: asking the people for approval. He finds the requirement humiliating.

The conflict makes pride difficult to separate from integrity. Refusing to flatter can look honest, but it may also reveal an inability to recognise other people as entitled to judge. The citizens have grievances and vulnerabilities of their own; the leaders who claim to speak for them have ambitions too.

Shakespeare gives political life an uncomfortable dependence on performance. A city needs courage, persuasion and compromise, but the person excellent at one may despise the others. Coriolanus's family adds another audience whose expectations have helped make him who he is.

Read for the difference between having earned respect and believing that earning it frees you from listening. What becomes of public service when the public itself feels beneath you?

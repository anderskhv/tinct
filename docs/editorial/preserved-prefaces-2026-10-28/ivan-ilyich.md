Ivan Ilyich has done what was expected. He has built a career, married suitably and learned how to make his home look right. Then illness makes those achievements strangely powerless.

Tolstoy begins with other people's response to Ivan's death, so the suspense lies elsewhere: in what it feels like to be the person dying. Polite reassurance can become a form of abandonment. A conversation about symptoms can avoid the question everyone knows is there. People who share a room may inhabit completely different realities.

The novella makes ordinary habits newly visible. Which pleasures mattered? Which choices were made because they were considered proper? What kind of company can someone offer when they cannot repair what is happening?

Its brevity gives these questions an unusual force. There is little room to hide in an elaborate life story. Read it while the questions are still available to you as choices: what would have to change if a successful life were no longer a sufficient answer?

A country gentleman reads so many books of chivalry that he decides to become a knight. He gives himself a new name, finds a horse and sets out to put the world right. The world has made no arrangements to receive him.

The mistakes are funny. They can also be painful. Don Quixote sees opportunities for courage and generosity where other people see inns, labourers or practical inconveniences. His companion Sancho Panza has a more immediate interest in food, money and surviving the next encounter, yet he too can be drawn into a promise.

Cervantes keeps changing the distance between mockery and affection. Is the knight's imagination an escape from reality, a refusal of its meanness, or both? What responsibility belongs to people who decide that another person's delusion would make excellent entertainment?

Read for the friendship that grows through the argument. How can you help someone see clearly without destroying what is most generous in the way they want the world to be?

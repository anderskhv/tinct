A young king prepares to claim another country's crown. He needs arguments, soldiers and a story about what their risk will mean. Shakespeare gives him the words to make listeners feel that taking part could define their lives.

The speeches are powerful. Around them, the play preserves other voices: men making jokes, doubting the justice of a cause, trying to survive or explaining what they owe a ruler. Political ambition reaches the people who will experience it as orders.

Henry must appear certain while carrying responsibilities that certainty cannot settle. A leader may inspire courage and still need to answer for what that courage serves. The chorus asks an audience to imagine greatness; individual scenes complicate the invitation.

Read the celebrated speeches aloud, then return to the quieter conversations. What should we ask of someone whose greatest gift is making other people willing to die?

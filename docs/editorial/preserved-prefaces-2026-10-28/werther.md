Werther writes as though every feeling reveals something essential about the world. A landscape can lift him into delight. A conversation can wound him for days. When he falls in love with Charlotte, who is already promised to another man, that intensity finds a consuming centre.

His letters bring us very close to the exhilaration of being alive to everything. They also narrow what we can see. We hear the explanations of a man who can describe his own suffering beautifully, while the independent lives of other people reach us through his needs.

Goethe makes us ask what sympathy requires. To feel with someone is not necessarily to accept the story they tell about their pain. How can love honour another person if it needs that person to resolve everything unbearable in oneself?

Read for the moments when Werther's language expands a possibility and the moments when it closes one down. Intensity can feel like proof. How would you recognise when a feeling has become the only evidence you are willing to hear?

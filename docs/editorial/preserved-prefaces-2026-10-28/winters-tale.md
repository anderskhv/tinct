A king becomes convinced that his wife has betrayed him. The conviction seems to grow from almost nothing, yet it rapidly acquires the force of public authority. Other people's lives now depend on a feeling he treats as knowledge.

The play begins by asking how suspicion becomes a closed system. Reassurance looks like deception; disagreement looks like complicity. A person in power can make an entire court inhabit the story inside his head.

Shakespeare then makes room for a different rhythm: passing years, changed surroundings and people growing beyond the moment that shaped their lives. The title promises the kind of tale in which improbable things may be heard, but emotional repair remains a difficult question.

Read without demanding that wonder erase responsibility. What would forgiveness require if the damage were real, and what could repentance mean when it cannot return the lost years?

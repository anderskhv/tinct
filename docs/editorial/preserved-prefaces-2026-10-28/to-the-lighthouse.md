A child wants to visit a lighthouse. His mother says they might go tomorrow. His father says the weather will be bad. Almost nothing has happened, yet the room is suddenly full of love, injury, hope and disappointment.

Virginia Woolf makes that small moment large enough to enter. We move among the Ramsays and their guests, hearing thoughts they cannot quite share. Someone wants admiration; someone needs reassurance; a painter wants to work without having her effort dismissed. An ordinary dinner becomes an attempt to hold these separate lives together.

Time will not cooperate. The house, the family and the painting all make different claims on what can last. The novel asks us to notice experiences we usually pass through on our way to something more important: being understood for a moment, looking at someone across a table, wanting a day to continue.

Read slowly enough to let a sentence change whose feelings you are inhabiting. What would an ordinary afternoon look like if you knew how much you would one day miss it?

Alice is bored; her sister’s book has no pictures or conversations. Then a White Rabbit runs past, takes a watch from his waistcoat pocket and hurries away. She follows because she wants to know more. Curiosity gives her a direction before she has any idea where she is going.

Once she is down the rabbit-hole, familiar resources become unreliable. The verses she remembers will not come out right. Her body changes size. Polite questions draw impatient replies, and creatures who seem certain of the rules are rarely willing to explain them.

Carroll builds these encounters out of very particular misunderstandings. Listen for a word used in two senses, a question answered too literally, or a rule that serves only the person announcing it. Alice can be sharp and resourceful, but she also brings assumptions that other creatures have good reason to dislike.

The twelve chapters move through conversations, songs, games and interrupted stories. Let the nonsense unfold at its own pace. Alice’s wish to understand makes even a conversation that goes nowhere worth following.

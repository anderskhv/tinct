Anna enters a family already in trouble. Her brother has betrayed his wife, and everyone hopes Anna can help restore peace. The opening makes a difficult question immediate: what does society forgive, and whom does it require to pay?

As Anna's own life changes, Tolstoy brings desire into contact with marriage, parenthood, reputation and money. Love can make the world feel newly alive. It cannot by itself decide what people owe those who already depend on them. The judgment of society is often cruel, yet the injuries involved are not imaginary.

Alongside this story, Levin searches for a way to live through work, doubt and family life. His questions give the novel room to consider quieter hopes as seriously as dramatic passions. Almost nobody is as consistent as their stated principles suggest.

Read without rushing to divide the characters into the brave and the conventional. When a feeling seems to justify everything, how do we judge the claims of people who do not share it?

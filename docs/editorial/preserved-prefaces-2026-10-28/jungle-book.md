A child is raised among wolves. He learns names, boundaries and the laws by which the jungle's creatures live. Knowing those laws may keep him alive. It does not settle where he belongs.

Mowgli's stories make growing up a negotiation between affection and authority. Teachers can protect him, rivals can recognise a weakness, and a community can decide that someone it once sheltered has become a threat. Freedom requires knowledge of the world one is moving through.

The book is wider than Mowgli. Other stories follow animals facing different dangers and obligations, and songs change the rhythm between tales. Courage may involve fighting, remembering a rule, enduring isolation or finding companions who will act together.

Kipling's ideas of order can be compelling and uncomfortable. Read for who is allowed to belong and who has the power to decide. When you move between two worlds, does learning their rules make you free, or teach you more precisely how you can be excluded?

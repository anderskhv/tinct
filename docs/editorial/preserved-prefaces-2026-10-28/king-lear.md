Lear wants to divide his kingdom and keep the privileges of being king. Before deciding who receives what, he asks his daughters to declare their love.

The request makes affection into a public competition. Beautiful language can win material rewards; an awkward truth can look like disobedience. The difficulty is larger than one family's quarrel. What happens when authority loses the ability to distinguish devotion from performance?

As familiar protections weaken, the play turns towards what people require simply to remain human. Clothing, company and a place to sleep acquire a force they seldom possess inside a palace. Shakespeare also makes care appear in people and gestures that official measures of loyalty fail to recognise.

Read for who can speak honestly without being heard, and who learns to listen after the cost has become terrible. If your position stopped protecting you, how would you know whose love could survive?

Hamlet's father is dead. His mother has married his uncle. The court wants the young prince to accept the new arrangement and carry on. Then a claim about the past makes acceptance seem impossible.

Shakespeare gives us the uncomfortable company of a mind that can find an objection to almost any course of action. Doubt can protect Hamlet from being used. It can also become a way of delaying the moment when a choice must have consequences. Around him, other people are watching, guessing and trying to control what can be known.

The play is funny, cruel and unexpectedly intimate. Listen for what changes when a speech has an audience and when a character seems alone. If you suspected that the world around you was built on a lie, what evidence would be enough to make you act?

Socrates stands before an Athenian jury accused of corrupting the young and failing to honour the city's gods. His task, if survival were the only measure, would be to make himself reassuring.

The speech he gives makes another demand. He asks his hearers to consider whether being irritated by a questioner is a reason to silence him. He also explains why he cannot simply promise to stop doing the work that has brought him to trial.

Plato lets the courtroom become an argument about the price of an examined life. What do we owe our community, and what happens when the community demands that we abandon what we believe makes life worth living? Confidence can look like courage or provocation depending on where the listener stands.

Read as a juror as well as an admirer. When does someone else's refusal to make you comfortable become a service you are unwilling to recognise?

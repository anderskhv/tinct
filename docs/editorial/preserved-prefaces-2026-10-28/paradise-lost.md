The defeated rebels are still speaking magnificently. They have lost Heaven, but their leader knows how to make refusal sound heroic. Milton places the reader inside the attraction of a voice whose confidence demands attention.

That attraction is one reason the poem remains difficult. Freedom, dignity and resistance can be invoked in the service of ambition. Obedience can sound like humiliation. How do we judge an argument when the person making it gives our own resentment such powerful language?

The scale eventually narrows to a garden and two people learning how to live together. Cosmic questions enter ordinary experiences: being trusted, wanting to be admired, misunderstanding someone we love. The fate of humanity becomes a matter of how a conversation proceeds.

Read aloud when the sentences grow grand. Then ask what their grandeur is helping you accept. Can the pleasure of hearing a case made brilliantly conceal the weakness of the case itself?

The modern economy can transform the world while leaving many of the people doing the work with little control over their lives. Marx and Engels make that tension the beginning of a call for political action.

The Manifesto is short, combative and designed to recruit. It presents capitalism as an astonishingly productive system whose achievements also create instability and conflict. Class becomes a way of reading relations that may otherwise appear private, voluntary or simply inevitable.

Its compression gives the prose force and leaves large questions open. Readers should distinguish its historical claims, predictions and political demands rather than treating agreement with one as agreement with all. The later history of movements invoking it does not remove the need to examine what the text actually says.

Read with attention to who can bargain and who must accept the available terms. When does a formally free choice conceal a very unequal distribution of power?

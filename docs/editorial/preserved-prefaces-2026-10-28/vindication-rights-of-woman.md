Women are described as weak, vain or dependent, then educated in ways that cultivate dependence. Wollstonecraft asks how a society can mistake the effects of its own arrangements for proof of natural inferiority.

Her argument reaches into manners, marriage and the ordinary training of desire. If approval is the chief reward available, learning to please becomes a survival strategy. Calling that strategy feminine nature conceals the choices that helped produce it.

The book insists on women as rational and moral beings who need education worthy of that claim. Its standards can be demanding, and some of its assumptions remain of its period. The central challenge reaches beyond a request for kinder treatment: people need the conditions in which they can exercise judgment.

Read for the difference between being protected and being enabled. What kinds of praise make a person smaller while appearing to honour them?

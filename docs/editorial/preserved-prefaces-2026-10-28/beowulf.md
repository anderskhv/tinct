A hall built for fellowship becomes a place people are afraid to enter. Something comes in the night, and the king cannot protect his guests. Beowulf arrives from overseas with a promise to confront it.

The monsters are memorable. So is the human world they threaten: shared meals, gifts, remembered loyalties and the wish to leave a name that will outlast the body. Courage matters because communities are vulnerable. It can also bind a person to expectations from which retreat becomes almost impossible.

The poet gives heroic confidence a long shadow. Every treasure has a history; every victory belongs to time; the bonds holding a people together can fail. Listen to the stories told inside the main story, where earlier achievements already carry the knowledge of what followed.

What do we ask of someone we call a hero? And when admiration becomes an expectation that they will always face the danger, what do we owe them in return?

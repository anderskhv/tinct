Faust has studied for years and feels that he has missed life itself. Knowledge has brought neither satisfaction nor a secure grasp of what matters. An offer from Mephistopheles turns that dissatisfaction into an experiment.

The promise is experience: to escape the limits of the study and encounter what thinking has failed to supply. It is an exciting promise, especially when weariness makes caution feel like cowardice. But the world contains other people, whose lives cannot be treated as material for one man's awakening.

Goethe moves between argument, song, mockery and scenes of painful intimacy. Mephistopheles is often funny, and his contempt can sound like clarity. Notice whose hopes become disposable when an intelligent person decides that ordinary restraints are beneath him.

What would count as finally having lived? And how would you recognise the point at which the search for a fuller life had begun to empty somebody else's?

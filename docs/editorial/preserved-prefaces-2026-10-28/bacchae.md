A god arrives in a city that does not want to recognise him. The king sees disorder and a threat to his authority. The stranger he confronts seems unusually calm.

The Bacchae makes the desire for control dangerous without making surrender safe. Dionysus offers release from ordinary restraints, yet divine power is not obliged to become humane. Pentheus's determination to expose what he despises reveals interests he does not fully understand in himself.

The play's fascination lies in that unstable meeting of fear, curiosity and certainty. People who believe they stand outside an attraction may be especially vulnerable to it. People seeking liberation can discover that they have entered another form of power.

Watch what happens when a public argument becomes a private obsession. How can you acknowledge forces you cannot master without handing yourself over to them? And what becomes of judgment when either side insists that doubt itself is an offence?

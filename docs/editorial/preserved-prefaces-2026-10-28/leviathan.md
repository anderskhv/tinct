Imagine living without a power capable of settling disputes or making agreements dependable. Even a person who would prefer peace has reasons to fear what others might do first.

Hobbes builds a case for common authority from that predicament. Security makes cooperation possible; a promise needs more than good intentions behind it. The solution requires individuals to authorise a sovereign whose power can end the competition among private judgments.

The cost of the solution becomes the reader's problem. How strong must authority be to protect people, and what happens when the power established for protection becomes frightening in its own right? Hobbes's arguments about religion belong within that struggle over competing sources of command.

Read with both fears in view: the fear of unchecked government and the fear of what happens when no government can hold. Which danger most shapes what you are willing to permit?

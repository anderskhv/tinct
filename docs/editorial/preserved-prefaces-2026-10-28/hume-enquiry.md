You expect fire to burn and bread to nourish. Experience supports the expectation, but what proves that the future must continue as the past has?

Hume uses ordinary confidence to open a difficult question about human understanding. We form beliefs through habits of mind that may do indispensable work without possessing the rational foundation we imagine. Recognising that does not make it possible simply to stop living by them.

The enquiry also examines testimony, miracles, freedom and necessity. Its scepticism is most interesting when it asks how far an argument can go before ordinary life resumes its claims. Human beings need to act with less than demonstrative certainty.

Read for the difference between having a good practical reason and possessing a proof that excludes every doubt. How would your thinking change if you became more precise about which kind of assurance you actually had?

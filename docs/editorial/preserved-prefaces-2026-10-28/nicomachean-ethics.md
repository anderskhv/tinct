Most people want a good life. Agreement becomes harder when we ask what good means: pleasure, honour, wealth, achievement, or something those aims cannot supply by themselves.

Aristotle asks us to look at the kind of activity a human life could sustain well. Character develops through repeated action; the choices we practise make later choices easier or harder. Knowing the name of a virtue does not yet give us the judgment to exercise it in a particular situation.

The inquiry includes pleasure, responsibility and friendship. It takes seriously the help and hindrance supplied by circumstances, rather than promising that a person can flourish entirely alone. Its account of human life also carries assumptions readers will want to challenge.

Read with an ordinary week in mind. Which repeated actions are making you more capable of the life you say you want, and which are quietly training you for another one?

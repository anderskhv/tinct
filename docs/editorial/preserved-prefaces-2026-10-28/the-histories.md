Herodotus wants to preserve remarkable deeds and investigate why peoples went to war. The inquiry takes him through customs, rivers, rulers, family stories and reports whose reliability he does not always accept.

That range is part of the pleasure. An account of conflict can pause to ask how another community buries its dead or understands a religious practice. The interruption makes the world larger than the immediate quarrel. It also exposes the ease with which people treat their own habits as the natural measure of everyone else's.

Stories of power repeatedly meet reversals of fortune. Success encourages a ruler to imagine that limits are for other people. A warning can be clear and still fail to matter to the person hearing it.

Read with curiosity about the sources as well as the tales. What would change in your judgment of a conflict if you took the other side's way of life seriously enough to ask how it made sense?

Romeo and Juliet meet in a world that already has a place for each of them. Their families are enemies. Other people have plans for their futures. Their attraction makes those arrangements suddenly seem unreal.

The play gives young love extraordinary verbal energy. The lovers can make a shared world in a few lines. Outside that world, friends joke, parents negotiate and insults demand a response. Private hope depends on events neither lover can command.

Shakespeare announces tragedy at the beginning, so knowledge of the outcome does not remove the suspense. We watch how often another possibility appears, and how speed, pride and miscommunication can close it. The adults are not simply absent; they are present in ways that often fail to recognise the urgency in front of them.

Listen to the different kinds of language people use to claim authority over love. What could help a powerful feeling survive contact with the world?

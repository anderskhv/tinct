Rosalind is forced to leave court and enters the Forest of Arden in disguise. Exile offers danger, but it also gives her room to try a different way of speaking, moving and directing her own life.

The forest attracts people with different ideas about escape. Some idealise country life; others notice its discomforts. Lovers find opportunities to be eloquent and to make fools of themselves. Rosalind can explore a relationship while her disguise changes what the other person thinks is happening.

Shakespeare makes role-playing a way to discover feelings rather than merely conceal them. Yet every experiment takes place among people with hopes of their own. Freedom from an old position does not mean freedom from responsibility.

Read for the delight of a character learning how much she can do with an unexpected opening. Which parts of you depend on the role other people have already agreed you should play?

Phaedrus has a speech he is eager to share. Socrates follows him outside the city, and an apparently manageable discussion of love opens into questions about the soul, persuasion and how people learn.

A speech can be elegant without being truthful. It can also be true without reaching the person who needs to hear it. Plato asks what a speaker must know about a subject and an audience before claiming to guide either.

The inquiry eventually turns towards writing. Written words travel, but they cannot answer an objection or distinguish a reader who understands from one who only repeats. A book makes this challenge to books particularly interesting.

Read for the changes of form as well as the claims: speeches, myths, corrections and conversation. When a piece of language moves you, what would show that it has helped you understand rather than simply taught you what to feel?

Crito has a plan. His friend Socrates can escape prison, and the people who love him are willing to help. Remaining seems almost incomprehensible when the sentence is unjust and an alternative is available.

Socrates asks whether the wrong done to him changes what he is permitted to do. The conversation puts friendship, reputation, family obligations and loyalty to the laws in tension. A person can owe much to a community and still be harmed by its judgment.

The argument is short enough to feel like a single urgent encounter. Its difficulty survives the brevity. Is remaining faithful to a principle admirable when others will bear the cost? What kind of agreement exists between a citizen and the institutions under which they have lived?

Read without assuming that either escape or obedience explains itself. What would you need to know before calling one of them the courageous choice?

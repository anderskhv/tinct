Suppose you could act without being seen or held responsible. Would justice still be worth choosing? Plato makes the challenge stronger by asking us to imagine a good person with a ruined reputation and a bad person who receives every reward.

Socrates responds by building a city in conversation. The discussion ranges through education, desire, political power, art and the difference between knowledge and convincing appearances. What begins as a question about your conduct becomes a question about the arrangements that shape what you want.

Some proposals are fascinating; others are disturbing. The dialogue gives readers reasons to examine both their familiar society and the authority of someone who claims to know what would be best for it.

Do not hurry to treat the imagined city as a finished programme. Keep the original challenge close: what would make justice good for the person practising it, even when the world rewarded the opposite?

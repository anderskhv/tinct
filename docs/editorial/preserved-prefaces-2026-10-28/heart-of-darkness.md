Marlow takes a job that will carry him up an African river to meet a man named Kurtz. Long before they meet, he hears what an exceptional person Kurtz is: gifted, persuasive, capable of extraordinary results.

The journey exposes a widening distance between those descriptions and the world around them. Trade and civilisation provide a respectable vocabulary. Exhaustion, brutality and indifference reveal what that vocabulary can hide. The question becomes what authority does to a person when its usual restraints have disappeared.

Conrad makes us listen through layers of narration. Marlow is recalling an experience he cannot comfortably explain, and his perceptions deserve scrutiny of their own. The African people around him are often denied the individuality his story gives to Europeans. That limitation is part of what later readers have challenged.

Read for what the narrator sees, what he cannot see and what he chooses to protect. How much of a moral identity depends on the presence of people who can answer back?

Your senses sometimes mislead you. Dreams can feel real while you are having them. Descartes asks how far doubt could go if we stopped making exceptions for familiar beliefs.

The experiment threatens the ordinary world, including the body doing the thinking. Its aim is not to remain permanently uncertain but to discover whether anything survives the strongest objections. From there, the meditations attempt to rebuild knowledge.

The route includes arguments about God, error and the relation between mind and body. Modern readers may find some steps much less convincing than others. That is a reason to slow down, not to treat the famous conclusion as the whole book.

Read each meditation as an attempted change in what you are entitled to claim. How would you distinguish a belief that feels impossible to abandon from one you actually have a reason to trust?

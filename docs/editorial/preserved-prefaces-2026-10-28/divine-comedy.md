A man realises that he has lost his way. The way forward will take him through Hell, Purgatory and Paradise, among people whose lives have become visible in an unsettling new form.

Dante's afterlife makes choices consequential. Habits have hardened into environments; desires shape how people encounter one another. Some speakers tell deeply moving stories about themselves. The pilgrim has to learn whether being moved is the same as understanding what he sees.

The poem grows beyond punishment. It asks what change would require, how love can become disordered, and what it might mean for desire to find its proper object. Along the way are political grudges, intimate recognitions, grotesque comedy and attempts to speak about joy.

Read with room for both attraction and resistance. You may dispute Dante's judgments and still feel the force of his question: if the patterns of your life became a place you had to inhabit, what kind of place would it be?

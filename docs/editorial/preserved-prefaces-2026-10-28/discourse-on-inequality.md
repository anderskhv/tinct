People differ in strength, age and ability. Rousseau asks how those differences became social arrangements in which some command and others must depend on them.

His inquiry imagines stages in the development of human society. Property, cooperation and comparison change what people want and what they fear losing. A person who once needed food and shelter can come to need superiority, admiration and the assurance that others are looking.

This is a philosophical reconstruction, not a modern archaeological account. Its force lies in making institutions that feel natural available for explanation. Inequality may endure partly because the people living inside it have learned to desire its rewards.

Read with attention to the difference between a need and a measure of status. How many of the things you consider essential would still matter if nobody knew whether you possessed them?

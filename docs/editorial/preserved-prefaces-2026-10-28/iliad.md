Achilles has been insulted, and he will not let the insult pass. He withdraws from the fighting. Other people will now discover what his wounded honour costs.

The Iliad makes anger magnificent before showing how much of a world it can consume. Warriors need recognition from one another, yet the struggle to secure it can destroy the people whose regard they value. A decision that seems intolerable to refuse may also be disastrous to pursue.

Homer keeps giving the enemy a face. Men preparing to fight have parents, wives, children and private fears. The poem can make courage thrilling while allowing us to feel what its public celebration leaves out. Even the greatest warrior cannot command the full meaning of what he does.

Read for the moments when someone briefly sees beyond the grievance that has organised their life. If you could make everybody acknowledge that you were wronged, what would you be willing to lose to do it?

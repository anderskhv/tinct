An old, blind man reaches a place where he is not supposed to remain. His name frightens people. Oedipus has become a story that seems to arrive before he does, determining how strangers think they must treat him.

The play asks whether that story is enough. What belongs to deliberate wrongdoing, what to ignorance, and what to circumstances nobody chose? A community must decide whether receiving a vulnerable stranger is a danger, an obligation or both.

Meanwhile, the exile's importance to other people's ambitions does not disappear with his power. Family and political claims gather around a man who wants the terms of his remaining life to be his own. Care can become difficult to distinguish from possession.

Read after Oedipus the King if possible. The question has changed from discovering what happened to deciding how a person may live with it. Who should have the authority to say that your life means only one thing?

Augustine remembers wanting things intensely and then discovering that having them did not settle the wanting. Praise, success, friendship and sexual desire all belong to the account. So do explanations that once made his choices seem reasonable.

The intimacy comes partly from his refusal to let hindsight make the earlier self too simple. He examines what he loved, what he feared losing and why an admitted truth could fail to change his conduct. A person can know what they ought to do and remain divided about wanting to do it.

The book's addressee is God. Its movement towards prayer, memory, time and Genesis is central to the project, rather than a detour from a modern autobiography. Augustine is asking what a life looks like when even its hidden motives are already known.

Read for the explanations you recognise in your own voice. What would honesty require if telling the story well were no longer enough?

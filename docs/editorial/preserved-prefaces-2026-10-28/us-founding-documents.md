A declaration gives reasons for political separation. A constitution distributes authority. Amendments place protections and new commitments into the structure. Read together, the founding documents show a political order trying to state what it is for and how it is meant to work.

The statements are powerful partly because they can be used against the failures of the people who first endorsed them. Claims about equality and liberty existed beside slavery and exclusion. Later struggles would ask whether the promises belonged to everyone or only to those initially able to claim them.

Pay attention to the different jobs the documents perform. An ideal does not by itself create an institution, and an institution can distribute power without settling every question of justice.

What should loyalty to a country's founding commitments require when those commitments become the strongest reason to demand that the country change?

It is easy to defend freedom when the person exercising it agrees with us. Mill is interested in the harder case: a person whose opinion or way of life we find mistaken, offensive or foolish.

The threat can come from government, but it can also come from neighbours and respectable opinion. A society may leave a choice legally available while making it socially almost impossible. Mill asks when interference is justified and when concern for another person's welfare becomes a claim to own their life.

His defence of disagreement also challenges our complacency about true beliefs. A conviction repeated without understanding can lose much of its meaning, even if it happens to be correct. Hearing objections is part of learning what we actually believe.

Read with someone you find difficult in mind. What harm would justify restricting their freedom, and how carefully have you separated that harm from your wish that they would live differently?

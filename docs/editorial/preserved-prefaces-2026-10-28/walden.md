A person needs somewhere to live, something to eat and a way to spend the day. Thoreau asks how those requirements became a life so crowded with work and possessions that there is little time to inhabit it.

His cabin becomes a place to examine costs. Money is one measure; time and attention are others. A convenience may consume more life than it saves. A plain afternoon may offer more than a project undertaken chiefly to appear successful.

The book is argumentative, observant and sometimes deliberately provocative. Its account of simplicity should be read alongside the relationships and circumstances that made the experiment possible. The pond is close to a town, and the writing grows through encounters as well as solitude.

Read for the moments when attention changes the value of something familiar. If you counted what you own in hours of your life, which possessions would still seem like bargains?

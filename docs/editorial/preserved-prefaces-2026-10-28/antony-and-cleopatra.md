Antony has responsibilities across the Roman world and a life with Cleopatra in Egypt that makes those responsibilities feel distant. Other people are watching the distance grow and calculating what it might offer them.

Shakespeare refuses to make either world sufficient. Political discipline can look cold and calculating; passionate freedom can become careless of the people required to sustain it. Antony and Cleopatra are lovers and rulers, so their private choices travel far beyond the room in which they are made.

Their language can make life seem larger, more generous and more beautiful. It can also make self-deception magnificent. The play asks what happens when a person's public identity and deepest attachment demand incompatible performances.

Listen for the struggle to determine how an action will be remembered. Which would matter more to you: living the life you wanted, or being seen to have lived a great one?

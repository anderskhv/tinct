Jason has found a marriage that offers him a better future. Medea, who helped him reach his present position, is expected to accept the arrangement. She is a foreigner with children and very little protection.

Her account of what has been done to her is compelling. Euripides makes us hear the vulnerability built into marriage and dependence, and the ease with which a man can present his advantage as everybody's best interest. Sympathy comes quickly. It will not remain a simple guide.

The play presses on what happens when an injury becomes a claim to unlimited retaliation. Recognition matters: Medea wants Jason to understand the cost of treating her as someone who can be put aside. Other people stand between that desire and its fulfilment.

Read for the difficult distance between hearing a grievance and endorsing what follows from it. If the person who hurt you finally felt your pain, what would that repair?

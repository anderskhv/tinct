Pip is a boy who comes to believe that the life he has is something to escape. An encounter with wealth and beauty makes his familiar world look coarse. Then an unexpected opportunity seems to promise that he can become the person he ought to have been all along.

Dickens makes this desire easy to understand and painful to watch. Pip wants education, refinement and love. He also begins measuring people by standards that make some of their best qualities difficult to see. Gratitude can feel humiliating when we are determined to have outgrown the person who needed help.

The novel is full of secrets, frightening encounters and extravagant personalities, but its sharpest suspense concerns Pip's interpretation of his own life. Who has helped him? What does he believe he deserves? What happens to affection when it becomes entangled with an imagined future?

Read with an ear for the older narrator looking back at his younger self. If you could become the person you have always wanted to be, who might you find it convenient to forget?

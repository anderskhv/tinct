Niels wants to be a poet. He also wants to live honestly, without religious assurances he cannot believe. These aspirations seem to promise freedom. Living them will prove less straightforward.

Jacobsen gives Niels an intense inner world: imagined futures, attractions, conversations and the feeling that life is about to begin in earnest. The distance between imagining an experience and meeting another person inside it can be painful. People have desires of their own, and they do not reliably become the figures we need in our private drama.

Questions of faith remain close to these disappointments. It is one thing to reject a consoling belief in argument; another to discover what will sustain you when consolation is what you want. The novel asks how conviction survives contact with loneliness and grief without pretending that suffering settles an argument.

Read for the delicacy with which a hope forms before the world has agreed to it. How much courage does it take to inhabit the life you actually have?

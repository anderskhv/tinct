A group of men agree to give speeches in praise of love. Each offers an account that seems to disclose something about the speaker as well as the subject. Love becomes courage, longing, appetite, education and the pursuit of something beyond possession.

Plato lets the conversation rise towards large claims without losing its setting. These are people with reputations, histories and complicated relationships. A beautiful explanation of love may be tested by the arrival of someone who has experienced the speaker's conduct more directly.

The question shifts from whom we desire to what we think that person could give us. Completion? Recognition? A way of participating in something that lasts? The answers can make romantic longing seem both more generous and more demanding.

Read the speeches beside the behaviour around them. What would it mean to love a person without requiring them to provide an answer to everything unfinished in yourself?

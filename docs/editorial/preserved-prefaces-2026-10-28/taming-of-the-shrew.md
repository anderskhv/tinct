People describe Katherine as difficult. That description allows them to treat marrying her as a problem somebody else must solve. Petruchio arrives with confidence, material ambitions and an interest in proving he can manage what others cannot.

The play turns courtship into bargaining, verbal combat and an exercise of authority. Some exchanges are exuberantly funny. Others depend on deprivation and humiliation. The question of how to perform them is inseparable from the question of what we are being asked to enjoy.

An opening frame introduces the possibility of a person being persuaded to inhabit a role arranged by others. Inside the main action, names, disguises and claims of obedience keep making appearances unstable.

Read with attention to who gets to define what another person's behaviour means. If someone agrees with you after you have made disagreement costly, what exactly have you won?

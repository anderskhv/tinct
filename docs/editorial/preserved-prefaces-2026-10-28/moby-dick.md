Ishmael goes to sea because remaining on land has become difficult to bear. On a whaling ship, he will find hard work, strange companionship and a captain whose purpose begins to absorb everyone else's.

Ahab wants a particular white whale. The voyage can be described as a commercial undertaking, but the captain's grievance gives it a different direction. How does one man's injury become the business of an entire crew? What makes people surrender their judgment to somebody who seems absolutely sure?

The book itself refuses to stay inside one purpose. It becomes a workplace account, a sermon, a joke, an argument about classification, a meditation on colour. Its sailors come from different parts of the world; its narrator keeps finding that the world is larger than any explanation he can give it.

Let the digressions widen the sea around the pursuit. Ahab's certainty can be intoxicating. Ishmael's willingness to wonder offers another way of being alive. Which voice do you trust when a person tells you that everything will make sense once one enemy has been destroyed?

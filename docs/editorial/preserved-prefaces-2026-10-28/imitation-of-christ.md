It is possible to know a great deal about goodness and remain difficult to live with. The Imitation of Christ returns insistently to that embarrassment.

Its short reflections ask what learning, reputation and spiritual ambition are worth if they do not change conduct. The challenge becomes personal in ordinary situations: resentment at correction, the desire to be noticed, impatience with another person's weakness. Even religious seriousness can become a way of admiring oneself.

The book speaks from within Christian devotion. Its goal is life with Christ, approached through humility, prayer, inward discipline and the relinquishing of self-importance. Some of its austerity will feel demanding to a modern reader; that pressure belongs to the work.

Read in small portions and test a sentence against a real encounter. Which would be harder for you: explaining the right principle, or quietly accepting the inconvenience of living by it?

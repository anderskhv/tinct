## Bletchley Park

**Bletchley Park** is an [English country house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_country_house) and estate in [Bletchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bletchley), [Milton Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Keynes) (Buckinghamshire), that became the principal centre of [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) [code-breaking during the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_cryptography). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), the estate housed the [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Code_and_Cypher_School) (GC&CS), which regularly penetrated the secret communications of the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) – most importantly the German [Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine) and [Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_cipher) ciphers. The GC&CS team of codebreakers included [John Tiltman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tiltman), [Dilwyn Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilwyn_Knox), [Alan Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing), [Margaret Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Rock), [Mavis Lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavis_Lever), [Harry Golombek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Golombek), [Gordon Welchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Welchman), [Hugh Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conel_Hugh_O'Donel_Alexander), [Donald Michie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Michie), [Bill Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._T._Tutte) and [Stuart Milner-Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Milner-Barry).

The team at Bletchley Park, [75% women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Bletchley_Park), devised automatic machinery to help with decryption, culminating in the development of [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_computer), the world's first programmable digital electronic computer. Codebreaking operations at Bletchley Park ended in 1946 and all information about the wartime operations was classified until the mid-1970s. After the war it had various uses and now houses the Bletchley Park museum.

## House and pre-war history

It was first known as Bletchley Park after its purchase in 1877 by the architect [Samuel Lipscomb Seckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Lipscomb_Seckham), who built a house there. The estate of 581 acres (235 ha) was bought in 1883 by Sir [Herbert Samuel Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Samuel_Leon), who expanded the house into what architect [Landis Gores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landis_Gores) called a "maudlin and monstrous pile", combining [Victorian Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Gothic), [Tudor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival_architecture), and [Dutch Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Baroque_architecture) styles. After the death of Herbert Leon in 1926, the estate continued to be occupied by his widow Fanny Leon (née Higham) until her death in 1937. In 1938, the mansion and much of the site was bought by a builder for a housing estate, but in May 1938 Admiral Sir [Hugh Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Sinclair), head of the [Secret Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service) (SIS or [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6)), bought the site for use in the event of war.

## World War II

### Preparation for War

![Bletchley's Polish Memorial, commemorating "the [prewar] work of Marian Rejewski, Jerzy Różycki and Henryk Zygalski, mathematicians of the Polish intelligence service, in first breaking the Enigma code. Their work greatly assisted the Bletchley Park code breakers and contributed to the Allied victory in World War II."](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Bp-polish-codebreakers-plaque.jpg/250px-Bp-polish-codebreakers-plaque.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Sinclair bought the mansion and 58 acres (23 ha) of land for use by SIS. A key advantage seen by Sinclair and his colleagues (inspecting the site under the cover of "Captain Ridley's shooting party") was Bletchley's geographical centrality. It was almost immediately adjacent to [Bletchley railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bletchley_railway_station), where the "[Varsity Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_Line)" between [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) and [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) – whose universities were expected to supply many of the code-breakers – met the main [West Coast railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Main_Line) connecting London, [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool), [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) and [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). [Watling Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watling_Street), the main road linking London to the north-west (subsequently the [A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_road_(Great_Britain))) was close by, and high-volume communication links were available at the telegraph and telephone repeater station in nearby [Fenny Stratford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenny_Stratford). Sinclair bought the park using £6,000 (£349,000 today) of his own money as the Government said they did not have the budget to do so.

### Section D and the explosives school

Before the site was considered for use by GC&CS, it had been initially selected by [Laurence Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Grand) and established to be a school for his own section of the SIS called the [Section for Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_for_Destruction), or Section D, a paramilitary outfit specialising in [irregular warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irregular_warfare), [guerrilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare), and [sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabotage). This site was selected to be an experimental [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) station and [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive) schoolhouse for a rather pyrotechnical team belonging to Section D. Modern sabotage from a scientific approach was a brand new field of science, and Bletchley was only one of many such early locations where new ingenious devices were studied. Grand was asked once to explain the conception behind this outfit, to which his response was: "We just want to blow off people's hats."

They began their programs here on 15 June 1939. The chemical engineer Colin Meek (D/X1), a recent graduate from [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University), employee of [Imperial Chemical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries) and one of the early members of the team to invent [plastic explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_explosive), was selected to train recruits in the composition and usage of [demolitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolitions) and [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives) under the explosives program schoolmaster, Dr. Drane (D/X).

However, when the war officially began, and the codebreakers finally moved in, they constantly complained that the noise of the explosives being tested by Section D made them lose their concentrations. The explosives testing of Section D was then moved to [Aston House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_House) in November, several months after the codebreakers had set up shop.

### Early work

Five weeks before the [outbreak of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), Warsaw's [Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów) revealed [its achievements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma) in breaking Enigma to astonished French and British personnel. The British used the Poles' information and techniques, and the [Enigma clone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Enigma_doubles) sent to them in August 1939, which greatly increased their (previously very limited) success in decrypting Enigma messages.

![Flow of information from an intercepted Enigma message](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Information_Flow_Bletchley_Park_Enigma_Messages.png/500px-Information_Flow_Bletchley_Park_Enigma_Messages.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

The first personnel of the [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Code_and_Cypher_School) (GC&CS) moved to Bletchley Park on 15 August 1939. The Naval, Military, and Air Sections were on the ground floor of the mansion, together with a telephone exchange, teleprinter room, kitchen, and dining room; the top floor was allocated to [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6). Construction of the wooden huts began in late 1939, and Elmers School, a neighbouring boys' boarding school in a Victorian Gothic redbrick building by a church, was acquired for the Commercial and Diplomatic Sections.

The only direct enemy damage to the site was done 20–21 November 1940 by three bombs probably intended for [Bletchley railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bletchley_railway_station); Hut 4, shifted two feet off its foundation, was winched back into place as work inside continued.

### Action This Day

During a morale-boosting visit on 9 September 1941, [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) reportedly remarked to Denniston or Menzies: "I told you to leave no stone unturned to get staff, but I had no idea you had taken me so literally." Six weeks later, having failed to get sufficient typing and unskilled staff to achieve the productivity that was possible, Turing, Welchman, Alexander and Milner-Barry wrote directly to Churchill. His response was "Action this day make sure they have all they want on extreme priority and report to me that this has been done."

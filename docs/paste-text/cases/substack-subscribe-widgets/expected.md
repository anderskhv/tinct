## Notes from a Slow Reading Group

Six weeks in, eleven of us are still reading, which is three more than I predicted. This week we reached Rome, and the honeymoon that is not one.

### Dorothea in the Vatican

Chapter 20 opens with Dorothea crying alone in her rooms, and Eliot will not let us look away from how ordinary the cause is. Nothing has happened. That is the point. As one member put it in the thread, _“the tragedy is a schedule.”_

Several of us marked the sentence about the roar on the other side of silence. It is quoted so often that it is easy to forget its job here: it explains why Dorothea cannot simply be told what is wrong.

### Will, at last

Will Ladislaw arrives as a breath of air, and we argued about whether Eliot wants us to trust that feeling. Half the group thinks he is the novel's conscience; the other half thinks he is a charming man with no plans.

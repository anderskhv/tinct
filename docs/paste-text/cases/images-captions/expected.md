## Field notes: the bookbinders of Leiden

The workshop is down three steps from the canal, and the first thing you notice is the smell: glue, leather, and old paper warming under a lamp. 📚

![The standing press, made in 1888 and still in daily use.](https://www.paperroutes.example/uploads/2026/09/press-2048x1366.jpg "A cast-iron book press with a stack of boards")

Marieke has bound books here for thirty-one years. She showed me how a text block is sewn on tapes, then rounded with a hammer until the spine curves like the back of a hand.

![Sewing the signatures.](https://www.paperroutes.example/uploads/2026/09/sewing-800.jpg "Hands sewing signatures on a frame")

![Rounding.](https://www.paperroutes.example/uploads/2026/09/rounding-800.jpg "Rounding the spine with a hammer")

![](https://www.paperroutes.example/uploads/2026/09/backing-800.jpg)

She does not use a computer for anything in the shop except the invoices.

![](https://www.paperroutes.example/uploads/2026/09/marieke-portrait.jpg "Marieke at her bench, holding a bone folder")

Above the bench hangs a hand-lettered sign. It reads, in Dutch, _Langzaam is ook een snelheid_ — slow is also a speed.

We are the only entities that create explanatory knowledge, knowledge can transform the universe (multiverse, actually) without limit, and so the future could be infinitely good. ## 2. Zero-based companies: why AI startups will disrupt enterprise

**Thesis:** Zero-based budgeting is the consultant's favourite idea that never works inside a running company. An AI-native startup is the only true zero-based budget, which is why this is the entrepreneurs' moment.

**The argument**
1. **ZBB is obviously right on paper:** "If you started from scratch today, what would you build?"
2. **It fails in practice, because a running company can't actually start from zero:**
   - *Information asymmetry:* in a large company nobody can see everything, so nobody knows what a cut really loses.
   - *Loyalty and caution:* you have good colleagues and don't want to lose them, so everyone errs on the side of keeping things.
   - *Budget is power:* headcount and budget are how managers measure status, so every line has a defender and none has an attacker.
   - *Loss aversion:* the cost of a cut is visible and immediate; the gain is diffuse and later.
   - *Snap-back:* ZBB is run as a one-off exercise. There's no feedback loop, so the organisation drifts back within a year or two.
   - *Unmeasurable value:* much of what people do (coordination, relationships, risk prevention) has no number attached, so it can't be weighed fairly.
3. **The deeper problem is top-down design.** ZBB assumes you can design the right shape of a company from the top. You can't; you discover it.
4. **An AI startup discovers it.** Start radically constrained and do everything with AI. That is a true zero base. Hire only when AI demonstrably can't do the job. As you learn, and as AI improves, the right shape of the company emerges from the bottom up.
5. **Incumbents can't copy this.** They'll launch an internal "disruptive unit" and never let it disrupt the core (Clayton Christensen's innovator's dilemma, applied to AI).
6. **Conclusion:** it's the entrepreneurs' turn to go after these companies.

**Make it personal (and connect to Tinct):** you're the example. A former SVP who saw this from the inside, now running a company of one, with AI doing the editions, the code and the narration. One line like "Tinct is 100 books, a reader, an AI companion and narration, built by one person" is the proof.

**Open questions to decide before writing**
- Counter-argument to address: incumbents still own distribution, data, customers and trust. Why doesn't that win? (Possible answer: those advantages protect the old cost structure too.)
- Which concrete industry or function to use as the example (back office, consulting itself, customer service)?
- Tone: a provocation ("your company can't zero-base, a startup can") or an insider's reflection ("what I saw from the SVP chair")? The insider angle is more credible and rarer.
